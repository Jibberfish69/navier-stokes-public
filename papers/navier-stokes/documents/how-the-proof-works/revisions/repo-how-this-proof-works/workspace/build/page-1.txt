Finite kinetic energy leaves room for a very specific danger. A vortex that stretches along its axis has to
narrow across it because the fluid is incompressible. If the velocity changes by an amount U across a
region of width r, then the gradient and the kinetic energy carried by that region scale differently:

As r falls, the gradient can rise while the energy remains finite. The fluid can concentrate its motion into
smaller regions without spending an infinite amount of kinetic energy.

Fix a smooth divergence-free datum u0 , a viscosity ν > 0, and the maximal classical solution (u, p) it
generates on [0, T∗ ):
                             ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,      ∇ · u = 0.

The transport term sharpens velocity through its gradients. Viscosity diffuses those gradients, while
pressure maintains incompressibility. Their balance at a narrowing spatial scale is fixed by the scaling of
the equation.

For λ > 1, set
                           uλ (x, t) := λu(λx, λ2 t),            pλ (x, t) := λ2 p(λx, λ2 t).

A width r becomes r/λ, and a duration δt becomes δt/λ2 . With y = λx and τ = λ2 t, the chain rule gives

                                          ∂t uλ (x, t) = λ3 (∂t u)(y, τ ),

                                   (uλ · ∇)uλ (x, t) = λ3 (u · ∇)u (y, τ ),
