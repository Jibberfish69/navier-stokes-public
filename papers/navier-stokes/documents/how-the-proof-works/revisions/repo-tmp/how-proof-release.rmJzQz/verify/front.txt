How the Proof Works
Part I. Overview
Then the one-line version is:
   u0 −→ {(Vn , Qn )}n≥0 −→ {RN,M [u0 ]}N,M <∞ −→ I[u0 ] −→ S0 −→ u∗ ∈ H ∞ −→ T∗ = ∞.
   This keeps S0 in its exact position in the full pipeline while compressing away (u, p), the endpoint jet,
and the explicit restart step.
   The same chain in words is
                    datum −→ derivative structure −→ finite rectangles −→ intersection
                          −→ smooth endpoint −→ restart −→ global smoothness.
    Read from left to right, the initial datum generates every later object. The derivative structure gives
the finite rectangles, their common intersection reaches T∗ smoothly, and the smooth endpoint lets the same
solution continue past T∗ . This rules out a finite maximal time.

With the intermediate objects shown, the same argument is
                          u0 −→ (u, p) −→ {(Vn , Qn )}n≥0 −→ {RN,M [u0 ]}N,M <∞

    In words:
           initial datum −→ one pressure-complete fluid history −→ its time-derivative structure
          −→ every finite mixed rectangle −→ compatible intersection −→ complete spatial row
        −→ smooth terminal state −→ complete terminal jet −→ extension of the same history −→
                                           global smoothness.
    The first arrow fixes the pressure-normalized maximal history (u, p) generated by u0 . Differentiating that
history in time produces Vn = ∂tn u and Qn = ∂tn p, with the velocity and pressure recurrences kept together.
For each finite temporal depth N and spatial depth M , the producer theorem places the required mixed
derivatives in RN,M [u0 ] through (0, T∗ ). Because all rectangles restrict to the same coordinates of the same
history, they form I[u0 ].
    The zeroth temporal projection of I[u0 ] is S0 , the row containing every finite spatial Sobolev order. That
row makes both terms on the right-hand side of the velocity equation integrable in time at every finite spatial
order, so the solution has a common terminal trace u∗ = u(T∗ ) ∈ H ∞ . Evaluating the pressure-complete
recurrences at T∗ recovers {Vn (T∗ ), Qn (T∗ )}n≥0 . Local existence from u∗ , followed by uniqueness on the
overlap, produces (˜︁

                       p) on [0, T∗ + δ0 /2] as an extension of the original history. That extension rules out
T∗ < ∞.
    Here is exactly how each arrow works.

So everything in the proof refers to this one u0 -generated solution.

3. The producer theorem: u0 supplies every finite rectangle

For every finite temporal height N and finite spatial width M , the same u0 -generated history supplies the
whole-terminal rectangle

                                                        (0, T∗ ).

                                              N ′ ≤ N,           M ′ ≤ M,

Every finite amount of temporal and spatial regularity belongs to one common fluid history all the way to

8. Restart from the smooth terminal datum

The terminal datum

satisfies the hypotheses for standard smooth local Navier–Stokes existence. It follows that a smooth solution
begins at T∗ .


9. Identify the restart with the original history

Uniqueness identifies that solution with the continuation of the original u0 -generated history. Consequently,
the history extends beyond T∗ .


10. Rule out finite maximal time

A finite maximal T∗ thus contradicts the endpoint supplied by the proof. Hence

Viscosity is the link that ties the temporal and spatial directions together. At temporal level n, the
pressure-complete recurrence is
                                              n (︃ )︃
                                             ∑︂   n
                               Vn+1 = ν∆Vn −          (Va · ∇)Vn−a − ∇Qn .
                                             a=0

The viscous term connects Vn+1 to two spatial derivatives of Vn . The nonlinear sum and the pressure
derivative travel in that same equation, so the next time row is never detached from the rest of the fluid
history. Repeating this relation at every finite n turns the temporal Tower and the Sobolev Tower into one
pressure-complete structure.
