                                How the Proof Works
                                               Thomas Birnie


The quickest route into the proof begins with the scale at which concentration becomes dangerous.
Under the Navier–Stokes scaling
                                      uλ (x, t) = λu(λx, λ2 t),
the homogeneous Sobolev norm changes by
                                                               1
                                    uλ (·, t) Ḣ s = λ,s− 2 u(·, λ2 t) Ḣ s .

Below s = 12 , concentration at smaller scales makes this norm smaller. Above s = 12 , it makes the
norm larger. At s = 12 , the scaling factor disappears. That is the critical height: it sees concentration
without weakening or amplifying it by a power of scale.

Picture a vortex tube stretching and thinning. Fluid around the narrowing core turns across a
smaller distance, so its gradients sharpen. Repeated at finer scales, this becomes a Zeno cascade:
energy can remain finite while smaller regions carry steeper gradients over shorter intervals. That
picture poses the question the proof actually answers.

In order for a singularity to form, velocity has to blow up. In order for that to happen, its acceleration
would also have to blow up, which means its jerk has to blow up, and so on, and so on:

                                      u,       ∂t u,           ∂t2 u,       ...

If a singularity were forming, then as we moved upward through these time derivatives, we would
expect to find less spatial regularity, not more.

More spatial regularity, not less

The viscous term gives the link between temporal order and spatial height. In three dimensions,

                                     ∆Vr = ∂x21 Vr + ∂x22 Vr + ∂x23 Vr .

At spatial height m − 1, elliptic regularity reads this relation as

                             ∥Vr ∥H m+1 ≲ν ∥Vr+1 ∥H m−1 + ∥Nr ∥H m−1
                                              + ∥∇Qr ∥H m−1 + ∥Vr ∥H m−1 .

Read from right to left, the relation takes the next temporal response, transport, and pressure at
height m − 1, and reaches the preceding response at height m + 1. Each temporal step reaches two
spatial derivatives higher through ν∆.

That is the reversal. A forming singularity led us to expect spatial regularity to deteriorate as
temporal order rose. The equations point upward through the Sobolev scale instead. They identify
higher spatial coordinates at every finite temporal order. The remaining job is to carry those
coordinates through the proposed terminal time.

This is where the opening expectation is overturned. Moving upward through velocity, acceleration,

The local smooth theory now starts from u∗ on an interval [T∗ , T∗ + δ]. Overlap uniqueness makes
