The tube gets longer and narrower. Rotation strengthens around a shrinking core, and the velocity
changes across a smaller distance. The kinetic energy can stay finite while the gradients rise. This
is the regularity contest in physical form: nonlinear transport can keep sharpening the fluid, while
viscosity has to smooth that concentration quickly enough to stop it.
That shrinking core is what makes scale relevant. If its width changes from ℓ to λ−1 ℓ, the NavierStokes comparison is
uλ (x, t) = λu(λx, λ2 t),

pλ (x, t) = λ2 p(λx, λ2 t).

Local acceleration, nonlinear transport, pressure, and viscosity all gain a factor of λ3 . Going to a
finer scale strengthens nonlinear sharpening and viscous smoothing together. Scale alone does not
decide which one wins.
