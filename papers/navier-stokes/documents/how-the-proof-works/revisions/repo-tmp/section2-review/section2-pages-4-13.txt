2     Critical Height and the Derivative Tower
Suppose the maximal smooth time is finite. This section follows what the same velocity field
would have to do as it approaches that endpoint, first through shrinking spatial scales and
then through the temporal and spatial derivative Tower generated by the equation.

2.1    Vortex Stretching
The momentum equation has four pieces:
                          ∂ut            +       (u · ∇)u          = −∇p +           ν∆u
                                                                                     | {z }         .
                          |{z}                   |   {z   }          | {z }
                    local acceleration       nonlinear transport     pressure   viscous diffusion

The central regularity problem lies in the competition between nonlinear transport and
viscosity. The nonlinear term transports the velocity field by itself, so its own motion can
sharpen the gradients it carries. Viscosity acts on those gradients in the opposite direction
and smooths sharp variations.
The hope is simple. Viscosity smooths the fluid faster than nonlinear transport can sharpen it.
If that remains true at every scale, the velocity stays smooth. A finite-time singularity would
require the sharpening to keep moving into finer structure until some derivative becomes
unbounded before viscosity smooths it out.

Navier–Stokes Scaling
A shrinking width changes space, velocity, pressure, and time together. The equation itself
fixes that relation. For λ > 0, define
                     uλ (x, t) := λu(λx, λ2 t),            pλ (x, t) := λ2 p(λx, λ2 t).
Each of the four momentum terms receives the same factor:
                                                                                     
           ∂t uλ (x, t) = λ3 (∂t u)(λx, λ2 t), (uλ · ∇)uλ (x, t) = λ3 (u · ∇)u (λx, λ2 t),

This scaling compares solutions at different scales. A cascade inside one fixed solution is an
additional dynamical claim.

2.2    The temporal Tower
A singularity would not appear beside the velocity field as a separate event. It would be
a loss of regularity taking shape inside that field as the terminal time is approached. The
velocity may be growing or sharpening, and the rate at which it changes has a rate of change
of its own. The same reading continues through every later response. At a fixed spatial
coordinate, the sequence is

                               u,       ∂t u,     ∂t2 u,         ∂t3 u,     ....

of the same field, not separate singularity scenarios. Together they form the temporal Tower.
