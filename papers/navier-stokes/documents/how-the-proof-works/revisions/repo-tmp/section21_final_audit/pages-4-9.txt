2     Critical Height and the Derivative Tower
Finite energy and the classical critical estimates still allow the velocity to concentrate on
smaller scales before a finite endpoint. Any such concentration has to be produced inside the
same Navier–Stokes evolution fixed in Section 1.

2.1    Vortex Stretching
The momentum equation puts the whole regularity problem into one balance:
                          ∂ut            +       (u · ∇)u          = −∇p +          ν∆u
                                                                                    | {z }         .
                          |{z}                   |   {z   }         | {z }
                    local acceleration       nonlinear transport    pressure   viscous diffusion

For regularity, the central competition is between nonlinear transport and viscosity. The
nonlinear term can sharpen the velocity field by carrying its existing variation into smaller
distances. Viscosity acts on that variation in the opposite direction and diffuses the resulting
gradients.
    The hope is simple. Viscosity smooths the flow faster than the nonlinear term can sharpen
it. If viscosity always wins this competition, the velocity remains smooth. A finite-time
singularity would require nonlinear sharpening to keep producing finer structure and larger
derivatives quickly enough for some derivative of u to become unbounded before viscosity
erases it. In three dimensions, vortex stretching is the motion that can keep this sharpening
alive.

2.2    The temporal Tower
A singularity would not appear beside the velocity field as a separate event. It would be
a loss of regularity taking shape inside that field as the terminal time is approached. The
velocity may be growing or sharpening, and the rate at which it changes has a rate of change
of its own. The same reading continues through every later response. At a fixed spatial
coordinate, the sequence is

                           u,      ∂t u,     ∂t2 u,      ∂t3 u,   ....

Each row is the Eulerian time derivative of the one before it. They are successive responses
of the same field, not separate singularity scenarios. Together they form the temporal Tower.
Write
                            Vn := ∂tn u,    Qn := ∂tn p,    n ∈ N0 .                   (2.2.1)
Every finite differentiation below is taken on a strict interval where the solution remains
classical.
    Navier–Stokes tells us exactly what those rows contain. For V0 = u,
