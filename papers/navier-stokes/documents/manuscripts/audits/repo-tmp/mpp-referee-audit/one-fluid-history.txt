    One Fluid History Through the Terminal Time
    Global Closure from Compatible Mixed Sobolev Rectangles for the
               Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes Equations

                                           Thomas Birnie


                                              Abstract
          For smooth divergence-free finite-energy data on R3 , the datum-generated whole-
      terminal theorem supplies every finite adjacent Sobolev rectangle of the one maximal
      Navier–Stokes history. We prove the global closure of that theorem. Direct differentia-
      tion produces the pressure-normalized mixed jet. Different finite parts agree because
      they are derivatives of the same solution, and their overlap gives its complete spatial
      row, not an infinite norm sum or a sequence of replacement solutions. The equation
      then makes the nonlinear pressure-completed interaction integrable at every Sobolev
      order. The velocity reaches the alleged terminal time in every H m , and the compatible
      traces define one endpoint in H ∞ . Adjacent rows carry every higher temporal response
      to that same time. The differentiated pressure and momentum equations identify
      the actual endpoint jet. A uniform late-slice local construction overlaps the original
      history, and uniqueness extends it beyond the alleged maximal time. This proves global
      smoothness from the datum theorem. The critical H 3/2 dissipation and H 1/2 height
      follow as consequences of the same compatible family.


1    The terminal problem belongs to one history
Fix ν > 0 and a real-valued divergence-free datum

                                             u0 ∈ Sσ (R3 ).

We consider the unforced incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

                 ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,             ∇ · u = 0,     u(0) = u0 .             (1)

The pressure is normalized by decay at spatial infinity. Classical local theory gives a
unique smooth solution on a maximal interval [0, T∗ ), where 0 < T∗ ≤ ∞; see [2, 3]. Global
regularity is the assertion that this maximal time cannot be finite in the whole-space
Millennium formulation [1].
    The point of departure is simple but decisive. If T∗ < ∞, the object approaching T∗ is
still the velocity field generated by u0 . Its time derivatives, spatial derivatives, pressure
responses, and endpoint traces are not rival evolutions. They are simultaneous readings
of one motion. The proof succeeds only if every finite reading reaches the same terminal
state.
    The ordinary energy calculation already preserves part of that identity. Taking the L2
inner product of (1) with u, using incompressibility, and integrating in time gives

Thus kinetic energy cannot be the quantity that disappears at a finite endpoint. What

be carried through the time at which classicality was alleged to stop?
   The whole-terminal datum theorem answers this question through finite adjacent
rectangles. Before giving the definitions, we state the closure it forces.

Theorem 1.1 (Global closure of the datum theorem). Let u0 ∈ Sσ (R3 ) and ν > 0. The
unique maximal classical solution of (1) obeying the whole-terminal datum theorem in
theorem 3.2 satisfies T∗ = ∞,

                                   u, p ∈ C ∞ ([0, ∞) × R3 ),

and the energy identity (2) holds for every finite time.

    Assume T∗ < ∞. The energy identity supplies only the base control

                             u ∈ L∞ (0, T∗ ; L2 ) ∩ L2 (0, T∗ ; H 1 ).

It does not place a chosen higher response and its time derivative in an adjacent Sobolev
pair uniformly through T∗ . Such a pair is exactly what creates a middle-order trace. The
datum theorem in section 3 supplies every fixed finite pair on the whole terminal interval.
Their shared entries are derivatives of the same history, so the complete spatial row reaches
one H ∞ endpoint. The equation recovers the actual pressure-complete jet there, and a
late-slice restart continues the history past T∗ . No bound uniform over all derivative orders
is used.

3     Whole-terminal adjacent rectangles
3.1   Why the basic unit has two adjacent rows
Suppose a Hilbert-valued function v satisfies

                       v ∈ L2 (0, T∗ ; H m+1 ),                 vt ∈ L2 (0, T∗ ; H m−1 ).

The first row measures one derivative more than the desired trace; the second measures
one derivative less. Their pairing lands exactly at the middle order. For smooth v,
                                                         Z t
                ∥v(t)∥2H m − ∥v(r)∥2H m = 2 Re                   ⟨vt (s), v(s)⟩H m−1 ,H m+1 ds.             (8)

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

                 ∥v(t)∥2H m − ∥v(r)∥2H m ≤ 2 ∥vt ∥L2 (r,t;H m−1 ) ∥v∥L2 (r,t;H m+1 ) .                      (9)

Density extends the calculation to the stated spaces. In particular, v has a unique
representative in C([0, T∗ ]; H m ); this is the standard Lions–Magenes trace mechanism [5].
   The estimate explains the geometry needed at the terminal time. One isolated Sobolev
row does not carry its own time trace. An adjacent pair does. A finite rectangle stores
these pairs for finitely many temporal rows and one chosen spatial depth.

3.2    The datum-generated terminal estimate

one-fluid compatible-intersection program [7]. It is the only nonstandard input used below.

maximal history generated by u0 ∈ Sσ (R3 ), and suppose T∗ < ∞. For every finite N, M ∈
N0 , there is a constant CN,M = CN,M (u0 , ν, T∗ ) < ∞ such that

Proposition 3.3 (Finite-coordinate realization). The rowwise datum estimate (13) is
equivalent to the family of finite bounds (11), and the resulting finite blocks obey (12).

Proof. Monotone convergence in the terminal cutoff turns each terminal-uniform rowwise

all entries with actual derivatives of the one maximal history. Restricting a larger finite

to each preterminal pressure would create a formal ambiguity in the scalar pressure values,
even though its gradient is unchanged. The decaying Riesz representation (3) fixes one
pressure at every time and passes that same normalization through the endpoint recurrence.


6     Restart of the same history
We now close the contradiction at the single Sobolev level H 3 . The endpoint u∗ ∈ Hσ∞ lies
in Hσ3 , and equation (21) gives

                                    K3 := sup ∥u(t)∥H 3 < ∞.
                                         0≤t≤T∗

Standard local well-posedness gives a lifespan δ = δ(ν, K3 ) > 0 for every datum in the
H 3 -ball of radius K3 . Choose t0 < T∗ so close to the endpoint that T∗ − t0 < δ/2. The
local solution launched from u(t0 ) exists at least until t0 + δ > T∗ . On [t0 , T∗ ) it shares its
datum with the original maximal history, so strong uniqueness identifies the two solutions
there. It is an extension of that history beyond T∗ .
    The embedding H 3 (R3 ) ,→ W 1,∞ (R3 ) closes the transport estimate and strong unique-
ness in this local theorem. Because the extending solution equals u on the open overlap,
all of their velocity derivatives and decaying pressure responses agree there. Continuity to
T∗ , together with proposition 5.1, identifies the right endpoint jet of the extension with
the original left endpoint jet. The continuation is one smooth pressure-complete history,
not only an H 3 extension.
    This extension contradicts the definition of T∗ as the maximal classical time. The
assumption T∗ < ∞ is impossible. We have proved T∗ = ∞, and Sobolev embedding at
each finite time gives
                                    u, p ∈ C ∞ ([0, ∞) × R3 ).
The energy identity (2) remains valid on every finite interval. Together with the Schwartz
datum, divergence constraint, zero forcing, and decaying pressure normalization fixed at
the outset, this supplies the whole-space finite-energy formulation. This completes the
proof of theorem 1.1.

A       Analytic facts used by the proof
We collect the standard estimates used above, both to fix conventions and to make their
finite-order dependence explicit.

A.1     Sobolev multiplication
For s > 3/2, H s (R3 ) is a Banach algebra. More generally, for each integer m ≥ 0, the
Leibniz and Sobolev embedding estimates give

                              ∥f g∥H m+1 ≤ Cm ∥f ∥H m+2 ∥g∥H m+2 .                                       (30)

Applying ∇· loses one spatial derivative. Since the Leray projection is a matrix of the
identity and compositions of Riesz transforms, it is bounded on every H m . Formula (30)
gives (16). These facts are standard; see [4, 6].
