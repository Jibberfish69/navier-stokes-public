                   A Quick Proof of Global Regularity
                  for Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes
             The datum-generated compatible intersection as an estimate generator

                                              Thomas Birnie

                                                August 2026


                                                  Abstract
          Fix one smooth divergence-free datum and one positive viscosity. They generate one maximal
      velocity–pressure history. The equation generates the complete mixed derivative jet of that history,
      and the datum-generated whole-terminal theorem places every finite block of the jet in a compatible
      Sobolev rectangle. The projective intersection of those rectangles is one all-orders object, not a
      collection of unrelated estimates. Its coordinate projections give every finite-order continuation
      bound, a common H ∞ endpoint, and the complete pressure-compatible endpoint jet. Smooth local
      existence and uniqueness then restart the same history across any proposed finite terminal time.
      The maximal time is infinite.

Theorem 1 (Global regularity). Let ν > 0 and let

                                    u0 ∈ H ∞ (R3 ; R3 ),       ∇ · u0 = 0.

The incompressible Navier–Stokes system on R3 has one global smooth velocity–pressure history
generated by u0 , with pressure fixed by the Riesz-transform normalization. In particular,

                                          u, p ∈ C ∞ ([0, ∞) × R3 ).

    The proof carries one object through the following chain:

                          u0 −→ (u, p) −→ {Jn,α , Πn,α }n,α −→ {RN,M [u0 ]}N,M
                                                                                                             (1)
                              −→ I[u0 ] −→ (u∗ , {Vn∗ , Q∗n }n≥0 ) −→ T∗ = ∞.

The point of the notation is custody: every arrow still refers to the same history generated by the
same datum.

4     Same-history restart
Smooth local existence from u∗ ∈ H ∞ produces a solution on [T∗ , T∗ + ε). Its right jet is generated by
(18)–(19), so it agrees with the left jet in (20). Local uniqueness on the overlap glues the restart to
the original history. The resulting smooth solution extends past T∗ , contradicting finite maximality.
Hence
                                                 T∗ = ∞.                                            (21)
Pressure is recovered from (3) at every time, and the mixed recurrence propagates all space–time
derivatives. This proves Theorem 1.
   The proof has now completed the chain in its original order:

                               u0 −→ {RN,M [u0 ]}N,M −→ I[u0 ] −→ u∗ ∈ H ∞
                                                                                                       (22)
              −→ pressure-complete endpoint jet −→ same-history restart −→ T∗ = ∞.

   These are fixed-order, finite-horizon consequences. A sum over every Sobolev order also needs
growth control, and infinite-horizon shell integrability also needs decay. Small-data thresholds,
symmetry classes, and vorticity-direction conditions keep their own assumptions. The list is not a
second route: each entry is a finite projection of the one datum-generated object that already reaches
the endpoint.
