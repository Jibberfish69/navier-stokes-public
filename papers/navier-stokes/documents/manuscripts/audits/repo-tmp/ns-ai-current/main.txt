                   One Navier–Stokes History
Temporal Escalation, Spatial Regularity, and the Smooth Endpoint
                                              Thomas Birnie

                                               August 2026


                                                  Abstract
          We prove that the maximal smooth solution of the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–
      Stokes equations generated by smooth, divergence-free, finite-energy data has T∗ = ∞. The
      proof follows one fixed-viscosity velocity–pressure history. We derive its critical-height balance,
      pressure-explicit temporal recurrence, mixed space–time jet, and the completed height rows
      that expose progressively higher Sobolev energies. The datum-generated compatible-rectangle
      estimate supplies every finite block of that jet across an alleged terminal interval, with the
      blocks agreeing on their shared coordinates. Their zeroth temporal projection lies in every
      time-integrated spatial Sobolev class. The original equation turns this complete spatial row into
      a common H ∞ terminal value; the recurrence recovers the actual endpoint jet; and smooth local
      existence and uniqueness restart the same history. This contradicts finite maximality and gives
      global smoothness.


1    The regularity problem
The three-dimensional Navier–Stokes regularity problem asks whether a smooth solution can lose its
smoothness in finite time [1]. Fix smooth, divergence-free, finite-energy initial data u0 , fix ν > 0,
and let (u, p) be the corresponding maximal classical solution on [0, T∗ ):

                      ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,          ∇ · u = 0,       u(0) = u0 .                     (1)

We want to prove that T∗ cannot be finite.
    Throughout, a smooth finite-energy datum means u0 ∈ H i nf tyσ (R3 ), with the whole-space decay
needed for the pressure normalization and the integrations by parts below.
    We follow this one solution throughout. The velocity is both the state of the fluid and the field
that transports that state. Incompressibility couples the transport to a pressure response across
the whole field, while viscosity acts on the spatial curvature of the same velocity. All three act
simultaneously on a single velocity field.
    The pressure is the first place this unity becomes visible. Taking the divergence of (1), using
∇ · u = 0, and fixing the decaying whole-space normalization gives

                   −∆p = ∂i ∂j (ui uj ),      p = Ri Rj (ui uj ),     Ri = ∂i (−∆)−1/2 .                    (2)

At each time, the pressure is determined by the current velocity field. It is therefore carried through
every differentiated equation below, even when its global pairing vanishes.

5    Why the two towers lead to an intersection
The Zeno picture now has two exact coordinates. Spatial concentration moves energy toward
frequencies Kj ↑ ∞, where (10) assigns increasing Sobolev weight. Compressing the same changes
into a finite time moves through the rows Vj , because (13) requires accumulated variation in the
next row. The mixed jet keeps both descriptions attached to one solution.

11     Conclusion
A finite-time concentration would have to pass through increasingly rapid changes of the velocity,
and hence through the acceleration, jerk, and higher temporal rows. Differentiating the critical
height shows that the same ascent, with the complete transport–pressure response retained, reaches

                                                  15
progressively higher Sobolev energies of the original velocity. The temporal and spatial movements
are balanced and opposing: increasing temporal depth exposes higher spatial regularity within the
same pressure-complete history.
    Smoothness belongs to the compatible family of all finite rows. The datum-generated rectangle
theorem supplies every finite row on the whole terminal interval, and compatibility places them in
the intersection belonging to one solution. The adjacent trace estimate then gives a single endpoint
in H ∞ ; the pressure-explicit recurrence identifies its actual jet; and smooth local existence restarts
the same solution. A finite maximal time would be a smooth joining time, contradicting maximality.
