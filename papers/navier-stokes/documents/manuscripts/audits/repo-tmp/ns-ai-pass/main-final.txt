                      The Reversible Mixed-Jet Intersection
                       in Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes
                                              Thomas Birnie

                                               August 2026


                                                  Abstract
         Begin with one smooth divergence-free velocity field and let it evolve by the three-dimensional
     incompressible Navier–Stokes equation. Its first Eulerian time response is not an independent
     object: the equation expresses it through viscosity, transport, and the simultaneous pressure
     response of that same field. Differentiating again expresses the next response through the same
     ingredients, now distributed across the earlier time derivatives by Leibniz’ rule. Repetition
     produces a pressure-complete binomial jet. Time order and spatial order become two independent
     ways of reading one fluid history.
         The scale-critical quantity H = 12 ∥Λ1/2 u∥22 makes the relation between those readings visible.
     Its first derivative contains the H 3/2 energy, its second contains the H 5/2 energy, and the pattern
     continues. In every row the full transport–pressure response must remain present. Once it is
     retained, the temporal height tower recovers the successive half-integer Sobolev levels of the
     original velocity.
         This creates the central tension. A finite-time runaway imagined through velocity, acceleration,
     jerk, and still higher responses climbs the temporal tower; the equation pairs that climb with
     increasing spatial regularity. Smoothness does not belong to any one row. It belongs to the
     compatible intersection of all finite rows of the same solution. We derive the finite rectangles
     on every classical subinterval and isolate the exact additional estimate required to carry each
     one across an alleged finite terminal time. From that whole-terminal estimate we prove the
     adjacent-row trace, recover one common H ∞ endpoint and its full pressure-explicit jet, and
     restart the same history. No infinite sum of derivative orders and no order-uniform constant
     is used. The endpoint and restart argument is complete; the zero-hypothesis derivation of the
     whole-terminal estimate is the remaining step.

1    The fluid and the laws it obeys
Consider an unforced viscous fluid filling R3 . Its motion is described by a velocity field u(t, x): at
each time and each point, u records both the speed and the direction of the local motion. The
pressure p(t, x) is part of the same state. It communicates the incompressibility constraint through
the fluid, so a local attempt to compress or dilate the motion is met by a response determined by
the surrounding field.
    The equation couples four facts about that motion. The field changes in time. It transports
its own momentum, because the velocity doing the carrying is the same velocity being carried.
Incompressibility forbids local creation or loss of volume. Viscosity responds to spatial variation and
smooths differences between neighboring velocities. These effects do not occur as separate stages.
At every instant they balance one another in one evolution law.
    This rules out two easy but false stories. Viscosity is not acting on a fluid whose nonlinear
transport has been switched off, and transport is not an outside source that can refill small scales for
free. Viscosity damps spatial variation; transport and pressure can rearrange or rebuild it; but every
such rearrangement is made by the same finite velocity field while incompressibility and viscosity act
at the same time. That simultaneous law, not viscosity by itself, is the physical setting of the tower
below.
    We keep that one evolving object in view throughout the argument. Choose

                                 u0 ∈ Hσ∞ (R3 ) ∩ L2 (R3 ),    ν > 0,

and let (u, p) be the maximal classical solution generated by this datum:

                     ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,        ∇ · u = 0,      u(0) = u0 .                 (1)

Here ν measures the strength of viscosity, and ∇ · u = 0 expresses incompressibility. The datum is
not replaced later, and ν is never changed. The velocity may stretch, rotate, transport itself, and
develop structure on finer spatial scales while viscosity and pressure respond at the same instant.
Every derivative and every energy used below is a different reading of that single evolution. An
intersection assembled from unrelated solutions would say nothing about the regularity of any one
fluid.

reading because its time and spatial derivative orders can be tracked independently; the transport
term keeps the particle motion inside that tower.

4    The finite-time escalation problem
Now imagine what a finite-time singularity would have to look like. The velocity is finite at ordinary
times, yet it is supposed to become unbounded as t approaches some finite T∗ . It cannot make that
journey without changing faster and faster. In any fixed norm X,
                                                       Z t
                             ∥Vj (t)∥X ≤ ∥Vj (t0 )∥X +     ∥Vj+1 (τ )∥X dτ.                       (27)
                                                              t0

If acceleration stays bounded over a finite interval, velocity cannot run to infinity there. If acceleration
is the quantity that runs away, a bounded jerk cannot drive it to infinity over that same finite
interval. The demand moves upward again:

                                       u,    ut ,   utt ,   uttt ,   ....

One can picture a Zeno cascade behind this demand: ever shorter episodes of growth crowd closer to
T∗ , and each episode must turn the motion more violently than the one before it. The picture is only
a way of locating the burden. Equation (27) does not prove that every time derivative diverges in
one norm. It says that a finite-time runaway cannot be led forever by responses that remain tame.
     This is where the mixed jet becomes surprising. We might expect a runaway to destroy spatial
regularity as it climbs the temporal tower. The differentiated equation points in the opposite
direction. Each Vn comes with its own spatial derivative column, and the completed critical-height
rows reveal successively higher Sobolev energies of u. Higher Sobolev order gives more spatial control:
for every integer k,

imagined singularity needs progressively more violent temporal change and progressively less spatial
control. The equation couples the temporal climb to progressively higher spatial order. We have not
yet ruled out a singularity; the identities alone do not say that these higher energies remain finite
at T∗ . They do tell us exactly what we should try to preserve. If the full Sobolev tower is present

12    What the historical argument discovered
The argument began with a simple physical question: if velocity were to run away in finite time,
what would have to lead it? Bounded acceleration cannot drive unbounded velocity across a finite
interval; bounded jerk cannot drive unbounded acceleration. Following that demand upward revealed
the temporal tower.
     The equation then supplied the unexpected half of the story. Its time derivatives do not peel away
spatial control. Each temporal row carries a full spatial column, and the completed critical-height
identities expose higher and higher Sobolev energies of the original velocity. Temporal escalation
and spatial regularity are therefore coupled inside the same fluid history.
     The key was to read that statement correctly. No individual row is smooth to all orders.
Smoothness appears when every finite row of the same solution is present and the rows agree on
their overlaps. The rectangles make that sentence quantitative. On every T < T∗ they follow from
ordinary smooth evolution. If their norms remain finite as T ↑ T∗ , neighboring spatial levels give the
terminal trace, compatibility turns all of those traces into one smooth terminal velocity, and the
original recurrence reconstructs the full jet at the joining time.
     We can now walk the argument in either direction. Starting from Navier–Stokes, we differentiate
to obtain the mixed jet, contract that jet into the critical height, and read the higher Sobolev levels
from the completed height rows. Starting from the whole-terminal compatible intersection, we
recover smoothness, the endpoint jet, and finally the same Navier–Stokes history beyond any alleged
finite terminal time. At no point do we sum the infinite tower. We ask only for each finite derivative
when it is needed, always as a derivative of the same fluid.
     That also leaves the exact frontier in view. The pressure-explicit recursion proves the mixed
identities and all preterminal finite blocks. The trace and restart argument proves what happens once
those blocks span (0, T∗ ). What is not supplied by either fact is the bound supT <T∗ RN,M (u; T ) < ∞
from the datum alone. A full global-regularity proof in this route must derive that estimate from
the signed transport–pressure–viscous evolution of the same fluid. Naming the intersection does not
establish that estimate; proving it would close the argument above.
