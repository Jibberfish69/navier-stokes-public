F.2    Finite Pack/Part/Field disjunction obligations
Some branch surfaces produce a finite list of same-solution alternatives rather than a single named

F.3    Inherited-authority obligations
Some entries carry already established CM content. They copy an installed terminal packet, an

    Their mathematical role is restraint. An inherited-authority entry contributes through the
theorem or face sweep it inherits from. It supplies no fourth membership requirement and no
new continuation branch. The manuscript keeps these entries visible so the reader can see why
already-paid material is not being counted again as an independent terminal alternative.

G     How Forward Material Enters the Proof
This appendix records how forward-positive material enters the proof after the CM test has been
fixed. The repeated source files contain many local estimates and route probes. The paper does not
ask the reader to remember those file names. It groups them by the kind of mathematical work
they can pay.
    There are four entry families:

Comparison context. Explains a nearby boundary object and then returns to the Navier–Stokes
                    record.

Export or legacy context.
                     Preserves a result whose force is spent only through a named proof hinge.

Forward supplier estimates.
                      Pays source, carrier, participation, or readout costs for the selected branch.

Receiver or endpoint readouts.
                     Reads the selected packet after Pack and Part have already survived.

Each family becomes proof material at the point where it selects Q, proves a face, or supplies the
member-side readout. Before that point, it is useful context and the proof says so plainly.

G.1    Comparison context
Comparison context is the material that shows why an obstruction shape is natural by placing it
beside a neighboring equation or a downstream export. Its admissible use is narrow. A comparison
may help identify a candidate packet, reveal a boundary behavior, or warn the reader that a route
has changed equation class. The class proof then returns to the same Navier–Stokes record:

                           QNS −→ PackQNS ,    PartN,QNS ,   FieldN,r,QNS .

The comparison object carries no separate membership conclusion. Its value is spent when the
Navier–Stokes packet has been selected and the exact face or readout paid on that packet.

G.2    Export and legacy context
Export and legacy context preserves results that were produced for another surface of the project.
The current manuscript uses them through named hinges only:

terminal packet capture,       membership readout,       class exit,     no-third-branch exclusion.

A legacy theorem that pays one of these hinges keeps its force. A legacy theorem that only repeats
a route label stays outside the terminal proof claim. This rule protects the paper from treating an
old status word as a theorem.

Forward supplier estimates are the gold-side material. They try to pay the equation estimates

G.4    Receiver and endpoint readouts
Receiver and endpoint readouts read what remains after the object and law have survived. On the
pass side they supply a continuation norm, such as the H s , s > 5/2, readout used in the main body.

This is why endpoint work belongs after the carrier and participation checks. A good endpoint
readout certifies the member branch. A failed endpoint readout, after the same carrier and same
participation law are still present, identifies the Field face.

The branch families are listed here so the reader can see how the current manuscript material is

This family accounts for the source-survival, zero-radius refinement, signed-current, no-free-sink,

H.2    Local translator family
Local translator material moves local L3 , Duhamel, or public-critical mass onto a terminal packet.

H.3    Same-ledger family
Same-ledger material asks whether the proposed terminal object is charged to the same witness
record. A preterminal reflection, imported donor, or exported branch has no membership conclusion
until it supplies the same datum, same viscosity, same solution branch, same transported carrier,
and same participation tower. After those data are supplied, the object enters the ordinary witness
tree.

H.4    Class-law family
Class-law material protects the pass-or-exit structure itself. A reopening branch has to produce a
genuine third possibility: an in-class nonsmooth terminal record that satisfies the membership data
and still breaks continuation. The current proof architecture sends such a challenge to one of three
places: terminal CM entry, finite Pack/Part/Field exhaustion, or the no-third-branch closure. A
challenge that pays none of those places is support for a later audit, not a new branch of the proof.

H.5    Local energy and elliptic family
Local energy and elliptic estimates are analytic tools for selecting packets, verifying pressure
participation, and reading positive field scales. They enter the proof at the line where they pay one
of those tasks. For example, elliptic pressure control may help show that the same pressure law still
participates on the carrier; local energy control may help keep the packet readable long enough for
Field. The estimate is proof material only at that paid line.

H.6    Comparison-boundary family
Comparison-boundary material is admitted only after it returns to a Navier–Stokes packet. It can
explain an Euler-like boundary, a fixed-viscosity loss, or a neighboring smoothness mechanism. The
class conclusion is still charged to the Navier–Stokes record. The comparison family closes when the
page writes the same packet, the shared carrier reading, the lost fixed-viscosity participation or field
scale, and the resulting boundary interpretation.
