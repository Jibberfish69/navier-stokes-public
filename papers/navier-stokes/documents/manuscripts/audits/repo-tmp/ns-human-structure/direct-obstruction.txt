5    The Same Terminal Record
The direct route and the CM route ask different questions about the same branch. The direct route
asks whether the equation supplies estimates strong enough to continue the original solution. The
CM route begins with the exact obstruction left after that accounting and asks whether the selected
terminal record is still a same-solution Navier–Stokes object.
    The reframing keeps the original datum, velocity-pressure branch, viscosity, pressure law,
incompressibility constraint, and terminal role fixed. A failed estimate becomes useful only after
it produces a record Q that can be tested as the alleged terminal record of that same branch.
The CM test then asks three concrete questions. Does Q still have a positive same-fluid carrier
or packet? That is Pack. Does the same pressure-viscosity-incompressibility law still participate
on the derivative tower being used? That is Part. Does the retained data still form a coherent
positive-scale velocity field? That is Field.

6    What a Terminal Failure Can Be
The forward work produces many named surfaces. The terminal proof uses them only after they
have been reduced to one object: a same-solution record offered as the terminal record of the original
Navier–Stokes branch. That record is the object under examination. It carries the initial datum,
viscosity, velocity-pressure law, pressure response, incompressibility condition, material carrier, and
terminal role inherited from the branch whose breakdown is being alleged.
