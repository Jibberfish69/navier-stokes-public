9. Identify the restart with the original history

On the left overlap [t0 , T∗ ), the pairs (u, p) and (wt0 , q t0 ) solve the same pressure-normalized problem with
the same datum u(t0 ). Uniqueness gives
                                          (wt0 , q t0 ) = (u, p)       on [t0 , T∗ ).
Continuity at the terminal face then gives
                                                         wt0 (T∗ ) = u∗ .
On the right overlap [T∗ , t0 + δ0 ], the pairs (wt0 , q t0 ) and (w∗ , q ∗ ) solve the same pressure-normalized problem
with datum u∗ . Uniqueness gives
                                      (wt0 , q t0 ) = (w∗ , q ∗ )     on [T∗ , t0 + δ0 ].
The late-slice solution agrees with the original history on the left and the endpoint restart on the right. It is
one local classical history crossing T∗ .
The derivative structure also matches across the terminal face. Let
                                      Wn+ := ∂tn w∗ (T∗ ),          Pn+ := ∂tn q ∗ (T∗ ).
Differentiating the restarted equations gives the same pressure and velocity recurrences as the left endpoint

                                               W0+ = u∗ = V0∗ .
Induction gives
                                       Wn+ = Vn∗ ,           Pn+ = Q∗n        (n ≥ 0).
Every time derivative of velocity and pressure agrees at T∗ . The preterminal solution and the restart join
smoothly as one pressure-complete Navier–Stokes history.
