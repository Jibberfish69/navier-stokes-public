    One Fluid History Through the Terminal Time
    Compatible Mixed Sobolev Rectangles and Global Regularity for the
               Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes Equations

                                           Thomas Birnie


                                              Abstract
          For smooth divergence-free finite-energy data on R3 , we prove that the maximal
      classical solution of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations is global and smooth.
      The proof follows one velocity–pressure history. Direct differentiation produces its
      pressure-normalized mixed jet. The central datum estimate places every finite part of
      that jet, on the entire alleged terminal interval, in an adjacent pair of Sobolev rows.
      Different finite parts agree because they are derivatives of the same solution. Their
      overlap supplies the complete spatial row of that solution, not an infinite sum and not
      a sequence of replacement solutions. The equation then makes the nonlinear pressure-
      completed interaction integrable at every Sobolev order. The velocity consequently
      reaches the alleged terminal time in every H m , and the compatible traces define one
      endpoint in H ∞ . The differentiated pressure and momentum equations identify the
      actual endpoint jet. Standard local well-posedness restarts from this endpoint, and
      uniqueness glues the restart to the original history, contradicting finite maximality. The
      argument also recovers the critical H 3/2 dissipation and H 1/2 height as consequences
      of the compatible intersection.


1     The terminal problem belongs to one history
Fix ν > 0 and a real-valued divergence-free datum

                                             u0 ∈ Sσ (R3 ).

We consider the unforced incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

                 ∂t u + (u · ∇)u + ∇p = ν∆u,             ∇ · u = 0,     u(0) = u0 .                (1)

The pressure is normalized by decay at spatial infinity. Classical local theory gives a
unique smooth solution on a maximal interval [0, T∗ ), where 0 < T∗ ≤ ∞; see [2, 3]. Global
regularity is the assertion that this maximal time cannot be finite.
    The point of departure is simple but decisive. If T∗ < ∞, the object approaching T∗ is
still the velocity field generated by u0 . Its time derivatives, spatial derivatives, pressure
responses, and endpoint traces are not rival evolutions. They are simultaneous readings
of one motion. The proof succeeds only if every finite reading reaches the same terminal
state.
    The ordinary energy calculation already preserves part of that identity. Taking the L2
inner product of (1) with u, using incompressibility, and integrating in time gives

Thus kinetic energy cannot be the quantity that disappears at a finite endpoint. What
remains is a derivative-level problem: can all finite Sobolev readings of the same solution
be carried through the time at which classicality was alleged to stop?
   Our central estimate answers this question through finite adjacent rectangles. Before
giving the definitions, we state the conclusion.

Theorem 1.1 (Global smoothness). Let u0 ∈ Sσ (R3 ) and ν > 0. The unique maximal
classical solution of (1) satisfies T∗ = ∞,

                                   u, p ∈ C ∞ ([0, ∞) × R3 ),

and the energy identity (2) holds for every finite time.

    The proof has one continuous movement. Assume T∗ < ∞. The datum generates
every finite, whole-terminal, pressure-complete mixed rectangle. Their shared entries agree
because they are derivatives of the same history. The resulting complete spatial row reaches
one H ∞ endpoint. The equation recovers the actual pressure-complete jet there. Local
existence and uniqueness then continue the same history past T∗ , contradicting maximality.
    The central issue is the phrase whole-terminal. Smoothness on every strict subinterval
[0, T ], T < T∗ , gives a finite estimate whose constant may diverge as T ↑ T∗ . Such estimates
do not create an endpoint. The datum theorem in section 3 supplies bounds uniform over
the terminal cutoff for each fixed finite coordinate. No bound uniform over all derivative
orders is asked for.

3     Whole-terminal adjacent rectangles
3.1   Why the basic unit has two adjacent rows
Suppose a Hilbert-valued function v satisfies

                       v ∈ L2 (0, T∗ ; H m+1 ),                 vt ∈ L2 (0, T∗ ; H m−1 ).

The first row measures one derivative more than the desired trace; the second measures
one derivative less. Their pairing lands exactly at the middle order. For smooth v,
                                                         Z t
                ∥v(t)∥2H m − ∥v(r)∥2H m = 2 Re                   ⟨vt (s), v(s)⟩H m−1 ,H m+1 ds.             (8)

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

                 ∥v(t)∥2H m − ∥v(r)∥2H m ≤ 2 ∥vt ∥L2 (r,t;H m−1 ) ∥v∥L2 (r,t;H m+1 ) .                      (9)

Density extends the calculation to the stated spaces. In particular, v has a unique
representative in C([0, T∗ ]; H m ); this is the standard Lions–Magenes trace mechanism [6].
   The estimate explains the geometry needed at the terminal time. One isolated Sobolev
row does not carry its own time trace. An adjacent pair does. A finite rectangle stores
these pairs for finitely many temporal rows and one chosen spatial depth.

Increasing M reveals more spatial resolution. On an overlap, there is nothing to choose:

even though its gradient is unchanged. The decaying Riesz representation (3) fixes one
pressure at every time and passes that same normalization through the endpoint recurrence.


7     Restart of the same history
We now close the contradiction. Choose any s > 5/2. The endpoint u∗ ∈ Hσ∞ lies in Hσs ,
and equation (25) gives
                             Ks := sup ∥u(t)∥H s < ∞.
                                         0≤t≤T∗
Standard local well-posedness gives a lifespan δ = δ(ν, Ks ) > 0 for every datum in the
H s -ball of radius Ks . Choose t0 < T∗ so close to the endpoint that T∗ − t0 < δ/2. The
local solution launched from u(t0 ) exists at least until t0 + δ > T∗ . On [t0 , T∗ ) it shares its
datum with the original maximal history, so strong uniqueness identifies the two solutions
there. It is an extension of that history beyond T∗ .
    The condition s > 5/2 is used only in this local theorem: the embedding H s (R3 ) ,→
W  1,∞ (R3 ) closes the transport estimate and strong uniqueness. The endpoint itself has
already been constructed at every Sobolev order. Its differentiated values also match:
passing t0 ↑ T∗ in the extended solutions and differentiating the equation gives (26)–(27),
which theorem 6.1 has identified with the original left jet. Thus the continuation is one
smooth pressure-complete history, not only an H s extension.
    This extension contradicts the definition of T∗ as the maximal classical time. The
assumption T∗ < ∞ is impossible. We have proved T∗ = ∞, and Sobolev embedding at
each finite time gives

A       Analytic facts used by the proof
We collect the standard estimates used above, both to fix conventions and to make their
finite-order dependence explicit.

A.1     Sobolev multiplication
For s > 3/2, H s (R3 ) is a Banach algebra. More generally, for each integer m ≥ 0, the
Leibniz and Sobolev embedding estimates give
                            ∥f g∥H m+1 ≤ Cm ∥f ∥H m+2 ∥g∥H m+2 .                             (33)
Applying ∇· loses one spatial derivative. Since the Leray projection is a matrix of the
identity and compositions of Riesz transforms, it is bounded on every H m . Formula (33)
gives (20). These facts are standard; see [5, 7].

A.2     The adjacent Hilbert triple
Let
               X1 = H m+1 (R3 ),         X0 = H m (R3 ),       X−1 = H m−1 (R3 ).
The dense continuous embeddings X1 ,→ X0 ,→ X−1 identify X−1 with the dual of X1
through the pivot X0 . If v ∈ L2 (0, T ; X1 ) and vt ∈ L2 (0, T ; X−1 ), smooth temporal
convolution gives approximants vε for which (8) is classical. The two L2 convergences pass
the right side to the limit, while (9) makes the X0 -norms Cauchy uniformly at Lebesgue
times. The limit is the unique X0 -continuous representative of v. This is the form of the
Lions–Magenes lemma used in theorem 4.3.

B     Problem fidelity
For the whole-space, zero-force problem, the prescribed datum is smooth, divergence-free,
and rapidly decaying. The construction above produces a global smooth velocity and the
decaying pressure fixed by (3). The energy identity gives
                                    sup ∥u(t)∥22 ≤ ∥u0 ∥22 .
                                    t≥0

At every finite time and every spatial derivative order, the complete Sobolev intersection and
Sobolev embedding give bounded continuous spatial derivatives. Thus the result supplies
the smoothness, incompressibility, zero forcing, pressure, and finite-energy requirements of
the standard whole-space formulation [1].
