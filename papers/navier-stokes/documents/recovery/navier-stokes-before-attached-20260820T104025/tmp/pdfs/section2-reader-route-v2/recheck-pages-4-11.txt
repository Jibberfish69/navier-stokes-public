2     Critical Height and the Derivative Tower
Finite energy does not prevent the velocity from sharpening in one part of the fluid. A
nontrivial change can be carried through less and less distance while the total L2 -size remains
bounded. That shrinking distance is the concentration a singularity would have to exploit.
In three dimensions, vortex stretching is the motion that can drive it.

2.1    Vortex Stretching
The surrounding strain pulls the rotating region along its axis, and the region lengthens.
Incompressibility preserves the volume of every material tube, so if that tube remains tube-like
while it stretches, its average transverse area has to decrease. The contraction need not be
the same in both transverse directions.
    The vorticity itself is not frozen inside that material tube. Viscosity continues to diffuse
it across the moving particle labels. The dangerous motion is the coupled one in which strain
keeps amplifying the rotation while the vorticity core remains concentrated across smaller
transverse widths. If the velocity difference across that core does not flatten at the same rate,
the same variation is being carried through less distance and the spatial gradient grows. This
is the stretching vortex we need to follow.

responses becomes part of the same object.

2.3    The temporal Tower
A singularity would not appear beside the velocity field as a separate event. It would be a
loss of smoothness taking shape inside that field as the terminal time is approached. The
velocity may be growing or sharpening, and the rate at which it changes has a rate of change
of its own. The same reading continues through every later response. At a fixed spatial
coordinate, the sequence is

                            u,      ∂t u,    ∂t2 u,    ∂t3 u,    ....

Each row is the Eulerian time derivative of the one before it. They are successive responses
of the same field, not separate singularity scenarios. Together they form the temporal Tower.
