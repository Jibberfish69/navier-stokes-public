\section{From Critical Height to Compatible Finite Views}\label{sec:FromCriticalHeightToCompatibleFiniteViews}

A finite-time singularity can hide above a harmless-looking velocity value and below the scale seen by kinetic energy.
Critical scaling identifies the first height that remains visible as the fluid is compressed toward finer structure.
Finite-time response then forces us upward through temporal derivatives and outward through the spatial derivatives already carried by the equation.
The resulting mixed jet opens several simultaneous Sobolev views of the same fluid, and their all-orders intersections give the exact analytic meaning of smoothness.
Finite adjacent rectangles make those intersections checkable one temporal depth and one spatial height at a time.
