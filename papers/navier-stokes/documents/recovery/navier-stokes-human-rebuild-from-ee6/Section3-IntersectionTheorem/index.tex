\section{The Intersection Inside the Tower}\label{sec:TheIntersectionInsideTheTower}

The Tower has left us with a distinction the rest of the argument cannot blur.
Viscosity exposes a higher spatial energy in every temporal row, but the
nonlinear transfers remain beside it, so exposure is not control.  We now find
the place where viscosity does make an honest spatial payment: first on a
fixed interval strictly before \(T_*\), then in the adjacent pair of rows that
can reach a time slice.  The trace calculation in
\Cref{lem:AdjacentHilbertScaleTrace} turns that pair into the smallest finite
rectangle.  Once
the cutoff is allowed to approach \(T_*\), the entire regularity problem is
concentrated in whether the same rectangle keeps one finite bound.
