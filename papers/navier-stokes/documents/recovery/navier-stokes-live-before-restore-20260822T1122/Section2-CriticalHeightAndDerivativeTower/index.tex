\section{Critical Height and the Derivative Tower}\label{sec:CriticalHeightAndDerivativeTower}

The question now is what the same fluid would have to do if \(T_*\) were
finite.  Its kinetic energy can remain bounded, so the singular motion has to
appear in the way the velocity changes across space.

Take the cleanest case: a perfectly stretching vortex, with no wobble or loss
of alignment to interrupt the amplification.  Keep that one vortex in view as
its cross-section narrows and the viscous clock shortens until infinitely many
smaller clocks appear capable of fitting inside a finite time.  But naming a
new scale does not move the fluid into it.  The velocity has to cross the
change.  Its first temporal response measures that motion; if it loses control,
the next asks what carried the loss.  Repeating the question creates the
derivative Tower: the proposed singularity must account for every response in
the same fluid.
