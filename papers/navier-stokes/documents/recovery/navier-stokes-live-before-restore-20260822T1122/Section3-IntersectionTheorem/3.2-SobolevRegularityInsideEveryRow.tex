\subsection{The same spatial gain inside every response}\label{sub:SobolevRegularityInsideEveryCompletedRow}

Fix \(r\in\Nzero\).  The scalar energy of the response \(V_r\) at spatial
height \(s\) is
\[
  E_{r,s}:=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^sV_r}_2^2.
\]
The transfer paired with that response is
\[
  \mathcal P_{r,s}
  :=-
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^sV_r}
  {\Lambda^s\!\left[
    \sum_{a=0}^{r}\binom{r}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{r-a}
    +\nabla Q_r
  \right]}_{L^2}.
\]
Pairing the \(r\)-th differentiated momentum equation at this height produces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowSpatialEnergy}
  E_{r,s}'=\mathcal P_{r,s}-2\nu E_{r,s+1}.
\end{equation}
Beginning at \(s=\tfrac12\) and repeating the same induction gives the
differentiated balance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowCompletedHeight}
  \partial_t^nE_{r,1/2}
  =(-2\nu)^nE_{r,n+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{r,j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
The same pattern now repeats inside every response.  Moving down changes the
temporal response; moving across exposes a higher spatial energy; and the
nonlinear transfer remains beside it.  Exposure is not yet payment.  To see
where the spatial gain is actually made, apply the Leray projection to the
differentiated equation.  It annihilates \(\nabla Q_r\) and commutes with
\(\partial_t\) and \(\Delta\), so
\[
  \partial_tV_r-\nu\Delta V_r=-F_r,
  \qquad
  F_r:=\Leray\sum_{a=0}^{r}\binom{r}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{r-a}.
\]
For an integer \(m\geq2\), the Sobolev multiplication used in
\Cref{prop:LocalMaximalHistory} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalResponseForcingEstimate}
  \norm{F_r}_{H^{m-1}}
  \leq
  C_{m,r}\sum_{a=0}^{r}
  \norm{V_a}_{H^m}\norm{V_{r-a}}_{H^m}.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
Pairing the equation one spatial step across this forcing, as in the linear
estimate inside \Cref{prop:LocalMaximalHistory}, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalResponseParabolicGain}
  \frac d{dt}\norm{V_r}_{H^m}^2
  +\nu\norm{V_r}_{H^{m+1}}^2
  \leq
  C_{\nu,m}\left(
    \norm{V_r}_{H^m}^2+\norm{F_r}_{H^{m-1}}^2
  \right).
\end{equation}
This is the local spatial payment.  On a fixed strict interval
\(I=(0,T)\), \(T<T_*\), persistence of the same classical history in
\Cref{prop:LocalMaximalHistory} gives, for the finite indices now in use,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyStrictIntervalResponseBound}
  \max_{0\leq a\leq r}
  \norm{V_a}_{L^\infty(0,T;H^m)}<\infty.
\end{equation}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Thus \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalResponseForcingEstimate} makes the right-hand side
of \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalResponseParabolicGain} integrable.  This is the
unexpected turn in the table: climbing to the next response does not simply
spend the spatial regularity already present.  Viscosity places \(V_r\) one
degree higher, while the equation places its next response one degree lower:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyStrictIntervalAdjacentResponsePair}
  V_r\in L^2(I;H^{m+1}),
  \qquad
  V_{r+1}=\partial_tV_r\in L^2(I;H^{m-1}).
\end{equation}

Now ask for any finite \(q\) time derivatives and \(k\) spatial derivatives.
Repeat the payment through finitely many responses and choose
\[
  R>q+\frac12,
  \qquad
  M>k+\frac32.
\]
The vector-valued time and three-dimensional spatial embeddings
\parencite[Theorem~7.28]{Cerda2010} give
\[
  H^R\bigl(I;H^M(\R^3)\bigr)
  \hookrightarrow
  C^q\bigl(\overline I;C^k(\R^3)\bigr).
\]
Since \(q\) and \(k\) were arbitrary, the fixed strict interval carries
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyFormalMixedIntersection}
  \mathscr V_\infty(I)
  :=\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}
  H^r\bigl(I;H^m(\R^3)\bigr)
  \hookrightarrow C^\infty(\overline I\times\R^3).
\end{equation}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Every finite position in the grid is now filled, but only after the strict
cutoff \(T<T_*\) has been fixed.  Neither
\eqref{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowCompletedHeight} nor
\eqref{eq:BodyTemporalResponseParabolicGain} makes it uniform as
\(T\uparrow T_*\).

The constants may depend on the selected \((r,m)\).  Smoothness asks for each
finite derivative in turn; it does not ask for one constant uniform over the
entire grid.  This is the regularity carried by one history before a chosen
cutoff.  It is not yet the compatible intersection at the terminal face.

A dot can now be finite on every strict subinterval and still diverge as the
endpoint is approached.  To carry a spatial derivative to one time slice, we
have to place it beside the temporal response directly below it.  Those
adjacent pairs determine the finite rectangles.
