L2 (0, T ; Ḣ 1 ). Since T is arbitrary, the identity holds globally. Smoothness and energy equality follow
from (129) and (133). If q is a distributional pressure paired with this velocity and p is the pressure
in (132), subtraction of the two momentum equations gives
                                  ∇(q − p) = 0 in D′ ((0, ∞) × R3 ).
A distribution on a product domain whose spatial gradient vanishes is the pullback of a scalar
distribution in time: testing first against spatial test functions of integral zero and then against
one fixed test function of integral one gives (359). The Riesz formula for p proves the canonical-
representative assertion. In the function-valued case, q(t, x) − p(t, x) = c(t) for almost every (t, x);
both spatial slices tend to zero as |x| → ∞, so c(t) = 0 for almost every t.


9    Conclusion
The proof follows one pressure-normalized velocity history from its initial datum to the endpoint of
its maximal smooth interval. Every finite temporal depth and every finite Sobolev depth determine
a whole-terminal rectangle, and the restriction identities make those rectangles compatible. Their
intersection places the velocity in L2 (0, T∗ ; H m ) for every finite m. The equation upgrades the zeroth
row to an H m -valued endpoint, while the pressure recurrence determines the complete endpoint jet.
Local well-posedness restarts from that state, and uniqueness on the overlap keeps the restarted
solution on the history already generated by the datum. A finite T∗ would no longer be maximal.
Thus the maximal interval is [0, ∞).
    The same rectangles retain more information than the continuation argument alone needs. Their
finite coordinates control the critical-height rows, continuation norms, dyadic tails, fixed weighted
dyadic shell sums, local scale content, and the relative-energy action. These consequences keep the
exact rectangle constants attached to the datum and viscosity; the small critical-data regime is the
separate case in which those constants close explicitly in the familiar scale-invariant norm.
    The resulting global solution is smooth, satisfies the exact energy identity, and carries the decay-
normalized pressure. Relative energy then identifies every Leray–Hopf solution with the same datum
with this smooth velocity, and the pressure is unique up to the unavoidable time-dependent gauge
unless the same decay normalization is imposed. The endpoint contradiction and these consequences
all belong to the one fixed-viscosity evolution constructed from u0 .
