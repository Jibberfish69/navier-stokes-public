## Detailed derivation

## The root

The object is one datum-anchored, fixed-viscosity, incompressible history on
one declared surface. At each smooth time the whole oriented velocity
configuration determines the pressure response and the complete tangent

\[
\partial_tu
=-\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u)+\nu\Delta u.
\]

Transport, pressure, viscosity, and incompressibility are constitutive rows of
that one motion. Every material path, derivative, frequency, norm, packet, and
flux is a reading of it.

The important refinement is this:

**one participant does not mean every coordinate couples to every other
coordinate; it means every actual coupling and every exact null direction are
decided by the same complete VPI tangent.**

## A local view retains the exterior pressure response

Participation is nonlocal even when the velocity is inspected locally. Let
\(D'\Subset D\) and split the whole pressure source

\[
\mathcal Q=|S|^2-\frac12|\omega|^2
\]

into a part retained near \(D\) and its exterior complement. On \(D'\),

\[
\boxed{
p=p_{\rm loc}+h,
\qquad
\Delta h=0.
}
\]

The local velocity jet determines the local Poisson source. The rest of the
same field and the declared boundary data determine the harmonic row \(h\),
which may have a nonconstant gradient and a nonzero trace-free Hessian inside
\(D'\). This is distinct from pressure gauge: adding a time-dependent constant
changes neither gradient nor Hessian, while the exterior harmonic response
changes both.

On decaying \(\mathbb R^3\), the directional geometry is explicit. At an
observation point translated to zero and for a unit vector \(e\),

\[
\boxed{
e\cdot\nabla^2p(0)e
=-\frac13\mathcal Q(0)
+\frac1{4\pi}\operatorname{p.v.}
\int_{\mathbb R^3}
\mathcal Q(y)
\frac{3(y\cdot e)^2-|y|^2}{|y|^5}\,dy.
}
\]

The first term fixes the trace. The integral is the whole-field quadrupole; in
spherical variables it is a logarithmic-distance integral of the
\(P_2(e\cdot\theta)\) angular moment. Polar and transverse source regions enter
with opposite signs. A smooth divergence-free perturbation supported away
from \(D\) can leave the complete local velocity jet unchanged while changing,
or reversing, this directional pressure curvature. Therefore no finite local
jet and no local sign of \(\mathcal Q\) determines the pressure-Hessian sign.
On the torus the corresponding statement uses the periodic Fourier kernel; on
a bounded domain the boundary and harmonic pressure data are part of the
declared surface.

## Nested terminal microscopes remain one history

Fix one alleged terminal point \((T,x_*)\) of the same preterminal smooth
solution. For radii \(r_j\downarrow0\), define

\[
v_j(s,y)
=r_j u(T+r_j^2s,x_*+r_jy),
\qquad
q_j(s,y)
=r_j^2p(T+r_j^2s,x_*+r_jy).
\]

Every \((v_j,q_j)\) solves Navier--Stokes with the same positive viscosity on
its transformed domain. If \(r_{j+1}=\lambda_jr_j\), same-solution ancestry
forces the exact compatibility

\[
\boxed{
v_{j+1}(s,y)
=\lambda_jv_j(\lambda_j^2s,\lambda_jy),
\qquad
q_{j+1}(s,y)
=\lambda_j^2q_j(\lambda_j^2s,\lambda_jy).
}
\]

Successive microscopes are therefore nested views of one event, not free
packets or independent solutions. The identity alone supplies compatibility,
not rigidity. Profile switching, translation, rotation, phase drift, and weak
defects can survive while the identity remains exact. A unique strong tangent
with \(\lambda_j\to\lambda\in(0,1)\) would inherit discrete self-similarity;
producing that uniqueness or another strict scale monotone quantity remains an
additional theorem. This fact rejects independently posed terminal profiles
and supplies no Gold closure by itself.

## Smooth ancestry and terminal weak limits

One datum has a global energy-class continuation, while classical participation
and weak participation have different regularity. A Leray--Hopf solution lies
in

\[
L^\infty_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);L^2)
\cap L^2_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);H^1)
\]

and obeys the distributional equation and global energy inequality. Whenever a
strong solution \(u\) exists with the same datum and surface, every
Leray--Hopf solution \(v\) coincides with it on that strong lifespan. Indeed,
for \(w=v-u\),

\[
\frac12\frac d{dt}\|w\|_2^2
+\nu\|\nabla w\|_2^2
\le
\|\nabla u\|_\infty\|w\|_2^2,
\]

and Gronwall gives \(w=0\). Weak--strong uniqueness is therefore an exact
same-history bridge while the strong class exists. It supplies no uniqueness
or classical relaunch after that class ends.

The nonlinear law can be lost when a terminal sequence converges only weakly.
If

\[
u_j\rightharpoonup u
\quad\text{in }L^2_{\rm loc},
\]

then, after subsequence extraction,

\[
u_j\otimes u_j
\rightharpoonup
u\otimes u+R
\]

may contain a nonzero Reynolds defect. The limiting equation is then

\[
\partial_tu
+\mathbb P\nabla\!\cdot(u\otimes u+R)
-\nu\Delta u=0
\]

until \(R=0\) is proved. The exact divergence-free countertest

\[
w_j(x)=e_2\sin(jx_1)
\rightharpoonup0,
\qquad
w_j\otimes w_j
\rightharpoonup\frac12e_2\otimes e_2
\]

shows that weak velocity convergence alone cannot pass the quadratic
transport. Strong local \(L^2\) convergence does pass it in \(L^1_{\rm loc}\).
Pressure reconstruction and boundary data must pass at the same time.

This fixes the terminal identity boundary. Every prelimit microscope is exactly
the same Navier--Stokes history by the nested-rescaling law. A weak limit with
unremoved \(R\) obeys a defect equation and has not yet been admitted as the
same Navier--Stokes participant. It is neither a Gold obstruction nor a Silver
class exit until a theorem eliminates or types the defect and preserves the
original pressure--viscosity--incompressibility law.

Suitable weak solutions add the local energy inequality and the pressure
integrability needed for local regularity. Classical partial regularity makes
their singular set parabolically one-dimensional-null, while a scale-critical
velocity--pressure quantity below the epsilon-regularity threshold on one
cylinder forces smoothness on a smaller cylinder. These facts constrain a
terminal history and permit a nonempty thinner singular set. A global
conclusion still needs coverage or a theorem forcing critical smallness at
every candidate terminal point.

## The same VPI law in material coordinates

The joint law becomes most explicit when it is followed with the fluid. Let

\[
v=u\circ X,
\qquad
\widetilde p=p\circ X,
\qquad
F=D_aX,
\qquad
\mathcal A=F^{-1},
\qquad
G=\mathcal A\mathcal A^T.
\]

Since \(\det F=1\), the exact pulled-back system is

\[
\partial_t v+\mathcal A^T\nabla_a\widetilde p
=\nu\,\operatorname{div}_a(G\nabla_av),
\qquad
\operatorname{div}_a(\mathcal A v)=0,
\]

and pressure is recovered on that same material surface from

\[
-\partial_{a_\ell}
\left(G_{\ell k}\partial_{a_k}\widetilde p\right)
=
\mathcal A_{\ell i}\mathcal A_{k j}
(\partial_{a_\ell}v_j)(\partial_{a_k}v_i).
\]

The deformation coefficients obey

\[
\partial_t\mathcal A=-\mathcal A(\nabla u\circ X),
\qquad
\partial_tG=-2\mathcal A(S\circ X)\mathcal A^T,
\qquad
\det G=1.
\]

On the whole periodic or decaying surface, the pulled-back constraint cancels
the pressure pairing and the same metric carries dissipation:

\[
\frac12\frac d{dt}\|v\|_{L_a^2}^2
+\nu\int G\nabla_av:\nabla_av\,da=0.
\]

This is the ordinary Eulerian kinetic-energy identity written on persistent
material labels. A material subregion retains its boundary traction and flux
terms; the coordinate change does not close its energy account.

Advection has disappeared as an explicit derivative because its complete
history now lives in \(\mathcal A\) and \(G\). The same inverse deformation appears in
the incompressibility constraint, the pressure gradient, the global elliptic
pressure solve, and the viscous operator. This is the exact mathematical sense
in which deformation, pressure, viscosity, and incompressibility remain one
response.

If \(s_i\) are the singular values of \(F\), the eigenvalues of \(G\) are
\(s_i^{-2}\) and their product is one. A material oscillation in a stretched
label direction has a longer Eulerian wavelength and weaker pulled-back
diffusion; compensating compression creates a shorter Eulerian wavelength and
stronger pulled-back diffusion in another direction. Viscosity is isotropic in
Eulerian space and deformation-coupled anisotropic in material coordinates.
These are two descriptions of the same viscous law.

The determinant constraint controls volume, not ellipticity. Arbitrarily large
condition number is compatible with \(\det G=1\). Exact periodic shears make
this boundary concrete: they generate a nontrivial anisotropic \(G\) while the
pressure is spatially constant and nonlinear transfer vanishes. Material
anisotropy alone therefore forces neither pressure action nor amplification;
the complete configuration decides the coupling.

## Vorticity is deformation plus one viscous defect

The same material variables give the exact Cauchy-defect identity

\[
\frac d{dt}
\left[F^{-1}\omega(X,t)\right]
=\nu F^{-1}\Delta\omega(X,t).
\]

At zero viscosity this is Euler's frozen-in Cauchy invariant. At fixed positive
viscosity, the material labels and volume persist while their vorticity can
change magnitude, direction, flux, and circulation through the displayed
viscous defect. Pressure contributes no independent curl source. It remains
active through the same nonlocal strain and deformation history that determine
\(F\), \(G\), and the later vorticity geometry.

Thus deterministic Navier--Stokes material identity means ancestry and volume,
not frozen vorticity content or frozen spectral support.

## Viscosity also has a coherent stochastic material representation

The same deterministic history has a second exact Lagrangian representation.
Let one spatially uniform Wiener process drive every material label:

\[
dX_t(a)
=u(X_t(a),t)\,dt+\sqrt{2\nu}\,dW_t,
\qquad
A_t=X_t^{-1}.
\]

Because the noise has no label derivative, each realization satisfies

\[
d(\nabla_aX_t)
=(\nabla u)(X_t,t)\nabla_aX_t\,dt,
\qquad
\det\nabla_aX_t=1.
\]

Each random map is therefore a volume-preserving diffeomorphism on the smooth
interval. The noise is common to the whole map. For two labels it cancels from
their chord equation:

\[
d\bigl(X_t(a)-X_t(b)\bigr)
=\bigl(u(X_t(a),t)-u(X_t(b),t)\bigr)\,dt.
\]

The random maps are coupled back to the one deterministic velocity through the
Constantin--Iyer fixed point

\[
u(x,t)
=\mathbb E\,\mathbb P
\left[
(\nabla A_t(x))^T u_0(A_t(x))
\right].
\]

The Leray projection is the nonlocal incompressibility and pressure closure.
Thus the stochastic paths do not remove pressure or create additional fluids;
they are exact proof coordinates of the same deterministic participant.

The associated stochastic Cauchy formula is

\[
\omega(x,t)
=\mathbb E
\left[
\nabla_aX_t(A_t(x))\,\omega_0(A_t(x))
\right].
\]

In two dimensions the deformation factor drops out and vorticity is a scalar
average of its initial values. In three dimensions the random deformation
gradient can stretch and turn the initial vorticity before the signed ensemble
average is taken. Hence three-dimensional vorticity growth requires
anisotropic stochastic deformation that survives cancellation; volume
preservation alone controls only the determinant and supplies no bound on the
largest singular value.

There is also an exact stochastic Kelvin object. Put

\[
\widetilde u_t
=\mathbb P\left[(\nabla A_t)^T(u_0\circ A_t)\right].
\]

Then \(u=\mathbb E\widetilde u\), and for every closed initial curve
\(\Gamma\), pathwise,

\[
\oint_{X_t(\Gamma)}\widetilde u_t\cdot dr
=\oint_\Gamma u_0\cdot dr.
\]

This does not restore deterministic Kelvin conservation: the loop and Weber
velocity are correlated random objects, while the deterministic material-loop
circulation still has the signed viscous defect stated above.

The backward version of the same common-noise flow gives an exact
fluctuation--dissipation identity for smooth unforced histories on
\(\mathbb T^3\), or on \(\mathbb R^3\) with sufficient decay and
integrability. Let

\[
\widehat d_s\xi_{t,s}(x)
=u(\xi_{t,s}(x),s)\,ds
+\sqrt{2\nu}\,\widehat dW_s,
\qquad
\xi_{t,t}(x)=x,
\]

and define the complete backward momentum history

\[
\mathcal U_{t,x}
=u_0(\xi_{t,0}(x))
-\int_0^t\nabla p(\xi_{t,s}(x),s)\,ds.
\]

For a smooth unforced history,

\[
u(x,t)=\mathbb E\mathcal U_{t,x},
\]

and backward Itô isometry gives the pointwise identity

\[
\operatorname{Var}\mathcal U_{t,x}
=2\nu\int_0^t
\mathbb E\left[
|\nabla u(\xi_{t,s}(x),s)|^2
\right]ds.
\]

Here \(\operatorname{Var}Z=\mathbb E|Z-\mathbb EZ|^2\) for a vector-valued
history. Pathwise volume preservation then yields

\[
\frac12\int
\operatorname{Var}\mathcal U_{t,x}\,dx
=\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla u(s)\|_2^2\,ds
=\frac12\left(\|u_0\|_2^2-\|u(t)\|_2^2\right).
\]

The history variable \(\mathcal U_{t,x}\) is not the random Weber velocity
\(\widetilde u_t\) above. Their expectations agree with the same deterministic
velocity, while their variances have no general identification without an
additional pathwise equivalence.

This is one joint participation identity. The initial momentum, material
transport, pressure history, incompressibility, and viscosity are all present
in the same random variable. Splitting the pressure integral from the advected
initial momentum produces two variances and their covariance; only the
covariance-completed total is identified with viscous loss in general. The
Brownian translation is isotropic in Eulerian coordinates, while the drift,
pressure history, deformation, and sampled velocity gradient retain whatever
anisotropic directional structure the given flow carries.

Applying the scalar fluctuation--dissipation identity componentwise to the
vorticity equation gives the corresponding stretching-inclusive corollary.
Define

\[
\mathcal O_{t,x}
=\omega_0(\xi_{t,0}(x))
+\int_0^t
(S\omega)(\xi_{t,s}(x),s)\,ds.
\]

Then

\[
\omega(x,t)=\mathbb E\mathcal O_{t,x},
\qquad
\frac12\int\operatorname{Var}\mathcal O_{t,x}\,dx
=\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla\omega(s)\|_2^2\,ds.
\]

In three dimensions one-half of the spatially integrated variance is exactly
the viscous enstrophy drain, not the net change of enstrophy, because the same
history variable contains the anisotropic stretching source \(S\omega\). In
the two-dimensional no-stretch sector it reduces to the net enstrophy loss.
Pressure has no separate curl source; it remains present through the same
velocity, strain, and stochastic paths that determine the stretching history.

Using the same backward path for momentum and vorticity also recovers the
signed helicity law as an off-diagonal fluctuation. Center the two complete
histories by

\[
\mathcal U'_{t,x}=\mathcal U_{t,x}-u(x,t),
\qquad
\mathcal O'_{t,x}=\mathcal O_{t,x}-\omega(x,t).
\]

Cross Itô isometry and volume preservation give

\[
\int\mathbb E
\left[\mathcal U'_{t,x}\cdot\mathcal O'_{t,x}\right]dx
=2\nu\int_0^t
\int\nabla u:\nabla\omega\,dx\,ds.
\]

For the helicity

\[
\mathcal K(t)=\int u(x,t)\cdot\omega(x,t)\,dx,
\]

the unforced smooth balance is

\[
\mathcal K'(t)
=-2\nu\int\nabla u:\nabla\omega\,dx.
\]

Hence

\[
\int\mathbb E
\left[\mathcal U'_{t,x}\cdot\mathcal O'_{t,x}\right]dx
=\mathcal K(0)-\mathcal K(t).
\]

The two coupled histories therefore form one positive-semidefinite covariance
matrix:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\displaystyle\int\operatorname{Var}\mathcal U\,dx
& \mathcal K(0)-\mathcal K(t)\\
\mathcal K(0)-\mathcal K(t)
& \displaystyle\int\operatorname{Var}\mathcal O\,dx
\end{pmatrix}
=2\nu\int_0^t
\begin{pmatrix}
\|\nabla u\|_2^2
& \langle\nabla u,\nabla\omega\rangle\\
\langle\nabla u,\nabla\omega\rangle
& \|\nabla\omega\|_2^2
\end{pmatrix}ds
\succeq0.
\]

In particular,

\[
|\mathcal K(0)-\mathcal K(t)|^2
\le
\left(\int\operatorname{Var}\mathcal U\,dx\right)
\left(\int\operatorname{Var}\mathcal O\,dx\right).
\]

Kinetic-energy loss and viscous enstrophy drain are the nonnegative diagonal
fluctuations; signed helicity change is their off-diagonal covariance. Using
independent Brownian copies would retain the two diagonal identities and erase
the helicity entry. The common history is therefore mathematically necessary
for this part of the participation law, not merely a convenient coupling.

This covariance geometry is scale-consistent without being Gold control. On
\(\mathbb R^3\), its momentum-variance diagonal scales like \(\lambda^{-1}\),
its vorticity-variance diagonal like \(\lambda\), and its helicity
off-diagonal is invariant. Periodic Beltrami heat histories can saturate the
covariance inequality while their projected nonlinear critical production is
zero; two-dimensional and shear sectors can make the off-diagonal vanish. The
matrix therefore constrains how the same history dissipates and changes
linkage, while supplying no datum-only critical-height bound.

The identity extends exactly to scalar Fourier-multiplier rungs of the
derivative tower. Let \(M\) be a time-independent, real-to-real scalar Fourier
multiplier commuting with \(\nabla\) and \(\Delta\), on a smooth interval where
every displayed term and norm is finite. Put

\[
v=Mu,
\qquad
S_M=[u\cdot\nabla,M]u-M\nabla p.
\]

Here

\[
[u\cdot\nabla,M]u
:=u\cdot\nabla(Mu)-M((u\cdot\nabla)u).
\]

Then

\[
\partial_tv+u\cdot\nabla v
=\nu\Delta v+S_M,
\]

and the complete history

\[
\mathcal U^M_{t,x}
=(Mu_0)(\xi_{t,0}(x))
+\int_0^tS_M(\xi_{t,s}(x),s)\,ds
\]

satisfies

\[
\frac12\int\operatorname{Var}\mathcal U^M_{t,x}\,dx
=\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla Mu(s)\|_2^2\,ds.
\]

At \(M=I\) this is the datum-bounded kinetic-energy identity above. At
\(M=\Lambda^{1/2}\), it becomes

\[
\frac12\int
\operatorname{Var}\mathcal U^{\Lambda^{1/2}}_{t,x}\,dx
=\nu\int_0^t\|\Lambda^{3/2}u(s)\|_2^2\,ds.
\]

The critical lift contains the nonlinear transport commutator and the
fractionally differentiated pressure history. Its right-hand side is the
scale-critical viscous action not bounded by the base kinetic-energy datum, so
the exact identity relocates rather than bounds the Gold quantity.

### Signed critical work is an off-diagonal relation of the complete history

The cross-covariance version of the same identity first fixes a sharp
boundary. For two fixed linear multipliers \(M,N\), driven by the same backward
Brownian path, let \(C_{M,N}(t)\) denote the spatial integral of the covariance
between their centered complete histories. Then

\[
C_{M,N}(t)
=2\nu\int_0^t
\langle\nabla Mu,\nabla Nu\rangle\,ds.
\]

Its instantaneous rate is quadratic and unchanged by \(u\mapsto-u\). The
critical production

\[
P_H(u)
=-\langle\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u),\Lambda u\rangle
\]

is cubic and changes sign. Linear complete histories can therefore read
viscous cross-action, including helicity change, but cannot carry the
critical-current sign for every smooth datum.

The sign becomes visible when the nonlinear row is kept inside the same
history. On the torus let \(\bar u\) be the conserved spatial mean and put
\(v=u-\bar u\). On decaying \(\mathbb R^3\), the decay class fixes the inertial
frame; set \(\bar u=0\) and \(v=u\). Define

\[
B(v)=\mathbb P((v\cdot\nabla)v)
=(v\cdot\nabla)v+\nabla p.
\]

Thus \(B\) is the pressure-completed nonlinear acceleration row of the current
field. The Leray projection does not remove pressure from the physics; it
performs the global pressure completion forced by incompressibility. Constant
mean transport contributes zero critical work, so

\[
P_H(u)=-\langle B(v),\Lambda v\rangle.
\]

On the torus this conserved-mean choice is Galilean covariant. In the
mean-zero frame it reduces to the formula above. A nonzero Galilean boost does
not preserve the decaying \(\mathbb R^3\) class.

The state tangent in that comoving frame is

\[
\dot v_{\mathrm{c}}
:=(\partial_t+\bar u\cdot\nabla)v
=-B-\nu\Lambda^2v.
\]

This is the change of the field configuration in the conserved-mean frame.
The Galilean-invariant material acceleration remains
\(D_tu=-\nabla p-\nu\Lambda^2v\); the stochastic histories below use the full
material drift \(u\).

Its geometry depends on which exact state metric reads it. In kinetic energy,

\[
\langle B,v\rangle=0,
\qquad
\langle\dot v_{\mathrm{c}},v\rangle=-\nu\|\Lambda v\|_2^2.
\]

The pressure-completed nonlinear row is tangent to the kinetic-energy sphere,
and viscosity points inward. In critical height, the same two coordinates read

\[
-\langle B,\Lambda v\rangle=P_H,
\qquad
-\nu\langle\Lambda^2v,\Lambda v\rangle=-\nu D.
\]

The nonlinear tangent can therefore point inward or outward in the critical
metric while remaining exactly tangent in the energy metric. Critical growth
inside a dissipating solution is motion toward shorter-scale directions on a
shrinking kinetic-energy sphere. This is one tangent viewed in two metrics,
not an energy source acting against a separate dissipative system.

The critical metric gives the same tangent an exact radial--tangential split.
Put

\[
\mathfrak f_H=\Lambda^{-1/2}B,
\qquad
\mathfrak g_H=\Lambda^{3/2}v,
\qquad
D=\|\mathfrak g_H\|_2^2.
\]

For \(D>0\), project the complete pressure-corrected nonlinear row onto the
current critical direction:

\[
\mathfrak f_{H,\parallel}
=\frac{\langle\mathfrak f_H,\mathfrak g_H\rangle}{D}\mathfrak g_H
=-\frac{P_H}{D}\mathfrak g_H,
\qquad
\mathfrak f_{H,\perp}=\mathfrak f_H-\mathfrak f_{H,\parallel}.
\]

Then

\[
\|\mathfrak f_H\|_2^2
=\frac{P_H^2}{D}+\|\mathfrak f_{H,\perp}\|_2^2,
\]

and the full critical-dual tangent satisfies

\[
\Lambda^{-1/2}\dot v_{\mathrm{c}}
=-\mathfrak f_H-\nu\mathfrak g_H.
\]

Its radial and tangential components are exactly

\[
\frac{\langle\Lambda^{-1/2}\dot v_{\mathrm{c}},\mathfrak g_H\rangle}{D}
=\frac{H'}{D}
=\frac{P_H}{D}-\nu,
\]

\[
(I-\Pi_{\mathfrak g_H})\Lambda^{-1/2}\dot v_{\mathrm{c}}
=-\mathfrak f_{H,\perp}.
\]

Viscosity is inward radial in this moving critical geometry. The nonlocal
pressure-completed nonlinear row supplies both the signed radial motion and
all instantaneous tangential reconfiguration. The projection moves with the
same field and therefore defines no independent radial or tangential fluid.
When \(D=0\), the mean-free field is zero and the split is trivial.

Energy tangency fixes which part of the critical radial direction is actually
available to the nonlinear row. Put

\[
\mathfrak z_H=\Lambda^{1/2}v,
\qquad
R=\|\mathfrak z_H\|_2^2,
\qquad
K=\langle\mathfrak g_H,\mathfrak z_H\rangle=\|\Lambda v\|_2^2.
\]

For \(R>0\),

\[
\langle\mathfrak f_H,\mathfrak z_H\rangle=\langle B,v\rangle=0.
\]

Thus, with

\[
\mathfrak g_{H,\mathrm{shape}}
=\mathfrak g_H-\frac KR\mathfrak z_H,
\]

the signed production and the available critical direction are exactly

\[
P_H=-\langle\mathfrak f_H,\mathfrak g_{H,\mathrm{shape}}\rangle,
\qquad
\|\mathfrak g_{H,\mathrm{shape}}\|_2^2
=D-\frac{K^2}{R}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
P_H^2
\le
\|\Lambda^{-1/2}B\|_2^2
\left(D-\frac{K^2}{R}\right).
}
\]

This is the raw critical Cauchy--Schwarz bound after the direction forbidden
by kinetic-energy conservation has been removed. If \(\vartheta\) is the
Hilbert-space angle between \(\mathfrak g_H\) and \(\mathfrak z_H\), then

\[
D-\frac{K^2}{R}=D\sin^2\vartheta.
\]

Equivalently, for the critical spectral probability measure

\[
d\mu_H(\xi)
=\frac{|\xi|\,|\widehat v(\xi)|^2}{R}\,d\xi,
\]

with the corresponding Fourier sum on the torus,

\[
D-\frac{K^2}{R}
=R\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_H}(|\xi|).
\]

On decaying \(\mathbb R^3\), the same width is already encoded by the
material-pair carrier. The exact pair/Fourier bridge below has kernel \(\Psi\) with
\(\Psi'(0)=-2/(3\pi)\). Therefore, with

\[
m_H=\frac KR,
\]

the carrier's escape slope from the material diagonal is

\[
m_H
=-\frac{3\pi}{2}
\left.\frac d{d\delta}
\Pi_t\{r\ge\delta\}\right|_{\delta=0+}.
\]

Its normalized common-noise quadratic-variation rate is

\[
d_H=\nu\frac{D}{H}=2\nu\frac DR.
\]

Hence the exact radial-width identity can also be read as

\[
\frac1R\left(D-\frac{K^2}{R}\right)
=\frac{d_H}{2\nu}-m_H^2.
\]

The Fourier variance is thus the excess viscous reshaping rate of the same
critical pair carrier above its squared diagonal escape rate. It is a second
coordinate of one material history, not a separate spectral fluid.

Critical production is therefore a cross-scale anisotropic alignment. Radial
spectral width supplies the nonzero direction; the pressure-completed
nonlinear row, generated by the oriented whole field, supplies the alignment
and its sign. Neither coordinate alone produces growth. On \(\mathbb T^3\), a
field supported on one nonzero Laplacian shell has zero spectral variance and
hence \(P_H=0\) at that instant, even when its angular distribution is
anisotropic. On \(\mathbb R^3\), a nonzero \(L^2\) field cannot have exact
support on one frequency sphere, though it can concentrate in arbitrarily
thin annuli.

The same constraint also forces tangential reconfiguration in the critical
metric. When \(RD>K^2\),

\[
\|\mathfrak f_{H,\perp}\|_2^2
\ge
\frac{P_H^2K^2}{D(RD-K^2)}.
\]

Thus nonzero critical production cannot be a purely radial nonlinear motion.
At a time when \(H'\ge0\), the balance \(P_H\ge\nu D\) gives the sharper
necessary participation law

\[
\|\Lambda^{-1/2}B\|_2^2
\left(D-\frac{K^2}{R}\right)
\ge\nu^2D^2.
\]

Viscosity, spectral spread, and the anisotropic pressure-completed source must
therefore occur together in every rising critical-height state. This is a
necessary same-state law. Neither factor on the left has a datum bound, so it
does not by itself close Gold.

The standard critical bilinear estimate sharpens the causal threshold. Define

\[
\mathcal S_H
=D-\frac{K^2}{R},
\qquad
\sigma_H^2=\frac{\mathcal S_H}{D},
\qquad
\mathcal T_H=\|\mathfrak f_{H,\perp}\|_2^2.
\]

On each declared surface there is a fixed constant \(C_\Omega\) such that

\[
\|\Lambda^{-1/2}B\|_2
\le C_\Omega\sqrt{RD}.
\]

The energy-centered projection therefore gives

\[
|P_H|
\le C_\Omega\sqrt{R D\mathcal S_H}
=C_\Omega\sqrt R\,D\sigma_H.
\]

Every rising state must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_H^2
\ge\frac{\nu^2}{C_\Omega^2R}.
}
\]

The threshold is amplitude dependent. Exact near-equilateral divergence-free
triads, followed by their Schwartz realization on \(\mathbb R^3\), have
\(\sigma_H^2\asymp\varepsilon^2\) and become rising when their amplitude is
of order \(\nu/\varepsilon\). Thus \(R\sigma_H^2\asymp\nu^2\): one shell is
an exact null sector, while arbitrarily thin nonzero annuli can rise at
sufficient critical amplitude. No uniform positive spectral-width gap exists.

Young's inequality now acts on two coordinates of the same tangent:

\[
H'
\le
\frac{C_\Omega^2}{4\nu}R\mathcal S_H
=\frac{C_\Omega^2}{2\nu}H\mathcal S_H.
\]

Hence

\[
H(t)
\le H(s)
\exp\left(
\frac{C_\Omega^2}{2\nu}
\int_s^t\mathcal S_H(\tau)\,d\tau
\right),
\]

and every first record doubling obeys the fixed shape-action charge

\[
\boxed{
\int_a^b\mathcal S_H\,dt
\ge\frac{2\nu\log2}{C_\Omega^2}.
}
\]

Energy tangency also quantifies the complementary branch. For
\(0<\sigma_H^2<1\),

\[
\mathcal T_H
\ge
\frac{P_H^2}{D}
\frac{1-\sigma_H^2}{\sigma_H^2}.
\]

At every rising time this gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_H+\nu^{-2}\mathcal T_H\ge D.
}
\]

Thus broad critical spectra pay through scale-shape action, while narrow
spectra force large tangential pressure-completed reconfiguration. Suppose
\(H(a)=M\) and \(b>a\) is the first time with \(H(b)=2M\), so
\(H\le2M\) on \([a,b]\). Then the positive-variation set satisfies

\[
\int_{\{t\in[a,b]:\,H'(t)>0\}}
\left(\mathcal S_H+\nu^{-2}\mathcal T_H\right)dt
\ge\frac{\sqrt M}{2C_\Omega}.
\]

These are record charges rather than datum budgets. Finite total
\(\mathcal S_H\)-action would bound \(H\) by the exponential inequality, while
bounded \(H\) gives smooth continuation and then
\(\mathcal S_H\le D\) is integrable. Its endpoint finiteness is therefore
Gold-equivalent. Energy controls only the lower-order scale-shape action

\[
\int_0^T K\sigma_H^2\,dt
\le\int_0^TK\,dt
=\frac{E(0)-E(T)}{\nu}
\le\frac{E_0}{\nu}.
\]

Fixed-viscosity Navier--Stokes scaling makes this subcritical control
arbitrarily small on a preserved upcrossing, while the critical
\(\mathcal S_H\)-action is invariant.

The material-pair identity derived below and this Hilbert projection are two
exact coordinates of the same event. Material-pair incidence explains the sign
as weighted inward or outward chord motion; the critical projection identifies
the part of the complete nonlinear action that changes the global height. A
nonlinear row can be nonzero with \(P_H=0\), in which case it is
instantaneously tangential to the critical height, and exact globally smooth
2D3C histories can have an outward radial component. Neither component alone
determines its own persistence.

Their equality at the global scalar \(P_H\) does not by itself identify a
localized density. On decaying \(\mathbb R^3\), they can be placed on the same
material-pair space using the incidence operator \(\mathcal I_t\) defined
below. With the same \(m_H=K/R\), put

k_{\mathrm{shape}}(a,b,t)
=2\,\mathcal I_t(-\Lambda^{-1}B)

The fractional bilinear identity gives

The native material-pair current \(j=-4e\alpha\), derived below, has the same

\[
\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{loc}}=j-k_{\mathrm{shape}},
\qquad
\iint\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{loc}}\,da\,db=0,

is a genuine pair-label localization defect for this declared representation.
On \(\mathbb T^3\), the same common-space construction uses the periodic
fractional kernel, equivalently the augmented family of lattice lifts.
The next literal kernel display is the decaying \(\mathbb R^3\) formula; on
\(\mathbb T^3\), its divergence and Piola pullback hold lift by lift for the

where

The first part is pair transport. The second is the forced pressure center

\((\nabla_x+\nabla_y)\mathsf k_{1/2}=0\) and \(v\) is divergence free. Under the

\(\operatorname{Div}_{a,b}\widetilde Q_{\mathrm{VPI}}\) in fixed pair labels.

for every measurable selected radius \(\rho(t)>0\), and

\mathcal F_\rho(t)

## Euler enters through a same-datum difference history

Let \(u^\nu\) be the fixed-positive-viscosity history and \(u^E\) the Euler

\qquad

=\partial_i\partial_j

thereby changes the simultaneous pressure response, strain--vorticity
geometry, scale transfer, material trajectories, and irreversible energy
history. An Euler configuration cannot be copied into the Navier--Stokes
ontology with an added damping term.

There is an exact time-asymmetry theorem at the inviscid-limit boundary. Let
\(X^{\ell,\nu}_{t,s}\) be the deterministic flow of a spatially mollified
Navier--Stokes velocity on \(\mathbb T^d\), \(d\ge3\), and let
\(\langle\cdot\rangle_R\) average initial separations at scale \(R\). For a
family of Leray solutions with uniformly bounded data, converging strongly in

\[
\lim_{R\to0}\lim_{\ell\to0}\lim_{\tau\to0}\lim_{\nu\to0}

\left\langle

\right\rangle_R
-
\left\langle

\right\rangle_R
}{4\tau^3}
=-\varepsilon[u],
\]

where \(\varepsilon[u]\) is the limiting energy-dissipation anomaly. The
order is part of the theorem: viscosity tends to zero first, followed by the
short-time, filter-scale, and separation-scale limits. These are deterministic
mollified trajectories, not the common-noise stochastic maps above.

Here

\[

\right\rangle_R.

\left\langle

where

in the unforced case. It contains the full pressure, viscous, and subgrid-stress

## The anisotropic relation has its own exact VPI tangent

Where \(\varrho=|\omega|>0\), set

\xi\cdot(\Delta S)\xi
+\frac2\varrho

\right].

\[

\qquad
\mathcal Q=|S|^2-\frac12|\omega|^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\mathsf P^\circ=0.

The trace-free tensor \(\mathsf P^\circ\) is the nonlocal directional pressure
response. Its co-diagonal part can change the aligned rate without turning the

has a universal sign.

so the exact transverse separation rate is

\[
e_j\cdot(D_t\xi-D_te_i)

+\frac{e_j\cdot\mathsf P^\circ e_i
-\nu e_j\cdot(\Delta S)e_i}
{\lambda_i-\lambda_j}.
\]

The first derivative of \(\xi\cdot e_i\) is automatically zero at perfect
alignment because both vectors have unit length. If
\(T_i=D_t\xi-D_te_i\) is the transverse vector assembled from the displayed
components, then

\[

\]

at that aligned instant. Thus \(T_i\), rather than the first derivative of the
cosine, is the actual first-order alignment-loss variable.

This is quantitative. On a pair interval with \(|\beta|>0\), if

-\int

The zero-turning and zero-compression sectors are genuine degeneracies, and

e

## The producing anisotropy is a complete-field tensor

The material-pair current has an exact spatial form. For
\(n=r/|r|\), define on decaying \(\mathbb R^3\)

\[
\mathsf T_u(z)
=\frac1{\pi^2}\int_0^1\int_{\mathbb R^3}
\frac{|u(z+(1-\theta)r)-u(z-\theta r)|^2}{|r|^4}
n\otimes n\,dr\,d\theta.
\]

This tensor distributes the whole field's relative-velocity content by the
directions of every current spatial chord passing through \(z\). It is
symmetric and positive semidefinite, and

\[
\int\operatorname{tr}\mathsf T_u\,dz=4\mathcal H_{1/2}.
\]

The exact nonlinear current is

\[
P_H=-\int S:\mathsf T_u\,dz
=-\int S:\mathsf T_u^\circ\,dz.
\]

Incompressibility deletes the isotropic part. Critical production is the
spatial correlation between local strain and the anisotropic distribution of
relative energy across all directions and separations. Where
\(\operatorname{tr}\mathsf T_u>0\), write
\(A=\mathsf T_u/\operatorname{tr}\mathsf T_u\). In a strain eigenframe,

\[
S:A=\lambda_1a_1+\lambda_2a_2+\lambda_3a_3,
\qquad
a_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_i a_i=1.
\]

Growth means that the same field has placed enough relative-energy weight in
its compressive strain directions to overcome the expansive directions and
the critical viscous loss. Neither an eigenvalue, an anisotropy magnitude, a
frequency radius, nor a material pair carries that sign by itself.

The tensor is an instantaneous whole-field configuration, not an independent
material state. It is exactly the incidence field of all straight chords with
material endpoints:

\[
\mathsf T_u(z)
=4\iint e_{ab}\int_0^1
n_{ab}\otimes n_{ab}\,
\delta\bigl(z-X(b,t)-sR_{ab}\bigr)\,ds\,da\,db.
\]

The endpoints move with the fluid, while the interior of the straight chord
generally does not. Its interpolation velocity

\[
w_s=(1-s)u(X(b,t),t)+s u(X(a,t),t)
\]

differs from the fluid velocity at the chord point by an exact second spatial
derivative of the same velocity field:

\[
w_s-u(X(b,t)+sR)
=\int_0^1G(s,\theta)(R\cdot\nabla)^2
u(X(b,t)+\theta R,t)\,d\theta,
\]

\[
G(s,\theta)
=\min(s,\theta)\bigl(1-\max(s,\theta)\bigr).
\]

Hence the tensor's local motion combines endpoint transport, chord turning,
relative-energy reweighting, and field curvature.

The explicit chord derivative is not an autonomous tensor equation: endpoint
transport, pressure-constrained redistribution, chord curvature, viscous heat
diffusion, and gradient-increment loss all remain. After normalization,
viscosity has no universal isotropizing sign; it reweights direction through
the scale--direction covariance already present in the same field. Exact heat
shears can become more anisotropic, less anisotropic, or keep their anisotropic
shape while their critical height decreases.

On the torus, the same statement requires the periodic fractional kernel, or
equivalently every lattice lift of each pair. One shortest geodesic is not the
global critical-height geometry.

## Pair space is the constrained image of the same velocity field

The reciprocal configuration is not an autonomous six-dimensional fluid.
Before inversion, write

\[
x=c+\frac R2,
\qquad
y=c-\frac R2,
\qquad
U(c,R)=\frac{u(x)+u(y)}2,
\qquad
V(c,R)=u(x)-u(y).
\]

The global integrability constraint is already visible at three points:

\[
V_{xy}+V_{yz}=V_{xz},
\qquad
V_{yx}=-V_{xy}.
\]

Conversely, the cocycle identity gives
\(V_{xy}=u(x)-u(y)\) after choosing one base point, with \(u\) determined up
to a spatial constant. Weighted pair incompressibility alone does not impose
this condition.

Differentiating the same sum and difference gives

\[
\boxed{
\nabla_cU=\nabla_RV,
\qquad
\nabla_cV=4\nabla_RU,
\qquad
\Delta_cV=4\Delta_RV=\delta\Delta u.
}
\]

Subtracting the two Navier--Stokes equations gives the exact current-pair
equation

\[
\boxed{
\left(\partial_t+U\cdot\nabla_c+V\cdot\nabla_R\right)V
+\nabla_c\delta p
=\nu\left(\frac12\Delta_c+2\Delta_R\right)V
=\nu\Delta_cV.
}
\]

The two displayed Laplacians are two coordinate readings of the same
endpoint diffusion. They are not separately available smoothing channels.

This becomes more deceptive after Kelvin inversion. Put

\[
Y=\frac R{|R|^2},
\qquad
\rho=|Y|,
\qquad
Q=I-2\widehat Y\otimes\widehat Y,
\qquad
W=\rho^2QV.
\]

For a pulled-back vector field, define

\[
\mathcal L_YW
=\rho^2Q\left[
\rho^4\Delta_Y(\rho^{-2}QW)
-2\rho^2Y\cdot\nabla_Y(\rho^{-2}QW)
\right].
\]

This is the exact conjugate of \(\Delta_R\). Its second-order principal part
grows like \(\rho^4\Delta_Y\), which initially looks like a new strong brake
at reciprocal infinity. The one-field compatibility proves instead

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_YW=\frac14\Delta_cW.
}
\]

The complete reciprocal equation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\partial_t+U\cdot\nabla_c+W\cdot\nabla_Y\right)W
+\rho^2Q\nabla_c\delta p
={}&
\nu\left(\frac12\Delta_cW+2\mathcal L_YW\right)\\
&+4\frac{Y\cdot W}{\rho^2}W
-2\frac{|W|^2}{\rho^2}Y.
\end{aligned}
\]

On a lawful velocity-increment graph, its viscous row collapses exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\Delta_cW+2\mathcal L_YW=\Delta_cW.
}
\]

The geometric acceleration

\[
\mathcal G(Y,W)
=4\frac{Y\cdot W}{\rho^2}W
-2\frac{|W|^2}{\rho^2}Y
\]

has the exact work

\[
W\cdot\mathcal G=-2\alpha|W|^2.
\]

It is the outward reciprocal work that produces the signed critical current.
The nonlocal pressure row is the center flux

\[
-\frac{\delta u\cdot\delta\nabla p}{r^4}
=-\nabla_c\cdot
\left(\frac{\delta p\,\delta u}{r^4}\right).
\]

Finally, with \(d\mu=dc\,\rho^{-6}dY\),

\[
\boxed{
\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2
=\frac1{2\pi^2}\int|\nabla_cW|_F^2\,d\mu.
}
\]

Thus critical viscosity is center Dirichlet dissipation of reciprocal velocity
on the constrained one-field graph. The center derivative already contains
the separation curvature through \(\Delta_cV=4\Delta_RV\); there is one
viscous action.

Its favorable sign is still global. The exact pressureless periodic heat shear

\[
u=(f(t,y),0,0),
\qquad
f=e^{-\nu t}\sin y+e^{-4\nu t}\sin2y
\]

has, for the pair \(y_\pm=\pm2\pi/3\),

\[
\delta u=\sqrt3(a-b)e_1,
\qquad
\delta\Delta u=\sqrt3(4b-a)e_1,
\qquad
a=e^{-\nu t},\quad b=e^{-4\nu t}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\nu\,\delta u\cdot\delta\Delta u
=3\nu(a-b)(4b-a)>0
\]

exactly on

\[
0<t<\frac{\log4}{3\nu}.
\]

Viscosity can activate one material pair while dissipating the complete
critical field. A pairwise viscous sign, a passive-scalar maximum principle
for \(W\), and an independently counted large-\(\rho\) diffusion channel are
all excluded by the exact same-field law. The reciprocal transform therefore
sharpens the ontology without producing a new datum-bound for Gold: any such
bound must control the anisotropic outward geometric work on this constrained
graph, with pressure redistribution and surrounding-field coupling retained.

## Pairwise pressure and viscous rows close after complete pair recombination

The relative material acceleration is

\[
\dot V_{ab}
=-\delta_{ab}\nabla p
+\nu\,\delta_{ab}\Delta u.
\]

Consequently each pair satisfies

\[
j_{ab}
=\dot e_{ab}
+\frac1{2\pi^2}|R_{ab}|^{-4}
V_{ab}\cdot\delta_{ab}\nabla p
-\frac\nu{2\pi^2}|R_{ab}|^{-4}
V_{ab}\cdot\delta_{ab}\Delta u.
\]

Neither of the last two terms has a pairwise sign. Only after summing every
compatible pair does the same-field pressure row cancel and the viscous row
become critical dissipation:

\[
\frac d{dt}\mathcal H_{1/2}
+\nu\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2
=\iint j_{ab}\,da\,db.
\]

This is the full VPI balance in the native Gold coordinate. Pressure changes
the pair distribution and its future strain geometry while doing no direct
global critical work. Viscosity changes every pair velocity without being
pairwise dissipative; its favorable sign also belongs to the complete field.

The exact relative-compression equation keeps those dependencies together:

\[
\dot\alpha_{ab}
=-\alpha_{ab}^2
+\frac{|V_{ab}^{\perp}|^2}{|R_{ab}|^2}
-\frac{R_{ab}\cdot\delta_{ab}\nabla p}{|R_{ab}|^2}
+\nu\frac{R_{ab}\cdot\delta_{ab}\Delta u}{|R_{ab}|^2}.
\]

It supplies no universal one-pair restoring sign. Exact positive-viscosity
cellular solutions contain material pairs that repeatedly contract and expand
while total energy and critical height decrease. Viscosity weakens each return;
it does not order pair distance monotonically.

This fixes the Gold boundary. A datum-bounded total of \([j_{ab}]_+\) over all
record intervals would already be a stronger version of the critical-height
peak theorem. It is not a smaller consequence of the ontology. Exact scaling
also permits order-one critical pair action on a heat-scale event whose Leray
energy cost is proportional to the shrinking radius. Therefore finite energy,
pair recurrence, or a separately priced pressure or viscous row cannot supply
the missing finite reserve.

## Critical pair compression selects its next carrier

Using the pair probability and selector defined above, the normalized
material-pair weight obeys

\[
\partial_t\Pi=\Pi g,
\qquad
g=\sigma-\mathbb E_\Pi\sigma,
\qquad
\sigma=-4\alpha+2\chi.
\]

This is a selection law on fixed material labels. A pair gains normalized
critical weight when its separation compression and complete relative
acceleration make its weight grow faster than the all-pair mean. Pressure and
viscosity remain one response here: after every pair is recombined,

\[
\mathbb E_\Pi\chi
=-\frac\nu2
\frac{\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2}{\mathcal H_{1/2}}.
\]

Their pairwise deviations still reassign critical weight even though their
complete mean is dissipative. This is how pressure can have no direct global
critical work and still alter the future Gold current.

The mean compression rate therefore satisfies

\[
\frac d{dt}\mathbb E_\Pi\alpha
=-\bigl(\mathbb E_\Pi\alpha\bigr)^2
-5\operatorname{Var}_\Pi(\alpha)
+\mathbb E_\Pi|\beta|^2
+\mathbb E_\Pi[n\cdot a_{\rm rel}]
+2\operatorname{Cov}_\Pi(\alpha,\chi).
\]

The mean-square term is longitudinal Riccati compression. Of the five variance
units, one comes from that pairwise Riccati term and four come from selection:
the \(-4\alpha\) row biases weight toward compressive pairs. The \(2\chi\)
row, transverse turning, and the complete pressure--viscous response can
oppose, reinforce, or redirect that bias. Treating pressure or viscosity as an
external payment erases the terms that decide which pairs carry the next
current.

The record consequence is exact. On every interval \([a,b]\),

\[
-\int_a^b\mathbb E_\Pi\alpha\,dt
=\frac14\log
\frac{\mathcal H_{1/2}(b)}{\mathcal H_{1/2}(a)}
+\frac\nu4\int_a^b
\frac{\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2}{\mathcal H_{1/2}}\,dt.
\]

Every critical-height doubling contains at least \(\frac14\log2\) of net
critical-weighted material-pair compression action. An unbounded record ladder
forces infinite cumulative action. Smoothness makes the mean compression rate
continuous, so every positive record gain contains a nonempty open interval of
net pair compression. It does not force a minimum clock-time or one permanent
pair carrier: the normalized weight may migrate among the same fluid's pairs
through the selection law. The physical Gold problem is now a coupled
persistence-or-turnover problem inside this one distribution.

For a finite-time unbounded record history, the turnover horn is forced. Let

\[
f_t=\frac{e_t}{\mathcal H_{1/2}(t)},
\qquad
g_t
=-4(\alpha-\mathbb E_\Pi\alpha)
+2(\chi-\mathbb E_\Pi\chi).

Then \(\partial_tf_t=f_tg_t\), so the normalized carrier has exact

\[
\frac12\|\partial_tf_t\|_{L^1(da\,db)}
=\frac12\mathbb E_\Pi|g_t|,
\qquad
\|\partial_t\sqrt{f_t}\|_{L^2(da\,db)}^2
=\frac14\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_t}(\sigma_t)
+\mathfrak z_0(t).
\]

The second equality is the almost-everywhere \(L^2\) metric speed; on every
positive-time portion of the history, \(\mathfrak z_0=0\).

If \(\mathcal H_{1/2}(t_n)\to\infty\) as \(t_n\uparrow T_*\), then for every
\(t_0<T_*\),

\[
\boxed{
\int_{t_0}^{T_*}\mathbb E_\Pi|g_t|\,dt=+\infty,
\qquad
\int_{t_0}^{T_*}
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_t}(\sigma_t)}\,dt=+\infty.
}
\]

The spatial behavior is forced concentration. The exterior \(X^1\)-bound in
the proof of Tao's Proposition 11.6 supplies one fixed ball \(B_{R_0}\) outside

Put \(\ell_n=E_0/\mathcal H_{1/2}(t_n)\) and choose

\[

Therefore

\[
C_n

obeys

\[
\boxed{

\qquad
|C_n|\le |B_R|\,|B_{\delta_n}|\longrightarrow0.

Every terminal high-record sequence is thus a genuine concentration of the

shows that such far-label ancestry occupies rare Lebesgue base-measure sets;

### Pair scarcity is invariant and terminal concentration is recruitment

The scarcity in the preceding statement is not created by the dynamics. Let

\[
\mathscr X_t(a,b)=(X(a,t),X(b,t)).
\]

Because the incompressible material map has unit Jacobian, its product map
does as well:

\[
(\mathscr X_t)_\#(da\,db)=dx\,dy.
\]

The change of variables

\[
c=\frac{x+y}{2},
\qquad
R=x-y
\]

also has unit absolute Jacobian. Hence, for every bounded center set \(K\) and
every \(\delta>0\),

\[
\left|
\left\{(a,b):c_t(a,b)\in K,
\ |R_{ab}(t)|<\delta\right\}
\right|
=|K|\,|B_\delta|.
\]

For the terminal sets \(C_n\), whose two current endpoints lie in \(B_R\), the
corresponding sufficient bound is

\[
|C_n|\le |B_R|\,|B_{\delta_n}|.
\]

The flow may replace the labels occupying this geometric set, but it cannot
increase its unweighted pair volume. The concentration

\[
\Pi_{t_n}(C_n)\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
|C_n|\longrightarrow0
\]

is therefore pure critical-weight selection inside one volume-preserving VPI
history. It is not fluid-mass concentration, material-pair creation, or loss
of flow-map invertibility.

This statement has a quantitative material-time consequence. Fix a positive
smooth reference time \(t_0\) with
\(\mathcal H_{1/2}(t_0)>0\), and write \(f_0=f_{t_0}\). Product-volume
preservation lets its label-space norm be computed in current pair variables:

=C\mathcal H_{1/2}(t_0)^{-p}
\int_{\mathbb R^3}|h|^{-4p}

\[
\|u(\cdot+h)-u\|_{L^{2p}}

so the radial integral at the pair diagonal is

\int_0^1r^{2-2p}\,dr.

\[
\boxed{

\quad\text{for every}\quad
1<p<\frac32.

The endpoint \(p=3/2\) is generically logarithmically divergent because a
smooth nonzero velocity increment makes \(f_0\) behave like \(|R|^{-2}\) near
the diagonal.

The normalized recruitment and the global record rejoin in the one

## The smooth state has unique ancestry despite irreversibility

At the declared smooth finite-energy scope, two histories cannot merge into
one full velocity state. Let \(u_1,u_2\) have the same surface and viscosity on

\qquad

\qquad

\[

\le C\bigl(\|w\|_2^2+\langle Lw,w\rangle\bigr).
\]

\[

\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
Q'\le C(1+Q),
\qquad
\frac d{dt}\log\|w\|_2^2\ge-C(1+Q).
\]

smooth interval.
