# Public Research Frontier

Updated: 2026-04-14

Purpose: record the current live public Navier-Stokes research frontier relevant to this lane.
This is a live support surface, not the proof-bearing authority corpus.
It is intentionally frontier-focused rather than an exhaustive all-time bibliography.

## Reading rule

- Treat the legacy Navier-Stokes corpus and lane-local theorem surfaces as the proof-bearing base.
- Treat the papers below as external public research that can invalidate, sharpen, redirect, or supply machinery for the live route.
- Separate claim-level papers from machinery papers and from obstruction papers.

## 1. Direct claim-level pathology papers

- Alexey Cheskidov, Mimi Dai, Stan Palasek, "Instantaneous Type I blow-up and non-uniqueness of smooth solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations," arXiv:2511.09556, submitted 2025-11-12, revised 2026-01-12.
  Link: [arXiv:2511.09556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09556)
  Why it matters: this is the closest current public claim to a direct negative resolution of the periodic Clay branch. It claims Type I blow-up and nonuniqueness from smooth periodic data via an inverse energy cascade from infinite to low frequencies.

- Thomas Hou, Yixuan Wang, Changhe Yang, "Nonuniqueness of Leray-Hopf solutions to the unforced incompressible 3D Navier-Stokes Equation," arXiv:2509.25116, submitted 2025-09-29, revised 2026-03-19.
  Link: [arXiv:2509.25116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25116)
  Why it matters: first public rigorous computer-assisted claim of nonuniqueness for unforced 3D Leray-Hopf solutions. Important both as a claim and as a proof-technology signal.

## 2. Pathology mechanism and nonuniqueness lane

- Stan Palasek, "Non-uniqueness in the Leray-Hopf class for a dyadic Navier-Stokes model," arXiv:2407.06179, submitted 2024-07-08.
  Link: [arXiv:2407.06179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06179)
  Why it matters: gives a concrete inverse-cascade / branching mechanism in a dyadic model. This is a mechanism paper, not a Clay claim, but it is one of the cleanest public sources for the pathology story.

- Matei P. Coiculescu, Stan Palasek, "Non-Uniqueness of Smooth Solutions of the Navier-Stokes Equations from Critical Data," arXiv:2503.14699, submitted 2025-03-18, revised 2025-07-21.
  Link: [arXiv:2503.14699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14699)
  Why it matters: constructs critical `BMO^{-1}` data with two distinct global smooth solutions for `t > 0`. This is a sharp public warning that critical-data uniqueness corridors are narrow.

- Stan Palasek, "Arbitrary norm growth in the 3D Navier-Stokes equations," arXiv:2509.18595, submitted 2025-09-23.
  Link: [arXiv:2509.18595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18595)
  Why it matters: shows arbitrarily large global solutions from smooth data bounded in `BMO^{-1}(T^3)`. Important as an obstruction to hoped-for critical-norm a priori control.

- Mikihiro Fujii, "Sharp non-uniqueness for the Navier-Stokes equations in scaling critical spaces," arXiv:2602.19846, submitted 2026-02-23, revised 2026-03-15.
  Link: [arXiv:2602.19846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19846)
  Why it matters: gives a sharp uniqueness/nonuniqueness classification in critical Besov spaces. Strategic value is mainly negative: it closes off sloppy critical-space uniqueness routes.

- Alexey Cheskidov, Hedong Hou, "On non-uniqueness of mild solutions and stationary singular solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations," arXiv:2603.03666, submitted 2026-03-04.
  Link: [arXiv:2603.03666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03666)
  Why it matters: extends unconditional nonuniqueness failure across negative-regularity Besov spaces through stationary singular solutions.

## 3. Quantitative regularity / rigidity / continuation lane

- Dallas Albritton, Tobias Barker, Christophe Prange, "Epsilon regularity for the Navier-Stokes equations via weak-strong uniqueness," arXiv:2211.16188, submitted 2022-11-29.
  Link: [arXiv:2211.16188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16188)
  Why it matters: one-scale epsilon regularity via weak-strong uniqueness. Foundational quantitative regularity machinery.

- Tobias Barker, Pedro Gabriel Fernandez-Dalgo, Christophe Prange, "Blow-up of dynamically restricted critical norms near a potential Navier-Stokes singularity," arXiv:2302.06509, submitted 2023-02-13, revised 2023-02-24.
  Link: [arXiv:2302.06509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06509)
  Why it matters: dynamically restricted endpoint critical norms and pressure-sensitive regularity machinery.

- Wen Feng, Jiao He, Weinan Wang, "Quantitative bounds for critically bounded solutions to the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations in Lorentz spaces," arXiv:2201.04656, submitted 2022-01-12, revised 2023-12-28.
  Link: [arXiv:2201.04656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04656)
  Why it matters: Tao-style quantitative regularity in critical Lorentz spaces.

- Tobias Barker, Henry Popkin, "Quantitative estimates for the forced Navier-Stokes equations and applications," arXiv:2602.09951, submitted 2026-02-10.
  Link: [arXiv:2602.09951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09951)
  Why it matters: current strongest public quantitative continuation / localisation / forced-Carleman machinery.

## 4. Axisymmetric singularity lab and geometric exclusion lane

- Thomas Y. Hou, "The potentially singular behavior of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations," arXiv:2107.06509, submitted 2021-07-14, revised 2022-05-26.
  Link: [arXiv:2107.06509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06509)
  Why it matters: still the main public exact-3D numerical candidate-singularity signal.

- Thomas Y. Hou, "Nearly self-similar blowup of generalized axisymmetric Navier-Stokes equations," arXiv:2405.10916, submitted 2024-05-17, revised 2025-06-29.
  Link: [arXiv:2405.10916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10916)
  Why it matters: not the exact 3D equation, but a serious self-similar blow-up program with dynamic rescaling and generalized axisymmetric structure.

- Tobias Barker, "Quantitative classification of potential Navier-Stokes singularities beyond the blow-up time," arXiv:2510.20757, submitted 2025-10-23, revised 2026-02-04.
  Link: [arXiv:2510.20757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20757)
  Why it matters: theorem-level scenario testing of Hou-style singularity candidates for approximately axisymmetric data.

- Qi S. Zhang, "On partial type I solutions to the Axially symmetric Navier-Stokes equations," arXiv:2604.07785, submitted 2026-04-09.
  Link: [arXiv:2604.07785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07785)
  Why it matters: removes a broad partial Type I axisymmetric corridor.

- Qi S. Zhang, "A blow up solution of the Navier-Stokes equations with a critical force," arXiv:2411.13896, submitted 2024-11-21, revised 2024-12-29.
  Link: [arXiv:2411.13896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13896)
  Why it matters: blow-up with critical forcing. Not the Clay statement, but directly relevant to singularity geometry and obstruction design.

- W. S. Ozanski, S. Palasek, "Quantitative control of solutions to the axisymmetric Navier-Stokes equations in terms of the weak L^3 norm," arXiv:2210.10030, submitted 2022-10-18, revised 2023-07-12.
  Link: [arXiv:2210.10030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10030)
  Why it matters: quantitative axisymmetric control from weak `L^3`.

- Zhen Lei, Xiao Ren, Gang Tian, "A geometric characterization of potential Navier-Stokes singularities," arXiv:2501.08976, submitted 2025-01-15.
  Link: [arXiv:2501.08976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08976)
  Why it matters: shows potential singularity geometry must satisfy a strong vorticity-direction condition; a useful geometric exclusion surface.

- Zhen Lei, Zhaojie Yang, Cheng Yuan, "Backward Uniqueness for 3D Navier-Stokes Equations with Non-trivial Final Data and Applications," arXiv:2311.02429, submitted 2023-11-04.
  Link: [arXiv:2311.02429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02429)
  Why it matters: backward uniqueness / symmetry propagation type machinery.

## 5. Transport / Lagrangian structure import

- Lucio Galeati, "Almost-everywhere uniqueness of Lagrangian trajectories for 3D Navier-Stokes revisited," arXiv:2406.12788, submitted 2024-06-18, revised 2025-03-10.
  Link: [arXiv:2406.12788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12788)
  Why it matters: strong transport/Lagrangian uniqueness input. For this repo, this is one of the clearest external supports for any route that needs transport coherence rather than pure critical-space uniqueness.

## 6. Strategic compression for this lane

- The public field is not one program. It is at least four overlapping lanes:
  1. direct pathology / nonuniqueness claims,
  2. pathology mechanisms and obstruction-building,
  3. quantitative regularity / rigidity / continuation machinery,
  4. axisymmetric and geometric singularity testing.

- The most dangerous public claim-level papers right now are `2511.09556` and `2509.25116`.

- The strongest public positive-route machinery paper right now is `2602.09951`.

- The most strategically important obstruction papers for this lane's function-space hygiene are `2602.19846`, `2603.03666`, and `2503.14699`.

- The most relevant transport/geometric import for routes that need real flow coherence is `2406.12788`.

## 7. Repo-use policy

- Use claim-level pathology papers as external threat monitoring, not as theorem authority.
- Use obstruction papers to kill false uniqueness or critical-space route drift early.
- Use quantitative regularity papers as machinery imports and benchmark surfaces.
- Use axisymmetric/geometric papers as singularity-lab constraints and candidate-exclusion surfaces.
- Do not let this public frontier note silently replace the lane's proof-bearing source base.
