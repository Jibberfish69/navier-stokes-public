## Missing Concepts Extracted From The Transcript

1. The fixed-cylinder objection is not cosmetic.

   The transcript shows the user pushing against a real target error: a fixed-radius cylinder is built from constant slices, while the live object has time-dependent slices. The dialectical PDF says this, but it does not carry the force of why the correction mattered: without the moving reference frame, the argument keeps bouncing between positive radius and radius collapse.

2. The moving-boundary term is only the first symptom.

   The transcript makes a sharper point: the cone changes the object. The boundary term is evidence that the object changed; it is not the whole story. The new sample states this before doing the energy identity.

3. The Gabriel's-horn analogy must be corrected, not simply repeated.

   The transcript first suggests finite interior budget with infinite wall demand, then corrects it: the naive physical wall cost can still be finite because the surface area shrinks. The real issue is the difference between discounted physical currency and undiscounted scale-critical currency.

4. The proof order matters.

   The user explicitly corrected the route: first treat the funnel as a forward-positive control object; only after a precise obstruction survives should CM exit classification enter. The dialectical PDF includes this, but the new sample makes it a governing transition.

5. The density-only jump has a motivation.

   The transcript's density section carries the inertia objection without letting region-mass vanish to zero. The final paper should preserve the underlying itch: pointwise velocity is controlled by pointwise kinetic-density over positive mass-density.

6. Mass-density and kinetic-density must be kept separate.

   The transcript repeatedly corrects a bad assistant drift where mass-density and energy-density get blurred. The new sample names `rho_0` as mass-density, `pi` as momentum density, and `k` as kinetic-density before using the pointwise relation.

7. Measurement order matters.

   The transcript contains a crucial correction: take the integral over a fixed ball first, then ask what happens if that same finite energy is collapsed to a point. Shrinking the ball before measuring changes the question. The new sample includes this as its own subsection because it explains the density closure obstruction cleanly.

8. Finite total kinetic energy is not finite pointwise kinetic-density.

   The transcript arrives at the real gap: the equation `|u|^2 = 2k/rho_0` closes velocity only after `k(x_0,T)` is known finite. Integrated energy gives `int k dx < infinity`; it does not give that pointwise premise.

9. A point-collapse creates a singular density object.

   Collapsing a fixed finite energy into zero volume produces a delta-like kinetic-density measure, not an ordinary classical Navier-Stokes field. This is useful as a side thought because it explains what the proof must exclude or reclassify.

10. The final density theorem is a density-to-density upgrade.

   The transcript's final useful target is: finite same-fluid scale-critical density ledger on the funnel should imply bounded kinetic-density near the tip. That is stronger and cleaner than a vague "energy controls velocity" statement.
