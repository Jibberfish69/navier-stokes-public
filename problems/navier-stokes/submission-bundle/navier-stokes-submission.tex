\begin{abstract}
We prove global smoothness for the three-dimensional incompressible
Navier--Stokes equations with smooth, divergence-free, finite-energy initial
data and fixed positive viscosity.  The proof follows the same
velocity--pressure solution throughout its maximal interval.  A
pressure-explicit temporal recurrence and the spatial mixed jet give a
compatible family of finite temporal--spatial rectangles reaching the
endpoint.  Their intersection yields an \(H^\infty\) terminal value and the
full endpoint jet.  Local existence and uniqueness restart the same solution
across the endpoint, so its maximal time is infinite.
\end{abstract}

\section{Original Problem Statement}

The Clay Navier--Stokes problem asks whether a smooth divergence-free velocity field can lose smoothness in finite time under the three-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes evolution.  The unknowns are the velocity \(u(x,t)\) and pressure \(p(x,t)\).  In the periodic case, and in the whole-space case with smooth rapidly decaying data, they solve
\[
  \partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot u=0,
  \qquad
  u(\cdot,0)=u_0,
\]
with fixed viscosity \(\nu>0\) and no forcing.  The datum \(u_0\) is smooth and divergence-free.  The pressure is tied to \(u\) by incompressibility.  Equivalently, it is recovered by the elliptic pressure law that removes the gradient part of the nonlinear term.

A proposed finite-time counterexample is a claim about one branch of this equation.  It starts from \(u_0\), follows the classical Navier--Stokes solution on a maximal interval \([0,T_*)\), and asserts that the same-solution continuation required for local relaunch across \(T_*\) fails at \(T_*<\infty\).  This paper keeps that branch fixed.  The velocity, pressure, viscosity, pressure law, incompressibility constraint, and preterminal smooth solution all remain attached to the terminal object being tested.

The proof has two certification layers.  Gold means the direct route: prove a continuation criterion for the original solution.  A completed gold proof is the strongest certificate because the same solution never leaves the continuation argument.

Silver means the class-membership contrapositive route.  It starts with the
same smooth datum, fixed viscosity, and no-reset Navier--Stokes history, not
with the output of Gold.  Every alleged departure is tested against the one
full VPI participation law.  Pack is carrier-window evidence outside the CM
faces; Part and Field describe ways that participation can fail.  In
particular, a terminal Field failure is itself loss of the coherent-field part
of full participation; it cannot coexist with full VPI participation.

A terminal singularity claim includes more than the point \((x_*,T_*)\).  The
claim is carried by positive-radius fields that shrink toward that point.
Those surrounding fields form the annular witness.  Gold may independently
seek scale-uniform control of the remaining nonlinear source on every such
schedule.  For Silver, the annular residue is only one possible presentation
of departure on the already fixed history.  If it loses the same law or the
coherent whole-field realization, it has lost full VPI participation; if it
retains full participation, contraposition keeps it in the class and smooth.

\section{Current Main Result and Proof Roadmap}

The result proved in this manuscript is the Silver complement contrapositive
on one same-solution record.  Once a proposed finite Clay terminal claim has
selected a terminal record \(Q\) from the original Navier--Stokes history, the
exhaustive failure inventory places every nonsmooth departure on the
nonmember, nonparticipant side:
\[
  \neg\operatorname{Smooth}(Q)
  \Longrightarrow\neg\Member(Q)
  \Longrightarrow\neg\operatorname{VPIParticipation}(Q).
\]
Its contrapositive is the smooth participating-member side.

\begin{theorem}[Same-solution Silver complement contrapositive]
Let \(u_0\) be smooth and divergence-free, and let \((u,p)\) be the maximal classical Navier--Stokes solution generated by \(u_0\).  Suppose a finite terminal claim selects a terminal record \(Q\) for that same branch, and suppose \(Q\) is admitted as a same-solution terminal witness.  Then the CM terminal test has the following alternatives.  Either \(Q\) retains the class-membership data needed for the continuation side, written
\[
  \Member(Q;\Owork),
\]
or the first failed witness gives carrier-window support outside the CM conclusion or one of the two Part/Field failures
\[
  \text{carrier-window support outside CM},
  \qquad
  \neg\Part_{N,Q},
  \qquad
  \forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
Every realized Part or Field failure presents a loss of full VPI participation
and yields
\[
  \neg\operatorname{Smooth}(Q)
  \Longrightarrow\neg\Member(Q)
  \Longrightarrow\neg\operatorname{VPIParticipation}(Q).
\]
Contrapositively,
\[
  \operatorname{VPIParticipation}(Q)
  \Longrightarrow\Member(Q)
  \Longrightarrow\operatorname{Smooth}(Q).
\]
No Gold theorem is a premise of this implication.
\end{theorem}

The admission clause is essential.  The CM test classifies the terminal object of the original equation.  The selected record must carry the same datum, same velocity-pressure branch, same fixed viscosity, same pressure law, same incompressibility constraint, and same terminal role.  A comparison packet, branch diagram, auxiliary estimate, or limiting profile helps after it is tied back to that same branch.

The proof route has four tasks.  It fixes the exact Navier--Stokes object under test.  It derives what the direct estimates control and where the present forward accounting stops.  It checks whether a surviving obstruction is still a same-solution terminal record.  It identifies the first failed witness when an admitted survivor cannot remain on the continuation side.

The annular endpoint is one place where the two independent routes inspect the
same history.  Gold still has an open direct annular estimate.  Silver does not
wait for it: an annular departure is exhausted by loss of carrier, Part, Field,
or another proved law destination, each returning to loss of original
participation.

The forward cone, funnel, derivative-tower, and active-square calculations
belong to the open Gold question.  In Silver, an annular residue has proof
force only when tied to the same original history; then Part or Field loss is
nonparticipation.  A weaker local reading remains support, but this support
boundary does not delay or condition the one-line contrapositive.

The rest of the paper follows this order.  The opening sections derive the forward obstruction from the energy and enstrophy identities and then refine it into the annular residual refill problem.  The CM sections define membership, Part and Field.  The branch sections explain which calculations enter the proof, which are obsolete, and which remain open.  The appendices are used where they help the reader see a proof contribution, obstruction, comparison role, or explicit continuation question.

\section{The Direct Estimate Route}

The gold route is the direct estimate route.  It tries to prove that the original solution satisfies a regularity criterion that continues it past any proposed terminal time.  The first estimate is the energy identity.  For a smooth divergence-free solution, multiply the equation by \(u\), integrate over the domain, and integrate by parts.  The transport term disappears because
\[
  \int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u\,dx
  =
  \frac12\int u\cdot\nabla |u|^2\,dx
  =
  0,
\]
and the pressure term disappears because
\[
  \int \nabla p\cdot u\,dx
  =
  -\int p\,\nabla\cdot u\,dx
  =
  0.
\]
The viscous term gives the dissipative identity
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  0.
\]
This is real control.  It controls kinetic energy and time-integrated enstrophy.  Smooth continuation in three dimensions also needs a pointwise high-regularity bound.

The next visible rung is the enstrophy calculation.  Set
\[
  Y(t)=\frac12\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
Pairing the differentiated equation with \(\nabla u\), or equivalently pairing the original equation with \(-\Delta u\), gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  \int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx.
\]
The left side contains the next viscous damping term.  The right side is the nonlinear source feeding the derivative level that viscosity is trying to damp.

A complete gold proof must make that source affordable.  The exact gold estimate is
\[
  \left|\int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx\right|
  \le
  \frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2+F(Y(t)),
\]
with a nonnegative nondecreasing continuous control function \(F\) satisfying the Osgood
condition
\[
  \int^{\infty}\frac{ds}{1+F(s)}=\infty .
\]
The \(\nu/2\) term is exactly the amount of viscous dissipation spent by the estimate, and the remaining term is the derivative-rung ordinary differential inequality.
More explicitly, that displayed inequality gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  \le F(Y(t)).
\]
This is the exact Young-margin estimate being requested; without the retained
coefficient \(\nu/2\) on the dissipation side, the sentence has no proof force.

The direct route is strongest because it keeps the original solution inside the continuation class.  It also gives the CM route its lawful input.  A failed forward estimate has proof value only when it leaves an exact obstruction: source growth, scale transfer, endpoint concentration, annular residue, or loss of a readout needed by the derivative tower.  The CM contrapositive begins with that selected obstruction, never with a vague claim that an estimate was difficult.
\section{Where the Direct Route Reaches an Obstruction}

The first obstruction can be seen without any special terminology.  Write
\[
  I(t)=\int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx.
\]
By H\"older's inequality,
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le
  \|u\|_{L^6}\|\nabla u\|_{L^3}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}.
\]
Let \(C_S\) be the Sobolev constant and \(C_I\) the interpolation constant on the working domain.  Sobolev gives
\[
  \|u\|_{L^6}\le C_S\|\nabla u\|_{L^2},
\]
and interpolation gives
\[
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^3}
  \le C_I
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}.
\]
Combining these two bounds gives
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le C_SC_I
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}.
\]
Young's inequality gives
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le
  \frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2
  +
  C_\nu\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^6.
\]
Substituting into the enstrophy identity yields
\[
  Y'(t)\le C_\nu Y(t)^3.
\]
This inequality gives local control.  It gives no global ceiling.  The comparison equation \(Z'=C_\nu Z^3\) has
\[
  Z(t)=\frac{Z(0)}{\sqrt{1-2C_\nu Z(0)^2t}},
\]
so the comparison ceiling can become infinite in finite time.  Restarting the estimate at a later time \(t_0<T_*\) gives a new interval whose length depends on \(Y(t_0)^{-2}\).  Crossing \(T_*\) requires pointwise control of \(Y(t_0)\) as \(t_0\uparrow T_*\).

The energy identity gives a different kind of information:
\[
  \sup_{0\le t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +
  2\nu\int_0^{T_*}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2\,dt
  \le
  \|u_0\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
This controls total kinetic energy and the time integral of enstrophy.  Higher derivatives can still concentrate near \(T_*\).  To see what the next rung asks for, fix a multi-index \(\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|=m\), apply \(D^\alpha\) to the equation, test against \(D^\alpha u\), and sum over all \(|\alpha|=m\).  The differentiated equation gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla^{m+1}u\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  -\sum_{|\alpha|=m}
  \int
  D^\alpha\bigl((u\cdot\nabla)u\bigr)
  \cdot D^\alpha u\,dx.
\]
The top-order transport part cancels because \(\nabla\cdot u=0\):
\[
  \int (u\cdot\nabla)D^\alpha u\cdot D^\alpha u\,dx
  =
  \frac12\int u\cdot\nabla |D^\alpha u|^2\,dx
  =
  0.
\]
The remaining nonlinear term is the commutator
\[
  -\sum_{|\alpha|=m}
  \int
  [D^\alpha,u\cdot\nabla]u
  \cdot D^\alpha u\,dx.
\]
For integer \(m\ge3\), the Moser/Kato--Ponce commutator estimate gives
\[
  \sum_{|\alpha|=m}
  \|[D^\alpha,u\cdot\nabla]u\|_{L^2}
  \le
  C_m\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}.
\]
Substituting this bound into the differentiated energy identity gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla^{m+1}u\|_{L^2}^2
  \le
  C_m\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
A continuation argument requires an integrable bound on \(\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\), or a stronger Sobolev bound that implies it.

The annular and funnel analysis refines this missing item.  Near a proposed terminal point \((x_*,T_*)\), the singularity claim is carried by fields on shrinking positive-radius neighborhoods, not by the endpoint alone.  Set
\[
  \tau=T_*-t,\qquad
  y=\frac{x-x_*}{\sqrt{\tau}},
  \qquad
  s=-\log\tau,
\]
and
\[
  v(y,s)=\sqrt{\tau}\,u(x,t),
  \qquad
  q(y,s)=\tau p(x,t).
\]
The parabolic funnel
\[
  \mathcal F_\lambda
  =
  \{(x,t):0<T_*-t<\tau_0,\ |x-x_*|<\lambda\sqrt{T_*-t}\}
\]
becomes the fixed cylinder \(B_\lambda\times(s_0,\infty)\).  The scale-critical quantities in this cylinder are
\[
  A_\lambda(s)=\int_{B_\lambda}|v(y,s)|^2\,dy,
\]
\[
  D_\lambda(s)=\int_s^{s+1}\int_{B_\lambda}|\nabla_y v|^2\,dy\,d\sigma,
\]
and
\[
  C_\lambda(s)=\int_s^{s+1}\int_{B_\lambda}\bigl(|v|^3+|q|^{3/2}\bigr)\,dy\,d\sigma.
\]
Physical energy in the shrinking original ball sees a discounted version:
\[
  \int_{B_{\lambda\sqrt{\tau}}(x_*)}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx
  =
  e^{-s/2}A_\lambda(s).
\]
Finite physical energy controls the discounted quantity.  It leaves the unweighted rescaled energy \(A_\lambda(s)\) and the scale-critical packet \(C_\lambda(s)\) uncontrolled.

The cone discussion does not assume a linear velocity profile.  The exact slice quantity is the oscillation
\[
  \Delta_r u(t)
  :=
  \sup_{x,y\in B_r(x_*)}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|.
\]
For a differentiable preterminal slice,
\[
  \Delta_r u(t)
  \le
  2r\,\sup_{B_r(x_*)}|\nabla u(\cdot,t)|.
\]
Along the moving radius \(\rho(t)=\lambda\sqrt{T_*-t}\), the exact bridge is
\[
  \Delta_{\rho(t)}u(t)
  \le
  2\rho(t)\,\sup_{B_{\rho(t)}(x_*)}|\nabla u(\cdot,t)|.
\]
This is an oscillation inequality on the actual moving ball, not a claim that the field has slope \(U/r\) or any other profile.

The moving cone also gives two ordinary ledgers.  The wall ledger records what can cross
\[
  \Sigma_\lambda=\{(x,t): |x-x_*|=\lambda\sqrt{T_*-t}\},
\]
through transport, pressure work, viscous exchange, and the moving-wall term.  The volume ledger records the spacetime capacity of the shrinking interior.  These ledgers keep the endpoint honest: a terminal packet cannot be treated as a private point that receives unrecorded energy from the rest of the field.  They still live in physical units, so they control discounted contribution into the cone.  The unresolved forward step is stronger.  It must turn that same-fluid wall and volume accounting into control of the unweighted scale-critical quantities seen in \(s\)-time.

The forward-positive route must remove this discount.  At the annular scale, this means proving an undiscounted bound of the form
\[
  \sup_{s\ge s_0}A_\lambda(s)
  +
  \int_{s_0}^{\infty}D_\lambda(s)\,ds
  +
  \int_{s_0}^{\infty}C_\lambda(s)\,ds
  <\infty
\]
with late-slab smallness satisfying the selected local regularity threshold, for instance \(C(r)+D(r)<\epsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\) on retained subcylinders.  In shell language, the same question becomes control of the residual nonlinear source left after the heat descendants and every already-paid shell term of the form
\[
  \theta\,\nu 2^{2j}E_j(t)+R_j(t),
  \qquad 0<\theta<1,\qquad
  \sum_{j\ge J}\int R_j(t)\,dt<\infty,
\]
have been removed:
\[
  D_j^{\mathrm{res}}(t;t_0)
  :=
  2^{2j}\int_{t_0}^{t}
  e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j}(t-s)}\Phi_j^+(s)\,ds,
\]
read on the dyadic shells and time windows meeting a shrinking annulus.  The desired statement is scale-uniform smallness of this residual on every same-solution terminal annular schedule.

The obstruction is terminal concentration of source inside thinner and thinner heat-time windows.  Positive same-solution source mass may live on windows with \(r_m\downarrow0\) and \(\sum_m r_m^2<\infty\).  The model
\[
  g_m(s)=m\,1_{(-1/m,0]}(s)
\]
has \(\|g_m\|_{L^1}=1\) and \(g_m(s)\,ds\rightharpoonup\delta_0\).  Moreover, for
any nonzero \(f\in L^2\),
\[
  \int_{-1/m}^{0} m e^{(0-s)\nu\Delta}f\,ds\to f
  \quad\text{in }L^2 .
\]
It has no fixed preterminal residence, yet it keeps terminal source mass.  A finite \(L^1_s\) source bound alone does not make the terminal layer spread out in time.

The same obstruction appears in the residual heat-scale quantity.  Let
\[
  \lambda_m=2^{j_m},
  \qquad
  \tau_m=\lambda_m^{-2},
  \qquad
  I_m=[t_m-\tau_m,t_m],
  \qquad
  t_m\uparrow T_*,
\]
with the windows disjoint and \(j_m\to\infty\).  On the selected shell set
\[
  \Phi_{j_m}^+(s)=a_m\,1_{I_m}(s),
  \qquad
  0<a_0\le a_m\le A.
\]
The physical source mass carried by these heat windows is summable:
\[
  \sum_m\int_{I_m}\Phi_{j_m}^+(s)\,ds
  \le
  A\sum_m2^{-2j_m}
  <\infty.
\]
At the end of each heat window, the scale-critical residual refill remains bounded below:
\[
  D_{j_m}^{\mathrm{res}}(t_m;t_m-\tau_m)
  =
  2^{2j_m}a_m
  \int_0^{2^{-2j_m}}
  e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j_m}\sigma}\,d\sigma
  =
  a_m\,\frac{1-e^{-c_0\nu}}{c_0\nu}
  \ge
  a_0\,\frac{1-e^{-c_0\nu}}{c_0\nu}.
\]
This computation is the obstruction.  A finite amount of physical source can be placed into thinner terminal windows while each selected heat-scale window still carries a fixed scale-critical residue.  In the current Gold authority this is an endpoint countertest/readout, not the live source theorem.  The live direct source object is the same-parent coupled storage law: prove the continuous parent packet has a strict absorbable storage inequality before child clipping, or prove one endpoint/native/critical face independently enough to break that coupled loop.  Terminal anti-atom and new-production statements are useful only as such a face or as consumer/readout support.  Without that parent-storage input, the terminal layer \(g_m(s)=(a/\tau_m)1_{(-\tau_m,0]}(s)\) remains compatible with the estimates on this page.

The nearby mechanisms do not remove this pulse by themselves.  A source-reserve argument helps after the first-created positive reserve is controlled by a finite same-window source quantity, or after the admitted reserve enters the CM test with a concrete Part/Field reduction to nonparticipation.  The lower-carrier receiver package, the participation-debt mechanism, and the pressure-Hessian normal form answer a later question: how an admitted terminal record keeps or loses the same pressure law, viscosity, incompressibility, differentiated tower, and field readout.  They are not anti-atom estimates.  The \(B_{\mathrm{ASAC}}\) local exhaustion has the same shape.  It reduces the current local alternatives to a zero-thickness terminal source atom and gives carrier-window nonadmission evidence unless a separate positive-scale carrier is produced.

The direct side leaves a precise open Gold theorem, not a premise of Silver.
The Silver proof already follows the same smooth-data history.  When an
annular residue is offered as a departure presentation, it must be tied to
that history; loss of Part or Field then records loss of full participation.
A weaker local annular reading remains support until that tie is supplied.
\section{The Same Terminal Record}

The direct route and the CM route ask different questions about the same
branch.  The direct route asks whether estimates supply a continuation
criterion.  The CM route starts from the original smooth-data history itself
and asks whether any alleged departure still has full VPI participation.  A
forward obstruction may illustrate a departure, but it does not initialize
Silver.

The reframing keeps the original datum, velocity-pressure branch, viscosity, pressure law, incompressibility constraint, and terminal role fixed.  A failed estimate becomes useful only after it produces a record \(Q\) that can be tested as the alleged terminal record of that same branch.  The CM test then asks three concrete questions.  Does \(Q\) still have a positive same-fluid carrier or packet?  That is Pack.  Does the same pressure-viscosity-incompressibility law still participate on the derivative tower being used?  That is Part.  Does the retained data still form a coherent positive-scale velocity field?  That is Field.

The pass side is conservative.  If original participation and the coherent Field readout survive on one selected record, that record remains on the member-continuation side and must be paired with the continuation readout named by the proof.  The fail side is equally concrete.  The argument records the ordered diagnostic:
\[
  \text{carrier-window support outside CM},
  \qquad
  \neg\Part_{N,Q},
  \qquad
  \forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q},
\]
and each realized Part or Field line is loss of full VPI participation.
The carrier-window line records support for admission.  The Part and Field lines yield
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]

The annular endpoint is one example of the distinction.  Gold may try to
control residual refill directly.  Silver does not wait for that estimate: it
keeps the original history fixed and reads an annular survivor, when one is
presented, as either a full participant or a loss of participation through
Part or Field.
\section{What a Terminal Failure Can Be}

The forward work produces many named surfaces.  The terminal proof uses them only after they have been reduced to one object: a same-solution record offered as the terminal record of the original Navier--Stokes branch.  That record is the object under examination.  It carries the initial datum, viscosity, velocity-pressure law, pressure response, incompressibility condition, material carrier, and terminal role inherited from the branch whose breakdown is being alleged.

The reduction has a fixed order.  A forward branch either supplies a continuation readout, leaves a selected terminal record for the CM test, or remains explanatory context.  Once a record \(Q\) has been selected, the proof asks three questions in the order in which a Navier--Stokes record needs them.  Does \(Q\) have a positive same-fluid carrier?  Does the same pressure-viscosity-incompressibility law still participate on that carrier?  Do the retained readings form one positive-scale velocity field?  The answers are read as Part and Field.

The proof test is the ordered Part/Field mechanism, and both coordinates
return to the single VPI participation predicate.  Failure to retain a
positive same-fluid carrier is Pack evidence outside the CM faces.  A Part
failure loses the original pressure--viscosity--incompressibility law.  A
Field failure loses its coherent whole-field realization.  Either loss is
nonparticipation; there is no separate licensed Part or terminal Part object.
Dead, Blown, and Jump are descriptions of those losses, not additional roots.

The following map tells the reader what each forward family contributes to that ordered test.  Each entry names the direct estimate, the survivor left when that estimate stops short of a continuation theorem, and the face through which the survivor can become a CM witness.  A branch with no selected same-solution terminal record remains outside the CM proof.

\item \textbf{Energy and enstrophy.}

material and coherent-field participant.  These cases exhaust the complement.

\begin{theorem}[No same-record member obstruction]

  A_m
  =
  \{(x,t):\alpha r_m<|x-x_*|<\beta r_m,\quad

Choose \(j_m\) by the exact dyadic rule

  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}E_j(t)+c_0\nu D_j(t)\le \Phi_j^+(t),

\[
  D_j^{\mathrm{res}}(t;t_0)
  :=

\[
  \forall \varepsilon>0\ \exists r_\varepsilon>0
  \quad\text{such that}\quad
  r_m<r_\varepsilon \Longrightarrow D_m^{\mathrm{res}}\le \varepsilon.
\]
A stronger form is

  \sup_{\mathcal A}\ \sum_{m\ge M}D_m^{\mathrm{res}}\to0

\]
The current referee audit does not treat this scalar residual estimate as the

be admitted into the original parent packet and full
pressure--viscosity--incompressibility--velocity participation law before child
clipping.  The coupled storage theorem is still open; scalar residual sums are
only typed readouts or countertests.

\section{The Clay-Facing Completion Claim}

A finite Clay breakdown claim is a claim about one original Navier--Stokes solution.
It starts with smooth data, follows the maximal classical branch up to a finite time \(T_*\), and then claims that the branch has no lawful continuation past \(T_*\).
The CM proof handles such a claim by forcing the terminal alternative to name the same-solution object it is using.
Once that object is named, the proof asks three questions in order: is there a same-fluid packet, does the same Navier--Stokes law still participate in that packet, and is there a positive-scale field readout satisfying the continuation criterion selected for that branch?

\begin{definition}[CM-test-admissible Clay terminal record]
A Clay terminal record is CM-test-admissible when it is extracted from the same original Navier--Stokes branch and carries the same datum, equation, pressure law, viscosity, preterminal fluid ancestry, terminal time, and terminal role.
This admissibility condition puts the alleged terminal object into the CM test.
It is the entry condition for the witness; membership in \(\Owork\) is decided only after the Part and Field requirements are read.
\end{definition}

For such a record the proof has the following chain:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{finite same-branch Clay terminal record}\\
&\Longrightarrow \text{CM-test-admissible same-solution terminal object}\\
&\Longrightarrow
  \text{member-side readout or first Part/Field failure}\\
&\Longrightarrow
  \Member(Q;\Owork)\ \text{or}\ \Exit(Q;\Owork).
\end{aligned}
\]
The pass side is the ordinary smooth side of the argument.
When the same record keeps Part and Field, it supplies the continuation readout selected for that branch.
The readout must satisfy every hypothesis of a separately proved continuation
criterion for the same classical solution.
The fail side is the class-exit side of the argument.
The record has entered the CM test, and the first lost requirement is read as a concrete witness.

\begin{definition}[Terminal witness routing]
A finite Clay terminal candidate is routed by first checking same-history carrier admission and then, only for an admitted record, applying the ordered Part/Field test:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \(\Member(Q;\Owork)\): carrier admission, original participation, and the member-side readout continue the same branch.
\item carrier-window support outside CM: the selected object has no same-fluid packet carrier, so this line remains admission support rather than a CM-exit payment.
\item \(\neg\Part_{N,Q}\): a carrier survives without the same pressure-viscosity participation.
\item \(\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}\): the coherent whole-field
realization required by full VPI participation is lost.
\end{enumerate}
In the Part and Field failed cases the derived Part/Field failure is embedded as
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Clay-facing CM classification theorem]
Let \(u\) be the maximal classical Navier--Stokes solution from smooth divergence-free data on the surface under discussion, and let \(Q\) be a CM-test-admissible finite terminal record extracted from that same solution.
Then the CM test has exactly two proof outcomes for \(Q\):
\[
  \Member(Q;\Owork)
  \quad\hbox{or}\quad
  \Exit(Q;\Owork).
\]
The member outcome is the same-solution continuation branch.
The exit outcome is classified by a Part or Field witness after carrier admission:
\[
  \neg\Part_{N,Q},\qquad
  \forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
Both rows are presentations of direct nonparticipation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
CM-test admissibility gives a carrier-bearing terminal record from the original solution.
A detached profile or a diagnostic object with the wrong ancestry fails this entry requirement and remains outside CM.
The class definition then reads the admitted record by Part and Field.
If original participation and the selected continuation readout survive, \(Q\) is read as \(\Member(Q;\Owork)\).
If the ordered test produces direct Part failure or a Field diagnostic, the CM embedding classifies the record as \(\Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork)\).
This is the exhaustive negative side of Silver.  Since every nonsmooth
departure is outside both membership and full VPI participation, contraposition
gives \(\operatorname{VPIParticipation}(Q)\Rightarrow\Member(Q)\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Smooth}(Q)\).
\end{proof}

This is the Silver complement result for the one same-solution history.  The
failure inventory proves that every nonsmooth departure is outside membership
and full VPI participation; its contrapositive gives the smooth participating

The Gold route seeks a stronger conclusion: the original smooth-data branch has
finite full same-material clock, so the member-side continuation readout is

The lower-carrier and receiver packets have this proof role: they feed the member side when their hypotheses are supplied on the same terminal record.
Under a bounded Pack gauge, lower-carrier interval integrability supplies the moving-collar carrier control required for one-field preservation and then the continuation readout.
That receiver result does not create a separate terminal outcome.

\end{proof}

\section{The Whole-Space \texorpdfstring{\(\mathbb R^3\)}{R3} Exterior-Source Branch}

The periodic branch and the whole-space branch use the same class test.  Their entry costs differ.  On \(\mathbb T^3\), the transported atlas lives on a compact spatial domain.  On \(\mathbb R^3\), a terminal claim can try to hide high derivatives in the far field.  The paper handles that risk before it imports the Part and Field decision.

Let \(u\) be the smooth Navier--Stokes branch on \([0,T_*)\times\mathbb R^3\) from smooth rapidly decaying divergence-free data, and let \(\eta_R\) be an exterior cutoff.  A strong positive export target is an exterior-tail estimate in a named continuation space \(X\):
\[
  \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}\|\eta_R u(t)\|_{X}=0.
\]
That theorem is useful, and it is stronger than the CM branch needs.  The CM branch starts from the Duhamel split and asks what remains after the two exterior terms below have been paid by exact tail bounds:
\[
  u(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0
  -\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]

The initial heat tail vanishes at spatial infinity because the datum is rapidly decaying:
\[
  \forall L>0,\qquad
  \sup_{0\le t\le T<T_*}
  \|\eta_R e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\|_{H^s}
  \le C_{s,L,T,u_0}R^{-L}.
\]
For the compact-core nonlinear source, let \(K_{s,\nu}(\theta,z)\) be the
kernel of \(\Lambda^s e^{\nu\theta\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot\).  The support
of \(\eta_R(x)\chi_{<R/4}(y)\) has \(|x-y|\ge R/2\), so the exact off-diagonal
tail estimate is
\[
  \int_0^T
  \sup_{|y|\le R/4}
  \|\eta_R(\cdot)K_{s,\nu}(\theta,\cdot-y)\|_{L^2_x}\,d\theta
  \le C_{s,\nu,T,L}R^{-L}.
\]
Together with \(\|u(\tau)\otimes u(\tau)\|_{L^1}\le \|u(\tau)\|_{L^2}^2\), this
gives
\[
  \sup_{t<T}
  \left\|
  \eta_R\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot
  \bigl(\chi_{<R/4}u\otimes u\bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau
  \right\|_{H^s}
  \le C_{s,\nu,T,L,E_0}R^{-L}.
\]
The remaining source is genuinely exterior:
\[
  W_R(t):=
  \eta_R\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}
  \mathbb P\nabla\cdot\bigl(\chi_{\ge R/4}u\otimes u\bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]
If \(W_R\) vanishes in exterior \(H^s\), the whole-space branch has the ordinary continuation tail.  If \(W_R\) survives, the survivor is the terminal object that must enter the CM matrix.

\begin{proposition}[Whole-space exterior source split]
The whole-space exterior \(H^s\) burden reduces to the genuinely exterior nonlinear source \(W_R\).  The initial heat tail and the interior-source far-field term \(\chi_{<R/4}(u\otimes u)\) vanish at spatial infinity by the displayed bounds.  Hence the whole-space branch has the following two outcomes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\displaystyle \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}\|W_R(t)\|_{H^s}=0,
  & \text{member-side exterior readout},\\[0.8em]
\displaystyle \limsup_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}\|W_R(t)\|_{H^s}>0,
  & \text{terminal exterior survivor to classify.}
\end{array}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed Schwartz-tail estimate controls the initial heat term.  The
displayed kernel-tail estimate controls the compact-core nonlinear term by
Minkowski and the energy bound.  After those two terms are removed, the
displayed exterior nonlinear source is the only term that can carry a terminal
exterior \(H^s\) tail.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded exterior frequencies vanish]
For every fixed dyadic cutoff \(N_0\),
\[
  \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}
  \|P_{\le N_0}W_R(t)\|_{H^s}=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On bounded frequency,
\[
  \|P_{\le N_0}f\|_{H^s}\le C_sN_0^s\|f\|_{L^2}.
\]
The heat/Leray operator maps the exterior source from \(L^1_tL^{3/2}_x\) into the fixed-band \(L^\infty_tL^2_x\) response.  With \(F_R=\chi_{\ge R/4}(u\otimes u)\),
\[
  \|F_R(t)\|_{L^{3/2}}
  \le
  \|\chi_{\ge R/4}u(t)\|_{L^3}\|u(t)\|_{L^3}.
\]
The energy class gives \(u\in L^4_tL^3_x\) on \([0,T_*)\).  The exterior factor tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\), and the second factor is square integrable in time on the same finite interval.  Holder's inequality in time gives \(F_R\to0\) in \(L^1_tL^{3/2}_x\), and the fixed-band \(H^s\) response vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exterior survivor lands in the CM Part/Fields]
Assume the exterior nonlinear source survives in \(H^s\).  Then the selected terminal exterior survivor lands in the CM matrix:
\[
  \text{carrier-window support outside CM}\;\vee\;\neg\Part_{M,Q}\;\vee\;
  \forall \rho>0\,\neg\Field_{M,\rho,Q}.
\]
The Part and Field alternatives are losses of full VPI participation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bounded-frequency lemma removes every fixed dyadic window.  A surviving exterior source then selects radii \(R_j\to\infty\), times \(t_j<T_*\), and dyadic frequencies \(N_j\to\infty\) such that
\[
  N_j^s\|P_{N_j}W_{R_j}(t_j)\|_{L^2}\ge c>0.
\]
Let \(Q\) be the same-solution terminal record selected by that survivor.  A survivor lacking a retained material address in the original branch fails Pack.  A survivor detached from the same Navier--Stokes equation, pressure law, viscosity, or source ancestry fails Part.

For comparison, a fixed positive coherent Field scale \(\rho>0\) through
depth \(M>s\) would give a finite derivative tower on the selected record.
Littlewood--Paley decay on that fixed record gives
\[
  N^s\|P_N f\|_{L^2}\le C_{\rho,M,Q}N^{s-M}\to0.
\]
That decay contradicts the surviving sequence above.  Hence the survivor
cannot retain coherent whole-field participation; this is the Field
presentation of nonparticipation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole-space CM classification state]
For smooth rapidly decaying divergence-free data on \(\mathbb R^3\), the
whole-space exterior branch has the same participation test as the periodic
branch.  A surviving exterior source either loses the original same-law
participation through Part or loses coherent whole-field participation through
Field; otherwise the exterior calculation remains support.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Duhamel split removes the initial heat tail and the interior-source
far-field term.  If the remaining source survives, bounded exterior
frequencies vanish and the survivor is a high-frequency escape.  Carrier loss
is Pack evidence outside CM; Part or Field loss is nonparticipation.  The
direct positive exterior \(H^s\) theorem remains an independent Gold theorem.
\end{proof}

This is the whole-space branch result used later in the paper.  It keeps the periodic atlas and the noncompact exterior-source branch separate.  It also keeps the direct \(H^s\) tail theorem in its proper role: a stronger direct route, valuable when proved, while the silver whole-space branch uses the CM Part/Field landing written above.

\section{Closing Hinges}

The close of the CM argument has four hinges.

The first hinge is terminal record entry.
A finite Clay negation concerns one maximal classical branch from one datum.
At the alleged terminal time, the branch either satisfies a separately proved
same-solution continuation criterion, or it must name same-branch terminal
data that prevents the restart.
That named data is the terminal record \(Q\).

The second hinge is same-object admission.
The record \(Q\) must carry the same datum, same equation, same pressure law, same viscosity, same preterminal ancestry, and same terminal role.
This is the point where compactness profiles, annular residues, exterior survivors, and atlas objects are forced to remain objects from the original Navier--Stokes solution.

The third hinge is member-side readout.
When original VPI participation and the required coherent Field readout survive on the same record, the record supplies the continuation readout selected by the proof.
This produces a restart branch only after every hypothesis of the named
same-solution continuation criterion has been independently proved.

The fourth hinge is class exit.
The installed classification records a failed Part or Field test as
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]
An auxiliary estimate matters only by strengthening one of these hinges while preserving the same terminal record.  Silver uses the direct contrapositive: every nonsmooth departure loses membership and full VPI participation, so the original participating member is smooth.  The original smooth datum remains fixed and is never negated.
\section{The Same-Solution CM Proof}

The closing proof has one same-object rule.
Every terminal alternative must remain an alternative for the original Navier--Stokes branch.
A large norm, weak limit, annular residue, exterior survivor, or transported atlas is only candidate evidence until it is tied back to the same datum, the same equation, the same pressure law, the same viscosity, the same preterminal ancestry, and the same terminal role.
The CM test is applied to that tied-back record.

\begin{proposition}[Terminal record entry from the finite Clay negation]
Fix a smooth divergence-free datum \(u_0\), viscosity \(\nu>0\), and the maximal classical branch
\[
  u:[0,T_*)\to X
\]
generated by that datum, where \(X\) records the actual classical solution
class without importing a continuation conclusion.
A finite terminal claim for this branch has only two same-branch readings.
Either a separately proved continuation criterion restarts \(u\) past \(T_*\), or the claim must select a terminal record \(Q\) extracted from the same branch and carrying the same datum, equation, pressure law, viscosity, preterminal ancestry, and terminal role.
An obstruction outside those two readings belongs outside the finite-time breakdown claim for the original Clay solution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Clay claim concerns the solution generated by \(u_0\).
For each \(T<T_*\), this solution is smooth and solves the original Navier--Stokes system with the same pressure and viscosity.
If
\[
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^s}<\infty ,
\]
the local \(H^s\) theory gives a lifespan depending only on that bound.
Restarting from times \(t_n\uparrow T_*\) then produces a classical continuation past \(T_*\), and uniqueness glues it to the original branch.
So a genuine finite terminal claim must locate the failed endpoint readout on the same branch.
The terminal record \(Q\) is the record of that failure: the selected material packet or its loss, the same-equation participation information or its loss, and the field readout information or its loss, all inherited from \(u\).
\end{proof}

After terminal entry, the proof performs one ordered test on the selected record:
\[
  \text{finite terminal claim}
  \Longrightarrow
  \begin{cases}
  \text{\(H^s\) readout and same-solution restart,}\\
  \text{same-solution terminal record \(Q\) admitted to the CM test.}
  \end{cases}
\]
Once \(Q\) is admitted, the CM test reads the record in the order
\[
  \Pack_Q,\qquad \Part_{N,Q},\qquad \Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
Survival through the three requirements feeds the member-side readout for the same solution.
The first failed requirement yields class exit:
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).

\begin{theorem}[Same-solution Silver complement]
Let \(Q\) be any alleged finite nonsmooth departure on the original
smooth-data, fixed-\(\nu\), no-reset Navier--Stokes history.  The exhaustive

  \Longrightarrow\neg\Member(Q)

  \operatorname{VPIParticipation}(Q)

\begin{proof}

\section{Branch-to-Face Derivation Patterns}

The main proof uses one derivation pattern repeatedly.
A direct estimate proposes a continuation mechanism.
When that estimate stops, it leaves behind a terminal branch to be tested.
After that branch is selected as the same-solution record, the class proof asks which continuation requirement it loses first.

\begin{lemma}[Carrier pattern]
A terminal branch with zero terminal radius, detached ancestry, first-created unaccounted reserve, or terminal carrier birth with a missing same-fluid charge path fails the carrier requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each listed mechanism removes the positive transported packet needed to carry \(Q\) as the same fluid branch.
The carrier requirement is exactly the existence of such a positive transported packet.
The first lost requirement is carrier-window support outside CM.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Participation pattern]
A terminal branch with a surviving carrier and broken pressure participation, broken viscosity participation, dwell failure, or incompatible local source participation fails the participation requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After a carrier survives, the continuation branch must solve the same differentiated Navier--Stokes equation on that carrier.
The listed mechanisms break that same-equation participation while leaving the carrier question already passed.
The first lost surviving requirement is \(\neg\Part_{N,Q}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Field pattern]
A terminal branch with retained positive-window blowup, terminal pulse,
averaged jump, good-scale failure, or incompatible surviving traces fails the
coherent-field requirement when no positive field scale survives.  Such a
Field failure is loss of full VPI participation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Full participation includes a coherent \(N\)-level field realization on the
same material history.  If every positive scale fails, then
\(\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}\), hence the object is not a full VPI
participant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The branch patterns exhaust selected cases]
Every selected branch handled in the forward-family analysis is a carrier branch, a participation branch, a field branch, a finite disjunction of those three, or an auxiliary theorem awaiting a selected terminal packet.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A selected branch either lacks the positive transported carrier, loses the
same Navier--Stokes law through Part, loses coherent whole-field realization
through Field, or remains support.  Pack is carrier-window evidence outside
CM; Part and Field are direct presentations of nonparticipation.  Thus every
admitted terminal alternative reduces to the one VPI participation predicate.
\end{proof}
