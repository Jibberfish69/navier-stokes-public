\begin{abstract}
This paper is a current-state, CMI-facing account of a same-solution
contrapositive program for finite-time breakdown in the three-dimensional
incompressible Navier--Stokes equations. The gold route is the usual
forward-positive route: prove estimates strong enough to carry smoothness
through every finite time. The live gold boundary in this manuscript is the
scale-uniform annular supplier: the residual scale-critical annular refill has
to be controlled on every shrinking schedule, or the discounted cone accounting
has to be upgraded to an undiscounted scale-critical cone budget.

The silver route is the class-membership exit contrapositive. It starts with the
alleged finite terminal record for the same original solution and tests whether
that record still has a positive material carrier, same-equation participation,
and a positive-scale coherent field readout. When all three survive, the record
belongs to the continuation side. When a first requirement fails, the failed
Pack, Part, or Field face is the class-exit witness for that same terminal
object.

Two current silver payments are installed on the page. On the periodic branch,
the transported whole-torus material atlas \(Q_{\mathrm{atlas}}\) pays terminal
selection and readout: the terminal object is the whole transported atlas, not a
private annular packet, and retained Pack, Part, and Field read out to the
global \(H^s\), \(s>5/2\), continuation norm. On the whole-space branch, the
exterior Duhamel split removes the initial heat tail and the fixed compact-core
far-field source. A vanishing exterior nonlinear source supplies the usual
whole-space continuation readout; a surviving exterior nonlinear source becomes
a dyadic high-frequency escape and lands in Pack, Part, or Field.

The manuscript keeps the endpoint annular witness explicit. The radius-zero
point is not enough by itself; the surrounding positive-radius fields remain the
live singularity witness until they are controlled by the gold route or landed
by the silver Pack/Part/Field analysis. Branch-family estimates, examples, and
failed positive-forward attempts are used as support only after they attach to a
selected same-solution object and pay one of those proof jobs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Original Problem Statement}

The problem is the incompressible three-dimensional Navier--Stokes existence and smoothness problem.
On the periodic three-torus \(\mathbb T^3\), and on the whole space \(\mathbb R^3\) with smooth rapidly decaying data, the velocity \(u(x,t)\) and pressure \(p(x,t)\) satisfy
\[
  \partial_t u +(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot u=0.
\]
The datum is a smooth divergence-free velocity field \(u_0\), and the solution starts from
\[
  u(\cdot,0)=u_0.
\]
Here \(\nu>0\) is fixed.
The external force is zero.
The question is whether the smooth solution determined by \(u_0\) can lose smoothness at a finite time.
The proof below treats the periodic branch through the transported whole-torus atlas and treats the whole-space branch through the exterior-source CM dichotomy.

\section{Gold Standard Approach (Explained) + Live Example}

The gold-standard approach is the direct estimate route.
It starts with quantities the equation controls and tries to climb from those quantities to the derivative bounds required for smoothness.
The first controlled quantity is kinetic energy.

The simplest example is energy.
For a smooth divergence-free solution on \(\mathbb T^3\),
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2=0.
\]
This identity says something very concrete.
The total kinetic energy cannot grow.
Viscosity spends energy through the gradient term \(\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2\).
At this level the equation behaves exactly the way one hopes: the fluid has a built-in damping law.

A smooth field asks for more than energy.
It asks that the velocity, its first derivatives, its second derivatives, and so on all remain finite on every finite time interval.
The next natural step is to ask for energy-style control one derivative higher.
Set
\[
  Y(t)=\frac12\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
The enstrophy calculation gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  \int_{\mathbb T^3}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx.
\]
The left side still contains viscosity.
The right side is the nonlinear source.
It measures how the motion of the fluid can feed the same derivatives that viscosity is trying to damp.

A successful gold-standard proof would control that source.
The dream estimate has the shape
\[
  \left|\int_{\mathbb T^3}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx\right|
  \le
  \frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2+F(Y(t)),
\]
  with \(F\) tame enough to integrate for all finite time.
Then viscosity absorbs the dangerous part, the remaining term stays under control, and the derivative stack stays finite.
That is the clean proof picture: estimate the source, absorb the hard part, continue the solution.

This route feels right because it starts where the equation gives us something real.
Energy works.
Viscosity really does spend motion.
The trouble begins when the source term requires a derivative bound that the known energy identity does not supply.

\section{Reaching Obstruction}

The obstruction already appears in the enstrophy estimate.
The nonlinear term is
\[
  I(t)=\int_{\mathbb T^3}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx .
\]
A forward proof needs a bound on \(I(t)\) that gives a global upper bound for
\[
  Y(t)=\frac12\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2.
\]

The standard estimate begins with H\"older:
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le
  \|u\|_{L^6}\|\nabla u\|_{L^3}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}.
\]
Sobolev gives \(\|u\|_{L^6}\lesssim \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}\).
Interpolation gives
\[
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^3}
  \lesssim
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}.
\]
So
\[
  |I(t)|\lesssim
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}.
\]
Young's inequality gives
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le
  \frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2
  +C_\nu\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^6.
\]

Put this back into the enstrophy identity.
The estimate gives
\[
  Y'(t)\le C_\nu Y(t)^3.
\]
This inequality is the wall.
The comparison equation
\[
  Z'(t)=C_\nu Z(t)^3
\]
has the explicit solution
\[
  Z(t)=\frac{Z(0)}{\sqrt{1-2C_\nu Z(0)^2t}}.
\]
The comparison bound becomes infinite at \(t=(2C_\nu Z(0)^2)^{-1}\).
The estimate proves short-time control only.
Global control requires more information.

Restart the comparison at a time \(t_0<T_*\).
The same calculation gives
\[
  Y(t)\le
  \frac{Y(t_0)}
  {\sqrt{1-2C_\nu Y(t_0)^2(t-t_0)}}
\]
as long as
\[
  t-t_0 < \frac{1}{2C_\nu Y(t_0)^2}.
\]
Near an alleged terminal time, the direct estimate passes through \(T_*\) only when this interval stays longer than \(T_*-t_0\).
That requires control of \(Y(t_0)\) as \(t_0\to T_*\).

Energy gives
\[
  \sup_{0\le t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +2\nu\int_0^{T_*}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2\,dt
  \le
  \|u_0\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
This is an \(L^2\) bound for velocity and a time-integral bound for \(Y\).
The restart argument needs a pointwise bound for \(Y\) near \(T_*\).
The energy law gives the first two quantities.
The pointwise derivative ceiling is the missing quantity.

The higher-rung estimate exposes the same missing quantity in a sharper form.
For \(m\ge2\), a standard commutator estimate gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla^{m+1}u\|_{L^2}^2
  \le
  C_m\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
To integrate this inequality, the forward route needs a time-integrable bound on \(\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\), or a Sobolev bound strong enough to imply it.
That is already derivative-level control beyond the energy law.
The estimate has named the missing control without producing it.

This is the obstruction.
The standard inequalities reach the exact nonlinear term, bound it honestly, and arrive at a differential inequality that permits finite-time blowup.
The next section changes the question: when a terminal picture is suggested by such a failure, is that picture still the Navier--Stokes object defined by the problem?

\section{Reframing the Problem}

This is the point where the paper adds a second question to the direct estimate route.
The forward estimates have named a possible terminal picture: derivative growth, scale transfer, residue, slip, or endpoint readout.
The paper then asks whether that picture is still a terminal record for the same original Navier--Stokes solution.

A class-test record must carry the same datum, the same equation, the same pressure, the same fixed viscosity, the same incompressibility law, the same velocity field, and the same terminal role.
A picture with no positive material carrier, no same-fluid ancestry, no pressure-viscosity participation, or no positive-scale coherent field readout is a different object from the Clay object.
The class question is the attachment test: does the alleged terminal record still describe the original smooth-data solution, or has the breakdown story changed the object under test?

The pass side is conservative.
When the same-solution record retains its carrier, participation, and field readout, it remains on the continuation side and still owes whatever positive estimate or readout the paper has named.
The fail side is equally concrete.
The proof records the first failed face of the same terminal object: Pack for lost carrier, Part for lost same-law participation, and Field for lost positive-scale coherent field readout.

This reframing keeps the problem fixed.
The silver route only receives terminal pictures supplied by the forward-support field, and only after the paper has selected the same-solution record being tested.
At the current state of the manuscript, the annular endpoint witness remains visible because its surrounding positive-radius fields are still the live witness until the gold route controls them or the silver route lands their first failed face.

\section{Defining Class Membership}

The paper now gets organized around one question.
When a terminal picture is placed in front of us, is it still the Navier--Stokes object named by the problem, or has it become some other imagined object?
That is the class-membership question.

Call the working Navier--Stokes class object \(\Owork\).
It is the original smooth-data incompressible Navier--Stokes evolution, with the same datum, the same equation, the same pressure, the same viscosity, the same incompressible transported material family, and the same terminal role.
For a terminal packet \(Q\),
\[
  \Member(Q;\Owork)
\]
means that \(Q\) is still part of that object.
Class exit means:
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]

This definition is deliberately plain.
A blowup story, a residue story, a slip story, or a source story can sound like a Navier--Stokes story.
The paper asks the simpler question: does the object still belong to the class named by the Clay problem?
The rest of the witness test exists to answer that question without guessing.

\subsection{Properties of NS Fluid}

The Navier--Stokes fluid here is an ideal mathematical fluid.
Water, honey, and oil can help the imagination, yet the object is fixed by the equation.
It has a velocity field \(u\), a pressure \(p\), fixed positive viscosity \(\nu>0\), normalized density through incompressibility, and smooth divergence-free initial data.
The force term is absent.
This paper works on the periodic three-torus \(\mathbb T^3\), so the fluid lives in a periodic box and the whole transported material atlas can be read on a compact domain.

The defining properties do not change after \(t=0\).
The viscosity does not become another viscosity.
The pressure does not become an optional correction.
The incompressibility law does not acquire a pressure-responsive density variable.
The velocity field moves under the same equation:
\[
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot u=0.
\]
Viscosity dissipates velocity differences over time, and pressure keeps the field inside the divergence-free constraint.
A terminal object that changes these properties has left the problem object.

\subsection{Euler Boundary Object}

Euler enters the manuscript as a boundary object for class exit.
Its base membership test is the inviscid transport-pressure law: incompressible flux-volume balance and weak momentum-pressure fidelity.
In the notation used by the source audits, that base object is \(\mathrm{EVol}+\mathrm{EMom}\).
Regularity, Lagrangian carrier quality, and one-field coherence are extra layers above that base Euler membership test.

A slip sheet is the simplest picture.
Two incompressible flows can meet with matching normal flux while their tangential velocities jump.
For Euler, that picture can still carry the flux and momentum balance.
For fixed positive viscosity, the same picture has lost the pressure-viscosity participation and one-field regularity demanded by Navier--Stokes.

This is the class-exit/class-entrance line used here.
When the selected nonsmooth Navier--Stokes branch loses Part or Field only through loss of viscous participation while retaining the inviscid flux-momentum law, the escaped object is read as an Euler boundary object.
The paper does not use this to solve Euler.
It uses it to say what the escaped object is in that clean special case.

\subsection{Viscosity}

Viscosity is often described as damping.
That is true.
The proof also needs the tethering role of viscosity.
Viscosity transmits motion between nearby parts of the fluid.
It acts like a tether between neighboring velocity values.
A fast piece pulls on a slow piece, and a slow piece pulls back on a fast piece.

The Couette-flow picture is the clean version.
Two boundary plates move at different speeds.
The viscous fluid between them forms a smooth velocity gradient.
Viscosity turns the plank-slip picture into one continuous velocity field.

A honey pour gives the same intuition in ordinary life.
The honey slows as it folds and piles up, yet the falling stream also pulls nearby honey along with it.
The stream carries mass and momentum through a viscous body.
The point for Navier--Stokes is that viscosity damps motion and shares motion.
It gives neighboring packets a common law of response.
A siphon gives the same kind of picture: once the moving column is coupled, one part of the motion pulls the next part along.

\subsection{Pressure, Viscosity, Incompressibility}

Pressure, viscosity, and incompressibility form one law in the Navier--Stokes class.
Incompressibility says that a transported material volume keeps its volume.
Pressure is the global response that keeps the velocity field divergence-free.
Viscosity diffuses velocity through \(\nu\Delta u\) and couples neighboring velocity readings.

These three features leave very little freedom for a terminal packet.
A packet may be visible as a region of space.
It may even carry finite velocity values.
It still has to take part in the same pressure-viscosity law on the same incompressible carrier.
This is the participation requirement.

This matters most for failure stories that appear as source residue, terminal atoms, or pressure response.
An incompressible Navier--Stokes packet has no separate density variable that can swell or shrink to pay for a terminal source.
It has no separate pressure law that acts on one packet while the rest of the fluid follows another law.
The packet either participates in the same law, or it has left the class object under test.

\subsection{Velocity Tower}

Smoothness is read through a tower of derivative information.
The base velocity \(u\) is the first reading.
The higher readings are \(\nabla u,\nabla^2u,\ldots\).
A smooth Navier--Stokes object carries these readings as one tower under one equation.

Write
\[
  U_k:=\nabla^k u,
  \qquad
  K_k:=-\nabla^{k+1}p+\nu\Delta U_k,
  \qquad
  D_t:=\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla.
\]
Then each rung has the schematic form
\[
  D_tU_k=K_k+B_k,
\]
where \(B_k\) contains lower-rung commutator terms.
The tower is needed because a terminal picture can fail above the base velocity.
The velocity may still be finite while a derivative rung breaks, jumps, or becomes unbounded.
The class test asks whether the same Navier--Stokes pressure-viscosity law still acts through the finite tower depth needed to read one smooth field.

\section{Participation and One-Field Coherence Witness Laws}

The physical discussion now becomes a witness test.
The packet first has to be a same-fluid body.
Then it has to participate in the same law.
Then nearby readings have to fit as one field on a positive scale.

The terminal object is read on one common record
\[
  \WitnessRecord=(u_0,\nu,u,p,Q,N,r,\Phi,T_*).
\]
Here \(u_0\) is the original datum, \((u,p)\) is the same preterminal solution, \(Q\) is the terminal packet, \(N\) is the tower depth, \(r\) is the positive field scale, \(\Phi\) is the transported same-fluid carrier, and \(T_*\) is the claimed terminal time.

\begin{definition}[Witness record]
A witness record is the common record
\[
  \WitnessRecord=(u_0,\nu,u,p,Q,N,r,\Phi,T_*),
\]
where each symbol has the role just stated.
All witness laws are read on this one record.
\end{definition}

Participation is the same-law witness.
It asks whether the packet still carries the Navier--Stokes pressure-viscosity tower on the same transported carrier.

\begin{definition}[Participation witness law]
\(\Part_{N,Q}\) means that, on the packet under test, the same Navier--Stokes equation acts through the velocity tower to depth \(N\):
\[
  D_tU_k=K_k+B_k,
  \qquad
  K_k=-\nabla^{k+1}p+\nu\Delta U_k,
  \qquad 0\le k\le N.
\]
The packet is participating in the same Navier--Stokes law.
\end{definition}

One-field coherence is the neighboring-field witness.
It asks whether nearby packet readings still fit together as one field on a positive scale.

\begin{definition}[One-field witness law]
\(\Field_{N,r,Q}\) means that, after the packet and participation readings are fixed, one positive scale \(r>0\) carries coherent field data through depth \(N\).
A convenient model is the finite-difference coherence bound
\[
  \sup_{\substack{x,x+h\in Q_t\\0<|h|\le r}}
  \sum_{k=0}^{N}
  \frac{|U_k(x+h,t)-U_k(x,t)|+|K_k(x+h,t)-K_k(x,t)|}{|h|}<\infty
\]
on the time slices under test.
This law says that neighboring packet pieces share one field on a positive scale.
\end{definition}

Participation and one-field coherence come from the physical meaning of pressure, viscosity, and incompressibility.
A packet that has lost the pressure-viscosity tower has lost same-law participation.
A packet whose neighboring towers fail to fit has lost one-field coherence.
These two laws are the Part and Field faces of the class test.

\section{Packet Witness Law}

The packet law comes first because the proof needs a same-fluid body before it can read participation or field coherence.
A terminal trace, source atom, or endpoint number gives too little by itself.
The object has to give a positive transported packet of the same fluid.

\begin{definition}[Packet witness law]
\(\Pack_Q\) means that the claimed terminal object has a positive same-fluid packet.
There is a transported carrier family for \(Q\), a finite cover with finite overlap, and a positive lower scale \(\rho_Q>0\).
The packet is moved by the same velocity field \(u\).
A trace with no positive carrier, or a donor history outside the same flow, fails this law.
\end{definition}

On the torus branch, the clean packet is the whole transported material atlas.
It is a finite same-fluid cover of \(\mathbb T^3\), with finite overlap and positive carrier scale.

One packet sees its own tower become unbounded.
A neighboring packet sees the positive-scale field relation fail.
That is why blown stays inside the witness tree as a Pack, Part, or Field event.

\section{The Support Boundary}

It still has to say which class requirement it protects.

\begin{proposition}[Support enters through a face]
Let \(S\) be a source, readout, comparison, or supplier statement.
The statement becomes a class-proof conclusion by landing a selected same-solution object in \(\Pack_Q\), \(\Part_{N,Q}\), \(\Field_{N,r,Q}\), membership readout, terminal packet capture, or no genuine finite exit.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The class proof has only three witness requirements and three closing hinges.
A supplier estimate can help prove one of them, and a readout estimate can explain the continuation consequence after the requirements survive.
A comparison estimate can show why a neighbouring theorem world behaves differently.
None of those roles is itself membership or exit.
The statement enters the proof only when its exact target is a class requirement or closing hinge.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Forward-positive estimates keep their value]
Demoting a forward-positive estimate from class authority preserves its value.
It assigns the estimate a proof job:
\[
  \text{support Pack},\quad
  \text{support Part},\quad
  \text{support Field},\quad
  \text{support readout},\quad
  \text{or support no genuine exit}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The estimate remains available exactly where it has mathematical force.
The class proof only prevents the estimate from pretending to be the class law by itself.

\end{proof}

\section{Whole-Space \(\mathbb R^3\) CM Exterior-Source Branch}

The periodic proof and the whole-space proof use the same CM discipline, but they do not have the same compactness boundary.
On \(\mathbb T^3\), the transported whole-torus material atlas has no spatial escape.
On \(\mathbb R^3\), a finite terminal claim can also try to hide mass or derivatives in the far field.
The direct positive export theorem is not just a copy of the periodic proof.
It would show that the original rapidly decaying datum cannot produce a terminal exterior \(H^s\) tail, for some \(s>5/2\), outside every large ball.
The CM-facing proof uses a different burden: it classifies any surviving exterior source as a Pack, Part, or Field failure for the same terminal object.

Let \(u\) be a smooth finite-energy Navier--Stokes solution on \([0,T_*)\times\mathbb R^3\) from smooth rapidly decaying divergence-free data.
Let \(\eta_R\) be an exterior cutoff supported outside \(R\) and equal to one outside \(2R\).
The whole-space tail burden is
\[
  \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}\|\eta_Ru(t)\|_{H^s(\mathbb R^3)}=0.
\]
The false shortcut is to replace this by exterior \(L^2\) tightness.
That loses derivatives and cannot prove the continuation norm.
The correct split uses Duhamel's formula:
\[
  u(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0
  -\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]
After splitting \(u\otimes u\) into a compact-core part and a genuinely exterior part, the exterior \(H^s\) tail has three sources.

\begin{proposition}[Whole-space exterior source split]
For rapidly decaying smooth data, the heat evolution of \(u_0\) contributes no terminal exterior \(H^s\) tail.
For a fixed compact core, the far-field effect of the compact-core nonlinear source also contributes no terminal exterior \(H^s\) tail, once the fixed-core readout is available.
Thus the stronger direct whole-space export theorem reduces to the exterior nonlinear source estimate
\[
  \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}
  \left\|
  \eta_R\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}
  \mathbb P\nabla\cdot\bigl(\chi_{\ge R/4}u\otimes u\bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau
  \right\|_{H^s(\mathbb R^3)}
  =0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The heat part is controlled by rapid decay of the initial datum.
Derivatives commute with the heat flow, and weighted heat estimates give a negative power of \(R\) for \(\eta_Re^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\) on every finite preterminal interval.

For the compact-core source, the kernel of \(\Lambda^se^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot\) is smooth away from the diagonal.
The compact source and the exterior cutoff are separated by distance comparable to \(R\).
Off-diagonal heat-kernel decay then gives a negative power of \(R\), with the fixed-core local readout supplying the finite number of derivatives needed for the \(H^s\) norm.

The only part not covered by those two mechanisms is the nonlinear source already living far out in space.
That is the displayed exterior nonlinear-source estimate for the stronger direct export route.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exterior source survivor lands in the CM faces]
Assume the exterior nonlinear source estimate above is not supplied, and let a terminal exterior high-frequency source survive after the Duhamel split and the fixed-band bounded-frequency elimination.
Then the survivor lands in the CM matrix:
\[
  \neg\Pack_Q\;\vee\;\neg\Part_{M,Q}\;\vee\;
  \forall \rho>0\,\neg\Field_{M,\rho,Q}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bounded-frequency part cannot carry the \(H^s\) tail.
On a fixed dyadic band, the heat/Leray multiplier maps the exterior nonlinear source from \(L^1_tL^{3/2}_x\) into the \(L^\infty_tL^2_x\) response.
After the compact-core source has been removed, this exterior source is \(F_R=\chi_{\ge R/4}(u\otimes u)\), and
\[
  \|F_R(t)\|_{L^{3/2}}
  \le
  \|\chi_{\ge R/4}u(t)\|_{L^3}\|u(t)\|_{L^3}.
\]
The energy class gives \(u\in L^\infty_tL^2_x\cap L^2_t\dot H^1_x\), hence \(u\in L^4_tL^3_x\).
Thus the exterior factor tends to zero in \(L^2_t\), while the second factor is in \(L^2_t\), so \(F_R\to0\) in \(L^1_tL^{3/2}_x\).
Fixed bounded frequencies vanish in \(H^s\).
Any survivor must escape to dyadic frequency: \(N_j\to\infty\) while \(N_j^s\|P_{N_j}W_{R_j}(t_j)\|_{L^2}\) stays bounded below.
If the surviving packet has no same-fluid material address, \(\Pack_Q\) fails.
If it is not carried by the same Navier--Stokes pressure, viscosity, and source ancestry, \(\Part_{M,Q}\) fails.
On the retained Pack+Part branch, a fixed positive Field scale \(\rho>0\) supplies a single participating packet and a finite derivative tower through some \(M>s+2\).
That tower is fixed on the witness record.
Littlewood--Paley localization then makes the high dyadic tail vanish on that record:
\[
  \lim_{N\to\infty} N^s\|P_N W_R(t)\|_{L^2}=0
\]
uniformly on the retained positive Field window.
This contradicts the survivor lower bound along \(N_j\to\infty\).
Thus retained Pack+Part forces failure of every fixed positive Field scale.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole-space CM classification state]
For smooth rapidly decaying divergence-free data on \(\mathbb R^3\), a finite terminal whole-space branch has the same CM pass-or-exit conclusion as the periodic branch.
This conclusion is not imported from the torus atlas; it is paid by the
exterior-source Duhamel split and the dyadic survivor face landing.
The branch either has the exterior \(H^s\), \(s>5/2\), tail needed for the usual continuation readout, or the surviving exterior source lands in
\[
  \neg\Pack_Q,\qquad
  \neg\Part_{M,Q},\qquad
  \forall \rho>0\,\neg\Field_{M,\rho,Q},
\]
and supports \(\Exit(Q;\Owork)\).
The \(\mathbb R^3\) exterior survivor branch is consumed by the same CM endpoint matrix.
A direct positive exterior \(H^s\) tail theorem would give more than this argument needs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the Duhamel split above.
The heat tail from the rapidly decaying initial datum is harmless.
The fixed compact-core nonlinear source is harmless in the far field after the fixed-core readout.
The remaining exterior nonlinear source has two outcomes.
When its \(H^s\) tail vanishes, the whole-space object has the continuation readout on the same terminal packet.
When a terminal exterior source survives, bounded exterior frequencies are removed by \(L^2\) tightness, so the survivor is a dyadic high-frequency escape.
The preceding proposition classifies exactly that survivor as Pack failure, Part failure, or Field failure after retained Pack and Part.
The CM embedding reads that first failed face as \(\Exit(Q;\Owork)\).
Thus the whole-space terminal claim has no in-class nonsmooth third branch inside the CM classification.  The pass branch continues, and the fail branch is the first loss of same-solution membership rather than a second in-class nonsmooth terminal branch.
\end{proof}

This is the whole-space CM pass-or-exit state.
The direct exterior \(H^s\) estimate remains a stronger positive export theorem.
The CM proof classifies the survivor branch: if the exterior source is still present at the terminal edge, its first failed Pack, Part, or Field face is the class-exit witness.  The class-exit witness is not an in-class nonsmooth terminal branch of the original solution.

A finite terminal Clay claim for this branch has only two same-branch forms.
Either it supplies the \(H^s\) endpoint readout that restarts \(u\) past \(T_*\), or it supplies a terminal record \(Q\) extracted from the original branch, with the same datum, same equation, same pressure law, same viscosity, same preterminal fluid ancestry, and the same terminal role.
An alleged obstruction that supplies neither object has not yet stated finite-time breakdown of the original Clay solution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Clay claim is not about an arbitrary pattern that resembles a singularity.
It is about the solution generated by \(u_0\).
For each \(T<T_*\), that solution is smooth, solves the original Navier--Stokes system, and carries its material history from the same datum.

If an \(H^s\), \(s>5/2\), endpoint readout for that same branch is available at \(T_*\), the local continuation theorem restarts the branch and the terminal claim fails.
If that restart readout is not available, the claim has to say what prevents the restart while staying attached to the same branch.
The record \(Q\) is that endpoint data: the selected material packet or its failure, the same-equation participation information or its failure, and the field readout information or its failure.
These are forced by the classical negation once the datum, equation, branch, and terminal role are kept fixed; they are not imported as a definitional shortcut from the CM grammar.
\end{proof}

The proof has five payments.
First, the finite Clay negation is reduced to the same-branch alternative above: restart readout, or terminal record \(Q\).
Second, \(Q\) is admitted to the CM packet tree, or the proposed obstruction is rejected as the wrong object for the original solution.
Third, the admitted record is read in the order
\[
  \Pack_Q,\qquad \Part_{N,Q},\qquad \Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
Fourth, the retained branch supplies the member-side readout chain for the same solution; \(L^2\), \(L^p\), averaged, receiver, read-cover, endpoint-matrix, and Sobolev readouts are typed by the proof layer where they occur.
When that chain reaches the classical Sobolev continuation consumer, it supplies the \(H^s\), \(s>5/2\), restart package.
Fifth, the failed branch is embedded as
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]
These payments are the proof route from the finite Clay negation to the same-solution dichotomy.

\begin{theorem}[Same-solution CM dichotomy]
Let \(Q\) be the canonical terminal record selected by a finite Clay terminal claim for the original Navier--Stokes solution.
In the CM test, \(Q\) has only two proof outcomes:
\[
  \Member(Q;\Owork)
  \quad\hbox{or}\quad
  \Exit(Q;\Owork).
\]
The member outcome gives the same-solution readout chain up to the selected continuation consumer.
The exit outcome is supported by a first failed witness face:
\[
  \neg\Pack_Q,\qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q},\qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The terminal record entry proposition gives the same-branch reduction.
The restart-readout case continues the same solution and cannot be terminal.
In the remaining case, the finite Clay claim supplies the terminal record \(Q\) from the original branch.
Membership in \(\Owork\) is the conjunction of material custody, same-equation participation, and positive-scale field readout on that same record.
When those requirements survive at the finite continuation levels, the member-side readout chain feeds the continuation criterion selected for that branch.
For the classical Sobolev endpoint, the local \(H^s\) theorem restarts the same solution beyond \(T_*\).
When the record loses one requirement, the first lost requirement is a Pack, Part, or Field face failure and gives \(\Exit(Q;\Owork)\).
No additional same-solution terminal class exists between these two outcomes.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the class-consumption theorem.
It does not replace the surface payments that make a terminal record legitimate.
The paper must still say, for each surface, which object \(Q\) is selected, what
readout is available on the member side, and which first face is lost on the
exit side.
That is the proof obligation that prevents the CM grammar from becoming a
labeling device.

The periodic branch is the compact model of this argument.
The transported whole-torus atlas supplies the selected terminal object, and terminal non-continuation is read through the first failed Pack, Part, or Field face on that atlas.
The whole-space branch uses the same argument after the Duhamel split.
Rapid decay removes the heat tail, compact-core far-kernel terms do not create an exterior terminal obstruction, bounded exterior frequencies vanish under exterior \(L^2\) tightness, and any surviving exterior high-frequency source enters the same CM packet tree.
After Pack and Part are retained, dyadic scale escape is incompatible with any fixed positive Field scale, so the survivor lands in the same face disjunction:
\[
  \neg\Pack_Q\vee\neg\Part_{N,Q}\vee\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
This is why a direct positive exterior \(H^s\) estimate is stronger than the argument needs.
The annular endpoint witness remains the live boundary around this closeout:
the surrounding positive-radius fields must be controlled by the gold annular
supplier or followed by the silver branch to a selected same-solution
Pack/Part/Field face before the paper can claim CMI finality.

\section{How Auxiliary Estimates Enter}

An auxiliary estimate becomes part of the proof only after it names the selected terminal record and proves CM entry plus one first face landing:
\[
  \text{terminal record }Q
  \Longrightarrow
  \text{CM-test entry}
  \Longrightarrow
  \neg\Pack_Q
  \vee
  \neg\Part_{N,Q}
  \vee
  \forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q},
\]
or after it supplies the member-side readout chain up to the continuation consumer selected for that same record.
Support material that does not identify that record remains support material.
It can explain why a branch was considered, but it does not create another terminal alternative.

\section{Branch Derivation Patterns}

The main proof uses one derivation pattern repeatedly.
The positive attempt supplies a proposed continuation mechanism.
The failed attempt supplies a selected terminal branch.
The class proof asks which continuation requirement that selected branch loses first.

\begin{lemma}[Carrier pattern]
A terminal branch with zero terminal radius, detached ancestry, first-created unpaid reserve, or terminal carrier birth without a same-fluid charge path fails the carrier requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each listed mechanism removes the positive transported packet needed to carry \(Q\) as the same fluid branch.
The carrier requirement is exactly the existence of such a positive transported packet.
The first lost requirement is \(\neg\Pack_Q\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Participation pattern]
A terminal branch with a surviving carrier and broken pressure participation, broken viscosity participation, dwell failure, or incompatible local source participation fails the participation requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After a carrier survives, the continuation branch still has to solve the same differentiated Navier--Stokes equation on that carrier.
The listed mechanisms break that same-equation participation while leaving the carrier question already passed.
The first lost surviving requirement is \(\Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Field pattern]
A terminal branch with surviving carrier and participation but retained positive-window blowup, terminal pulse, averaged jump, good-scale failure, or incompatible surviving traces fails the field requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After carrier and participation survive, membership readout needs one positive scale where the coherent \(N\)-level field remains finite.
Each listed mechanism removes that positive-scale readout while preserving the earlier requirements for the purpose of the case split.
The first lost remaining requirement is \(\Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No hidden branch outside the patterns]
Every selected branch handled in the forward-family analysis is a carrier branch, a participation branch, a field branch, a finite disjunction of those three, or a support theorem awaiting a selected terminal packet.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A selected branch either lacks a positive transported packet, keeps such a packet and loses the same Navier--Stokes law through the velocity tower, keeps both and loses positive-scale coherent field readout, or has not yet supplied the selected packet needed for the class test.
The first three alternatives are Pack, Part, and Field.
The last alternative is support for a later selected packet.
There is no fourth continuation requirement in the working class object.
\end{proof}
