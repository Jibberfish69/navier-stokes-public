\begin{abstract}
This manuscript states the current Navier--Stokes proof surface exactly.  It
proves the same-solution terminal classification theorem after a terminal record
has been admitted to the class-membership test, and it keeps the remaining
direct annular source-pulse theorem visible as an open Gold-side burden.  The
classical problem asks whether smooth divergence-free initial data can lead to a
finite terminal time \(T_*\) at which the same Navier--Stokes solution loses the
continuation regularity required to restart the classical solution.

The proof program has two standards.  The Gold Standard is the forward-positive
proof: it tries to close the estimates directly, as in public mainstream
attempts and in the original four body method that tried to handle all
obstructions at once.  That route keeps bootstrapping estimates and narrowing
the possible terminal failure by example.  The repeated chase exposes the
central methodological problem: the forward route can begin to feel as if it is
inventing imaginary failures that may not even be possible and then trying to
account for them one by one.

The Silver Standard is the class membership and contrapositive proof.  It keeps
the same Navier--Stokes branch fixed and asks what an alleged finite-time
breakdown must be.  A finite-time breakdown is a same-solution terminal-object
claim at \(T_*\).  Once a terminal record \(Q\) is selected, the class
membership test asks whether \(Q\) still belongs to the one-fluid Navier--Stokes
object determined by the initial data, pressure, viscosity, incompressibility,
and continuation readout.  Failure of the first required witness face gives
\[
  \Exit(Q):=\neg\Member(Q).
\]

The witness faces are Pack, Part, and Field.  Pack is the smoothness
certification packet: the alleged terminal object must be packaged as something
the smoothness requirements can test.  Part is participation: the object must
still participate in the same pressure-viscosity-incompressibility law.  Field
is one-field coherence: the whole field must respond as one time-dependent
fluid field rather than as detached local fragments.  The proof program is
complete only when every nonsmooth terminal scenario is exhausted by these
faces, the necessary admission/readout hypotheses have been paid on the same
branch, and no hidden third terminal alternative remains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Original Problem Statement}

The Clay Navier--Stokes problem asks whether a smooth divergence-free velocity field can lose smoothness in finite time under the three-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes evolution.  The unknowns are the velocity \(u(x,t)\) and pressure \(p(x,t)\).  In the periodic case, and in the whole-space case with smooth rapidly decaying data, they solve
\[
  \partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot u=0,
  \qquad
  u(\cdot,0)=u_0,
\]
with fixed viscosity \(\nu>0\) and no forcing.  The datum \(u_0\) is smooth and divergence-free.  The pressure is tied to \(u\) by incompressibility.  Equivalently, it is recovered by the elliptic pressure law that removes the gradient part of the nonlinear term.

A proposed finite-time counterexample is a claim about one branch of this equation.  It starts from \(u_0\), follows the classical Navier--Stokes solution on a maximal interval \([0,T_*)\), and asserts that the \(H^s\), \(s>5/2\), continuation readout needed for local relaunch across \(T_*\) fails at \(T_*<\infty\).  This paper keeps that branch fixed.  The velocity, pressure, viscosity, pressure law, incompressibility constraint, and preterminal smooth solution all remain attached to the terminal object being tested.

The proof has two certification layers.  Gold means the direct route: prove a continuation criterion for the original solution.  A completed gold proof is the strongest certificate because the same solution never leaves the continuation argument.

Silver means the class-membership contrapositive route.  It begins after the forward work has left a precise terminal obstruction.  The question then changes from ``can the estimate be strengthened?'' to ``is the selected terminal record still a same-solution Navier--Stokes object?''  The class-membership test asks for three pieces of data.  Pack is the positive same-fluid carrier or packet.  Part is participation in the same pressure-viscosity-incompressibility law.  Field is one coherent positive-scale velocity-field readout.

A terminal singularity claim includes more than the point \((x_*,T_*)\).  The claim is carried by positive-radius fields that shrink toward that point.  Those surrounding fields form the annular witness.  The Gold obligation at that boundary is scale-uniform control of the remaining nonlinear source on every same-solution terminal annular schedule: equivalently, exclusion of a positive terminal heat-window source pulse, stated as \(\mathrm{TerminalTimeFaceAntiAtom.A}\) or the same-content theorem \(\mathrm{TerminalNewProductionTheorem}_{B_{\mathrm{ASAC}}}\mathrm{.A}\).  Silver starts at the surviving annular residue only after selection from the same branch, a same-solution positive-radius carrier, and the continuation-readout bridge have all been supplied.  After that readout-complete admission, Pack is already established for the annular test, so a finite nonsmooth readout-complete admitted residue must leave the class through Part or Field.

\section{Current Main Result and Proof Roadmap}

The result proved in this manuscript is a same-solution terminal classification theorem after admission.  It is the silver classification statement for an admitted terminal record.  The front annular pages keep the open gold endpoint burden visible.  Once a proposed finite Clay terminal claim has selected a terminal record \(Q\) for the original Navier--Stokes branch and that record has entered the CM terminal test, the selected record is on one of two sides:
\[
  \Member(Q;\Owork)
  \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]
The member side is the continuation side.  The exit side is witnessed by the first failed face in the Pack/Part/Field tree.

\begin{theorem}[Same-solution terminal classification with annular boundary]
Let \(u_0\) be smooth and divergence-free, and let \((u,p)\) be the maximal classical Navier--Stokes solution generated by \(u_0\).  Suppose a finite terminal claim selects a terminal record \(Q\) for that same branch, and suppose \(Q\) is admitted as a same-solution terminal witness.  Then the CM terminal test has the following alternatives.  Either \(Q\) retains the class-membership data needed for the continuation side, written
\[
  \Member(Q;\Owork),
\]
or the first failed witness face gives
\[
  \neg\Pack_Q,
  \qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q},
  \qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
In the second case the first failed face yields
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]
Hence one admitted same-solution terminal record cannot be both the member-continuation record and the same finite nonsmooth terminal witness.
\end{theorem}

The admission clause is essential.  The CM test classifies the terminal object of the original equation.  The selected record must carry the same datum, same velocity-pressure branch, same fixed viscosity, same pressure law, same incompressibility constraint, and same terminal role.  A comparison packet, branch diagram, auxiliary estimate, or limiting profile helps after it is tied back to that same branch.

The proof route has four tasks.  It fixes the exact Navier--Stokes object under test.  It derives what the direct estimates control and where the present forward accounting stops.  It checks whether a surviving obstruction is still a same-solution terminal record.  It identifies the first failed witness face when an admitted survivor cannot remain on the continuation side.

The annular endpoint witness is the first place where these tasks meet.  A radius-zero point is too small to carry the Clay breakdown claim by itself.  The claim is carried by surrounding positive-radius fields as their radii shrink toward \(0\).  The direct annular theorem still needed is scale-uniform control of the remaining source on every same-solution terminal annular schedule, and the exact open child is \(\mathrm{TerminalTimeFaceAntiAtom.A}\) or \(\mathrm{TerminalNewProductionTheorem}_{B_{\mathrm{ASAC}}}\mathrm{.A}\).  The residual annular case is sorted in proof-program order: a gold pass if that child is proved; the terminal heat-window pulse obstruction while it remains open; a no-admission or Pack boundary before the annular residue becomes the admitted record; a readout-complete admitted Member branch; or a readout-complete admitted finite nonsmooth branch exiting through Part or Field.

The proof state used in this opening is deliberately narrow.  The forward cone, funnel, derivative-tower, and active-square calculations reduce the live direct question to source-balanced annular control.  They do not prove a raw volume-drain, cone-surface, or energy-only closure.  The open direct-side theorem is the terminal time-face source obstruction: exclusion of a finite-mass heat-window source pulse, or an equivalent new-production theorem for the same terminal source atom.  The contrapositive counterpart begins only after a surviving positive-radius annular residue has been admitted as the same-solution terminal record with the continuation-readout bridge supplied.  At that point the Pack question has already been answered by selection and carrier admission.  A finite nonsmooth readout-complete admitted residue must leave through Part or Field, while a record retaining Part and continuation-readout Field is on the member-continuation side.  A weaker local annular reading remains support until it supplies the same admission and readout data.

The rest of the paper follows this order.  The opening sections derive the forward obstruction from the energy and enstrophy identities and then refine it into the annular residual refill problem.  The CM sections define membership, Pack, Part, and Field.  The branch sections explain which calculations enter the proof, which are obsolete, and which remain open.  The appendices are used where they help the reader see a proof contribution, obstruction, comparison role, or explicit continuation question.

\section{The Direct Estimate Route}

The gold route is the direct estimate route.  It tries to prove that the original solution satisfies a regularity criterion that continues it past any proposed terminal time.  The first estimate is the energy identity.  For a smooth divergence-free solution, multiply the equation by \(u\), integrate over the domain, and integrate by parts.  The transport term disappears because
\[
  \int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u\,dx
  =
  \frac12\int u\cdot\nabla |u|^2\,dx
  =
  0,
\]
and the pressure term disappears because
\[
  \int \nabla p\cdot u\,dx
  =
  -\int p\,\nabla\cdot u\,dx
  =
  0.
\]
The viscous term gives the dissipative identity
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  0.
\]
This is real control.  It controls kinetic energy and time-integrated enstrophy.  Smooth continuation in three dimensions also needs a pointwise high-regularity bound.

The next visible rung is the enstrophy calculation.  Set
\[
  Y(t)=\frac12\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
Pairing the differentiated equation with \(\nabla u\), or equivalently pairing the original equation with \(-\Delta u\), gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  \int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx.
\]
The left side contains the next viscous damping term.  The right side is the nonlinear source feeding the derivative level that viscosity is trying to damp.

A complete gold proof must make that source affordable.  The exact gold estimate is
\[
  \left|\int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx\right|
  \le
  \frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2+F(Y(t)),
\]
with a nonnegative nondecreasing continuous control function \(F\) satisfying the Osgood
condition
\[
  \int^{\infty}\frac{ds}{1+F(s)}=\infty .
\]
The \(\nu/2\) term is exactly the amount of viscous dissipation spent by the estimate, and the remaining term is the derivative-rung ordinary differential inequality.
More explicitly, that displayed inequality gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  \le F(Y(t)).
\]
This is the exact Young-margin estimate being requested; without the retained
coefficient \(\nu/2\) on the dissipation side, the sentence has no proof force.

The direct route is strongest because it keeps the original solution inside the continuation class.  It also gives the CM route its lawful input.  A failed forward estimate has proof value only when it leaves an exact obstruction: source growth, scale transfer, endpoint concentration, annular residue, or loss of a readout needed by the derivative tower.  The CM contrapositive begins with that selected obstruction, never with a vague claim that an estimate was difficult.
\section{Where the Direct Route Reaches an Obstruction}

The first obstruction can be seen without any special terminology.  Write
\[
  I(t)=\int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx.
\]
By H\"older's inequality,
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le
  \|u\|_{L^6}\|\nabla u\|_{L^3}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}.
\]
Let \(C_S\) be the Sobolev constant and \(C_I\) the interpolation constant on the working domain.  Sobolev gives
\[
  \|u\|_{L^6}\le C_S\|\nabla u\|_{L^2},
\]
and interpolation gives
\[
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^3}
  \le C_I
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}.
\]
Combining these two bounds gives
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le C_SC_I
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}.
\]
Young's inequality gives
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le
  \frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2
  +
  C_\nu\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^6.
\]
Substituting into the enstrophy identity yields
\[
  Y'(t)\le C_\nu Y(t)^3.
\]
This inequality gives local control.  It gives no global ceiling.  The comparison equation \(Z'=C_\nu Z^3\) has
\[
  Z(t)=\frac{Z(0)}{\sqrt{1-2C_\nu Z(0)^2t}},
\]
so the comparison ceiling can become infinite in finite time.  Restarting the estimate at a later time \(t_0<T_*\) gives a new interval whose length depends on \(Y(t_0)^{-2}\).  Crossing \(T_*\) requires pointwise control of \(Y(t_0)\) as \(t_0\uparrow T_*\).

The energy identity gives a different kind of information:
\[
  \sup_{0\le t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +
  2\nu\int_0^{T_*}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2\,dt
  \le
  \|u_0\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
This controls total kinetic energy and the time integral of enstrophy.  Higher derivatives can still concentrate near \(T_*\).  To see what the next rung asks for, fix a multi-index \(\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|=m\), apply \(D^\alpha\) to the equation, test against \(D^\alpha u\), and sum over all \(|\alpha|=m\).  The differentiated equation gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla^{m+1}u\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  -\sum_{|\alpha|=m}
  \int
  D^\alpha\bigl((u\cdot\nabla)u\bigr)
  \cdot D^\alpha u\,dx.
\]
The top-order transport part cancels because \(\nabla\cdot u=0\):
\[
  \int (u\cdot\nabla)D^\alpha u\cdot D^\alpha u\,dx
  =
  \frac12\int u\cdot\nabla |D^\alpha u|^2\,dx
  =
  0.
\]
The remaining nonlinear term is the commutator
\[
  -\sum_{|\alpha|=m}
  \int
  [D^\alpha,u\cdot\nabla]u
  \cdot D^\alpha u\,dx.
\]
For integer \(m\ge3\), the Moser/Kato--Ponce commutator estimate gives
\[
  \sum_{|\alpha|=m}
  \|[D^\alpha,u\cdot\nabla]u\|_{L^2}
  \le
  C_m\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}.
\]
Substituting this bound into the differentiated energy identity gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla^{m+1}u\|_{L^2}^2
  \le
  C_m\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\|\nabla^m u\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
A continuation argument requires an integrable bound on \(\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\), or a stronger Sobolev bound that implies it.

The annular and funnel analysis refines this missing item.  Near a proposed terminal point \((x_*,T_*)\), the singularity claim is carried by fields on shrinking positive-radius neighborhoods, not by the endpoint alone.  Set
\[
  \tau=T_*-t,\qquad
  y=\frac{x-x_*}{\sqrt{\tau}},
  \qquad
  s=-\log\tau,
\]
and
\[
  v(y,s)=\sqrt{\tau}\,u(x,t),
  \qquad
  q(y,s)=\tau p(x,t).
\]
The parabolic funnel
\[
  \mathcal F_\lambda
  =
  \{(x,t):0<T_*-t<\tau_0,\ |x-x_*|<\lambda\sqrt{T_*-t}\}
\]
becomes the fixed cylinder \(B_\lambda\times(s_0,\infty)\).  The scale-critical quantities in this cylinder are
\[
  A_\lambda(s)=\int_{B_\lambda}|v(y,s)|^2\,dy,
\]
\[
  D_\lambda(s)=\int_s^{s+1}\int_{B_\lambda}|\nabla_y v|^2\,dy\,d\sigma,
\]
and
\[
  C_\lambda(s)=\int_s^{s+1}\int_{B_\lambda}\bigl(|v|^3+|q|^{3/2}\bigr)\,dy\,d\sigma.
\]
Physical energy in the shrinking original ball sees a discounted version:
\[
  \int_{B_{\lambda\sqrt{\tau}}(x_*)}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx
  =
  e^{-s/2}A_\lambda(s).
\]
Finite physical energy controls the discounted quantity.  It leaves the unweighted rescaled energy \(A_\lambda(s)\) and the scale-critical packet \(C_\lambda(s)\) uncontrolled.

The cone discussion does not assume a linear velocity profile.  The exact slice quantity is the oscillation
\[
  \Delta_r u(t)
  :=
  \sup_{x,y\in B_r(x_*)}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|.
\]
For a differentiable preterminal slice,
\[
  \Delta_r u(t)
  \le
  2r\,\sup_{B_r(x_*)}|\nabla u(\cdot,t)|.
\]
Along the moving radius \(\rho(t)=\lambda\sqrt{T_*-t}\), the exact bridge is
\[
  \Delta_{\rho(t)}u(t)
  \le
  2\rho(t)\,\sup_{B_{\rho(t)}(x_*)}|\nabla u(\cdot,t)|.
\]
This is an oscillation inequality on the actual moving ball, not a claim that the field has slope \(U/r\) or any other profile.

The moving cone also gives two ordinary ledgers.  The wall ledger records what can cross
\[
  \Sigma_\lambda=\{(x,t): |x-x_*|=\lambda\sqrt{T_*-t}\},
\]
through transport, pressure work, viscous exchange, and the moving-wall term.  The volume ledger records the spacetime capacity of the shrinking interior.  These ledgers keep the endpoint honest: a terminal packet cannot be treated as a private point that receives unrecorded energy from the rest of the field.  They still live in physical units, so they control discounted contribution into the cone.  The unresolved forward step is stronger.  It must turn that same-fluid wall and volume accounting into control of the unweighted scale-critical quantities seen in \(s\)-time.

The forward-positive route must remove this discount.  At the annular scale, this means proving an undiscounted bound of the form
\[
  \sup_{s\ge s_0}A_\lambda(s)
  +
  \int_{s_0}^{\infty}D_\lambda(s)\,ds
  +
  \int_{s_0}^{\infty}C_\lambda(s)\,ds
  <\infty
\]
with late-slab smallness satisfying the selected local regularity threshold, for instance \(C(r)+D(r)<\epsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\) on retained subcylinders.  In shell language, the same question becomes control of the residual nonlinear source left after the heat descendants and every already-paid shell term of the form
\[
  \theta\,\nu 2^{2j}E_j(t)+R_j(t),
  \qquad 0<\theta<1,\qquad
  \sum_{j\ge J}\int R_j(t)\,dt<\infty,
\]
have been removed:
\[
  D_j^{\mathrm{res}}(t;t_0)
  :=
  2^{2j}\int_{t_0}^{t}
  e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j}(t-s)}\Phi_j^+(s)\,ds,
\]
read on the dyadic shells and time windows meeting a shrinking annulus.  The desired statement is scale-uniform smallness of this residual on every same-solution terminal annular schedule.

The obstruction is terminal concentration of source inside thinner and thinner heat-time windows.  Positive same-solution source mass may live on windows with \(r_m\downarrow0\) and \(\sum_m r_m^2<\infty\).  The model
\[
  g_m(s)=m\,1_{(-1/m,0]}(s)
\]
has bounded \(L^1_s\) mass and converges weakly to a terminal atom.  It has no fixed preterminal residence, yet it keeps terminal source mass.  A finite \(L^1_s\) source bound alone does not make the terminal layer spread out in time.

The same obstruction appears in the residual heat-scale quantity.  Let
\[
  \lambda_m=2^{j_m},
  \qquad
  \tau_m=\lambda_m^{-2},
  \qquad
  I_m=[t_m-\tau_m,t_m],
  \qquad
  t_m\uparrow T_*,
\]
with the windows disjoint and \(j_m\to\infty\).  On the selected shell set
\[
  \Phi_{j_m}^+(s)=a_m\,1_{I_m}(s),
  \qquad
  0<a_0\le a_m\le A.
\]
The physical source mass carried by these heat windows is summable:
\[
  \sum_m\int_{I_m}\Phi_{j_m}^+(s)\,ds
  \le
  A\sum_m2^{-2j_m}
  <\infty.
\]
At the end of each heat window, the scale-critical residual refill remains bounded below:
\[
  D_{j_m}^{\mathrm{res}}(t_m;t_m-\tau_m)
  =
  2^{2j_m}a_m
  \int_0^{2^{-2j_m}}
  e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j_m}\sigma}\,d\sigma
  =
  a_m\,\frac{1-e^{-c_0\nu}}{c_0\nu}
  \ge
  a_0\,\frac{1-e^{-c_0\nu}}{c_0\nu}.
\]
This computation is the obstruction.  A finite amount of physical source can be placed into thinner terminal windows while each selected heat-scale window still carries a fixed scale-critical residue.  The direct theorem still missing is \(\mathrm{TerminalTimeFaceAntiAtom.A}\), or the equivalent new-production theorem \(\mathrm{TerminalNewProductionTheorem}_{B_{\mathrm{ASAC}}}\mathrm{.A}\).  The presently known ways to prove that theorem all have the same mathematical job: prove temporal thickness or super-\(L^1_s\) integrability; dominate the selected source by a finite same-window reserve with a terminal strip modulus; prove transported no-incoming flux together with exclusion of terminal-face source production; show that first appearance contributes to a finite time-weighted source quantity, source integrability, or a rigid no-atom subclass; or produce \(B_{\mathrm{ASAC}}\) inside \(B_{\mathrm{ASAC}}^{TA}\).  Without one of those inputs, the terminal layer \(g_m(s)=(a/\tau_m)1_{(-\tau_m,0]}(s)\) remains compatible with the estimates on this page.

The nearby mechanisms do not remove this pulse by themselves.  A source-reserve argument helps after the first-created positive reserve is controlled by a finite same-window source quantity, or after the admitted reserve enters the CM test with a concrete first face failure.  The lower-carrier receiver package, the participation-debt mechanism, and the pressure-Hessian normal form answer a later question: how an admitted terminal record keeps or loses the same pressure law, viscosity, incompressibility, differentiated tower, and field readout.  They are not anti-atom estimates.  The \(B_{\mathrm{ASAC}}\) local exhaustion has the same shape.  It reduces the current local alternatives to a zero-thickness terminal source atom and gives evidence toward Pack failure before admission unless a separate positive-scale carrier is produced.

The direct side now leaves a precise open theorem, not a vague annular gap.  The CM side begins only after a surviving residual annular witness is admitted as the same-solution terminal object with the continuation-readout bridge supplied.  For that readout-complete admitted annular witness, Pack has already been answered by admission.  A smooth readout-complete branch is \(Member(Q)\); a finite nonsmooth readout-complete branch must leave the class through Part or Field.  A weaker local annular reading remains support until it supplies the same admission and continuation-readout bridge.
\section{The Same Terminal Record}

The direct route and the CM route ask different questions about the same branch.  The direct route asks whether the equation supplies estimates satisfying a continuation criterion for the original solution.  The CM route begins with the exact obstruction left after that accounting and asks whether the selected terminal record is still a same-solution Navier--Stokes object.

The reframing keeps the original datum, velocity-pressure branch, viscosity, pressure law, incompressibility constraint, and terminal role fixed.  A failed estimate becomes useful only after it produces a record \(Q\) that can be tested as the alleged terminal record of that same branch.  The CM test then asks three concrete questions.  Does \(Q\) still have a positive same-fluid carrier or packet?  That is Pack.  Does the same pressure-viscosity-incompressibility law still participate on the derivative tower being used?  That is Part.  Does the retained data still form a coherent positive-scale velocity field?  That is Field.

The pass side is conservative.  If Pack, Part, and Field survive on one selected record, that record remains on the member-continuation side and must be paired with the continuation readout named by the proof.  The fail side is equally concrete.  The argument marks the first failed face:
\[
  \neg\Pack_Q,
  \qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q},
  \qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
That first face failure yields
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).
\]

The annular endpoint is the example that keeps the distinction honest.  The singularity claim is carried by positive-radius annular fields shrinking toward the endpoint.  The proof follows those fields.  Gold tries to control the residual annular refill directly.  Silver receives a surviving annular residue only after it has been selected as the same-solution terminal record with the continuation-readout bridge supplied.  At that moment carrier is already part of admission.  The readout-complete admitted nonsmooth annular branch must fail through Part or Field, while a branch retaining Part and continuation-readout Field belongs to the member-continuation side.
\section{What a Terminal Failure Can Be}

The forward work produces many named surfaces.  The terminal proof uses them only after they have been reduced to one object: a same-solution record offered as the terminal record of the original Navier--Stokes branch.  That record is the object under examination.  It carries the initial datum, viscosity, velocity-pressure law, pressure response, incompressibility condition, material carrier, and terminal role inherited from the branch whose breakdown is being alleged.

The reduction has a fixed order.  A forward branch either supplies a continuation readout, leaves a selected terminal record for the CM test, or remains explanatory context.  Once a record \(Q\) has been selected, the proof asks three questions in the order in which a Navier--Stokes record needs them.  Does \(Q\) have a positive same-fluid carrier?  Does the same pressure-viscosity-incompressibility law still participate on that carrier?  Do the retained readings form one positive-scale velocity field?  The answers are read as Pack, Part, and Field.

The proof test is the ordered Pack/Part/Field mechanism.  The older endpoint words are secondary glosses.  A Pack failure means no positive same-fluid body survives for the selected record.  A Part failure means a body remains, but it no longer carries the same Navier--Stokes pressure, viscosity, incompressibility, and differentiated tower. If Pack and Part both survive, the remaining retained amplitude is read as a Field readout question.  If the retained object has no positive terminal scale, it is the Zeno/Pack subcase.
\end{proof}

Height-flux and frame-variation arguments keep a retained-amplitude branch from floating between faces.  The branch must create carrier gain, pay its dwell, enter a licensed Field readout, or collapse to a terminal atom before it can affect the terminal proof.

\subsection{Signed Current And No-Free-Sink}

Signed-current arguments try to turn source-current motion into a no-free-sink law.  The obstruction is that strain production has variable sign, while terminal source charge can move through scale-critical channels.  The class proof uses signed current as a face-routing tool.

\begin{lemma}[Signed current has no independent face]
A signed-current branch returns to Pack, Part, retained Field, Zeno, or context awaiting a selected record.  It returns to the three witness requirements.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Signed current measures how the source economy moves.  The class object asks whether the selected terminal packet retains the carrier, the participation tower, and coherent field data.  Current information can prove or strengthen one of those requirement statements.  It supplies information for one of the requirements in \(\CM_{N,r,Q}\).
\end{proof}

The terminal signed-saturation reduction is valuable because it makes this routing explicit.  Failed saturation is resolved by retained partners, legal exits, pair-weight charge, finite donor balance, or else becomes a terminal zero-radius source residue.  Those alternatives are Pack, Part, Field, or context awaiting one of those terminal outcomes.

\subsection{Endpoint, No-Pulse, And Good-Scale Readout}

Endpoint arguments are closest to classical continuation.  They ask for a good scale, a read cover, pulse exclusion, terminal-tail control, and coherent field output.  They are essential on the pass side.  They become misleading when they are treated as the primitive class test.

\begin{proposition}[Readout role]
Endpoint readout proves smooth continuation from a member packet.  It certifies membership only after the Pack, Part, and Field requirements hold on one witness record.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The smooth continuation readout reads a packet that already satisfies the coherent-field requirement. Readout is the consequence of the requirements, and the requirements remain the class test.
\end{proof}

A failed branch gives class exit after the selected terminal record has lost Pack, Part, or Field.  The selected record supplies the packet; endpoint estimates then read that packet on the pass side and help locate the first failed face on the fail side.

\section{The Whole-Space \texorpdfstring{\(\mathbb R^3\)}{R3} Exterior-Source Branch}

The periodic branch and the whole-space branch use the same class test.  Their entry costs differ.  On \(\mathbb T^3\), the transported atlas lives on a compact spatial domain.  On \(\mathbb R^3\), a terminal claim can try to hide high derivatives in the far field.  The paper handles that risk before it imports the Pack, Part, and Field decision.

Fix \(s>5/2\).  Let \(u\) be the smooth Navier--Stokes branch on \([0,T_*)\times\mathbb R^3\) from smooth rapidly decaying divergence-free data, and let \(\eta_R\) be an exterior cutoff.  The strong positive export theorem is the exterior \(H^s\)-tail estimate
\[
  \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}\|\eta_R u(t)\|_{H^s(\mathbb R^3)}=0.
\]
That theorem is useful, and it is stronger than the CM branch needs.  The CM branch starts from the Duhamel split and asks what remains after the two exterior terms below have been paid by exact tail bounds:
\[
  u(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0
  -\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]

The initial heat tail vanishes at spatial infinity because the datum is rapidly decaying:
\[
  \forall L>0,\qquad
  \sup_{0\le t\le T<T_*}
  \|\eta_R e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\|_{H^s}
  \le C_{s,L,T,u_0}R^{-L}.
\]
For the compact-core nonlinear source, let \(K_{s,\nu}(\theta,z)\) be the
kernel of \(\Lambda^s e^{\nu\theta\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot\).  The support
of \(\eta_R(x)\chi_{<R/4}(y)\) has \(|x-y|\ge R/2\), so the exact off-diagonal
tail estimate is
\[
  \int_0^T
  \sup_{|y|\le R/4}
  \|\eta_R(\cdot)K_{s,\nu}(\theta,\cdot-y)\|_{L^2_x}\,d\theta
  \le C_{s,\nu,T,L}R^{-L}.
\]
Together with \(\|u(\tau)\otimes u(\tau)\|_{L^1}\le \|u(\tau)\|_{L^2}^2\), this
gives
\[
  \sup_{t<T}
  \left\|
  \eta_R\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot
  \bigl(\chi_{<R/4}u\otimes u\bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau
  \right\|_{H^s}
  \le C_{s,\nu,T,L,E_0}R^{-L}.
\]
The remaining source is genuinely exterior:
\[
  W_R(t):=
  \eta_R\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-\tau)\Delta}
  \mathbb P\nabla\cdot\bigl(\chi_{\ge R/4}u\otimes u\bigr)(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]
If \(W_R\) vanishes in exterior \(H^s\), the whole-space branch has the ordinary continuation tail.  If \(W_R\) survives, the survivor is the terminal object that must enter the CM matrix.

\begin{proposition}[Whole-space exterior source split]
The whole-space exterior \(H^s\) burden reduces to the genuinely exterior nonlinear source \(W_R\).  The initial heat tail and the interior-source far-field term \(\chi_{<R/4}(u\otimes u)\) vanish at spatial infinity by the displayed bounds.  Hence the whole-space branch has the following two outcomes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\displaystyle \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}\|W_R(t)\|_{H^s}=0,
  & \text{member-side exterior readout},\\[0.8em]
\displaystyle \limsup_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}\|W_R(t)\|_{H^s}>0,
  & \text{terminal exterior survivor to classify.}
\end{array}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed Schwartz-tail estimate controls the initial heat term.  The
displayed kernel-tail estimate controls the compact-core nonlinear term by
Minkowski and the energy bound.  After those two terms are removed, the
displayed exterior nonlinear source is the only term that can carry a terminal
exterior \(H^s\) tail.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded exterior frequencies vanish]
For every fixed dyadic cutoff \(N_0\),
\[
  \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t<T_*}
  \|P_{\le N_0}W_R(t)\|_{H^s}=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On bounded frequency,
\[
  \|P_{\le N_0}f\|_{H^s}\le C_sN_0^s\|f\|_{L^2}.
\]
The heat/Leray operator maps the exterior source from \(L^1_tL^{3/2}_x\) into the fixed-band \(L^\infty_tL^2_x\) response.  With \(F_R=\chi_{\ge R/4}(u\otimes u)\),
\[
  \|F_R(t)\|_{L^{3/2}}
  \le
  \|\chi_{\ge R/4}u(t)\|_{L^3}\|u(t)\|_{L^3}.
\]
The energy class gives \(u\in L^4_tL^3_x\) on \([0,T_*)\).  The exterior factor tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\), and the second factor is square integrable in time on the same finite interval.  Holder's inequality in time gives \(F_R\to0\) in \(L^1_tL^{3/2}_x\), and the fixed-band \(H^s\) response vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exterior survivor lands in the CM faces]
Assume the exterior nonlinear source survives in \(H^s\).  Then the selected terminal exterior survivor lands in the CM matrix:
\[
  \neg\Pack_Q\;\vee\;\neg\Part_{M,Q}\;\vee\;
  \forall \rho>0\,\neg\Field_{M,\rho,Q}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bounded-frequency lemma removes every fixed dyadic window.  A surviving exterior source then selects radii \(R_j\to\infty\), times \(t_j<T_*\), and dyadic frequencies \(N_j\to\infty\) such that
\[
  N_j^s\|P_{N_j}W_{R_j}(t_j)\|_{L^2}\ge c>0.
\]
Let \(Q\) be the same-solution terminal record selected by that survivor.  A survivor lacking a retained material address in the original branch fails Pack.  A survivor detached from the same Navier--Stokes equation, pressure law, viscosity, or source ancestry fails Part.

It remains to read the retained Pack+Part branch.  A fixed positive Field scale \(\rho>0\) through depth \(M>s\) gives a finite derivative tower on the selected record.  Littlewood--Paley decay on that fixed record gives
\[
  N^s\|P_N f\|_{L^2}\le C_{\rho,M,Q}N^{s-M}\to0.
\]
That decay contradicts the surviving sequence above.  Every fixed positive Field scale fails on the retained Pack+Part branch.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole-space CM classification state]
For smooth rapidly decaying divergence-free data on \(\mathbb R^3\), the whole-space exterior branch has the same pass-or-exit form as the periodic branch, with its own noncompact branch result.  The exterior Duhamel split selects the relevant terminal object.  A vanishing exterior nonlinear source gives the member-side exterior \(H^s\) readout.  A surviving exterior nonlinear source lands in Pack, Part, or Field failure and yields \(\Exit(Q;\Owork)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Duhamel split removes the initial heat tail by the Schwartz-tail bound and removes the interior-source far-field term \(\chi_{<R/4}(u\otimes u)\) by the off-diagonal kernel-tail bound.  The remaining exterior nonlinear source either vanishes in \(H^s\) or survives.  In the vanishing case, the whole-space branch has the continuation tail needed by the member-side readout.  In the surviving case, bounded exterior frequencies vanish, so the survivor is a dyadic high-frequency escape.  The preceding proposition reads that escape as Pack failure, Part failure, or Field failure on the same terminal record.  The CM embedding reads the first failed face as \(\Exit(Q;\Owork)\).  The direct positive exterior \(H^s\) theorem remains a stronger gold/export theorem.
\end{proof}

This is the whole-space branch result used later in the paper.  It keeps the periodic atlas and the noncompact exterior-source branch separate.  It also keeps the direct \(H^s\) tail theorem in its proper role: a stronger direct route, valuable when proved, while the silver whole-space branch uses the CM face landing written above.

\section{Closing Hinges}

The close of the CM argument has four hinges.

The first hinge is terminal record entry.
A finite Clay negation concerns one maximal classical branch from one datum.
At the alleged terminal time, the branch either has the \(H^s\), \(s>5/2\), endpoint readout that restarts it, or it must name same-branch terminal data that prevents the restart.

An auxiliary estimate matters only by strengthening one of these hinges while preserving the same terminal record.
The remaining Clay-facing bridge keeps original smooth data inside the same pass-or-exit CM reading.
\section{The Same-Solution CM Proof}

The closing proof has one same-object rule.
Every terminal alternative must remain an alternative for the original Navier--Stokes branch.
A large norm, weak limit, annular residue, exterior survivor, or transported atlas is only candidate evidence until it is tied back to the same datum, the same equation, the same pressure law, the same viscosity, the same preterminal ancestry, and the same terminal role.
The CM test is applied to that tied-back record.

\begin{proposition}[Terminal record entry from the finite Clay negation]
Fix \(s>5/2\), a smooth divergence-free datum \(u_0\), viscosity \(\nu>0\), and the maximal classical branch
\[
  u:[0,T_*)\to H^s
\]
generated by that datum.
A finite terminal claim for this branch has only two same-branch readings.
Either the \(H^s\) endpoint readout satisfies a continuation criterion that restarts \(u\) past \(T_*\), or the claim must select a terminal record \(Q\) extracted from the same branch and carrying the same datum, equation, pressure law, viscosity, preterminal ancestry, and terminal role.
An obstruction outside those two readings belongs outside the finite-time breakdown claim for the original Clay solution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Clay claim concerns the solution generated by \(u_0\).
For each \(T<T_*\), this solution is smooth and solves the original Navier--Stokes system with the same pressure and viscosity.

\[
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^s}<\infty ,
\]
the local \(H^s\) theory gives a lifespan depending only on that bound.
Restarting from times \(t_n\uparrow T_*\) then produces a classical continuation past \(T_*\), and uniqueness glues it to the original branch.
So a genuine finite terminal claim must locate the failed endpoint readout on the same branch.
The terminal record \(Q\) is the record of that failure: the selected material packet or its loss, the same-equation participation information or its loss, and the field readout information or its loss, all inherited from \(u\).
\end{proof}

After terminal entry, the proof performs one ordered test on the selected record:
\[
  \text{finite terminal claim}
  \Longrightarrow
  \begin{cases}
  \text{\(H^s\) readout and same-solution restart,}\\
  \text{same-solution terminal record \(Q\) admitted to the CM test.}
  \end{cases}
\]

\[
  \Pack_Q,\qquad \Part_{N,Q},\qquad \Field_{N,r,Q}.

The first failed requirement yields class exit:
\[
  \Exit(Q;\Owork):=\neg\Member(Q;\Owork).

\begin{theorem}[Same-solution CM dichotomy]
Let \(Q\) be the canonical terminal record selected by a finite terminal claim for the original Navier--Stokes solution, and suppose \(Q\) has been admitted as the same-solution record under test.
Inside the CM test, \(Q\) has exactly two readings:
\[
  \Member(Q;\Owork)
  \quad\hbox{or}\quad
  \Exit(Q;\Owork).
\]

\[
  \neg\Pack_Q,\qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q},\qquad
  \Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The terminal record entry proposition gives the same-branch reduction.
The restart-readout case continues the same solution.
At the Sobolev endpoint this means: if
\(\sup_{t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^s}=M_s<\infty\) for some fixed \(s>5/2\), the local
theory gives a lifespan \(\tau(\nu,s,M_s)>0\); relaunching from
\(t_j\uparrow T_*\) with \(T_*-t_j<\tau/2\) and using uniqueness on the overlap
continues the original branch beyond \(T_*\).
In the remaining case, the finite terminal claim supplies the terminal record \(Q\) from the original branch.
Membership in \(\Owork\) requires a positive same-fluid carrier, same-equation pressure-viscosity participation, and positive-scale field readout on that same record.
When those requirements survive at the continuation depth, the member-side readout chain feeds the continuation criterion selected for that branch.

Loss of the carrier gives \(\neg\Pack_Q\).
Carrier without same-equation participation gives \(\Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q}\).

  \Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
Each first face failure gives \(\Exit(Q;\Owork)\).
The same admitted terminal record has no third CM reading.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No same-record third branch]

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The member branch retains the three witness requirements and supplies the selected continuation readout for the same solution.
A first face failure is exactly the failure of one of those witness requirements on the same record.
The same record cannot simultaneously retain a requirement and lose it as its first failed face.
\end{proof}

This theorem is a classification theorem after terminal-record admission.
It says what happens once the alleged terminal object has been tied to the original solution and placed inside the CM test.

\section{Branch-to-Face Derivation Patterns}

The main proof uses one derivation pattern repeatedly.
A direct estimate proposes a continuation mechanism.
When that estimate stops, it leaves behind a terminal branch to be tested.
After that branch is selected as the same-solution record, the class proof asks which continuation requirement it loses first.

\begin{lemma}[Carrier pattern]
A terminal branch with zero terminal radius, detached ancestry, first-created unaccounted reserve, or terminal carrier birth with a missing same-fluid charge path fails the carrier requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each listed mechanism removes the positive transported packet needed to carry \(Q\) as the same fluid branch.
The carrier requirement is exactly the existence of such a positive transported packet.
The first lost requirement is \(\neg\Pack_Q\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Participation pattern]
A terminal branch with a surviving carrier and broken pressure participation, broken viscosity participation, dwell failure, or incompatible local source participation fails the participation requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After a carrier survives, the continuation branch must solve the same differentiated Navier--Stokes equation on that carrier.
The listed mechanisms break that same-equation participation while leaving the carrier question already passed.
The first lost surviving requirement is \(\Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Field pattern]
A terminal branch with surviving carrier and participation plus retained positive-window blowup, terminal pulse, averaged jump, good-scale failure, or incompatible surviving traces fails the field requirement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After carrier and participation survive, membership readout needs one positive scale where the coherent \(N\)-level field remains finite.
Each listed mechanism removes that positive-scale readout while preserving the earlier requirements for the purpose of the case split.
The first lost remaining requirement is \(\Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The branch patterns exhaust selected cases]
Every selected branch handled in the forward-family analysis is a carrier branch, a participation branch, a field branch, a finite disjunction of those three, or an auxiliary theorem awaiting a selected terminal packet.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A selected branch either lacks a positive transported packet, keeps such a packet and loses the same Navier--Stokes law through the velocity tower, keeps both and loses positive-scale coherent field readout, or still awaits the selected packet needed for the class test.
The first three alternatives are Pack, Part, and Field.
The last alternative is context for a later selected packet.
The working class object has exactly these three continuation requirements.
\end{proof}
