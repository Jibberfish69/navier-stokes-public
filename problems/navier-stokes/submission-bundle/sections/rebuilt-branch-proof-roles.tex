\section{How Branch Families Pay the CM Proof}
\label{sec:branch-proof-roles}

The forward branches are useful because they tell the paper what the terminal object might be.  They are not independent endings of the proof.  After a branch has located a possible terminal object, the manuscript asks one common question: can that object still be read as the same Navier--Stokes solution record?

The answer is recorded through the same five outcomes used in the main proof:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{member readout} &
  \Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\Field_{N,r,Q}
  \text{ plus the continuation norm,}\\[1mm]
\text{Pack failure} & \neg\Pack_Q,\\[1mm]
\text{Part failure} & \Pack_Q\wedge\neg\Part_{N,Q},\\[1mm]
\text{Field failure} &
  \Pack_Q\wedge\Part_{N,Q}\wedge\forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q},\\[1mm]
\text{context} &
  \text{a real estimate whose selected record or face payment is still unwritten.}
\end{array}
\]
This is the proof map the reader needs.  A forward argument may pay a source term, rule out a donor refill, locate a terminal atom, or sharpen an endpoint readout.  It becomes proof text only at the line where it names \(Q\), names the class data carried by \(Q\), and pays one of the outcomes above.

The annular-limit row is the live boundary for the current forward branch.  The radius-zero point by itself is a point-object reading.  The singularity claim is carried by the surrounding positive-radius fields.  Gold closes that boundary by proving scale-uniform annular control.  Silver closes it only after the annular residue has been admitted as the same-solution terminal witness and the page shows the resulting Part or Field failure.  Until that line is written, the annular material remains an open forward obstruction with a precise CM counterpart.
