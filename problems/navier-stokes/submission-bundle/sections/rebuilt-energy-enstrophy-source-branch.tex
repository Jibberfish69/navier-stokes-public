\section{Energy, Enstrophy, and Source Reserves}
\label{sec:energy-enstrophy-source-branch}

\subsection{Endpoint Reserve Currency on the Selected Carrier}
\label{subsec:endpoint-reserve-selected-carrier}

The active forward-gold reserve question can be stated in the same shell language as the residual refill load.  The selected endpoint activity is denoted by \(a_j(s)\).  The proof needs an action currency \(B_j(s)\) carried by the same selected shell family and the same solution record.  The desired comparison is
\[
  a_j(s)\le \lambda B_j(s)+\ell_j(s),
  \qquad
  \int_{-1}^{0}\ell_j(s)\,ds=o_j(1),
\]
with a terminal-family reserve
\[
  \lim_{N\to\infty}
  \sum_{j:\,r_j\le2^{-N}}
  \int_{-1}^{0}B_j(s)\,ds=0.
\]
This is the endpoint Littlewood--Paley reserve form of the same-carrier bridge.

The factorial tower and shifted-gamma calculations identify candidate currencies for \(B_j\).  The moving-radius identity supplies the nonnegative spend measure \(D_S^{\rm rad}\).  The shifted saddle supplies a balanced high-rung price.  The residual refill object \(D_j^{res}\) supplies the shell-level endpoint load.  The active gold bridge asks for these currencies to live on one selected carrier rather than on parallel bookkeeping surfaces.

In this formulation, a successful forward proof gives the member-side continuation readout by exhausting the endpoint reserve.  A surviving same-solution endpoint object is passed to the class-membership test, where the first failed Part or Field face records the exit.  Thus the reserve calculation and the CM witness calculation are two readings of the same selected carrier, with the forward route paying domination and the CM route classifying any admitted survivor.

The energy branch is the first place where the equation pays for itself.  Take
a smooth divergence-free solution of
\[
  \partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,
\]
on the periodic surface.  Pair the equation with \(u\) and integrate in
space.  The transport term vanishes because
\[
  \int_{\mathbb T^3}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u\,dx
  =
  \frac12\int_{\mathbb T^3}u\cdot\nabla |u|^2\,dx
  =0,
\]
and the pressure term vanishes because \(u\) is divergence-free:
\[
  \int_{\mathbb T^3}\nabla p\cdot u\,dx
  =
  -\int_{\mathbb T^3}p\,\nabla\cdot u\,dx
  =0.
\]
The viscous term gives the exact energy identity
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2=0.
\]
After integration in time,
\[
  \sup_{0\le t<T}\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +2\nu\int_0^T\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2\,dt
  \le \|u_0\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
This is the baseline control.  It is global, exact, and same-solution.  It
controls total kinetic energy and the time-integrated first derivative.  A
continuation proof needs more: it has to stop the solution from feeding that
controlled energy into smaller and smaller scales.

The next native quantity is enstrophy.  Put
\[
  Y(t)=\frac12\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
Pair the equation with \(-\Delta u\).  The time derivative gives
\[
  \int_{\mathbb T^3}\partial_tu\cdot(-\Delta u)\,dx
  =
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2,
\]
the pressure drops out after integration by parts, and the viscous term gives
\(\nu\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2\).  The enstrophy balance is
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  \int_{\mathbb T^3}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\,dx.
\]
The right side is the source term for derivative growth.  Denote it by
\(I(t)\).  Let \(C_S\) be the Sobolev constant and \(C_I\) the interpolation
constant on the periodic surface.  By Holder, Sobolev, and interpolation,
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  |I(t)|
  &\le
  \|u\|_{L^6}\|\nabla u\|_{L^3}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}  \\
  &\le C_SC_I
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}
  \|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}
  \|\Delta u\|_{L^2}.
  \end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le C_SC_I
  \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^{3/2}.
\]
Young's inequality gives
\[
  |I(t)|
  \le
  \frac{\nu}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}^2
  +C_\nu\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^6,
\]
so
\[
  Y'(t)\le C_\nu Y(t)^3.
\]

This inequality explains both the strength and the weakness of the branch.
It is a local continuation estimate: a bounded \(Y(t)\) controls the next
derivative payment.  It is also too weak to prove global smoothness by
itself, since the comparison equation \(Z'=C_\nu Z^3\) has finite-time
growth.  The branch has reached the precise source channel that the rest of
the proof must pay.

To see the remaining channel at the scale where the singularity question
lives, decompose the solution into dyadic shells.  Let
\[
  u_j=\Delta_j u,\qquad
  E_j(t)=2^{2j}\|u_j(t)\|_{L^2}^2,\qquad
  D_j(t)=2^{4j}\|u_j(t)\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
The projected shell equation is
\[
  \partial_tu_j-\nu\Delta u_j
  =
  -\Delta_j\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u).
\]
Taking the \(L^2\) pairing with \(2^{2j}u_j\) gives the scalar shell
inequality
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}E_j(t)+c_0\nu D_j(t)
  \le \Phi_j^+(t),
\]
where
\[
  \Phi_j(t)
  =
  -2^{2j}
  \left\langle
  \Delta_j\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u),u_j
  \right\rangle,
  \qquad
  \Phi_j^+(t)=\max\{\Phi_j(t),0\}.
\]
The negative part of \(\Phi_j\) helps drain the shell.  The positive part is
the same-fluid nonlinear refill that can keep a high shell alive after heat
decay has been charged.

Solving the scalar inequality from an entrance time \(t_0\) gives
\[
  E_j(t)
  \le
  e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j}(t-t_0)}E_j(t_0)
  +C\int_{t_0}^t
    e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j}(t-s)}\Phi_j^+(s)\,ds.
\]
Write
\[
  H_j(t;t_0)
  =
  e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j}(t-t_0)}E_j(t_0),
  \qquad
  R_j^{src,+}(t;t_0)
  =
  \int_{t_0}^t
    e^{-c_0\nu2^{2j}(t-s)}\Phi_j^+(s)\,ds.
\]
Then
\[
  E_j(t)\le H_j(t;t_0)+C R_j^{src,+}(t;t_0).
\]
In \(D_j\)-currency this reads
\[
  D_j(t)
  \le
  2^{2j}H_j(t;t_0)+C D_j^{res}(t;t_0),
  \qquad
  D_j^{res}(t;t_0):=2^{2j}R_j^{src,+}(t;t_0).
\]
The object \(D_j^{res}\) is the residual refill load.  It is the source
reserve after exact heat decay, legal through-flow, inherited parent mass,
and already-paid dissipation have been separated away.

The residual definition matters because raw high-frequency size can be
harmless.  The exact shear solution
\[
  u(x,t)
  =
  \left[
    a e^{-\nu2^{2k}t}\sin(2^kx_2)
    +
    b e^{-\nu2^{2j}t}\sin(2^jx_2)
  \right]e_1
\]
is a smooth Navier--Stokes solution with
\[
  (u\cdot\nabla)u=0,\qquad
  \Phi_j(t)=0,\qquad
  D_j^{res}(t;t_0)=0.
\]
A proof that tries to control raw \(D_j^2\) would spend effort on pure heat
decay.  The proof-bearing target is narrower: positive nonlinear refill by
the same fluid.

The current gold-side target is the residual lower-prefix active-square
estimate.  With
\[
  \mathcal P_j^\downarrow(t)=\sum_{k\le j-C_1}E_k(t),
\]
the desired residual estimate has the form
\[
  \int_0^T
  \sum_{j\ge N}
  \mathcal P_j^\downarrow(t)\,
  2^{-j}
  \bigl(D_j^{res}(t;t_0(j,t))\bigr)^2\,dt
  \le
  \varepsilon\nu\int_0^TD_N(t)\,dt
  +C_*2^{-2\delta N}.
\]
This is a forward theorem, and the repo does not record it as proved from
the present finite-energy, local-energy, pressure, cutoff, transport, and
first-moment inputs.  The paper keeps it as the honest gold obligation: prove
residual source depletion, residual CTS/FCTS, or a stronger source-residue
rigidity theorem.

The source-reserve obstruction also has a known false shortcut.  A
fixed positive terminal reserve with zero first moment does not force visible
active-height flux.  Let \(W_m=(-\tau_m,0]\) with \(\tau_m\downarrow0\), and
define a terminal-layer positive source measure by
\[
  d\nu_m^+(y,s)
  =
  \rho(y)\tau_m^{-1}\chi(s/\tau_m)\,dy\,ds,
  \qquad
  \int_{-1}^0\chi=1.
\]
Then
\[
  \nu_m^+(W_m)=1,
  \qquad
  \int_{W_m}|s|\,d\nu_m^+
  \le
  \tau_m\int_{-1}^0|\sigma|\chi(\sigma)\,d\sigma
  \to0.
\]
The reserve can retain fixed positive mass while the terminal first moment
vanishes.  A signed partner can cancel the signed moment while the one-sided
positive branch remains.  This blocks the standalone forward claim from
reserve mass and zero first moment to active-height visibility.

The CM reading starts exactly there.  The terminal layer is not treated as a
hidden continuation branch.  It is tested as a same-solution witness.  The
source-reserve root now uses three theorem surfaces.  First,
\(\mathrm{ActiveHeightFluxTrichotomy.A}\) separates positive relative time
thickness from terminal collapse.  Then
\(\mathrm{SignedPairReserveVisibility.A}\) decides whether the signed
partner is present on the same ledger.  Finally,
\(\mathrm{TerminalSignedSaturationCMFaceReduction.A}\) routes the remaining
terminal signed-saturation failure to a Part or Field witness.
A positive relative time thickness gives the active-height branch.
A missing, incompatible, or off-ledger signed partner gives a Part/Field failure.
A same-ledger retained partner is paid by the installed pair-weight or
finite-donor ledgers.

The face depends on how much of the same-solution record survives.  A source
reserve with no positive carrier fails \(\Pack_Q\).  A reserve with a carrier
and broken equation participation fails \(\Part_{N,Q}\).  A reserve with
carrier and participation has reached the one-field question; the remaining
finite-terminal failure is
\[
  \Part plus Field-window evidence_{N,Q}
  \wedge
  \forall r>0\,\neg\Field_{N,r,Q}.
\]
At the annular supplier boundary the carrier and selection have already
been granted for the admitted positive-radius witness, so Pack no longer
supplies the argument there.  The admitted nonsmooth branch must exit through
Part or Field.

This branch now has a precise endpoint role.  Gold remains the direct
residual-refill theorem: prove that the same-fluid positive source reserve
cannot accumulate enough scale-critical load.  Silver uses the same terminal
object after it has survived into the CM test: the pass branch gives the lawful
member readout, and the fail branch is paid by the first Part/Field face
failure.  The derivation above reaches the residual source object, identifies
the false forward shortcut, and gives the CM counterpart as a class-exit
statement.  A stalled positive estimate is left in its honest role: it records
the obstruction that the CM test then classifies.
