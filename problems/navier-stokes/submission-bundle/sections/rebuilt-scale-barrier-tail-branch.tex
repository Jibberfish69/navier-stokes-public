\section{Scale Barrier And High-Frequency Tail Branch}
\label{sec:scale-barrier-tail-branch}

The scale-barrier branch asks for a direct way to stop a finite-time breakdown
from being carried by smaller and smaller spatial scales. Its positive-forward
aim is simple: find a scale beyond which viscosity dominates nonlinear transfer,
then sum the derivative tail. This branch is attractive because the equation
already contains the competing terms. The nonlinearity transfers activity across
scales, while viscosity damps high frequencies at rate comparable to
\(\nu |k|^2\).

A dyadic version of the branch begins by decomposing
\[
  u=\sum_{j\geq -1} u_j,
  \qquad u_j=P_j u,
\]
where \(P_j\) denotes a Littlewood--Paley projection to frequencies
\(|\xi|\simeq 2^j\). The ideal estimate would show that for large active scales
\(j\), the nonlinear transfer into the \(j\)-th band remains below the viscous
payment:
\[
  \left|\int_{\mathbb T^3} P_j((u\cdot\nabla)u)\cdot u_j\,dx\right|
  \leq c\nu 2^{2j}\|u_j\|_{L^2}^2 + \mathrm{summable\ lower\ terms},
\]
with a constant \(c<1\). Such an estimate would turn the high-frequency tail into
a paid bill. Once the tail is paid, high derivatives can be recovered by summing
weighted dyadic energies.

The branch becomes difficult because the nonlinear term is triangular in scale.
High-high, high-low, low-high, and lower-prefix interactions each carry a
different payment demand. A schematic decomposition gives
\[
  P_j((u\cdot\nabla)u)
  =
  P_j((u_{\sim j}\cdot\nabla)u_{\sim j})
  +P_j((u_{<j-C}\cdot\nabla)u_{\sim j})
  +P_j((u_{\sim j}\cdot\nabla)u_{<j-C})
  +\mathrm{tail}.
\]
The viscosity term pays the diagonal high-frequency bill. The lower-prefix terms
ask for active-window control of low-frequency strain. The tail terms ask for
summability across separated scales.

The positive-forward program tries to prove a scale barrier of the
form
\[
  \mathcal T_j(t)
  \leq \frac{\nu}{2}2^{2j}E_j(t)+R_j(t),
  \qquad
  \sum_j w_j R_j(t)<\infty,
\]
where \(E_j(t)=\|u_j(t)\|_{L^2}^2\), \(w_j\) is the derivative weight, and
\(\mathcal T_j\) is the nonlinear transfer into the active band. The whole branch
turns on the role of \(R_j\). If the remainder is summable with the needed
weight, the scale barrier pays the tail. If the remainder carries an active
lower-shell or source-reserve factor, the terminal obstruction survives.

This is the first high-frequency version of the survivor pattern. Energy gives
an integrated gradient budget. Enstrophy exposes a pointwise derivative source.
The scale branch asks for bandwise transfer domination. The unpaid object now
appears as lower-prefix memory, active-window strain, lifted-band leakage, or
upper-tail residue.

The stopping point of the branch is a payment mismatch:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{available payment} &:\ \text{integrated energy/enstrophy budget},\\
  \text{required payment} &:\ \text{pointwise active-scale transfer control}.
  \end{aligned}
\]
The mismatch can be reduced by localization, kernel gains, shell separation, or
Volterra ordering. Each refinement creates a more precise terminal object. The
branch remains valuable because it names the object whose final role must be
classified.

In the CM-exit program, a scale-tail survivor has to enter the same-solution
witness test. If the terminal tail remains attached to a positive material
carrier, participates in the same pressure-viscosity equation, and admits a
positive-scale field readout, it belongs to the continuation side of the branch.
If the tail loses one of these requirements, the alleged breakdown witness exits the
working class.

The scale-barrier branch supplies a family of CM targets. A lower-shell
remainder asks whether the selected packet still has a positive carrier. A
pressure-supported tail asks whether the same Navier--Stokes participation law
remains intact. A separated high-frequency residue asks whether a positive-scale
field can still be read from the witness. The positive-forward estimate produces
the survivor; the CM-exit method decides its terminal witness role.
