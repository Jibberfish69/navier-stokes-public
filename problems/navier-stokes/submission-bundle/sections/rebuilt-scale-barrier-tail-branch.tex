\section{Scale Barriers and High-Frequency Tails}
\label{sec:scale-barrier-tail-branch}

\subsection{Factorial Radius Spend and Same-Carrier Reserve}
\label{subsec:factorial-radius-spend-same-carrier-reserve}

The current forward-gold scale branch has a factorial tower reading.  For mixed derivatives
\[
  W_{m,\alpha}=\partial_s^m\partial_y^\alpha V,
  \qquad
  Q_{m,\alpha}=\partial_s^m\partial_y^\alpha Q,
\]
the differentiated nonlinearity carries the mixed Leibniz coefficient
\[
  {m\choose a}{\alpha\choose\beta}.
\]
With
\[
  w_{m,\alpha}(\tau,\rho)={\tau^m\rho^{|\alpha|}\over m!\,\alpha!},
\]
the coefficient is absorbed exactly:
\[
  w_{m,\alpha}{m\choose a}{\alpha\choose\beta}
  =w_{a,\beta}w_{m-a,\alpha-\beta}.
\]
The binomial factor is therefore paid by the tower normalization.  The remaining cost is the loss of mixed analytic radius on the same selected carrier.

Let \(\tau(\sigma)\) and \(\rho(\sigma)\) be the time and space radii.  The finite tower storage
\[
  L_S(\sigma)=\sum_{2m+|\alpha|\le N}w_{m,\alpha}(\tau,\rho)E_{m,\alpha}(\sigma)
\]
satisfies
\[
  dL_S+dD_S^{\rm rad}=dJ_{SQ},
  \qquad
  D_S^{\rm rad}
  =\sum_{2m+|\alpha|\le N}
  \left(-m{\tau'\over\tau}-|\alpha|{\rho'\over\rho}\right)
  w_{m,\alpha}E_{m,\alpha}\ge0.
\]
This is the coupled analytic-radius spend supplied by the Stirling/binomial route.

The next forward-gold bridge is the same-carrier reserve.  With selected activity \(a_j(s)\) and same-carrier action currency \(B_j(s)\), the certificate is
\[
  a_j(s)\le \lambda B_j(s)+\ell_j(s),
  \qquad
  \int_{-1}^{0}\ell_j(s)\,ds=o_j(1),
\]
together with
\[
  \lim_{N\to\infty}
  \sum_{j:\,r_j\le2^{-N}}
  \int_{-1}^{0}B_j(s)\,ds=0.
\]
This pair of estimates rules out a retained endpoint chain carrying a fixed positive amount of selected activity.

The shifted-gamma saddle gives the current candidate price.  For
\[
  w_n^{(\beta)}(\tau)={\tau^n\over\Gamma(n+\beta)},
\]
the middle-band multiplier is
\[
  R_\beta(n,k)
  ={n\choose k}{\Gamma(k+\beta)\Gamma(n-k+\beta)\over\Gamma(n+\beta)}
  =2^{2-2\beta}n^{\beta-1}\bigl(1+O_\beta(n^{-1/2})\bigr)
\]
on \(k=n/2+O(\sqrt n)\).  The open bridge is
\[
  \text{SameCarrierShiftedGammaSpendReserve.A}.
\]
It requires shifted-gamma extraction on the selected carrier and an unweighted reserve for the same action currency.

The scale-barrier branch asks a direct question about the Navier--Stokes
equation.  Can high-frequency transfer be made summable before it can carry a
finite terminal event?  This is a forward branch because it tries to pay the
estimate directly.  Viscosity damps a shell at frequency \(2^j\) at rate
bounded below by a fixed multiple of \(\nu2^{2j}\), once the
Littlewood--Paley cutoff convention is fixed.  The nonlinearity can move energy
into that same shell.  A direct proof has to show that the damping wins after
the harmless lower-order pieces have been separated away.

Write one smooth member of the classical approximation family as \(u\).  Let
\(\Delta_j\) be the Littlewood--Paley projection with Fourier support in the
annulus \(c_{LP}2^j\le |\xi|\le C_{LP}2^j\), and set \(u_j=\Delta_j u\).  For a
dyadic threshold \(N\), define the high tail and its dissipation by
\[
  E_N(t)=\sum_{j\ge N}\|u_j(t)\|_{L^2_x}^2,
  \qquad
  D_N(t)=\sum_{j\ge N}2^{2j}\|u_j(t)\|_{L^2_x}^2.
\]
Projecting the equation and pairing the \(j\)-th shell with \(u_j\) gives
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|u_j(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  +\nu\|\nabla u_j(t)\|_{L^2}^2
  =
  -\left\langle
  \Delta_j\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u),u_j
  \right\rangle
\]
Bernstein gives constants \(0<c_B\le C_B<\infty\), fixed by the cutoff
convention, such that
\[
  c_B2^{2j}\|u_j\|_2^2
  \le
  \|\nabla u_j\|_2^2
  \le
  C_B2^{2j}\|u_j\|_2^2.
\]
Summing over \(j\ge N\) gives the tail inequality
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}E_N(t)+c\nu D_N(t)
  \le \mathcal F_N(t),
\]
where
\[
  \mathcal F_N(t)
  :=
  \sum_{j\ge N}
  \left|
  \left\langle
  \Delta_j\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u),u_j
  \right\rangle
  \right|.
\]
The whole branch is now reduced to one task: prove that the time integral of
\(\mathcal F_N\) can be absorbed into the viscous tail plus a summable
remainder as \(N\to\infty\).

The useful target estimate has the form
\[
  \int_0^T \mathcal F_N(t)\,dt
  \le
  \eta\nu\int_0^TD_N(t)\,dt
  +C_0 2^{-2\delta N},
  \qquad 0<\eta<c,
  \tag{SB}
\]
after every lower-mode coefficient that remains has been paid on the same
classical surface.  If \((\mathrm{SB})\) holds for all large \(N\), then the
high tail cannot retain a fixed positive terminal residue.  The tail energy
inequality gives
\[
  E_N(T)+2(c-\eta)\nu\int_0^T D_N(t)\,dt
  \le
  E_N(0)+2C_0 2^{-2\delta N}.
\]
Smooth initial data make \(E_N(0)\to0\), and the displayed inequality forces
the high tail to vanish along large thresholds.  This is the gold scale
barrier.

The reason this estimate is difficult appears in the paraproduct split.  Put
\(S_m=\sum_{k<m}\Delta_k\).  For each \(j\ge N\), decompose
\[
  \Delta_j((u\cdot\nabla)u)
  =
  \mathcal T^{LH}_j+\mathcal T^{HL}_j+\mathcal T^{HH}_j,
\]
with
\[
  \mathcal T^{LH}_j
  =
  \Delta_j((S_{j-4}u\cdot\nabla)u_j),
  \qquad
  \mathcal T^{HL}_j
  =
  \Delta_j((u_j\cdot\nabla)S_{j-4}u),
\]
and
\[
  \mathcal T^{HH}_j
  =
  \Delta_j\!\left(
  \sum_{k\ge j-3}\Delta_k u\cdot\nabla\widetilde\Delta_k u
  \right),
  \qquad
  \widetilde\Delta_k=\Delta_{k-1}+\Delta_k+\Delta_{k+1}.
\]
The low-high and high-low terms contain the low-mode strain seen by the
active shell.  The high-high term contains the near-diagonal cascade.

Freeze the low-mode coefficient at the threshold:
\[
  S_{j-4}u
  =
  S_{N-4}u
  +(S_{N+M}-S_{N-4})u
  +(S_{j-4}-S_{N+M})u,
\]
where \(M\) is a fixed collar width.  The first term is the strict low-mode
piece.  Since \(\nabla\cdot S_{N-4}u=0\),
\[
  \left\langle (S_{N-4}u\cdot\nabla)u_j,u_j\right\rangle=0
\]
The projected term is the commutator
\[
  \left\langle
  [\Delta_j,S_{N-4}u\cdot\nabla]u_j,u_j
  \right\rangle
\]
The fixed-gap commutator is estimated by writing the commutator with the
Littlewood--Paley kernel.  The mean-value theorem puts one derivative on the
low-frequency velocity \(S_{N-4}u\), and the \(L^1\) size of the rescaled
kernel is uniform at fixed gap.  The fixed-gap calculation gives a constant \(C_{\mathrm{comm}}\),
depending only on the cutoff convention and the fixed gap, such that
\[
  \left|
  \left\langle
  [\Delta_j,S_{N-4}u\cdot\nabla]u_j,u_j
  \right\rangle
  \right|
  \le C_{\mathrm{comm}}
  \|\nabla S_{N-4}u\|_{L^\infty}\|u_j\|_2^2.
\]
With
\[
  \mathcal L_N(t)
  =
  1+\sum_{k\le N-4}2^{3k/2}\|\Delta_k u(t)\|_2,
\]
Bernstein and Young give, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
  \sum_{j\ge N}
  \left|
  \left\langle
  \mathcal T^{SLH}_j+\mathcal T^{SHL}_j,u_j
  \right\rangle
  \right|
  \le
  C_\varepsilon \mathcal L_N(t)E_N(t)+\varepsilon D_N(t).
\]
The strict low modes are then accounted for.  They leave a definite lower-mode
coefficient \( \mathcal L_N E_N\), together with two packets created by the
frozen cutoff.

The threshold-local collar is
\[
  (S_{N+M}-S_{N-4})u.
\]
It contains only finitely many shells around \(N\).  Its contribution is a
finite collar packet.  The proof has a concrete target for that packet:
estimate it by the same \(\mathcal L_NE_N\) currency plus an arbitrarily
small fraction of \(D_N\).  This is a proposition-level payment inside the
scale route.

The genuine high-high packet is more favorable.  Fourier support forces it
near the diagonal:
\[
  \left|
  \left\langle
  \mathcal T^{HH}_j,u_j
  \right\rangle
  \right|
  \le
  C\sum_{|k-j|\le M}
  2^j\|\Delta_k u\|_2
  \|\widetilde\Delta_k u\|_2
  \|u_j\|_2
\]
Hence
\[
  \sum_{j\ge N}
  \left|
  \left\langle
  \mathcal T^{HH}_j,u_j
  \right\rangle
  \right|
  \le
  C\sum_{j\ge N-M}2^j\|\Delta_j u\|_2^3.
\]
The energy bound supplies the extra large-threshold factor:
\[
  2^j\|\Delta_j u\|_2^3
  =
  \bigl(2^{2j}\|\Delta_j u\|_2^2\bigr)
  \bigl(2^{-j}\|\Delta_j u\|_2\bigr)
  \le
  C_E^{1/2}2^{-N+M}\,
  2^{2j}\|\Delta_j u\|_2^2.
\]
Choosing \(N\) large absorbs the cubic high-high packet into the viscous
dissipation.  This is a real payment in the scale branch.

The live obstruction is the lifted high-side remainder
\[
  b_j=(S_{j-4}-S_{N+M})u.
\]
It enters through
\[
  \mathcal T^{lift}_j
  =
  \Delta_j((b_j\cdot\nabla)u_j)
  +
  \Delta_j((u_j\cdot\nabla)b_j).
\]
The transported part is skew before localization, so the surviving term is
a commutatorized stress-strain flux.  The packet is controlled by a
mesoscopic density of the form
\[
  \Pi_N^{lift,dy}(t)
  =
  \sum_{j\ge N}
  \int_{\mathbb R^3}
  \Sigma^{meso}_j(x,t)\,
  \tau^{H^1}_j(x,t)\,dx,
\]
where \(\Sigma^{meso}_j\) measures the strain of \(b_j\) at the shell scale
and \(\tau^{H^1}_j\) measures the \(H^1\)-strength of the shell increments.
The exact missing gold theorem is
\[
  \int_0^T\Pi_N^{lift,dy}(t)\,dt
  \le
  \frac{\eta\nu}{4}\int_0^TD_N(t)\,dt
  +C_*2^{-2\delta N}.
  \tag{L-Flux}
\]
This is the present scale-barrier wall.  It is smaller than the whole
Navier--Stokes problem, because the strict low-mode pieces, the finite collar,
and the genuine high-high cubic tail have all been separated.  The inherited
finite-energy and enstrophy inputs still leave this estimate open.

The later scale notes refine \((\mathrm{L\text{-}Flux})\) further.  The
signed lifted-band descent isolates an upper boundary packet and a middle-band
residual.  The middle-band residual reduces to a localized stress-strain flux.
The far-corona form then asks for a cumulative tail-stress or one-sided
square estimate.  These refinements are valuable because they name the exact
descendant of the scale obstruction.  They remain open gold refinements
from the installed Euclidean inputs.

The high-frequency gain route records the downstream payoff.  If
\[
  \mathrm{HFG}(T):
  \qquad
  \sum_{j\ge0}2^{3j/2}
  \|\Delta_j\omega\|_{L^1_tL^2_x(0,T)}
  <\infty,
  \qquad \omega=\nabla\times u,
\]
then Bernstein gives
\[
  \int_0^T\|\omega(t)\|_{B^0_{\infty,1}}\,dt
  \le
  C\sum_{j\ge0}2^{3j/2}
  \|\Delta_j\omega\|_{L^1_tL^2_x(0,T)}
  <\infty
\]
The critical vorticity continuation criterion then continues the classical
solution.  A pure spectral-gap transfer theorem is too weak for this route
because the near-diagonal low-mode strain channel has no large gap to spend.
A successful all-scale theorem must also control the diagonal amplitude, or
it must supply an equivalent mixed enstrophy-gradient or carrier-export
primitive on the same classical dyadic surface.

The CM counterpart consumes the same terminal tail selected by this gold
analysis.  A paid scale barrier supplies continuation readout and belongs to
the member side.  A selected tail lacking a material carrier gives a
\(\Pack_Q\) Part/Field failure.  A selected carrier whose pressure, viscosity, or
incompressibility participation detaches from the original equation gives a
\(\Part_{N,Q}\) Part/Field failure.  A retained carrier with retained participation
and zero surviving positive field scale gives a \(\Field_{N,r,Q}\) Part/Field failure.  The
scale branch has now done its paper job: it shows the direct gold payment,
identifies the exact lifted-flux obstruction, and supplies the CM Part/Fields that
classify any same-solution terminal tail left by that obstruction.
