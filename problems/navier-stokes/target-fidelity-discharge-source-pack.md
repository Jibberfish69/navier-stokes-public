## Exact Bridge Claim

The Clay target is the classical three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equation on the admissible data class fixed by the lane. A proof that a modified equation is globally smooth after adding extra dissipation, hyper-viscosity, morphic smoothing, curvature terms, or any other non-classical correction does not close the original problem unless one also proves, on the same admissibility surface, that the modified solutions converge back to a smooth solution of the original classical equation with every theorem-critical bound preserved in the limit.

Equivalently, theorem-target fidelity forces the route to distinguish:

1. smoothness for a modified or regularized equation, and
2. smoothness for the original classical Navier-Stokes equation.

Without a same-surface transfer theorem from (1) to (2), the modified-equation result remains diagnostic only.

## Why The Bridge Is Local And Exact

- The bridge does not add a new theorem target.
- The bridge does not require a new theory chapter.
- The bridge only states the exact transfer burden needed before any modified-equation success could count toward the classical Clay target.
- The bridge therefore belongs inside the existing theorem spine as a bounded local target-fidelity discharge.

## Proof Skeleton

1. Fix the theorem target: the original classical 3D Navier-Stokes equation on admissible data.
2. Observe that any added dissipative, morphic, geometric, or surrogate term changes the dynamical system being solved.
3. A global-regularity statement for that altered system therefore proves regularity for the altered system only.
4. To recover a classical theorem from the altered system, one needs a same-surface limit theorem showing that the extra term becomes inessential while all regularity-critical bounds survive in the limit.
5. If no such transfer theorem is supplied, the modified-equation success cannot be counted as a proof of the original problem.

## Consequence For The Live Route

Once target fidelity is enforced, the active route has to remain on the classical equation and pass through classical energy control, classical dyadic tail control, classical compactness, and classical gradient control.
