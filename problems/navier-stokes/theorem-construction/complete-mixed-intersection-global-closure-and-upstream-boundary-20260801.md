# The first complete spatial row closes the velocity tower and global smoothness

Date: 2026-08-01

Status: global-smoothness closure proved from the endpoint-spanning complete
spatial row. The full mixed temporal-spatial intersection is a consequence,
not an additional premise. The handoff from the earlier height/VPI theorem is
exactly its asserted \(L_t^1\) spatial output (CMI.21).

## 1. The exact first-row object

Let \(u_0\in H_x^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\cap L_x^2(\mathbb R^3)\) be
divergence-free, let \(\nu>0\), and let \((u,p)\) be the one maximal classical
Navier--Stokes history generated by \(u_0\) on \([0,T_*)\).

For a finite interval \(I\), define the complete first spatial row

\[
\mathscr S_0(I)
:=
\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}L^2(I;H_x^m).
\tag{CMI.1}
\]

This is one row of one velocity field with its entire nested spatial Sobolev
column present. It is not a selection of one Sobolev order and it requires no
constant uniform in \(m\).

The stronger complete mixed object is

\[
\mathcal J^\infty(I)
:=
\bigcap_{N=0}^{\infty}\bigcap_{M=0}^{\infty}
H_t^N(I;H_x^M).
\tag{CMI.2}
\]

The key correction is

\[
\boxed{\mathscr S_0(I)\Longrightarrow\mathcal J^\infty(I)}
\tag{CMI.3}
\]

for a Navier--Stokes history on a finite interval. Thus the temporal rows do
not have to be paid independently after the complete first spatial row is
known.

More generally, suppose one temporal depth \(q\ge1\) is the first row supplied
with its whole spatial intersection:

\[
V_q=\partial_t^qu
\in
\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}L^2(I;H_x^m).
\tag{CMI.3a}
\]

The smooth finite datum and the pressure-explicit recurrence give
\(V_j(0)\in H_x^\infty\) for \(0\le j<q\). Since \(L_t^2\subset L_t^1\) on
the finite interval,

\[
V_{j-1}(t)
=
V_{j-1}(0)
+
\int_0^tV_j(s)\,ds
\tag{CMI.3b}
\]

puts \(V_{q-1}\) in \(C_tH_x^m\) for every \(m\), hence also in
\(L_t^2H_x^m\). Iterating (CMI.3b) reaches \(u=V_0\in\mathscr S_0(I)\).
Therefore every backward level has its own complete-row implication:

\[
\boxed{
\text{one actual same-history row }V_q
\in\bigcap_mL^2(I;H_x^m)
\Longrightarrow
\mathscr S_0(I)
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal J^\infty(I).
}
\tag{CMI.3c}
\]

This is the precise sense in which the first row encountered already contains
the nested spatial regularity needed by the velocity solution.

## 2. The pressure-completed interaction is integrable in every spatial row

Write the pressure and the complete transport-pressure response as

\[
p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j),
\qquad
\mathcal B(u,u)
:=(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p
=\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u).
\tag{CMI.4}
\]

Pressure here is the simultaneous whole-field response required by
incompressibility. For every integer \(m\ge0\), the Sobolev product theorem
and boundedness of \(\mathbb P\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathcal B(u,u)\|_{H^m}
&\le C_m\|u\otimes u\|_{H^{m+1}}\\
&\le C_m\|u\|_{H^{m+2}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{CMI.5}
\]

If \(u\in\mathscr S_0(0,T_*)\), then

\[
\int_0^{T_*}\|\mathcal B(u,u)\|_{H^m}\,dt
\le
C_m\|u\|_{L_t^2H_x^{m+2}}^2
<\infty.
\tag{CMI.6}
\]

On the same finite interval,

\[
\int_0^{T_*}\|\Delta u\|_{H^m}\,dt
\le
\sqrt{T_*}\,\|u\|_{L_t^2H_x^{m+2}}
<\infty.
\tag{CMI.7}
\]

The original pressure-explicit equation

\[
u_t=\nu\Delta u-\mathcal B(u,u)
\tag{CMI.8}
\]

therefore gives

\[
u_t\in L^1(0,T_*;H^m)
\qquad\text{for every }m.
\tag{CMI.9}
\]

This is the exact interaction-smoothness step contained in the first complete
row: the full nonlinear and pressure response is controlled by the deeper
spatial coordinates already present in that same row.

## 3. The velocity itself reaches the endpoint in \(H^\infty\)

Because \(u\in L^2_tH_x^m\subset L^1_tH_x^m\) on a finite interval, (CMI.9)
implies

\[
u\in W^{1,1}(0,T_*;H^m)
\hookrightarrow
C([0,T_*];H^m)
\qquad\text{for every }m.
\tag{CMI.10}
\]

More explicitly, the same velocity is recovered from its finite initial datum
by

\[
u(t)
=
u_0
+
\int_0^t
\bigl(\nu\Delta u-\mathcal B(u,u)\bigr)(s)\,ds,
\]

and (CMI.6)--(CMI.7) give the coordinatewise bound

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^m}
\le
\|u_0\|_{H^m}
+
\nu\sqrt{T_*}\|u\|_{L_t^2H_x^{m+2}}
+
C_m\|u\|_{L_t^2H_x^{m+2}}^2.
\tag{CMI.10a}
\]

Every quantity on the right is finite for its fixed \(m\); no uniformity in
\(m\) is required.

For \(m_2>m_1\), the \(H^{m_2}\)-valued and \(H^{m_1}\)-valued continuous
representatives agree almost everywhere and hence everywhere. Their terminal
values are one distribution \(u_*\), with

\[
\boxed{
u_*:=u(T_*)\in\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}H_x^m
=H_x^\infty.
}
\tag{CMI.11}
\]

Coordinatewise continuity also gives

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^m}<\infty
\qquad\text{for every fixed }m.
\tag{CMI.12}
\]

Thus the nested spatial intersection has already produced a smooth terminal
velocity before any separate temporal-row estimate is introduced.

## 4. The original equation recursively produces the entire temporal tower

Set

\[
V_N=\partial_t^N u,
\qquad
P_N=\partial_t^N p.
\]

The pressure-explicit binomial recurrence is

\[
P_N
=
R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^{N}\binom Na
V_{a,i}V_{N-a,j},
\tag{CMI.13}
\]

\[
V_{N+1}
=
\nu\Delta V_N
-
\sum_{a=0}^{N}\binom Na
(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{N-a}
-
\nabla P_N.
\tag{CMI.14}
\]

We now prove inductively that

\[
\boxed{
V_N,P_N\in C([0,T_*];H_x^\infty)
\qquad\text{for every }N.
}
\tag{CMI.15}
\]

Assume \(V_0,\ldots,V_N\in C([0,T_*];H_x^\infty)\); the base \(V_0=u\) is
(CMI.10). For each target order \(m\), their spatial rows \(m+2\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left\|
\sum_{a=0}^{N}\binom Na
(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{N-a}
\right\|_{H^m}
+
\|\nabla P_N\|_{H^m}\\
&\qquad\le
C_{m,N}
\sum_{a=0}^{N}
\|V_a\|_{H^{m+2}}\,
\|V_{N-a}\|_{H^{m+2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{CMI.16}
\]

The right side of (CMI.14) is therefore continuous in \(H^m\) for every
\(m\), and (CMI.13) gives \(P_N\in C([0,T_*];H_x^\infty)\). On
\((0,T_*)\), the classical recurrence identifies that continuous right side
with the actual derivative \(\partial_tV_N\). Integrating from any
\(t_0<T_*\) and passing \(t\uparrow T_*\) shows that
\(V_N\in C^1([0,T_*];H^m)\), with endpoint derivative equal to the
recursively defined \(V_{N+1}\). This completes the induction, proves
(CMI.15), and gives

\[
u\in C^\infty([0,T_*];H_x^m)
\qquad\text{for every }m.
\tag{CMI.17}
\]

Since the interval is finite, (CMI.15)--(CMI.17) imply every coordinate in
(CMI.2). This proves (CMI.3).

## 5. Every temporal row carries its own spatial smoothness intersection

Fix \(N\) and a spatial derivative order \(k\). Choose \(m>k+3/2\).
Sobolev embedding and (CMI.15) give

\[
V_N\in C([0,T_*];H^m)
\hookrightarrow
C([0,T_*];C_b^k).
\tag{CMI.18}
\]

Because \(N\) and \(k\) are arbitrary,

\[
\boxed{
u,p\in C^\infty([0,T_*]\times\mathbb R^3).
}
\tag{CMI.19}
\]

This is the requested row-by-row intersection logic. The velocity row first
uses its complete spatial column to make the pressure-completed interaction
integrable. The original equation then produces acceleration in that same
intersection; the binomial recurrence produces every later temporal response
of the same velocity field.

The critical height

\[
H(t)=\frac12\|\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)\|_2^2
\]

also has a finite terminal value. Indeed, (CMI.10) with \(m=1\), together
with

\[
\|\Lambda^{1/2}f\|_2^2
\le
\|f\|_2\|\nabla f\|_2,
\tag{CMI.20}
\]

shows that \(t\mapsto H(t)\) is continuous on \([0,T_*]\). Hence terminal
critical-height divergence is incompatible with the first complete spatial
row.

### 5.1 Terminal height divergence forces the aligned positive part to diverge

Independently of the hypothesis \(u\in\mathscr S_0\), every preterminal
classical segment obeys the following exact contrapositive readout. Put

\[
y(t)=\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2,
\qquad
z(t)=\|\nabla^2u(t)\|_2^2,
\]

and decompose the symmetric strain into its positive and negative spectral
parts,

\[
S(u)=S_+(u)-S_-(u).
\]

Define the extensional and compressive vorticity interactions

\[
A(t):=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\omega\cdot S_+(u)\omega\,dx,
\qquad
C(t):=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\omega\cdot S_-(u)\omega\,dx.
\]

Both are nonnegative. The exact first spatial-row identity is

\[
\frac12y(t)
+
\nu\int_0^t z(s)\,ds
+
\int_0^t C(s)\,ds
=
\frac12y(0)
+
\int_0^t A(s)\,ds.
\tag{CMI.20a}
\]

Fourier interpolation and the kinetic-energy law give

\[
2H(t)
=
\|\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)\|_2^2
\le
\|u(t)\|_2\|\nabla u(t)\|_2
\le
\|u_0\|_2\,y(t)^{1/2}.
\tag{CMI.20b}
\]

Combining (CMI.20a)--(CMI.20b) yields the quantitative aligned-action bound
for \(u_0\ne0\):

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^t A(s)\,ds
\ge
\frac{2H(t)^2}{\|u_0\|_2^2}
-
\frac12y(0).
}
\tag{CMI.20c}
\]

If \(u_0=0\), same-datum uniqueness gives \(u\equiv0\), so the blow-up branch
is empty. For nonzero datum, \(A\) is finite on every compact subinterval of
\([0,T_*)\), because the solution is classical there.

Therefore any sequence \(t_j\uparrow T_*\) with \(H(t_j)\to\infty\) forces

\[
\int_0^{T_*}A(s)\,ds=\infty,
\qquad
\limsup_{t\uparrow T_*}A(t)=\infty.
\tag{CMI.20d}
\]

The forced statement is accumulated positive extensional alignment, with
unbounded aligned production along a terminal sequence; it does not assert
monotone pointwise growth.

## 6. Exact handoff from the critical-height tower

Let

\[
E_s(t):=\frac12\|\Lambda^su(t)\|_2^2.
\]

The supplied complete critical-height/VPI rows obey

\[
R_n
=
\frac{
H^{(n)}
-
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
(-2\nu)^j\partial_t^{\,n-1-j}P_{j+1/2}
}{
(-2\nu)^n
}
=E_{n+1/2}.
\]

Here \(P_{j+1/2}\) denotes the signed Sobolev/VPI production current at that
height, not the pressure derivative \(P_N\) in (CMI.13).

Assume the preceding complete height/VPI theorem has proved, on the whole
interval \((0,T_*)\), that every row \(R_n\) displayed above belongs to
\(L^1(0,T_*)\). Since \(R_n=E_{n+1/2}\), its exact spatial output is

\[
E_{n+1/2}\in L^1(0,T_*)
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge0.
\tag{CMI.21}
\]

For each \(n\),

\[
\int_0^{T_*}
\|\Lambda^{n+1/2}u(t)\|_2^2\,dt
=
2\int_0^{T_*}E_{n+1/2}(t)\,dt
<\infty.
\tag{CMI.22}
\]

The kinetic-energy identity supplies the low-frequency \(L_t^2L_x^2\) part.
For any integer \(m\), choose \(n+1/2\ge m\); interpolation between \(L^2\)
and \(\dot H^{n+1/2}\) then gives

\[
u\in L^2(0,T_*;H^m).
\tag{CMI.23}
\]

Intersecting all finite \(m\) yields precisely \(\mathscr S_0(0,T_*)\).
Therefore the backward nesting closes as

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{the external premise }(R_n)_{n\ge0}\subset L^1(0,T_*)
&\Longrightarrow
\mathscr S_0(0,T_*)\\
&\Longrightarrow
u\in C([0,T_*];H^\infty)\\
&\Longrightarrow
(V_N,P_N)_{N\ge0}\subset C([0,T_*];H^\infty)\\
&\Longrightarrow
(u,p)\in C^\infty([0,T_*]\times\mathbb R^3).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{CMI.24}
\]

No additional temporal mixed row occurs between the first and last lines.

## 7. Smooth restart contradicts a finite maximal time

The energy identity passes to the endpoint, so \(u_*\) is finite-energy and
divergence-free. Smooth local existence from

\[
u_*\in H_x^\infty
\]

produces a smooth solution on \([T_*,T_*+\delta)\). Its initial pressure and
temporal jet are determined by the same recurrences (CMI.13)--(CMI.14), so
they agree with the left endpoint jet in (CMI.15). Smooth same-datum
uniqueness therefore glues one continuation of the same velocity history
past \(T_*\), contradicting maximality.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr S_0(0,T_*)
\text{ on an alleged finite maximal interval}
\Longrightarrow
\text{smooth extension past }T_*
\Longrightarrow
T_*=\infty.
}
\tag{CMI.25}
\]

Equivalently, if the preceding complete height/VPI theorem proves (CMI.21) on
every alleged finite terminal interval, then the pressure-explicit
Navier--Stokes solution generated by the initial finite smooth datum is global
and

\[
\boxed{
u,p\in C^\infty([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^3).
}
\tag{CMI.26}
\]

The first complete row contains the rest of the velocity tower.

## 8. The nested-row theorem resolves the three one-fluid pictures

The conclusion above acts on the whole continuing velocity field, not on a
classification of geometries. It therefore returns directly to the three
physical pictures in their original order:

1. the simple pulse or spring winding, where motion normally travels through
   neighboring windings and the proposed concentration branch forces one
   winding into an increasingly narrow, intense deformation;
2. the imbalanced stretched vortex, where asymmetric inward momentum can make
   the center gyrate, mix, waste coherence, and fragment; and
3. the perfectly balanced rip-cord vortex, where the center remains exact
   while exterior motion of the same fluid draws outward and the inner spiral
   tightens and spins faster.

These are distinct geometric readouts of the same continuing fixed-viscosity
incompressible velocity-pressure field, with pressure the simultaneous
whole-field response required by incompressibility. The balanced case is
admitted directly: no wobble, symmetry loss, or external supplier is assumed.

### 8.1 The common fieldwide consequences

Under the complete-row hypothesis of (CMI.25), (CMI.15), Sobolev embedding,
and compactness of the time interval give, for every temporal order \(N\) and
spatial order \(k\),

\[
\boxed{
M_{N,k}(T_*):=
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}
\|\nabla^k\partial_t^Nu(t)\|_{L^\infty}<\infty.
}
\tag{CMI.27}
\]

Thus velocity, strain, vorticity, acceleration, and every higher
spatial-temporal response remain bounded through \(T_*\). Put
\(L_*:=M_{0,1}(T_*)\).

The same conclusion gives a uniformly vanishing ultraviolet critical tail.
For \(m>1/2\) and \(K\ge1\), define

\[
H_{>K}(t)
:=\frac12\int_{|\xi|>K}|\xi|\,|\widehat u(t,\xi)|^2\,d\xi.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}H_{>K}(t)
&\le
\frac12K^{1-2m}
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}\|u(t)\|_{\dot H^m}^2,
\\[2mm]
&\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}H_{>K}(t)=0.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{CMI.28}
\]

No nonzero critical-height content can remain at the infinitesimal-scale
boundary near a finite terminal time.

There is also a direct material consequence. Let \(X(a,t)\) and \(X(b,t)\)
be trajectories of two distinct material labels. The Lipschitz bound gives

\[
\frac d{dt}|X(a,t)-X(b,t)|
\ge
-\|\nabla u(t)\|_\infty |X(a,t)-X(b,t)|,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
|X(a,t)-X(b,t)|
\ge
|a-b|\exp\!\left(
-\int_0^t\|\nabla u(s)\|_\infty\,ds
\right)>0
}
\tag{CMI.29}
\]

for every \(t\le T_*\). Distinct material labels cannot collapse onto one
another in finite time.

### 8.2 Simple pulse or spring winding

Choose two points \(x(t),y(t)\) spanning a winding, and define its width and
velocity contrast by

\[
\ell(t):=|x(t)-y(t)|,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pulse}}(t)
:=|u(t,x(t))-u(t,y(t))|.
\]

The mean-value theorem and (CMI.27) give

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pulse}}(t)
\le L_*\ell(t).
}
\tag{CMI.30}
\]

If the contrast has a positive lower limit, the winding has a positive lower
width. If \(\ell(t)\to0\), then
\(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pulse}}(t)\to0\): the concentrating feature loses its
velocity contrast instead of becoming singular. Equation (CMI.28) rules out
hiding nonzero critical height in successively finer windings. The pulse may
propagate, deform, spread, or lose amplitude, but it cannot become one
nonzero, zero-width winding at finite time.

### 8.3 Imbalanced stretched vortex

The inward asymmetry can produce the stated gyration, mixing, wasted
organization, and fragmentation. The proof does not require that morphology.
Whether the vortex fragments or temporarily recoheres, (CMI.27) gives

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}
\bigl(\|\omega(t)\|_\infty+\|S(t)\|_\infty\bigr)<\infty,
}
\tag{CMI.31}
\]

where \(\omega=\nabla\times u\) and
\(S=(\nabla u+\nabla u^T)/2\). No fragment can retain nonzero velocity
contrast while its width tends to zero, and (CMI.28) excludes a nonzero
critical tail at infinite frequency. Thus wobble and waste are compatible
with the theorem but are not its payment: even temporary recoherence remains
inside the same smooth velocity intersection.

### 8.4 Perfectly balanced rip-cord vortex

Keep the center perfectly balanced. Keep the exterior pull, simultaneous
whole-field pressure response, and inner tightening joined in one history.
There are two useful material readings. They need not exhaust hybrid
recruitment, which remains subject to the same fieldwide bounds.

#### Persistent material core

Suppose fixed material labels remain in the tightening core. Choose two such
labels with positive initial separation. Equation (CMI.29) gives

\[
\boxed{
r_{a,b}(t):=|X(a,t)-X(b,t)|
\ge
r_{a,b}(0)
\exp\!\left(
-\int_0^t\|\nabla u(s)\|_\infty\,ds
\right)>0.
}
\tag{CMI.32}
\]

If those labels span the core, its material diameter cannot vanish. The
exterior rip-cord action may shrink that diameter, but its total logarithmic
contraction before \(T_*\) is finite.

Let \(x_c(t)=X(c,t)\) be a material center and let \(\Gamma(r,t)\) be the
circulation around a centered circle of radius \(r\). Since the circulation
of the constant vector \(u(t,x_c(t))\) around a closed curve is zero,
(CMI.27) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|u(t,x)-u(t,x_c(t))|
&\le L_*|x-x_c(t)|,\\
\frac{|(u(t,x)-u(t,x_c(t)))\cdot\tau|}{r}
&\le L_*,\\
&\boxed{
|\Gamma(r,t)|
\le 2\pi L_*r^2\longrightarrow0
\qquad(r\downarrow0).
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{CMI.33}
\]

Thus the relative angular rate stays bounded, and an exact centered core
cannot retain nonzero circulation while reaching zero radius.

#### Recruited core

Suppose instead that successively finer visible cores use different material
labels. Then (CMI.32) is not the relevant description of the visible core,
but changing labels does not change the fieldwide bounds (CMI.27). For any two
points \(x,y\) in the visible core,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|
&\le L_*|x-y|,\\
|\omega(t,x)-\omega(t,y)|+|S(t,x)-S(t,y)|
&\le C M_{0,2}(T_*)|x-y|.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{CMI.34}
\]

The values of vorticity and strain are bounded by (CMI.31), their contrasts
vanish across a shrinking width by (CMI.34), and (CMI.27) gives the same
statement at every higher derivative order. A recruited visible core may
shrink and disappear smoothly; it cannot develop singular spin, strain,
vorticity, or higher response. Separately, in the critical-height coordinate
used by this concentration picture, (CMI.28) says that the same history has no
nonvanishing \(\dot H^{1/2}\) Fourier tail at infinite frequency. Every
recruited piece remains part of the same pressure-complete velocity field and
the same complete row.

### 8.5 The common physical verdict

The simple pulse cannot become a nonzero zero-width winding. The imbalanced
vortex may wobble and fragment, but every fragment remains smooth. The
perfectly balanced rip-cord may remain exactly centered and tighten violently,
but a persistent material core cannot collapse, a recruited core cannot carry
nonzero critical height to infinite frequency, and its centered angular rate
and circulation remain regular.

At the alleged terminal time, all three pictures have the same endpoint:

\[
\boxed{
u(T_*)\in H_x^\infty,
\qquad
p(T_*)\in H_x^\infty,
\qquad
(V_N,P_N)_{N\ge0}\subset C([0,T_*];H_x^\infty).
}
\tag{CMI.35}
\]

Here pressure uses the decaying Riesz-transform normalization in (CMI.4).
The same velocity-pressure history restarts smoothly beyond \(T_*\).
Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr S_0(0,T_*)
\Longrightarrow
\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{simple pulse,}\\
\text{imbalanced vortex, or}\\
\text{perfectly balanced rip-cord}
\end{array}
\right\}
\text{ cannot realize a finite-time singularity.}
}
\tag{CMI.36}
\]

The theorem does not select the detailed post-peak morphology: translation,
mixing, fragmentation, and smooth continued tightening all remain possible.
The three pictures are physical archetypes rather than an exhaustive
classification. Their common conclusion comes from the theorem acting on the
entire velocity field. This section is the exact downstream physical
consequence of the complete-row hypothesis in (CMI.25); it does not replace
the upstream task of supplying that row from the finite datum.
