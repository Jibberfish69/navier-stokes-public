## What Cycle 7 Gets Right

The Cycle 6 merge was correctly killed: a scalar pressure \(P_2\) shell moment
is not identical to an oriented Biot-Savart drift sign.

Cycle 7's replacement parity filter is useful. Under the global reversal
\[
  \mathcal R u(x,t)=-u(x,-t),
\]
Euler is invariant after reversing time, while fixed-viscosity Navier-Stokes is
sent to the anti-viscous equation, equivalently \(\nu\mapsto-\nu\). The
pressure source
\[
  Q=-\Delta p=\operatorname{tr}(\nabla u)^2
\]
is quadratic in \(\nabla u\), so the pressure Hessian and the \(m=\pm1\) tilt
row are \(\mathcal R\)-even. The Biot-Savart drift is linear in \(u\) or
\(\omega\), so its oriented velocity-side row is \(\mathcal R\)-odd. Therefore
their contraction is \(\mathcal R\)-odd.

This proves a filter:
\[
  \text{purely quadratic / magnitude / scale-covariant bills cannot decide }
  \operatorname{sgn}\sigma .
\]
Any sign theorem for \(\sigma\) must use an \(\mathcal R\)-breaking datum or a
signed retained orientation row. In fixed-viscosity Navier-Stokes, viscosity is
one possible \(\mathcal R\)-breaking mechanism; parity alone does not prove it
has the restoring sign.

The mirror/reflection bookkeeping is also compatible with the earlier B2 gates
after the coordinates are kept separate. Helicity \(u\cdot\omega\) is odd under
an orientation-reversing spatial mirror, but it is even under the global
velocity/time reversal \(u\mapsto -u\). Thus Cycle 7 is right not to fold
helicity into \(\sigma\) by this table.
