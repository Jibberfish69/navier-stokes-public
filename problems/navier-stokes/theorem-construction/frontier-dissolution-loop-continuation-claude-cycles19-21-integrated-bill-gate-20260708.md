Cycle 21's peak-production theorem is accepted for smooth prelimit histories.
From
\[
  D_t|\omega|^2=2\,\omega\cdot S\omega
    +\nu\Delta|\omega|^2-2\nu|\nabla\omega|^2,
\]
the maximum-principle/Rademacher argument gives, at almost every regular time,
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}\|\omega(t)\|_\infty
  \le \alpha(x_{\max}(t),t)\|\omega(t)\|_\infty,
  \qquad
  \alpha=e\cdot Se.
\]
Thus advection, rearrangement, merger language, and diffusion do not themselves
raise the sup norm in a smooth prelimit; stretching at the running maximizer is
the producer. This refutes the loose "merger can produce the peak" objection,
but it is not W7 closure through a critical limit and not W8/W9 payment/action.

The integrated-bill deletion fails at the Riccati accounting step. On the
aligned branch the accepted scalar row has the form
\[
  D_t\alpha=-\alpha^2-e\cdot He+\nu R_\alpha,
  \qquad H=\nabla^2p,
\]
so
\[
  -e\cdot He=D_t\alpha+\alpha^2-\nu R_\alpha.
\]
Integrating gives a pressure-demand lower bound only after controlling the
endpoint term \(\alpha(T)-\alpha(0)\), the viscous residual
\(\int\nu R_\alpha\,dt\), and the exchange times of the running maximizer. Those
terms are not currently bounded by a checked certificate. In the blowup regime
\(\alpha\) is precisely allowed to become large, so the endpoint contribution
cannot be dismissed as a bounded boundary term without proof.

Therefore the chattering/trichotomy issue is demoted but not eliminated. An
integrated bill can be stronger than episode bookkeeping, but it still has to
prove that the same field pays
\[
  \int (-e\cdot He)\,dt
\]
with a coherent \(P_2\) supply cap along the running maximizer, through the
critical graph limit and the selected original-data payer. That theorem is not
installed by the smooth maximum-principle inequality alone.
