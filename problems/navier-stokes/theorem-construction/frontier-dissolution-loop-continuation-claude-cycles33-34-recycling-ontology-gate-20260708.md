## Ontology Audit

The suspect assumption in the buildup is separable stock ownership. A spent
ring, a re-contractor, a roll-up, and a wake are not independent payers inside
Navier-Stokes; they are rows of the same velocity field. Any proof that counts
stock without a closed field current risks double-counting service and charging
loss to a different object than the one delivering service.

The second suspect assumption is discrete episode ownership. A conveyor episode
must be a BV or measure-valued event graph stable under exchange, tilt,
nesting, roll-up, chattering, and weak limits. Without that graph, W7 is still
open and the phrase "per cycle" has no adversarial target.

The third suspect assumption is that viscous wake loss is automatically a
usable lower bound. Global dissipation is nonnegative, but the needed fact is
stronger: conveyor motion must have a dissipation component orthogonal to
service-producing strain and impossible to recover as another internal flux.
That is the exact \(c_*>0\) certificate, not a consequence of naming a wake.

The fourth suspect assumption is the reservoir. \(\Gamma_{\rm gather}(u_0)\)
still needs a precise signed gathering measure, a pedigree surface family, a
folding/cancellation rule, and a Navier-Stokes circulation-diffusion
no-creation statement. Without those, the finite-per-datum log-growth bound is
not a theorem.

The fifth suspect assumption is projection covariance for tilt. First-order
dipole projection may preserve the delivery/expulsion ratio, but next-order
strain curvature, response flow, nested partners, and non-axisymmetric station
trades are not swept by the coaxial computation. These may be dominated, but
they are not deleted without the variational theorem inside the same current.
