differential-form / bicomplex / Hopf-style exploration into one explicit
Navier--Stokes theorem program.

The point is not to add a new slogan.
The point is to say, step by step:

1. what the exact object is,
2. what equation or theorem shape is wanted at that step,
3. what conditional conclusion that step gives,
4. what obstruction remains.

The route described here is:


## Step 1. The visible engine is still the mixed tower

Define the time tower

```math
V_n:=\partial_t^n u,
\qquad
P_n:=\partial_t^n p.
\tag{1}
```

Split the `n`-th time-differentiated Navier--Stokes equation as

```math
L_n:=\nu\Delta V_n-\nabla P_n,
\qquad
M_n:=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}(V_k\cdot\nabla)V_{n-k},
\tag{2}
```

so that

```math
V_{n+1}=L_n-M_n.
\tag{3}
```

Conclusions already supported by the lane:

- the full differentiated tower closes back to NS form;
- factorial/exponential weighting turns the binomial tower into an ordinary
  quadratic product law on the generating series;
- the mixed spread is the exact visible nonlinear burden.

Remaining obstruction:

```math
\boxed{
\text{the tower still appears as a large binomial mixed spread rather than one canonical survivor object.}
}
\tag{4}
```

## Step 2. Recenter on transport and compress the tower to one defect

Let

```math

\qquad
```



```math

=
\nu\Delta U_\alpha
```

Conclusion:

```math

\tag{9}
```
