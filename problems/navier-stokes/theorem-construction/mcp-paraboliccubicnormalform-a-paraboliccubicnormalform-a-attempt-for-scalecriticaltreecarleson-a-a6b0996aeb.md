## Step 1. Quadratic Bellman exposes the right positive density

Let

```math
E_j(t):=\frac12\|\Delta_j u(t)\|_2^2,
\qquad
D_j(t):=\nu\|\nabla\Delta_j u(t)\|_2^2,
\qquad
c_D\nu2^{2j}E_j(t)\le D_j(t)\le C_D\nu2^{2j}E_j(t).
```

Set

```math
\mathcal B_{2,N}(t):=\sum_{j\ge N}2^jE_j(t)^2.
```

The dyadic shell energy identity has the form

```math

\le F_j(t)+L_j(t),
```

where `F_j` is the retained nonlinear source and `L_j` is pressure/cutoff/off-packet legal loss after the accepted local routing.  Multiplying by `2^jE_j` and summing gives

```math

+c\sum_{j\ge N}2^jE_jD_j
\le
\mathcal C_{3,N}(t)+\mathcal L_N(t).
```

Since `c_D\nu2^{2j}E_j\le D_j\le C_D\nu2^{2j}E_j`,

```math
C_D^{-1}\nu^{-1}2^{-j}D_j^2
\le
2^jE_jD_j
\le
c_D^{-1}\nu^{-1}2^{-j}D_j^2.
```

Thus

```math

+c_0\sum_{j\ge N}2^{-j}D_j(t)^2
```

The quadratic Bellman therefore produces the desired active-square density.  The remaining obstruction is the cubic source `\mathcal C_{3,N}`.
