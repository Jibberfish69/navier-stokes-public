# Annular Density Termwise Control Audit

Date: 2026-06-25

## 0. Result

The full gauge-reduced annular derivative density is the same-fluid object, but
termwise absolute control is not a primary proof from current inputs.

The density is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_\phi^\sharp
:={}&
|\nabla\cdot T^\sharp|\,|T^\sharp|\,|\nabla\phi|
+
|U|\,|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal E_P|\,|\nabla\phi|\\
&+
|\nabla\cdot T^\sharp|\,|U|\,|\nabla^2\phi|
+
|\nabla\cdot T^\sharp|\,|\nabla U|\,|\nabla\phi|\\
&+
|U|\,|\nabla U|^2|\nabla\phi|
+
|U|\,|T^\sharp|\,|\nabla U|\,|\nabla\phi|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{TCA.1}
```

Here

```math
D_tU=\nabla\cdot T^\sharp,
\qquad
T^\sharp=-PI+2\nu S(U),
\qquad
\nabla\cdot U=0.
\tag{TCA.2}
```

Every term in `(TCA.1)` is inside the pressure-viscosity-incompressibility-
velocity packet. That preserves participation. The problem is not detachment.
The problem is strength: estimating `(TCA.1)` by absolute values demands exactly
the full material service clock that the route is trying to produce.

## 1. The acceleration-stress term

The first term is

```math
S_1=|\nabla\cdot T^\sharp|\,|T^\sharp|\,|\nabla\phi|.
\tag{TCA.3}
```

Since \(\nabla\cdot T^\sharp=D_tU\), this is material acceleration times stress.
Ordinary energy gives

```math
U\in L_t^\infty L_x^2,
\qquad
\nabla U\in L_{t,x}^2,
\tag{TCA.4}
```

and pressure only at the local energy level. It does not give an unweighted
terminal bound for \(D_tU\) on shrinking heat-scale samples. In stress form,
\(D_tU=\nu\Delta U-\nabla P\), so `(TCA.3)` requires one derivative beyond the
energy dissipation and pressure service on the same annulus.

Therefore \(S_1\) is admissible as a full-clock term, not as legal residual.

## 2. The pressure-time elliptic service term

The second term is

```math
S_2=|U|\,|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal E_P|\,|\nabla\phi|.
\tag{TCA.5}
```

The source \(\mathcal E_P\) is generated by differentiating the pressure law:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_P={}&
\left(\partial_i\partial_kT^\sharp_{jk}
-\partial_iU_k\partial_kU_j\right)\partial_jU_i\\
&+
\partial_iU_j
\left(\partial_j\partial_kT^\sharp_{ik}
-\partial_jU_k\partial_kU_i\right)\\
&-2\partial_iU_k\partial_{ik}P-(\Delta U_k)\partial_kP .
\end{aligned}
\tag{TCA.6}
```

Thus \(S_2\) contains the elliptic response to

```math
\partial^2T^\sharp\,\partial U,
\qquad
(\partial U)^3,
\qquad
\partial U\,\partial^2P,
\qquad
\Delta U\,\nabla P.
\tag{TCA.7}
```

Calderon-Zygmund estimates keep this inside the same pressure tower, but they
do not lower its order. A bound for \(S_2\) is a bound for the pressure-time
service of the material record. That is continuation-strength data unless a
signed same-packet cancellation removes it before absolute values.

So \(S_2\) is the main obstruction to a termwise proof.

## 3. Collar acceleration terms

The third and fourth terms are

```math
S_3=|\nabla\cdot T^\sharp|\,|U|\,|\nabla^2\phi|,
\qquad
S_4=|\nabla\cdot T^\sharp|\,|\nabla U|\,|\nabla\phi|.
\tag{TCA.8}
```

Both contain \(D_tU\). The transported collar keeps them on the same material
annulus; it does not make \(D_tU\) energy-controlled. These terms can be paid by
full material acceleration/tower action, or by a signed integration-by-parts
identity inside the full packet. They cannot be placed into subcritical legal
residuals.

## 4. Cubic gradient term

The fifth term is

```math
S_5=|U|\,|\nabla U|^2|\nabla\phi|.
\tag{TCA.9}
```

This is the cubic transport/strain service term. The local energy class gives
\(U\in L^{10/3}_{t,x}\) and \(\nabla U\in L^2_{t,x}\), but this does not imply

```math
|U|\,|\nabla U|^2\in L^1_{t,x}
\tag{TCA.10}
```

with terminal-scale-uniform control on the shrinking annular family. The
missing factor is exactly higher integrability or a strain-clock bound. In the
material picture, \(S_5\) is the positive deformation service that records the
spike-wave's thinning/stretching. It is not a fake obstruction, but it is also
not a lower-order remainder.

## 5. Stress-gradient transport term

The sixth term is

```math
S_6=|U|\,|T^\sharp|\,|\nabla U|\,|\nabla\phi|.
\tag{TCA.11}
```

Splitting \(T^\sharp=-PI+2\nu S(U)\), this contains both pressure-transport and
viscous-strain transport. As with \(S_5\), the standard local-energy exponents
are below the threshold for a scale-uniform terminal bound. This term belongs
inside the same full material action clock.

## 6. Exact conclusion

The termwise absolute-value route proves the following reduction:

```math
\int \mathcal S_\phi^\sharp<\infty
\quad\Longleftarrow\quad
\text{finite full material acceleration/pressure-time/cubic-gradient clock}.
\tag{TCA.12}
```

That is not a proof of the full clock from original data. It is the full clock
written in annular-density coordinates.

The noncircular alternatives are therefore exactly two:

```math
\text{keep }F_\phi'\text{ in a signed same-packet identity and use cancellation before }|\cdot|,
\tag{TCA.13}
```

or

```math
\text{prove a global positive material-action clock controlling }S_1+\cdots+S_6.
\tag{TCA.14}
```

This audit refutes a detached termwise estimate. It does not refute the physical
route. It confirms the physical route's demand: the spike-wave's annular return,
pressure-time service, and cubic strain transport must be controlled as one
coupled material participation law, not one component at a time.
