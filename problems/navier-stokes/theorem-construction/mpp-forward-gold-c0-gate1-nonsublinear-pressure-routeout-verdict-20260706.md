# Gate 1 non-sublinear pressure route-out verdict

## 1. Physical question

The remaining route-out theorem asks whether a persistent pressure collar tail
is automatically outside the retained relay record:

```math
\texttt{NonSublinearOwnedPressureCollarIsRouteOut.A}.
\tag{NPR.1}
```

The pressure tail is still generated by the same incompressible velocity field.
The question is not source ownership. The question is whether a same-carrier
pressure flux that keeps crossing every enlarged material collar must break the
retained packet, terminal participation, or Field coherence.

## 2. What route-out must mean

The allowed participation source keeps the face meanings separate:

1. `Pack` is usable incompressible packing / control-volume structure.
2. `Part` is the shared pressure-viscosity carrier law, with a stronger terminal
   reading when readout control is retained.
3. `Field` is neighboring tower coherence through finite differences.

So a pressure collar tail is route-out only after one of the following is
proved:

```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Pack:} & \text{the finite retained control-volume/collar structure fails},\\[1mm]
\text{Part:} & \text{the pressure-viscosity carrier is not terminally retained},\\[1mm]
\text{Field:} & \text{neighboring tower coherence fails at the boundary},\\[1mm]
\text{legal:} & \text{the collar flux is covered by the legal flux/cutoff theorem.}
\end{array}
\tag{NPR.2}
```

The source does not let us call a surviving boundary term route-out merely
because it is inconvenient for compactness.

## 3. Direct route-out test

Let

```math
\mu^{press}_{N,h,W}(\operatorname{collar}(R))
=
\int_W\int_{\operatorname{collar}(R)}
\sum_{k=0}^{N}\lambda_k|\Pi^{press}_{k,h}|\,dx\,dt .
\tag{NPR.3}
```

The non-sublinear branch is

```math
\limsup_{R\to\infty}\limsup_j
{1\over R}\,
\mu^{press}_{N,h_j,W_j}(\operatorname{collar}(R))
>0 .
\tag{NPR.4}
```

This does not by itself prove a Pack face failure. The common flow map can still
exist, and finite-window usability can still hold on every fixed window while a
pressure tail persists farther out in the exhaustion.

It does not by itself prove a Part face failure. The pressure remains the same
incompressible pressure correction and the same pressure-viscosity carrier law
may still be the written tower law. Terminal `Part` would fail only after the
tail is shown to defeat the retained readout/control needed by the witness.

It does not by itself prove a Field face failure. The localized estimate records
the pressure contribution as a flux functional, and says that this flux must be
legal or absorbable before localized closure is usable. The absolute collar flux
is not identical to neighboring-tower decoherence.

Therefore `(NPR.4)' is not automatically route-out under the allowed source
surface.

## 4. Proved finite-exhaustion sublemma

There is one clean route-out statement that is already provable.

**Lemma.** Suppose a retained relay pressure flux measure has fixed finite
material support after the legal recentering: there is \(R_0<\infty\) such that
the pressure flux measure assigned to the retained relay record is supported in
the retained material core \(K_{R_0}\). Let the collars
\(\operatorname{collar}(R)\) be disjoint from \(K_{R_0}\) for all sufficiently
large \(R\). Then

```math
\mu^{press}_{N,h,W}(\operatorname{collar}(R))=0
\qquad
\text{for all large }R,
\tag{NPR.5}
```

and hence

```math
\lim_{R\to\infty}
{1\over R}\,
\mu^{press}_{N,h,W}(\operatorname{collar}(R))
=0 .
\tag{NPR.6}
```

**Proof.** For all large \(R\), the collar is disjoint from the support of the
retained relay pressure flux measure. The measure of the collar is therefore
zero. Dividing by \(R\) gives `(NPR.6)'. QED.

Physical meaning: a pressure contribution truly retained inside a fixed finite
material packet is eventually invisible to farther collars. A non-sublinear
collar tail is therefore not a finite-support retained pressure record.

## 5. Missing bridge

The lemma does not prove `(NPR.1)' because retained relay windows in the active
compactness theorem are not already known to have fixed finite pressure-flux
support after recentering. A non-sublinear pressure tail has two possible
readings:

```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{tail route-out:} &
\text{the record has escaped the finite retained packet/collar},\\[1mm]
\text{retained pressure tail:} &
\text{the same parent carries pressure flux through every enlarged collar.}
\end{array}
\tag{NPR.7}
```

The allowed sources do not choose between these readings.

The exact missing theorem is:

```math
\texttt{RetainedPressureTailExhaustionToFaceFailure.A}.
\tag{NPR.8}
```

Statement. For every retained same-fluid relay sequence with \(R(W_j)=1\) and
\(B(W_j)\to0\), any owned pressure collar route measure satisfying `(NPR.4)'
either is not a retained relay record or produces a concrete Pack, terminal
Part, Field, or legal collar face under the source meanings in `(NPR.2)'.

## 6. Consequence for the active c_0 goal

The route-out half of the Gate 1 split is not installed. The first exact failed
gate remains the pressure collar:

```math
\texttt{OwnedPressureCollarCarlesonSublinear.A}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\texttt{RetainedPressureTailExhaustionToFaceFailure.A}.
\tag{NPR.9}
```

Proving either theorem would clear the pressure-collar obstruction left after
the material-cutoff and viscous-collar reductions. Neither follows from the
allowed sources as currently written.
