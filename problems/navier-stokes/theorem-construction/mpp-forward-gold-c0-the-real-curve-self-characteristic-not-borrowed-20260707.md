# The curve we are actually after: the record carrier's self-characteristic

Physics-first. No borrowed curves. This describes the actual object; it closes
nothing.

## 1. The curve, in the fluid's own terms

There is a material carrier — the sharpening concentration / root that holds the
record. The curve is its **worldline as it descends its own participation tower**.
Its defining feature, which the borrowed curves discard, is **no external
reference**:

- **Its own source.** The strain that pulls and sharpens the carrier is generated
  *by the carrier itself*, reaching back through incompressible participation.
- **Its own clock.** Its only timescale is its own turnover.
- **One-way.** Viscosity makes each descent step irreversible.

A **self-sourced, self-clocked, irreversible descent** — a self-characteristic.

## 2. Its defining law (read from the fluid, not imported)

The stretching rate `α` the carrier feels along its own worldline obeys the
participation-constrained Riccati
```math
\frac{D\alpha}{Dt} \;=\; -\,\alpha^2 \;-\; \Pi \;+\; \nu(\cdots),
```
- `−α²`: the carrier stretching itself (self-amplification);
- `−Π`: the **non-local participation-Hessian** subtraction — the field keeping the
  carrier a coherent participant;
- `+ν(…)`: the irreversible payment.

The curve is the integral curve of this. Every term is the carrier acting on
itself. `c_0 > 0 ⟺` `Π`'s net subtraction along the curve is strictly positive per
unit climb.

## 3. Why the borrowed curves were only shadows

Both are the **degenerate external-reference versions** of this one:

- **Radiodrome** externalizes the *target*: it treats the sharpening spin as a
  moving target chased by the payer. But the spin is *self-generated* — so it is
  self-pursuit, and the "lag" is `Π` misread as a chase. That is why its leading
  order is covariant/marginal: it dropped the self.
- **Brachistochrone** externalizes the *potential*: least-time descent through a
  fixed landscape. But the landscape is *self-generated*, with no fixed endpoints —
  self-descent. Marginal for the same reason.

The self-reference `Π` **is** the participation Hessian. Both curves pretend the
fluid's self-action is an outside agent; the real curve keeps it inside.

## 4. The wall, in the object's own voice

The self-similar profile is the **fixed point of the self-reference**: its descent
reproduces itself at every scale. So on the actual curve,
```math
\boxed{\ c_0 > 0 \iff \text{the self-sourced irreversible descent CANNOT lock onto its own fixed point (always overpays }\Pi\text{ vs the marginal self-similar rate).}\ }
```
Not a pursuit, not a variational optimum — a **rigidity of a self-referential
irreversible curve**: it cannot sit on its own scale-invariant fixed point. That is
route-b (backward-DSS exclusion), stated in the object's own terms.
