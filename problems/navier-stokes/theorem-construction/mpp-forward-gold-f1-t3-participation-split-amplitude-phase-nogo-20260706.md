# t3: participation = amplitude x phase; the static route is a no-go

## N1. The static no-go (exact)

"Mass at good angles implies monomial participation" is false as a
static statement. The monomial is a trilinear pairing; a trilinear
convolution can vanish identically by phase cancellation even when
every factor's modulus fully populates the good-angle region —
multiply one packet's Fourier transform by a phase tuned against
its partners. No static lower bound on \(|M|\) from amplitude
population alone can exist. This closes the naive proof class and
locates the true content of t3.

## N2. The split

\[
  \text{participation}
  = \text{amplitude population} \times \text{phase population}.
\]

**Amplitude half — paid (pending gate).** The Bernstein packet
lemma with the essential-support variant: mass fraction
\(\ge 1/2\) at angle \(\ge \theta_*^{\rm ess} = \pi/(14a^3)\) from
every collinear plane; G-ratio floor on the populated shape class.

**Phase half — installed.** The campaign already refuses static
phases: the triad phase is dynamical, and the installed machinery
prices its motion. The un-parking law — extraction of fraction
\(f\) moves the separatrix distance to \(\ge f\) regardless of
prior tuning; parking survives only in the band
\(K \in [\pi/2, \tfrac12\ln(16/f)]\) — forbids dwelling at
cancellation poses on financed windows. The finite-chain F1
population was closed by exactly this route: the paid pushforward
at the angle-from-coupling row.

## N3. Regated lift request

The original "domain match" formulation is superseded by the Codex
domain-match audit. The paid pushforward acts on active monomial mass;
the Bernstein lemma acts on packet-leg amplitude mass. Fable's static
no-go is exactly the separator.

The remaining t3 bridge now has two finite routes:

1. bad-cone capacity is smaller than financed active monomial, forcing
   good-angle active monomial participation;
2. predator-slot phase sweep gives an oriented time-integrated lower
   bound for \((\operatorname{Re}M_{\rm pred})_+\) on climbing windows.
