# Full Participation Material-Tower No-Exit Direct Attempt

Date: 2026-06-22

## 0. Target

This note tests the strongest possible same-packet no-exit idea:

```math
\boxed{
\text{the full pressure-viscosity-incompressibility-velocity law}
\Longrightarrow
\text{terminal same-packet record survival.}
}
\tag{FMT.1}
```

The test keeps the packet coupled.  No pressure-only, viscosity-only,
incompressibility-only, strain-only, or collar-only estimate is allowed to stand
as the route.

## 1. Exact material pullback

Let \(X(a,t)\) be the material map and define

```math
v(a,t)=u(X(a,t),t),
\qquad
q(a,t)=p(X(a,t),t),
\tag{FMT.2}
```

with

```math
F=D_aX,
\qquad
A=F^{-1},
\qquad
G=A A^\top .
\tag{FMT.3}
```

Since \(u\) is incompressible,

```math
\det F=1.
\tag{FMT.4}
```

The Navier--Stokes equation pulls back exactly to

```math
\partial_t v
+
A^\top\nabla_a q
=
\nu\,\operatorname{div}_a(G\nabla_a v),
\tag{FMT.5}
```

and the divergence constraint becomes

```math
\operatorname{div}_a(A v)=0.
\tag{FMT.6}
```

This is the full participation law in material coordinates.  Transport has not
been discarded; it has been absorbed into the moving coordinate system.  The
pressure, viscosity, incompressibility, and velocity channels still move
together.

## 2. Base energy is exact but not enough

Testing `(FMT.5)` against \(v\) and using `(FMT.6)` gives the pulled-back
energy identity

```math
{1\over2}{d\over dt}\int |v|^2\,da
+
\nu\int G\nabla_a v:\nabla_a v\,da
=0
\tag{FMT.7}
```

on a closed material packet or after the already typed collar terms are kept in
the same packet.

The coercive part depends on \(G\).  Uniform ellipticity of \(G\) is exactly a
bounded material deformation statement:

```math
\lambda |\xi|^2
\le
\xi^\top G(a,t)\xi
\le
\Lambda |\xi|^2.
\tag{FMT.8}
```

The base identity therefore controls the material gradient only after the same
carrier deformation gauge is retained.  It does not create that gauge.

## 3. Differentiated material tower

For \(|\alpha|\le N\), set

```math
V_\alpha=D_a^\alpha v,
\qquad
Q_\alpha=D_a^\alpha q.
\tag{FMT.9}
```

Differentiating `(FMT.5)` gives

```math
\partial_t V_\alpha
+
A^\top\nabla_a Q_\alpha
-
\nu\,\operatorname{div}_a(G\nabla_a V_\alpha)
=
\mathcal C_\alpha^{mat},
\tag{FMT.10}
```

where the commutator is exactly

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_\alpha^{mat}
= {}&
-
\sum_{0<\beta\le\alpha}
{\alpha\choose\beta}
\left(D_a^\beta A^\top\right)
\nabla_a D_a^{\alpha-\beta}q  \\
&+
\nu
\sum_{0<\beta\le\alpha}
{\alpha\choose\beta}
\operatorname{div}_a
\left(
\left(D_a^\beta G\right)
\nabla_aD_a^{\alpha-\beta}v
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{FMT.11}
```

The differentiated incompressibility constraint is

```math
\operatorname{div}_a(A V_\alpha)
=
-
\sum_{0<\beta\le\alpha}
{\alpha\choose\beta}
\operatorname{div}_a
\left(
(D_a^\beta A)D_a^{\alpha-\beta}v
\right).
\tag{FMT.12}
```

So the material derivative tower is coupled exactly to the coefficient tower
\(D_a^\beta A\) and \(D_a^\beta G\).  Factorial weights can absorb the binomial
splitting in `(FMT.11)`--`(FMT.12)`, but they do not bound the coefficients.

## 4. Coefficient tower is the same packet record

The coefficient tower is generated by the same velocity gradient history.  The
first rung is

```math
\partial_tF
=
(\nabla_xu)(X(a,t),t)F,
\tag{FMT.13}
```

and therefore

```math
\partial_tA
=
-A(\nabla_xu)(X(a,t),t).
\tag{FMT.14}
```

Higher \(a\)-derivatives of \(A\) and \(G\) contain transported spatial
derivatives of \(u\) and products of lower coefficient rungs.  Thus the
closure condition for `(FMT.10)` is a finite same-carrier material coefficient
and velocity-pressure tower.  One explicit sufficient norm is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak P_N^{mat}(t)
:={}&
\sum_{|\beta|\le N+1}
\left(
\|D_a^\beta A(t)\|_{L^\infty(A_\sharp)}
+
\|D_a^\beta G(t)\|_{L^\infty(A_\sharp)}
\right)\\
&+
\sum_{|\alpha|\le N}
\left(
\|D_a^\alpha v(t)\|_{H^1(A_\sharp)}
+
\|D_a^\alpha q(t)\|_{H^1(A_\sharp)}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{FMT.15}
```

The required record is

```math
\sup_{t<T_*}
\mathfrak P_N^{mat}(t)
<\infty.
\tag{FMT.16}
```

This is stronger than a minimal packet topology, but it is concrete and it
closes the displayed commutator identity.  It is also exactly the kind of
finite-depth same-carrier record being tested.  In the older notation, it is
the material form of
\(Part_{N,Q}\).

## 5. Why this does not prove no-exit

The full material packet identity preserves participation exactly, but it does
not prove the terminal record survives.  The attempted proof would need to use
`(FMT.16)` to control the commutators in `(FMT.11)`--`(FMT.12)`, then conclude
`(FMT.16)`.  That is circular unless a separate coercive estimate produces the
coefficient/tower bound from data already known before packet admission.

The heat-scale calculation in the material-clock note shows why physical
energy is not that estimate:

```math
\text{material strain-metric clock per heat-scale packet}\simeq 1,
\qquad
\text{physical dissipation cost}\simeq r.
\tag{FMT.17}
```

Thus a dyadic terminal stack can remain finite for physical energy while the
material strain-metric/coefficient record diverges.
