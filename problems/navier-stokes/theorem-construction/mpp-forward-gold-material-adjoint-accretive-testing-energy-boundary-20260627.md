# Material adjoint accretive testing data: energy boundary

## 1. Target

The previous note reduced the stopped BMO producer to construction of local testing functions \(b_P\) for the stopped same-material transform \(T_S\). The needed bounds are

```math
|\langle b_P\rangle_P|\ge c,
\qquad
\|b_P\|_{L^2(P,\mathcal R)}^2\le C\mathcal R(P),
\tag{1}
```

and

```math
\|T_S(b_P\nu^S)\|_{L^2(P,\mathcal R)}^2
\le C\mathcal R(P)+C R_{legal}(P),
\tag{2}
```

with testing defects Carleson over stopped subtrees:

```math
\sum_{P\subseteq P_0} Def_{test}(P)
\le C\mathcal R(P_0)+C R_{legal}(P_0).
\tag{3}
```

## 2. Direct adjoint construction

A natural construction is to solve a stopped material adjoint problem on \(P\subseteq S\):

```math
\mathcal L_A^*\Phi_P=g_P,
\qquad
\Phi_P|_{\partial P}=0,
```

where \(g_P\) is a normalized selector-compatible testing source, and then set schematically

```math
b_P=1+\mathcal A_P\Phi_P,
```

where \(\mathcal A_P\) is the relevant pressure-Helmholtz--Leray / material testing functional.

The hope is that elliptic energy gives

```math
\int_P |\nabla_A\Phi_P|^2\,d\mathcal R
\lesssim \mathcal R(P),
\tag{4}
```

and that the adjoint identity supplies cancellation:

```math
\langle \nu^S,b_P\rangle_P
= \text{root/stop/legal terms}.
\tag{5}
```

## 3. What closes formally

If the adjoint tests satisfy the nondegenerate mean bound

```math

\tag{6}
```

```math
+
```

## 4. Direct obstruction

Adjoint solvability gives a function \(\Phi_P\), but it does not by itself give accretivity `(6)`. The correction term \(\mathcal A_P\Phi_P\) can cancel the mean of \(1\), or concentrate so strongly on the selected carrier that the \(L^2\) size is controlled only by the same selected amplification tail being estimated.

Similarly, elliptic energy `(4)` controls the adjoint solution in a Dirichlet energy norm, but the stopped transform testing bound `(2)` is stronger. It needs compatibility between:

```math
\text{adjoint energy},\quad
\text{selector measure }\nu^S,\quad
\text{stopped transform }T_S,\quad
\text{material geometry }\mathcal R.
```

That compatibility is not a formal consequence of solving \(\mathcal L_A^*\Phi_P=g_P\).

Thus the direct proof fails at exactly two points:

1. nondegenerate mean/accretivity of the adjoint test;
2. Carleson packing of adjoint testing defects.
