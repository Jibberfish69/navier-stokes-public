## 1. Exact strain equation

Let

```math
D_t=\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla,
\qquad
B=\nabla u,
\qquad
S={B+B^T\over2},
\qquad
\Omega={B-B^T\over2}.
\tag{SE.2}
```

Differentiating Navier-Stokes gives

```math
D_tB+B^2+\nabla^2p=\nu\Delta B.
\tag{SE.3}
```

Taking the symmetric part and using

```math
\operatorname{sym}(B^2)=S^2+\Omega^2
\tag{SE.4}
```

gives the exact material strain equation

```math
D_tS+S^2+\Omega^2+\nabla^2p=\nu\Delta S.
\tag{SE.5}
```

So every positive material stretching event is generated by the same coupled
packet:

```math
\text{strain square}
+\text{rotation/vorticity geometry}
+\text{pressure Hessian}
+\text{viscous diffusion}.
\tag{SE.6}
```

There is no free external signal.

## 2. Material line growth

For a material line element `ell(t)` obeying

```math
{d\over dt}\ell=B\ell,
\tag{SE.7}
```

the rotation part drops out:

```math
{1\over2}{d\over dt}|\ell|^2=\ell\cdot S\ell.
\tag{SE.8}
```

Thus a rising/thinning spike is measured by positive strain along material
directions:

```math
{d\over dt}\log|\ell|
={\ell\cdot S\ell\over |\ell|^2}.
\tag{SE.9}
```

This is the physical annular shape law.  A spike that rises by stretching a
material direction and thinning transverse directions must pay through this same
strain record.  The record is not detached from the annulus.

## 3. Eigenvalue growth law

Let `lambda_max(S)` be the largest eigenvalue of `S`.  At a smooth point where
`lambda_max` is simple, with unit eigenvector `e`, equation `(SE.5)` gives

```math
D_t\lambda_{max}
+\langle e,S^2e\rangle
+\langle e,\Omega^2e\rangle
+\langle e,\nabla^2p\,e\rangle
=\nu\langle e,\Delta S\,e\rangle.
\tag{SE.10}
```

At a spatial maximum of `lambda_max`, the viscosity term has the favorable
viscosity-sign in the viscosity-solution sense:

```math
\nu\langle e,\Delta S\,e\rangle\le0.
\tag{SE.11}
```

Also

```math
\langle e,S^2e\rangle=\lambda_{max}^2.
\tag{SE.12}
```

Since `Omega` is skew-symmetric, `Omega^2` is nonpositive, so

```math
-\langle e,\Omega^2e\rangle=|\Omega e|^2\ge0.
\tag{SE.13}
```

Therefore the top-strain growth inequality has the schematic exact content

```math
D_t\lambda_{max}
\le
-\lambda_{max}^2
+|\Omega e|^2
-\langle e,\nabla^2p\,e\rangle.
\tag{SE.14}
```

The pressure Hessian term has no scalar sign, and the rotation/vorticity term
can feed the positive top-strain direction.  This is not a fake obstruction; it
is the exact pressure-incompressibility-velocity coupling of the same material
packet.

## 4. What incompressibility gives and does not give

Incompressibility gives

```math
\operatorname{tr}S=0.
\tag{SE.15}
```

It also ties pressure to velocity by

```math
-\Delta p=\partial_i u_j\,\partial_j u_i.
\tag{SE.16}
```

Equivalently,

```math
-\Delta p=\operatorname{tr}(S^2)+\operatorname{tr}(\Omega^2)
=|S|^2-|\omega|^2/2
\tag{SE.17}
```

with the usual vorticity normalization.  This controls the trace/source of the
pressure equation, but it does not give a one-sided bound for the directional
quantity

```math
\langle e,\nabla^2p\,e\rangle.
\tag{SE.18}
```

That directional pressure Hessian is exactly how incompressibility redistributes
strain between directions and annular layers.
