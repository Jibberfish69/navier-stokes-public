## 2. Transport-action participation

The tested idea was that a speed-limit or transport-action lower bound should
force a terminal packet to pay for concentrating at the center.

At fixed positive radius this is correct.  If a physical packet has radius
\(r\ge r_0>0\), the physical action and normalized action are comparable.

At heat scale, the cost remains radius-discounted.  A packet with
\(|u|\sim r^{-1}\) on volume \(r^3\) has physical kinetic energy

\[
E_r\simeq r.
\]

Moving that physical energy a distance \(r\) over time \(r^2\) costs

\[
E_r\,{r^2\over r^2}\simeq r.
\]

So a Zeno chain can have finite physical action

\[
\sum_j r_j<\infty
\]

while retaining one order-one normalized event per scale.  Transport-action
therefore supports the fixed-radius participation throttle but does not remove
the heat-scale radius discount.
