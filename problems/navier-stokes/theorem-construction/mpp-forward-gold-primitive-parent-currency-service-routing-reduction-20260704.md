# Primitive parent-currency service routing reduction

## 1. Physical object

Work with one primitive signed-channel event in one admitted stopped chamber:

\[
z=(\sigma,t,x,\kappa),
\qquad
d\mu_{\kappa,C}=|Y_{\kappa,C}|\,d\sigma dt .
\tag{PCSR.1}
\]

The parent-currency face theorem tries to place this same event in one of three
parent rows before the proof spends its sign:

\[
\text{root/log face},
\qquad
\text{endpoint seed face},
\qquad
\text{active full-exchange face}.
\tag{PCSR.2}
\]

Service routing is the complementary physical statement. If \(z\) is not
lawfully owned by one of these rows, the proof cannot put it into a generic
"paid" bin. The same Navier-Stokes packet must show which physical mechanism
took responsibility for the event before selected readout.

There are three mechanisms at this level:

1. the material chart or exact pressure-Hodge projector failed, transferred, or
   generated paid projector service;
2. the pressure detector and selector-announced signed channel failed to align,
   or a detector-silent / near-silent component became same-packet service;
3. the event left the parent-currency face through boundary, collar, cutoff,
   face/selector/address motion, legal loss, stopping, or class exit.

These are physical alternatives for one evolving velocity field. Transport
moves the packet through the material chart, pressure and incompressibility
force the projector/detector readout, viscosity and collars provide real service
slots, and sign/alignment decide whether the work is face-owned or routed out.

\tag{PCSR.3}

\Longrightarrow

\]

\[

The installed nonselector-coordinate reduction lowers this to pressure-active

The remaining lower face is:

\[
\texttt{PrimitiveParentCurrencyOffFaceServiceExhaustionOrExit.A}.
\tag{PCSR.7}
\]

This is the same-event exhaustion statement for the leftover material routes.
It says that any primitive chamber event not captured by parent-currency face
ownership, chart/projector routing, or detector/channel routing is already
charged as same-packet boundary/collar/cutoff service, selector or address
motion, local face-trace boundary service, legal loss, stop, or Pack/Part/Field

The current source evidence for `(PCSR.7)' is the material-channelization
partition and address-change charge split:

\[
\begin{aligned}

This is still open. The first input partitions the primitive signed-channel
measure into same-material events and paid off-carrier residue before readout.
The second input pays the selector, chart/projector, detector/channel, and
face-trace motions once the primitive address process exists. Together they
would make the off-face service exhaustive for the same event.

## 3. Strict reduction

Assume `(PCSR.3)', `(PCSR.5)', and `(PCSR.7)'. Let \(z\) be an unpaid primitive
event that is not owned by the root/log, endpoint seed, or active full-exchange
row.

If the failure is that the material chart or exact projector does not lawfully
carry \(z\), `(PCSR.3)' routes it to paid chart/projector service or exit.

If the failure is that the pressure-visible detector coordinate does not match
the selector-announced channel, or that the relevant stress is detector-silent,
`(PCSR.5)' routes it to paid detector/channel service or exit.

If neither coordinate failure accounts for the event, then \(z\) is a leftover
same-material off-face event: boundary, collar, cutoff, selector/address motion,
face-trace motion, legal loss, stop, or exit. By `(PCSR.7)' it is already paid
or removed before selected readout.

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{PrimitiveStoppedChamberChartProjectorLiftOrPaidExit.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveStoppedChamberDetectorChannelLiftOrPaidService.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveParentCurrencyOffFaceServiceExhaustionOrExit.A}
\end{aligned}
\Longrightarrow
\texttt{PrimitiveParentCurrencyServiceRoutingOrExit.A}.
\tag{PCSR.9}
\]

Combining `(PCSR.4)', `(PCSR.6)', and `(PCSR.8)' gives the fully exposed lower
form:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{PrimitiveStoppedChamberC11MetricTowerOrExit.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveStoppedChamberHigherProjectorServiceOrExit.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveStoppedChamberPressureActiveAngleLockOrPaidRotation.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveStoppedChamberDetectorSilentReserveOrPaidService.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveSignedChannelPreReadoutMaterialChannelizationPartition.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveChannelPreReturnAddressChangeCharge.A}
\end{aligned}
\Longrightarrow
\texttt{PrimitiveParentCurrencyServiceRoutingOrExit.A}.
\tag{PCSR.10}
\]

## 4. Checked obstruction

The detector-silent reserve audit shows why local service routing is not enough.
The silent part of pressure work does have a same-packet physical destination:
strain/exchange, complete-frame compression, viscous/radius loss, collar or
annular service, legal loss, stop, or exit. But the half-tail model allows
finite local service while the record-weighted selected service diverges.

Thus detector-silent routing is only proof-grade after an independent primitive
full-action reserve, strict half-barrier, or bounded-below same-material
Lyapunov law. It cannot be obtained by cycling through record-edge channel
admission.

The same warning applies to `(PCSR.7)'. Naming boundary, collar, cutoff, and
address motion is not a proof of finite service. The proof still needs the
pre-readout material partition and address-change exhaustion to attach those
payments to the original primitive event.
