# Production-extremizer pressure-Hessian frame-rotation test

## 1. Referent

The pose is the parked, aligned, rigid cascade profile: delivery is locally
maximal, channel motion is minimized, and the enemy is trying to sit at the
turning point long enough for the static supremum to become a time-averaged
trajectory.

In the normal-form relay notes this is the parked return-race profile. In the
continuum test it is an explicit Navier-Stokes field that maximizes instantaneous
production per unit dissipation, or an explicit finite-time enstrophy-amplifying
extremal, after the normalization chosen for the retention constant.

## 2. Proved model mechanism

The relay-drift lock proves the model mechanism: in the triad phase equations,
relay transfer and phase/frequency drift use the same interaction coefficient.
An active relay changes amplitudes; amplitude change moves the twist balance;
therefore the alignment constraint has nonzero second Lie derivative. The model
statement is:

\[
\text{active relay} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{normal escape from the aligned pose}.
\tag{PER.1}
\]

This is the line-2 tax at normal-form level. It is not yet the continuum
retention theorem.

## 3. Continuum shadow test

Choose one explicit production-optimal Navier-Stokes field \(u_\ast\) from the
external extremizer literature, normalized to the unit-Reynolds collar scale.
Let

\[
S_\ast=\frac12(\nabla u_\ast+\nabla u_\ast^T),
\qquad
-\Delta p_\ast=\partial_i u_{\ast j}\partial_j u_{\ast i},
\tag{PER.2}
\]

and let \(P_\ast\) be the spectral projector onto the production eigendirection
or eigenspace selected by the extremal pose.

The first continuum check is:

\[
\left[(I-P_\ast)\,D_t P_\ast\right]_{\rm prod}
\ne 0,
\tag{PER.3}
\]

where \(D_t=\partial_t+u_\ast\cdot\nabla\) is evaluated using the actual
Navier-Stokes vector field at \(u_\ast\), including the pressure Hessian
\(\nabla^2p_\ast\) and viscosity. The measured component must be the same
production/retention channel used by the retention constant, not a free
Eulerian frame motion.

Equivalent sign-level wording:

\[
\text{the pose's own production rotates the pose's own frame}.
\tag{PER.4}
\]

The required coupling check is that the nonzero term in (PER.3) is carried by
the same quadratic interaction that gives the positive production at \(u_\ast\).

## 4. What counts as a positive result

For one verified production extremizer \(u_\ast\), prove an identity or strict
sign statement of the form

\[
\mathcal T_{\rm frame}(u_\ast)
\ge c_\ast\,\mathcal P_{\rm prod}(u_\ast),
\qquad c_\ast>0,
\tag{PER.5}
\]

where \(\mathcal P_{\rm prod}\) is the production/retention functional and
\(\mathcal T_{\rm frame}\) is the same-packet pressure-Hessian/eigenframe
rotation tax.

This is not the MPP. It is one-pose evidence that the normal-form
transfer-powers-drift mechanism survives in the real field.

## 5. What counts as a negative result

The route fails at this door if the explicit production extremizer satisfies

\[
\left[(I-P_\ast)\,D_tP_\ast\right]_{\rm prod}=0
\tag{PER.6}
\]

or if the nonzero frame motion is generated only by an unrelated gauge,
selector, boundary, or coordinate artifact rather than by the same coupling
that produces the stretching.

In that case the relay-drift lock remains a normal-form/chain fact and should
not be promoted as the continuum mechanism deciding the retention constant.

## 6. Open inputs

1. Select and verify the exact external extremizer formula or numerical field.
2. Normalize it to the unit-Reynolds collar family used by the retention
   constant.
3. Compute \(\nabla^2p_\ast\), \(D_tS_\ast\), and \(D_tP_\ast\) from the real
   Navier-Stokes vector field.
4. Project the result onto the same production/retention channel.
5. Decide (PER.5) or (PER.6).

This is the smallest continuum bridge currently exposed by the transversality
lane: one pose, one first-variation frame tax, one yes-or-no structural result.
