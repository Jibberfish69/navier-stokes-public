# The rate gap starves the market it protects

## 1. The observation (from Codex's own display)

The bottom-horn rate

\[
  R_{\rm horn}(m,B)
  =
  \frac{\alpha\,A\sqrt B}{C_{\rm per}\,K(A/B)}
\]

is independent of \(r_-\) — Codex's Section 2, verbatim. The horn
construction (\(r_-=\varepsilon\), \(A,B\) fixed) is therefore
available at EVERY \((m,B,\alpha)\) in the compact domain,
including the exact parameters of every financed cell:

\[
  \Pi(\mathcal H_0)\ \supseteq\ \Pi(\text{financed family}),
\]

where \(\Pi\) projects onto \((m,B,\alpha)\).

## 2. The incompatibility

Suppose the rate gap holds:
\(\sup_{\mathcal H_0}R_{\rm horn}<r_0(f)\tau_{\min}\). Then every
\((m,B,\alpha)\) in the shared projection has rate strictly below
the floor — so EVERY periodic Gate-6 cell over the financed domain,
degenerate or not, is sub-financed in the same currency, and the
financed family is EMPTY there. The product row's own producer
\(\Phi_0=G_{\mathfrak e}^{\rm int}>0\) dies with it.

Conversely, a nonempty financed family contains a cell with
\(R\ge\) floor; the horn built at its parameters is financed with
\(r_-\downarrow0\) — no gap.

\[
  \boxed{\ \text{rate gap}\ \wedge\ \text{nonempty financed family}
  \ =\ \text{impossible on the shared domain}\ }
\]

Honest caveat: the incompatibility assumes the financed-family
definition does not constrain \(r_-\) or \(G_0^2\) directly — which
is exactly the current source state, and the absence of such a
constraint WAS the original obstruction.
