# Record-face primitive-channel trace-return budget reduction

## 1. Physical object

Fix one retained positive record face \(F_e\) in one smooth Navier-Stokes
velocity field after the record edge has been admitted to a stopped selector
primitive channel \(\kappa_e\). The face is the same material packet: high
carrier, low pressure/strain/current coefficient, transported collar, detector
branch, chart, parent address, sign, scale, and time window.

The primitive PLS decomposition supplies a finite family of signed channel
densities \(Y_{\kappa,S}\) generated by pressure, transport/frame motion,
viscosity, incompressibility, material collar motion, and legal/stopped error
terms on the stopped chamber.

The remaining question is not whether that chamber has a signed balance. It is
whether the exact positive record face is already visible to that primitive
channel before the positive readout, so that the physical signed work on the
face is

\[
dJ_e:=\mathbf 1_{F_e}Y_{\kappa_e,S}\,d\sigma dt .
\tag{TRB.1}
\]

The negative return needed later is then

\[
dJ_e^-=\mathbf 1_{F_e}[Y_{\kappa_e,S}]_-\,d\sigma dt,
\tag{TRB.2}
\]

the same channel returning on the same admitted face. It is not a total
negative-variation account for the whole chamber.

## 2. The two nonduplicate jobs

`RecordFacePrimitiveChannelTraceAndReturnBudget.A` splits into two physical
theorems.

First, the face has to be a lawful pre-readout trace:

\[
\texttt{RecordFacePrimitiveChannelPredictableTrace.A}.
\tag{TRB.3}
\]

This says \(F_e\) is measurable in the stopped selector/primitive-channel
sigma-algebra before positive record readout, and same-edge no-detachment plus
local face thickness keep that face attached to the same packet under limiting
or stopping operations.

Second, the negative part on that traced face has to be budgeted from original
history:

\[
\texttt{PrimitiveChannelSelectedFaceNegativeReturnBudget.A}.
\tag{TRB.4}
\]

This says

\[
\sum_e\int \mathbf 1_{F_e}[Y_{\kappa_e,S}]_-\,d\sigma dt
\le
C_N(u_0)+Paid+Legal+Stop+Exit+Err .
\tag{TRB.5}
\]

This is the same physical return requirement isolated in the repeated-core
record/return sharpener: positive reuse of one material history is paid either
by record growth or by negative return of the same primitive record. Here the
same law must hold after restricting to the selected admitted face.

Thus the strict lower route is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{RecordFacePrimitiveChannelPredictableTrace.A}\\
&+\texttt{PrimitiveChannelSelectedFaceNegativeReturnBudget.A}\\
&\Longrightarrow
\texttt{RecordFacePrimitiveChannelTraceAndReturnBudget.A}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{TRB.6}
\]

Together with stopped primitive-channel admission and primitive PLS signed
channel decomposition, `(TRB.6)' gives the trace-and-return input needed by the
installed face-local signed-channel construction reduction.

## 3. Why scalar BV and chamber balance do not close it

The scalar BV identity is proof-grade only after the selected object is one
primitive record on one lawful history. It says positive variation is endpoint
growth plus negative variation. It does not decide which face the negative
variation belongs to.

A chamber-level primitive signed channel can satisfy

\[
\int [Y_{\kappa,S}]_-\,d\sigma dt<\infty
\tag{TRB.7}
\]

while the selected face has no negative return:

\[
\int \mathbf 1_{F_e}[Y_{\kappa,S}]_-\,d\sigma dt=0.
\tag{TRB.8}
\]

The chamber has return, but the positive face does not. This is the physical
wrong-face subsidy that the same-face localization proof forbids.

## 4. Checked obstructions

### 4.1 Post-readout face selection

Let \(Y\) have alternating positive and negative lobes inside one stopped
chamber. If \(F\) is chosen after reading \(Y_+\), then \(F\) can select the
positive lobes and avoid the negative lobes:

\[
\int_F [Y]_+\,d\sigma dt>0,
\qquad
\int_F [Y]_-\,d\sigma dt=0.
\tag{TRB.9}
\]

This does not contradict chamber balance. It means \(F\) was not a lawful
pre-readout record face for the primitive channel. A proof that defines the
face from the positive lobe and then asks for return on that face is circular.

### 4.2 Wrong-face return

Two admitted faces can share a chamber-level budget while only one receives the
return:

\[
dL_A=dx,\quad
\mathbf 1_A[Y]_-\,d\sigma dt=0,
\qquad
dL_B=0,\quad
\mathbf 1_B[Y]_-\,d\sigma dt=dx.
\tag{TRB.10}
\]

The total negative variation balances total lost positive mass, but face \(A\)
has no same-face return. This is not a Navier-Stokes counterexample; it is a
type obstruction to using total return as local physical payment.

### 4.3 Thin-face escape

The Radon-Nikodym and local thickness notes show a separate obstruction. A
positive subface can shrink to zero carrier thickness while preserving unit
record mass. Whole-record charge or whole-carrier compactness can survive while
the selected positive subface becomes singular. The trace theorem must therefore
use same-edge no-detachment and local thickness/charge on the actual face, not
only on the surrounding record interval.

## 5. Current proof truth

The repo now has:

\[
\text{formal same-face localization proof}
\quad+\quad

\quad+\quad
\text{record/return scalar sharpener}.
\tag{TRB.11}
\]

These are not yet the theorem. They become a Gold-sign input only after the
selected positive face is a pre-readout trace of the selector-owned primitive
channel and after the negative part of that traced channel is bounded from the
same original history:

\[
\texttt{RecordFacePrimitiveChannelPredictableTrace.A}
+\texttt{PrimitiveChannelSelectedFaceNegativeReturnBudget.A}.
\tag{TRB.12}
\]

So the remaining wall is physically narrow: prove that the same retained face
which records positive signed critical-height work is already the face of a
stopped primitive NS channel, and that any loss of that positive sign is paid by
negative return on that same face before aggregation.
