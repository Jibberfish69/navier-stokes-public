# Sheet2 B1/B3: the octave bill and the demanded toll

## 1. Normalization

Work in the one-octave package currency used by the chapter-8 recurrence.
Let \(E_j\) be the forward-admitted package energy entering octave \(j\).
The recurrence is homogeneous in \(E_j\), so the Sheet2 comparison may be
written after normalizing
\[
E_j=1.
\]
The unnormalized version is recovered by multiplying all throughput
quantities by \(E_j\); the log toll is per unit of admitted package.

## 2. B1: the octave financing floor

The horn-(a) bridge says that a self-throttled window passes at most the
fixed fraction \(f\) of its package downscale. In the normalized octave
currency, a descent that is still financing the octave must therefore
present at least that much admitted downscale throughput:
\[
F_{\rm oct}(f):=f.
\]
Equivalently, before normalization,
\[
F_{\rm oct}^{(j)}(f):=fE_j.
\]

This is exactly the quantity used in the poor-count split:
\[
\sum_{\rm rich} J_i\ge \frac12 F_{\rm oct}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\sum_{\rm poor} J_i> \frac12 F_{\rm oct}.
\]

## 3. B3: the demanded octave toll

The same horn-(a) bridge displays the per-octave multiplier:
\[
M_f:=\max\{f,\,1-r_0(f)\}<1.
\]
The Sheet2 payments are additive log tolls: per-window taxes add as
logs of retained factors. Hence the demanded octave toll is
\[
T_{\rm oct}(f):=-\log M_f
=
-\log\max\{f,\,1-r_0(f)\}.
\]

This is the log amount that must be paid to make the octave multiplier
at most \(M_f\).

## 4. Sheet2 after B1-B3

Using the B2 ceiling \(A_{\max}^{\rm S2}\), the Sheet2 poor-horn line is
now fully displayed:
\[
r_0(f)\tau_{\min}
\frac{f}{8A_{\max}^{\rm S2}}
\ge
-\log\max\{f,\,1-r_0(f)\}.
\]

In unnormalized form the count line carries \(fE_j\) and the resulting
payment is compared to the per-unit log toll after dividing by the
admitted package \(E_j\).
