# Transfer matrix at scaling tier: same-extent divergence; the bath

## 1. T1: coil suppression (conceded to the enemy)

Symmetric holder pairs cancel coherent leakage at the target at
leading order for a factor two in strength. Residual leakage is one
finite-size order down. Conceded throughout.

## 2. T2: the coherent channel transfers demand at ratio one

Rotation-rate demand imposed by strength g at range rho: ~ s g /
rho^4. Supply of the cancelling rotation from range rho': ~ s g' /
rho'^4. Equal ranges: g' = g. Undiminished transfer. The differential
channel decays (fifth power, strength asymmetry) - the coherent
channel is the binding one, and it is exactly marginal.

## 3. T3: no spacing schedule converges (same extent)

Wider: quartic penalty. Tighter: relief until contact (~ extent),
where asymptotics fail and order-one contact back-reactions force
strict supercriticality. Shrinkage buys finitely many layers.
Conclusion T4: same-extent scaffolds diverge. Their dense crowds
cannot beat the tax at finite cost; composition inherits them.

## 4. T5: the bath display (the true remaining object)

Extent leverage: supply ~ g * extent^2 / range^4. Large-and-far
holders are cheap in circulation. Price them in energy
(~ g^2 * extent * core-log). The question that remains - the dense
cell's final form, and the physically real one:

    Can large-scale strain hold small-scale producing pairs in their
    producing pose at energy cost sublinear in the lifetime bought?

Sublinear => finite-energy unbounded production => exit (ii).
Linear or worse => all of item 4b folds into item 5's starvation
race, and the crowd cell is decided in the floor's favor modulo
composition. This is the annular-storage question in crowd form: the
bath IS the collar, priced at last in the right currency.

## 5. Codex gate

(G-T-1) T2's dimensional bookkeeping; (G-T-2) the contact-floor
supercriticality claim; (G-T-3) the bath display's exact statement:
admissible bath family, energy functional, the sublinear/linear
dichotomy's precise form.
