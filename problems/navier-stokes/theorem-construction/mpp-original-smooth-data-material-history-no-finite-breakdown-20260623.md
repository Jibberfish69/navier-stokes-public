# Original Smooth Data Material-History No Finite Breakdown

Date: 2026-06-23

## 0. Claim

On the active periodic surface, original smooth divergence-free data do not
produce a finite-time classical breakdown.

The proof does not use a detached endpoint object.  It uses the same material
history generated by the original datum: the whole transported torus atlas, the
center spike readout, the annular layers underneath the spike, and the later
stress/strain return all belong to one preterminal Navier--Stokes evolution.

## 1. Finite breakdown supplies the terminal record

Assume, toward contradiction, that the maximal classical solution on \(T^3\)
has finite terminal time \(T_*<\infty\).  For any fixed \(s>5/2\), the standard
continuation criterion gives

```math
\limsup_{t\uparrow T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^s(T^3)}=\infty .
\tag{MH.1}
```

Indeed, if

```math
\sup_{t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^s(T^3)}<\infty ,
\tag{MH.2}
```

then the classical \(H^s\) local theory relaunches the same solution from
times \(t_j\uparrow T_*\) past \(T_*\), contradicting maximality.  Thus a
finite breakdown supplies a same-solution terminal readout failure.

## 2. The readout object is the whole material atlas

Use the transported whole-torus material atlas \(Q_{\rm atlas}\).  This is the
readout-complete terminal object on the periodic surface.  The continuation
packet has exactly three services:

```math
Pack_{Q_{\rm atlas}},
\qquad
Part_{N_s,Q_{\rm atlas}},
\qquad
Field_{N_s,r,Q_{\rm atlas}},
\tag{MH.3}
```

where \(N_s\) is a finite depth high enough to read the \(H^s\), \(s>5/2\),
continuation norm.

The torus material-atlas capture theorem says that any terminal
non-continuation must appear as the first failure of one of these services:

```math
\neg Pack_{Q_{\rm atlas}}
\quad\vee\quad
\neg Part_{N_s,Q_{\rm atlas}}
\quad\vee\quad
\forall r>0\,\neg Field_{N_s,r,Q_{\rm atlas}}.
\tag{MH.4}
```

The remaining job is not to call this class exit a contradiction.  The job is
to exclude the three first-failure alternatives from original smooth data using
the same material-history full clock.

## 3. Full clock excludes material-atlas loss

Loss of \(Pack_{Q_{\rm atlas}}\) means the transported material atlas loses the
positive carrier, finite material compatibility, or deformation control needed
for the \(H^s\) readout.

For the original preterminal material flow, those losses are material geometry
or material selection/interface changes.  The prelimit material-history clock
contains exactly those positive variations:

```math
d\Omega_N
=
dA_{4B,N}
+
C_{\log}d[\log(1+\mathcal P_N^{mat})]_+
+
d\mathcal K_{\rm iface}
+
d\mathcal L_{4B,N}.
\tag{MH.5}
```

The prelimit master balance gives

```math
\int_{\sigma_0}^{\infty}d\Omega_N<\infty .
\tag{MH.6}
```

If the atlas degenerated at the terminal time, then along the terminal tail one
of the following would have to occur:

1. an unbounded material deformation ratio;
2. infinitely many selected-material/interface changes accumulating at
   \(T_*\);
3. a terminal storage/overlap pulse not carried by a positive-thickness
   material annular return.

The first case forces infinite positive logarithmic material-record growth.
The second forces infinite interface/loss clock by the fixed same-material
selection quantum.  The third is excluded by the uniform terminal-Zeno annular
return theorem:

```math
\lim_{\theta\downarrow0}\limsup_m
\omega_m^{cs}((-\theta,0])=0.
\tag{MH.7}
```

So \(Pack_{Q_{\rm atlas}}\) survives on the terminal tail.

## 4. Full clock excludes participation-tower loss

Loss of \(Part_{N_s,Q_{\rm atlas}}\) means the same transported atlas survives,
but the pressure-viscosity-incompressibility tower through depth \(N_s\) is no
longer retained on that same material history.

At the smooth prelimit level the tower is not optional.  It is the differentiated
same Navier--Stokes law in material variables:

```math
\partial_s v+A^\top\nabla_a q
=
\nu\,\operatorname{div}_a(G\nabla_a v),
\qquad
\operatorname{div}_a(Av)=0.
\tag{MH.8}
```

The prelimit master balance was built before the terminal limit, on this same
smooth material packet.  Positive tower growth, coefficient growth, material
frame growth, and interface loss are all placed into the left-side clock in
\((MH.5)\).  Anything nonsummable or critical is therefore not a right-side
residual after compactness; it is part of \(d\Omega_N\).

Since \((MH.6)\) is finite, the finite \(N_s\) material tower cannot lose
retention at the terminal tail.  Thus \(Part_{N_s,Q_{\rm atlas}}\) survives.

## 5. Full clock excludes positive-scale field loss

Assume \(Pack_{Q_{\rm atlas}}\) and \(Part_{N_s,Q_{\rm atlas}}\) survive but no
positive field scale survives.  Then the same readout-complete material atlas
still carries the Navier--Stokes participation tower, but its positive-scale
one-field coherence fails at continuation depth.

That failure is exactly a terminal spike/thinning recurrence or a retained
non-gauge critical packet on the same material history.

The original-data spike-thinning trichotomy says every genuine height or
readout doubling is one of:

```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{material geometry thinning}
&\leadsto C_{\log}d[\log(1+\mathcal P_N^{mat})]_+,\\[1mm]
\text{material selection/interface change}
&\leadsto d\mathcal K_{\rm iface}+d\mathcal L_{4B,N},\\[1mm]
\text{same-material storage/stress-work change}
&\leadsto dA_{4B,N}+d\mathcal L_{4B,N}.
\end{array}
\tag{MH.9}
```

The terminal storage/overlap subcase is ruled out by the uniform annular-return
modulus.  The retained non-gauge critical-packet subcase is ruled out by the
same-packet lower bound:

```math
\mathcal C^\perp(P_j)\ge\varepsilon_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_{P_j}dA_{4B,N}\ge c(\varepsilon_0)>0.
\tag{MH.10}
```

Infinitely many terminal readout doublings or critical packets would therefore
force

```math
\int_{\sigma_0}^{\infty}d\Omega_N=\infty,
\tag{MH.11}
```

contradicting \((MH.6)\).  Hence a positive field scale survives:

```math
\exists r>0:\quad Field_{N_s,r,Q_{\rm atlas}}.
\tag{MH.12}
```

## 6. Relaunch contradiction

The three services survive together:

```math
Pack_{Q_{\rm atlas}}
\wedge
Part_{N_s,Q_{\rm atlas}}
\wedge
Field_{N_s,r,Q_{\rm atlas}}.
\tag{MH.13}
```

Because \(Q_{\rm atlas}\) is the whole transported torus atlas with finite
overlap, \((MH.13)\) gives the global continuation readout:

```math
\sup_{t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^s(T^3)}<\infty,
\qquad s>5/2.
\tag{MH.14}
```

The classical \(H^s\) local theory then relaunches the same solution past
\(T_*\), contradicting the assumption that \(T_*\) was the finite maximal
classical time.

Therefore no finite-time classical breakdown occurs on the active periodic
surface.
