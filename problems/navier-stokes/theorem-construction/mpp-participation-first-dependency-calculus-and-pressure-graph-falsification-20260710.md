## 0. Declared object and correction boundary

\[
\partial_tu+\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0.
\tag{PD.0}
\]

## 2. P1: exact law for a view of one participant

Let \(A(t)\) be a differentiable linear operator on smooth fields, set
\(v=Au\), let \(L=u\cdot\nabla\), and use \([A,B]=AB-BA\). Direct application
of \(A\) to `(PD.0)` gives

\[
(\partial_t+L-\nu\Delta)v+A\nabla p
=
\dot A\,u-[A,L]u+\nu[A,\Delta]u.
\tag{PD.1}
\]

Thus a derivative, multiplier, packet, band, or moving window has no independent
dynamics. Its exact source is the commutator generated by taking that view of
the one equation.

With \(G=A^*A\), pairing `(PD.1)` with \(Au\) gives

\[
\frac12\frac d{dt}\|Au\|_2^2+\nu\|\nabla Au\|_2^2
=
\frac12\langle(\dot G+[L,G])u,u\rangle
+\nu\langle[A,\Delta]u,Au\rangle
+\langle p,\nabla\cdot(Gu)\rangle.
\tag{PD.2}
\]

The composition law

\[
[L,AB]=[L,A]B+A[L,B]
\tag{PD.3}
\]

organizes nested views by dependency. Differentiating and then localizing, for
example, must produce the two parent commutators in `(PD.3)`; a derivation that
loses either term has broken participation.

### Truth tests for P1

- \(A=I\) recovers `(PD.0)`.
- \(A=\partial^\alpha\) gives the installed derivative-tower commutator F4.2.
- A constant-coefficient scalar Fourier multiplier commutes with gradient,
  divergence, and Laplacian, leaving only the nonlinear commutator.
- A frozen band or window is not scale invariant: under Navier--Stokes scaling
  the view itself must be conjugated.

**Status:** proved organizing identity; no ontology novelty by itself.

## 3. P2--P3: divergence inheritance and the stress-pressure lock

The divergence of a view is

\[
\nabla\cdot(Au)=[\nabla\cdot,A]u.
\tag{PD.4}
\]

For a constant-coefficient scalar operator \(A\), define

\[
v=Au,
\qquad q=Ap,
\qquad
\tau_A=A(u\otimes u)-v\otimes v.
\tag{PD.5}
\]

Then `(PD.0)` and F1.2 give the exact coupled pair

\[
\partial_tv+\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)+\nabla q
=\nu\Delta v-\nabla\cdot\tau_A,
\qquad \nabla\cdot v=0,
\tag{PD.6}
\]

\[
-\Delta q
=
\partial_i\partial_j\bigl(v_iv_j+\tau_{A,ij}\bigr).
\tag{PD.7}
\]

If \(-\Delta\pi_v=\partial_i\partial_j(v_iv_j)\), then

\[
-\Delta(q-\pi_v)=\partial_i\partial_j\tau_{A,ij}.
\tag{PD.8}
\]

The same tensor \(\tau_A\) is both the nonlinear evolution defect and the
pressure defect. A band cannot keep one and discard the other.

The deleted-freedom witness is explicit. On \(\mathbb T^3\), a freely posed
view system may take

\[
v=0,\qquad q=0,\qquad (\tau_A)_{11}=\cos x_1,
\qquad (\tau_A)_{ij}=0\ \text{otherwise}.
\tag{PD.8a}
\]

It retains a nonzero evolution-stress defect, while deliberately omitting the
forced pressure defect because

\[
\partial_i\partial_j(\tau_A)_{ij}=-\cos x_1\ne-\Delta q.
\]

Such independent ownership is available to the free counterfactual and is
forbidden for a participant by `(PD.7)`.

For an exact split \(u=v+w\), the pressure decomposition is

\[
p[u]
=p[v]+p[w]
+R_iR_j\bigl(v_iw_j+w_iv_j\bigr).
\tag{PD.9}
\]

The cross-pressure may vanish in special invariant classes. It is generically
present and has no universal sign: replacing \(w\) by \(-w\) reverses the cross
term while leaving the two self-pressure terms fixed.

### Exact two-mode cross-coupling test

On \(\mathbb T^3\), take

\[
k=(1,0,0),\quad a=(0,1,0),
\qquad
\ell=(0,1,0),\quad b=(1,0,1).
\tag{PD.10}
\]

Both modes are divergence free. At \(k+\ell\), their nonlinear cross
coefficient has a nonzero Leray-projected component, and the cross-pressure
source is nonzero. Disjoint input frequencies therefore do not create
independent pressure owners; the one participant creates sum-frequency stress
and pressure together.

**Status:** `(PD.6)`--`(PD.9)` are proved. They are a candidate refinement of
F2.2. Live-burden effect: support only.

## 4. P4: no spatially nontrivial universal scalar cutoff

For \(A=M_{\chi(t,x)}\), `(PD.1)` becomes

\[
(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)(\chi u)+\nabla(\chi p)
=
(D_t\chi-\nu\Delta\chi)u
-2\nu(\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla)u
+p\nabla\chi,
\tag{PD.11}
\]

while

\[
\nabla\cdot(\chi u)=u\cdot\nabla\chi.
\tag{PD.12}
\]

Suppose \(\chi\) maps every smooth divergence-free field on connected
\(\mathbb T^3\) to a divergence-free field. Testing `(PD.12)` against the
three constant divergence-free solutions gives \(\partial_j\chi=0\) for every
\(j\), hence \(\chi(t,\cdot)\) is spatially constant. On \(\mathbb R^3\), compactly supported
divergence-free test fields can attain an arbitrary prescribed value at any
point, giving the same conclusion. Therefore

\[
\nabla\cdot(\chi u)=0\ \text{for every smooth solenoidal }u
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\nabla_x\chi=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi(t,x)=c(t).
\tag{PD.13}
\]

A spatially compactly supported such \(\chi\) on \(\mathbb R^3\) is
identically zero at each time. A time-only multiplier \(c(t)\) preserves
divergence but remains a global amplitude view with the source \(\dot c(t)u\);
it is not a spatial localization.

On \(\mathbb T^3\) the full universal scalar-multiplier statement is sharper.
If \(v=c(t)u\) must solve the same unforced, same-viscosity equation for every
participant \(u\), with some slaved pressure for \(v\), Leray projection gives

\[
\dot c(t)u+c(t)(c(t)-1)\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u)=0.
\tag{PD.13a}
\]

Constant solutions force \(\dot c=0\). The two-mode field `(PD.10)`, used as
smooth initial data, has nonzero projected nonlinearity and then forces
\(c(c-1)=0\). Conversely, \(c=0\) and \(c=1\) work. Therefore

\[
u\longmapsto\chi u\ \text{is a universal periodic scalar-multiplier map
into same-law participants}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi\equiv0\ \text{or}\ \chi\equiv1.
\tag{PD.13b}
\]

The theorem is only about multiplication maps. Other universal symmetries,
such as spatial translations on \(\mathbb T^3\), are outside this class.
The divergence-free repair is necessarily nonlocal:

\[
\mathbb P(\chi u)
=
\chi u-\nabla\Delta^{-1}(u\cdot\nabla\chi),
\tag{PD.14}
\]

and localized pressure becomes the collar forcing

\[
\mathbb P(\chi\nabla p)=-\mathbb P(p\nabla\chi).
\tag{PD.15}
\]

For a fixed special solution, a spatially nonconstant \(\chi\) may satisfy
\(u\cdot\nabla\chi=0\). The theorem is the universal statement `(PD.13)`;
it does not forbid solution-adapted localizers carrying their exact induced
rows.

**Deleted freedom:** an arbitrary free field may be cut into independent local
pieces. Incompressibility prevents a scalar-cut piece from being a universal
independent participant.

**Status:** proved spatial-localizer candidate negative fact sharpening F2.3.
Live-burden effect: support only.

## 5. Pressure-Hessian sign convention

Let

\[
Q[u]=\operatorname{tr}((\nabla u)^2),
\qquad -\Delta p=Q[u].
\]

The exact isotropic/traceless split is

\[
\partial_i\partial_jp
=
-\frac{\delta_{ij}}3Q[u]+\mathcal T_{ij}Q[u],
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal T_{ij}f}(\xi)
=
\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}3-\frac{\xi_i\xi_j}{|\xi|^2}\right)\widehat f(\xi).
\tag{PD.16}
\]

The multiplier \(\mathcal T\) is trace free. Restricted Euler retains the
local isotropic third and removes this traceless nonlocal row. Formula
`(PD.16)` fixes the kernel convention left ambiguous in the first walk.

## 6. P5: exact failure of weak pressure-graph closure

Work on \(\mathbb T^3=(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^3\). Set

\[
k_n=(n,0,0),\qquad \ell_n=(n,-1,0),
\]

\[
a=(0,1,1),\qquad b_n=\left(\frac1n,1,0\right),
\]

and

\[
u_n(x)=a\cos(k_n\cdot x)+b_n\cos(\ell_n\cdot x).
\tag{PD.17}
\]

Since \(a\cdot k_n=b_n\cdot\ell_n=0\), every \(u_n\) is smooth and
divergence free. Its Fourier support escapes to infinity, so

\[
u_n\rightharpoonup0
\quad\text{in every finite }L^p.
\tag{PD.18}
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
Q[u_n]
=
-\cos x_2+\cos(2nx_1-x_2).
\tag{PD.19}
\]

The exact mean-zero slaved pressure is

\[
p_n
=
-\cos x_2
+\frac1{4n^2+1}\cos(2nx_1-x_2),
\tag{PD.20}
\]

so

\[
p_n\longrightarrow-\cos x_2\quad\text{uniformly},
\qquad p[0]=0.
\tag{PD.21}
\]

Weak velocity-to-pressure graph closure is false.

The weak tensor limit is

\[
u_n\otimes u_n\rightharpoonup R_0+C\cos x_2,
\]

\[
R_0=\frac12a\otimes a+\frac12e_2\otimes e_2,
\qquad
C=\frac12(a\otimes e_2+e_2\otimes a).
\tag{PD.22}
\]

Hence

\[
\nabla\cdot R
=
-\sin x_2\left(e_2+\frac12e_3\right).
\tag{PD.23}
\]

The low pressure gradient cancels the longitudinal \(e_2\) part, but the
projected nonlinear graph retains

\[
(u_n\cdot\nabla)u_n+\nabla p_n
\longrightarrow
-\frac12e_3\sin x_2
\quad\text{in }\mathcal D'(\mathbb T^3).
\tag{PD.24}
\]

Thus the defect is not merely a pressure gauge. Two high-frequency components
of one divergence-free state create a fixed low-frequency transverse defect.
Each \(u_n\) is legitimate smooth periodic Navier--Stokes initial data, but the
sequence changes datum and is not a same-history terminal witness. It tests the
weak graph claim and carries no Silver Part/Field inference.

**Status:** exact counterexample. It refutes weak velocity-to-pressure and weak
projected-nonlinearity graph closure. Live-burden effect: checked route
obstruction; full W7 remains open.

## 7. P5: sufficient topologies that close specified pressure rows

Let \(p_n,p\) be mean-zero periodic pressures.

### Velocity-strength row

If

\[
u_n\to u\quad\text{strongly in }L^3,
\]

then

\[
\|u_n\otimes u_n-u\otimes u\|_{3/2}
\le
(\|u_n\|_3+\|u\|_3)\|u_n-u\|_3\to0,
\tag{PD.25}
\]

and Calderon--Zygmund boundedness gives

\[
\|p_n-p\|_{3/2}\to0.
\tag{PD.26}
\]

At this strength the hypothesis guarantees
\(Q[u_n]\to Q[u]\) in \(W^{-2,3/2}\); it does not guarantee convergence of
the pressure source as an \(L^{3/2}\) function.

### Derivative-strength row

If instead

\[
\nabla u_n\to\nabla u\quad\text{strongly in }L^3,
\]

then

\[
\|Q[u_n]-Q[u]\|_{3/2}
\le
C(\|\nabla u_n\|_3+\|\nabla u\|_3)
\|\nabla u_n-\nabla u\|_3\to0,
\tag{PD.27}
\]

and

\[
\|\nabla^2p_n-\nabla^2p\|_{3/2}\to0.
\tag{PD.28}
\]

On periodic \(\mathbb T^3\), or globally on decaying \(\mathbb R^3\), the
Korn/Hodge estimate converts strong strain \(L^3\) convergence into the same
full-gradient conclusion under the standard mean/decay normalization. A
localized version is not proved here; it additionally requires explicit
boundary, collar, and tail hypotheses. The global conclusion also passes the
cubic service:

\[
\|\operatorname{tr}(S_n^3)-\operatorname{tr}(S^3)\|_1
\le
C(\|S_n\|_3^2+\|S\|_3^2)\|S_n-S\|_3.
\tag{PD.29}
\]

Strong velocity convergence cannot be substituted for `(PD.27)`. The exact
sequence

\[
v_n(x)=\frac1n\bigl(e_1\cos(nx_2)+e_2\cos(nx_1)\bigr)
\tag{PD.30}
\]

converges uniformly to zero, while

\[
Q[v_n]
=
\cos n(x_1-x_2)-\cos n(x_1+x_2)
\tag{PD.31}
\]

has nonvanishing finite-\(L^r\) size and \(\nabla^2p_n\) has order-one
oscillation. Here

\[
p_n
=
\frac{\cos n(x_1-x_2)-\cos n(x_1+x_2)}{2n^2}
\longrightarrow0
\tag{PD.32}
\]

uniformly. This separates pressure value from pressure source/Hessian passage.

Named spectral projectors additionally require a uniform spectral gap.
Material-frame and material-time rows require flow/path compactness beyond
spatial \(L^3\). On a localized \(\mathbb R^3\) packet, `(PD.25)` closes the
local Riesz pressure; harmonic/far pressure still requires gauge, collar, and
tail control.

**Status:** `(PD.25)`--`(PD.29)` are proved sufficient-condition statements,
and `(PD.30)`--`(PD.32)` prove the derivative-order separation. No sharp or
minimal topology claim is made. These results refine the pressure-row
dependency inside W7; they do not close W7.

## 8. P6--P7: exact effect on W7 and the live Gold burden

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{strong velocity }L^3
&&\Longrightarrow
\text{transport product and pressure value pass},\\
&\text{strong full-gradient/strain }L^3
&&\Longrightarrow
Q,\ \nabla^2p,\ \text{and cubic strain service pass},\\

The final line remains conditional. Carrier stability, harmonic/collar pressure,

\[
\frac d{dt}\frac12\|\Lambda^{1/2}u\|_2^2

\frac12\langle[u\cdot\nabla,\Lambda]u,u\rangle.
\tag{PD.34}

This is the exact self-transport commutator form of the installed signed-height
balance. Pressure has no direct global work. The right side has no universal

finite non-recounted resource charging every record doubling.
