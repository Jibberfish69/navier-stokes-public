# MPP Retained Endpoint Mass Shell-Pair Threading Direct Attempt

## Status

`RetainedEndpointMassShellPairThreading.A` is not proved by the current theorem
stack.

This pass tests whether the already absorbed fixed shell-pair commutator family
can be used as a noncircular substitute for the weighted endpoint theorem
needed by the retained positive affine endpoint-mass route.

## Target

The endpoint theorem needed upstream is

```math
\int_0^T H_w(t)\|M_0(t)\|_{L_x^2}^2\,dt
\le
\frac{\eta\nu}{C}\int_0^T D_N(t)\,dt+C_*2^{-2\delta N},
\tag{EP}
```

where the positive endpoint mass is

```math
M_0(t)=\int_J \widetilde Z(\sigma,t)\,d\sigma.
```

The fixed-collar shell-pair surface controls the commutator family

```math
\mathcal C_{m,j}(t)
:=
2^{2j}
\left\langle
[\Delta_j,\Delta_m u(t)\cdot\nabla]\Delta_j u(t),
\Delta_j u(t)
\right\rangle
```

through

```math
\sum_{m\in\mathcal B_I(N)}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal B_J(N)}
|\mathcal C_{m,j}(t)|
\le
\varepsilon\nu D_N(t)
+
C\widetilde\Lambda_N^\sharp(t)E_N(t).
\tag{SP}
```

The desired threading would have to prove, without using `(EP)` or the closed
far-corona packet as an input, that `(SP)` or the associated finite-collar
bilinear packet controls `(EP)`.

## Endpoint mass versus commutator observability

The endpoint mass is positive. It records the accumulated active stress
generated by the positive source `\widetilde Z`. The shell-pair term is a
commutator pairing. It contains cancellation, a low-gradient slot, and a local
overlap requirement between the low shell and the active stress.

Those structures do not give a lower comparison from endpoint mass to
commutator size.

A spatial-separation test exposes the gap. Take a low-frequency divergence-free
packet `a_m` and an active-frequency packet `b_j`, and translate `b_j` by a
large vector `R`. The scalar quantities entering the endpoint-square reduction,
such as `E_m` and the active stress size behind `M_0`, are essentially preserved
under this translation. The convolution-overlap term

```math
\int_{\mathbb R^3}
(\rho_j*|\nabla a_m|)(x)\,\tau_j^{H^1}[b_j(\cdot-R)](x)\,dx
```

tends to zero as `|R|\to\infty`. Consequently the shell-pair commutator family
can be arbitrarily small while the endpoint-square quantity remains positive.

This rules out the needed comparison in the current theorem language. The
commutator family measures local low-gradient/high-stress interaction. `(EP)`
measures a positive endpoint mass weighted by the low-shell energy. The present
finite-collar setup does not include an observability theorem forcing those two
objects to co-locate.

## Finite-collar bilinear packet test

The sharper shell-pair estimate also gives the bilinear envelope

```math
|\mathcal C_{m,j}(t)|
\lesssim
E_m(t)^{1/2}2^{-j/2}D_j(t).
\tag{B}
```

Pointwise, the square of the right-hand side is the local finite-band endpoint
square term:

```math
\left(E_m(t)^{1/2}2^{-j/2}D_j(t)\right)^2
=
E_m(t)2^{-j}D_j(t)^2.
\tag{B2}
```

So a direct bilinear route to `(EP)` would need a theorem of the form

```math
\int_0^T
E_m(t)2^{-j}D_j(t)^2\,dt
\le
\frac{\eta\nu}{C_{I,J}}\int_0^T D_N(t)\,dt+C_*2^{-2\delta N}
\tag{FBE}_{m,j}
```

for the fixed collar family.

The installed commutator estimate does not prove this. It gives an upper bound
for `|\mathcal C_{m,j}|` by the bilinear packet, and `(SP)` absorbs the
commutator family after a coefficient split. It does not bound the square of
the bilinear packet. The same spatial-separation test makes
`\mathcal C_{m,j}` small while the global quantity in `(B2)` remains positive,
so commutator absorption cannot be recycled into `(FBE)_{m,j}`.

An amplitude test points the same way. Under velocity scaling, the bilinear
packet scales cubically and its square scales sextically, while `D_N` scales
quadratically. Without an independent smallness, Carleson, or localization
mechanism, `(FBE)_{m,j}` is not a consequence of the shell-pair upper estimate.

## Mu_N Carleson route

The upgraded gradient interface defines

```math
d\mu_N(t)=\widetilde\Lambda_N^\sharp(t)E_N(t)\,dt.
```

The fixed-collar estimate lands on the integrand of this measure, so an
independent Carleson/tent-space theorem for `\mu_N` would be a valid way to
thread the shell-pair family back into the endpoint route.

That theorem is not installed. The inequality `(SP)` has the form

```math
\sum |\mathcal C_{m,j}|
\le
\varepsilon\nu D_N+\mu_N\text{-density}.
```

This is a one-sided upper estimate with `\mu_N` as the coefficient cost. It is
not a bound on `\mu_N` itself. Using the endpoint theorem or closed far-corona
packet to control `\mu_N`, then returning to prove `(EP)`, would be circular.

## Verdict

`RetainedEndpointMassShellPairThreading.A` is reduced, not proved.

The endpoint-mass-to-commutator comparison fails as a theorem target in the
current language: positive endpoint mass is not observable from a cancelling
shell-pair commutator family without an extra localization or stress
observability theorem. The finite-collar bilinear packet is the square-root of
the endpoint-square burden, but the installed estimates control only the
commutator side, not the square of the bilinear packet. The `\mu_N` surface is
a correct downstream interface, not an independent Carleson theorem.

The exact next target is:

```math
\textbf{RetainedAccumulatedStressL2Carleson.A}.
```

It must prove one of the direct far-corona objects already isolated in
`far-corona-carleson-direct-attack.md`:

1. the two-scale `L_x^2` Carleson theorem controlling the accumulated stress
   field `T_r` against the low-scale dissipation measure;
2. a direct one-sided square / Carleson theorem for the exact coupled object
   `B_r`;
3. a positive stress-to-commutator observability theorem strong enough to
   recover the endpoint mass from the finite-collar shell-pair family.
