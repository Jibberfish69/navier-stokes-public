## Theorem `SYNC.2` (nine proof-dependency bridge spine)

The current claim ladder consists of ten ordered claims. The nine links between
successive claims are discharged as source-facing bridge implications below.

### Bridge 1: Clay target to surrogate insufficiency

The Clay target is the original three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes
equation on `R^3` or `T^3`. A regularized, hyper-viscous, morphic-regularized,
or otherwise modified equation solves a different evolution unless an exact
identity/lift theorem returns it to the original equation on the same data and
time interval. Hence success for a surrogate is diagnostic support, not closure
of the original problem.

### Bridge 2: surrogate insufficiency to the named monotone bridge

Once surrogate success is parked, the route needs a same-equation control layer.
The recovered source stack names this layer as a monotone functional `Q(t)`.
This is not unnamed background energy: it is the explicit bridge object that
keeps the argument on the original classical surface.

### Bridge 3: named monotone bridge to energy/enstrophy insufficiency

The `Q(t)` layer records global energy/enstrophy control, but `MUP.EI` proves
that such control is necessary infrastructure rather than a closure mechanism.
Thus the monotone bridge must be followed by a scale-local obstruction analysis.

### Bridge 4: energy/enstrophy insufficiency to cascade obstruction

If global energy/enstrophy does not by itself force continuation, the missing
information is exactly the dangerous transfer into high frequencies. The live
obstruction is therefore the small-scale or high-frequency cascade, not the
mere existence of a global energy ledger.

### Bridge 5: cascade obstruction to periodic shell or dyadic tail suppression

The active internal theorem branch is the periodic zero-force branch. On that
surface, a cascade obstruction can only be neutralized by an estimate that
controls the shell flux or dyadic tail on the same periodic carrier. A bound on
an unrelated carrier or on a downstream Euclidean export surface is not the
needed bridge.

### Bridge 6: shell/tail suppression to monotone stabilization and compactness

A periodic shell or tail estimate suppresses the dangerous high-frequency
transfer. Combined with the `Q(t)` global layer, it stabilizes the approximation
family enough for the compactness package to operate on the same periodic
classical surface.

### Bridge 7: monotone plus scale-barrier to compactness and nonlinear closure

Weak compactness is not enough for Navier-Stokes closure. The monotone layer and
scale-barrier input must provide strong local compactness and enough convergence
to pass

```math
u^{(n)}\otimes u^{(n)} \longrightarrow u\otimes u
\tag{SYNC.3}
```

on the periodic/operator approximation family.

### Bridge 8: compactness closure to heat-governed continuation

Strong compactness and nonlinear tensor closure produce a classical-limit
surface, but they do not by themselves yield the required continuation
control. The fourth bridge must therefore be a heat-governed deformation or
continuation mechanism on the same periodic theorem surface.

### Bridge 9: continuation bridge to the periodic Clay branch

If the scale-barrier, monotone-functional, compactness, and continuation
packages all hold on the active periodic branch with `f=0`, then the periodic
Clay branch follows. Any whole-space `R^3` statement remains a downstream
export/discharge theorem and does not retake the internal branch lock.
