### 4. The word "mass" was too coarse

Transcript anchor: raw note lines 282-326 and 328-330.

The user forced a distinction among different possible Zeno stories:

1. finite analytic mass sits in a shrinking observation region;
2. a finite concentration is followed while the observation radius shrinks to
   zero;
3. the whole field's mass-energy is being compressed into one point.

The second story is the serious Zeno concentration picture. The first is
underspecified. The third is mostly the wrong Clay framing for finite-energy
Navier-Stokes.

Home:

- Do not write "analytic mass" as if it names one object.
- Future notes must name the actual concentrating quantity: energy density,
  enstrophy, vorticity, strain, circulation, angular velocity, pressure, or a
  scale-critical norm.
- The finite amount alone is not the smoothness verdict; the scaling, density,
  derivative, or continuation readout carries the verdict.

### 9. `omega=v/r` degenerates at the center

Transcript anchor: raw note lines 682-745 and 1017-1020.

Tangential speed and angular velocity are related by

```math
\omega = v/r.
```

That relation requires a radius. At `r=0`, orbital angular velocity loses the
ordinary circular-motion object because there is no orbit. This is the exact
place where the ontology question appears.

Home:

- Collapsed orbital quantity: classical field-limit question.
- Intrinsic point spin: extra state variable, outside classical NS.

### 10. Particle spin analogy supplies the decisive fork

Transcript anchor: raw note lines 1017-1020 and 1167-1236.

The user did not use particle spin as a metaphor for vorticity. The point was
that intrinsic spin and its field effects are different ontological levels.
That distinction prevents a fake proof move where orbital angular velocity is
declared to become a new singleton property at `r=0`.

Home:

Classical Navier-Stokes carries:

```text
u, p, and quantities derived from u and p.
```

It does not carry an independent point-spin variable `S(t)`.

Therefore:

- if `S(t)` is uncoupled from `u,p`, it is invisible to NS smoothness;
- if `S(t)` couples to `u,p`, the model is no longer classical NS;
- if `S(t)` is reconstructed from `omega=v/r`, it is a collapsed field-limit
  quantity and enters `Pack/Part/Field`.
