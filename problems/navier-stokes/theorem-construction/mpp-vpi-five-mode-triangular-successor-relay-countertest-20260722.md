# The five-mode Leray relay serves its successor completely before return

## 1. Custody and scope

This note executes the first falsification test in the moving-front
successor-service program.  The object is one real, divergence-free periodic
Fourier field projected onto one symmetric finite mode set.  The
\(p,q,r,k,\ell\) coefficients are simultaneous coordinates of that one
projected field.  In particular, \(k\) is the same coefficient first created
as a daughter and then used as the advecting leg of the \(p+k=\ell\)
interaction.

The calculation is an exact true-Leray Galerkin comparison.  It is not a
solution of the full periodic equation, because interactions leaving the
finite mode set are projected away, and it is not the decaying
\(\mathbb R^3\) Clay history.

## 2. The exact finite system

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 p&=(1,0,0),&q&=(0,-1,0),&r&=(0,0,-1),\\
 k&=q+r=(0,-1,-1),&&&\ell&=p+k=(1,-1,-1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{GR.1}
\]

and let

\[
 S=\{\pm p,\pm q,\pm r,\pm k,\pm\ell\}.
\tag{GR.2}
\]

For \(m\in S\), the inviscid Galerkin equation is

\[
 \partial_t\widehat u_m
 =-i\,\mathbb P_m
   \sum_{\substack{\alpha+\beta=m\\\alpha,\beta\in S}}
   (\widehat u_\alpha\!\cdot\!\beta)\widehat u_\beta,
 \qquad
 \widehat u_{-m}=\overline{\widehat u_m}.
\tag{GR.3}
\]

This retains the actual Leray projector and every convolution whose input and
output modes all belong to \(S\).

## 3. An invariant four-scalar ansatz

For real \(a,b,c,d\), set

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 m&\widehat u_m\\ \hline
 p&(0,-a,a)\\
 \ell&(0,b,-b)\\
 q&(c,0,1)\\
 r&(c,1,0)\\
 k&(id,0,0).
\end{array}
\tag{GR.4}
\]

Every row is perpendicular to its wave vector.  Direct substitution into
(GR.3), including both orders of every convolution pair, gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 m&\partial_t\widehat u_m\\ \hline
 p&(0,-bd,bd)\\
 \ell&(0,-ad,ad)\\
 q&(-d,0,0)\\
 r&(-d,0,0)\\
 k&(2ic,0,0).
\end{array}
\tag{GR.5}
\]

Thus the ansatz is invariant and its exact reduced system is

\[
 \boxed{
 a'=bd,\qquad b'=-ad,\qquad c'=-d,\qquad d'=2c.}
\tag{GR.6}
\]

The decisive feature is triangularity.  The \((c,d)\) oscillator drives the
\((a,b)\) rotation, but \((a,b)\) do not occur in the equations for \((c,d)\).
Within this exact normal form, using the \(k\)-daughter to create the
\(\ell\)-successor neither depletes nor detunes the \((q,r,k)\) oscillator.

## 4. Exact solution of the MF.35 datum

The datum in the moving-front note is

\[
 a(0)=1,\qquad b(0)=0,\qquad c(0)=-1,\qquad d(0)=0.
\tag{GR.7}
\]

The \(k\)-mode is initially absent, and (GR.5) gives
\(\partial_t\widehat u_k(0)=(-2i,0,0)\): it is created immediately by the
\(q+r=k\) interaction.  The complete solution of (GR.6) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c(t)&=-\cos(\sqrt2t),&
 d(t)&=-\sqrt2\sin(\sqrt2t),\\
 \theta(t)&=1-\cos(\sqrt2t),&
 a(t)&=\cos\theta(t),&
 b(t)&=\sin\theta(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{GR.8}
\]

The first return of the \(k\)-coefficient to zero occurs at

\[
 t_{\rm ret}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt2}.
\tag{GR.9}
\]

Because \(\pi/2<2\), there is an earlier time

\[
 t_*=\frac{\arccos(1-\pi/2)}{\sqrt2}<t_{\rm ret}
\tag{GR.10}
\]

at which \(\theta(t_*)=\pi/2\).  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{a(t_*)=0,\qquad b(t_*)=1.}
\tag{GR.11}
\]

The entire kinetic energy of the \(\pm p\) pair has moved into the
\(\pm\ell\) pair before the shared \(k\)-mode returns.  The catalyst is still
nonzero at the handoff:

\[
 d(t_*)^2
 =2\!\left[1-(1-\pi/2)^2\right]
 =2\pi-\frac{\pi^2}{2}>0.
\tag{GR.12}
\]

More generally, replacing \(a(0)=1\) by \(a(0)=A\) gives
\(a=A\cos\theta\), \(b=A\sin\theta\), and transfers all \(2A^2\) units of
predecessor-pair kinetic energy to the successor pair at the same \(t_*\).
There is no amplitude contraction in the isolated relay.

## 5. Exact invariant and critical-height readings

With kinetic energy normalized by
\(\frac12\sum_{m\in S}|\widehat u_m|^2\), (GR.6) gives

\[
 a^2+b^2=1,
 \qquad
 2(c^2+1)+d^2=4,
 \qquad
 E_S=6.
\tag{GR.13}
\]

Each polarization in (GR.4) has zero modal helicity, so the total Galerkin
helicity is identically zero.  The critical-height reading is nevertheless
not constant:

\[
 H_S
 =2a^2+2\sqrt3\,b^2+2(c^2+1)+\sqrt2\,d^2.
\tag{GR.14}
\]

It rises from \(H_S(0)=6\) to

\[
 H_S(t_*)
 =2\sqrt3+2\left[1+(\pi/2-1)^2\right]
  +\sqrt2\left(2\pi-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\right)
 \mathrel{\approx}8.023.
\tag{GR.15}
\]

This rise is only a finite Galerkin comparison.  The theorem proved here is
the complete successor service with no isolated back-reaction tax, not a
Galerkin blowup or a critical-height theorem.

## 6. What viscosity can and cannot restore

Scale the wave vectors by \(N\) and all amplitudes by \(\lambda\).  In the
dimensionless time \(\tau=N\lambda t\), the viscous Galerkin system is the
inviscid vector field (GR.3) plus linear damping with coefficient

\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{\nu N}{\lambda}.
\tag{GR.16}
\]

Finite-dimensional continuous dependence implies that, as
\(\lambda/(\nu N)\to\infty\), the viscous solution through the finite
dimensionless interval ending at \(\tau_*\) converges to (GR.8).  Its
predecessor-to-successor energy-transfer fraction therefore tends to one.
Viscosity cannot supply a universal data-independent deficit for this
isolated chain.

This scaling compares different periodic Galerkin data.  It does not rule out
a datum-dependent viscous payment in one fixed full-PDE history, and it does
not preserve the fixed finite-energy Clay datum while taking the limit.

## 7. Route ruling

The first branch of the falsification program is resolved:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{isolated two-triad return/detuning does not force a strict successor
 contraction.}}
\tag{GR.17}
\]

Consequently, the proposed estimate (MF.38) cannot be justified by the
isolated triad-spring mechanism with a geometry-only \(\delta>0\).  An error

uncontrolled current.  Any live contraction theorem must instead expose a
specific effect of the interactions omitted from \(S\): side-band coupling,
additional receiver competition, full-network phase incompatibility, or a
different same-history finite payer.  Those interactions must be restored
and estimated, not grouped as harmless errors.

This is a checked obstruction to one proposed proof mechanism.  It is not a
strict reduction of Gold to an already smaller theorem.  The signed
singleton-helicity time integral remains open, Gold remains open, and Silver
is unchanged.
