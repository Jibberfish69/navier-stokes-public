# Homochiral envelope motion is internal; critical work is singleton-helicity acquisition

## 1. Custody of the object

Let \(u\) be one smooth, decaying, unforced Navier--Stokes solution on
\(\mathbb R^3\), with one fixed viscosity \(\nu>0\), throughout its classical
interval. Write

\[
 u=u_++u_-,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{curl}u_\pm=\pm\Lambda u_\pm .
 \tag{SH.1}
\]

The q-envelope, the helical rows, the curl-spectral current, and the critical
height below are simultaneous readings of this same velocity field. The
finite Fourier datum in Section 5 is used only as a periodic comparison
countertest. It is not the decaying Clay fluid and supplies no terminal
history.

## 2. Exact one-triad factorization

Consider one geometric helical triad. Let its three wave-number magnitudes be
\(\kappa_i>0\), its helicity signs be \(s_i\in\{+1,-1\}\), and its nonlinear
modal kinetic-energy rates be \(T_i\). Put \(h_i=s_i\kappa_i\). The two
inviscid identities on this one triad are

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^3T_i=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^3h_iT_i=0.
 \tag{SH.2}
\]

Consequently, for one signed scalar triad current \(\gamma\),

\[
 (T_1,T_2,T_3)
 =\gamma(h_3-h_2,h_1-h_3,h_2-h_1),
 \tag{SH.3}
\]

and the triad's total nonlinear critical production is

\[
 P_{1/2}^{\rm tri}:=\sum_{i=1}^3\kappa_iT_i.
 \tag{SH.4}
\]

If the triad is homochiral, \(s_1=s_2=s_3=\sigma\), the second identity in
(SH.2) is exactly

\[
 \sum_i\kappa_iT_i=0.
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{P_{1/2}^{\rm tri}=0\quad\hbox{for every homochiral triad}.}
 \tag{SH.5}
\]

Now suppose the triad is heterochiral. There is one unique index \(o\) whose
helicity sign is opposite to the other two. If the paired sign is \(\sigma\),
then \(s_o=-\sigma\). The helicity identity gives

\[
 \sum_{i\ne o}\kappa_iT_i=\kappa_oT_o.
 \tag{SH.6}
\]

Adding the singleton contribution proves the exact factorization

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{1/2}^{\rm tri}=2\kappa_oT_o.
 }
 \tag{SH.7}
\]

This has a direct sign meaning:

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{1/2}^{\rm tri}>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T_o>0.
 }
 \tag{SH.8}
\]

Positive critical work by one heterochiral triad is precisely kinetic-energy
acquisition by its unique-helicity leg, with the frequency valuation applied
once. The two same-helicity legs have the same net critical contribution
\(\kappa_oT_o\), although their net kinetic-energy change is \(-T_o\). Thus
one sector acquires kinetic energy while the other loses kinetic energy and
simultaneously shifts its remaining energy outward. These are two coupled
consequences of one triad, not two mechanisms.

For \(0<\kappa_1<\kappa_2<\kappa_3\), the three sign families make (SH.7)
explicit:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{same-helicity pair}&P_{1/2}^{\rm tri}\\ \hline
(1,2),\ s_1=s_2=\sigma=-s_3
&2\sigma\gamma\,\kappa_3(\kappa_2-\kappa_1)\\
(1,3),\ s_1=s_3=\sigma=-s_2
&-2\sigma\gamma\,\kappa_2(\kappa_3-\kappa_1)\\
(2,3),\ s_2=s_3=\sigma=-s_1
&2\sigma\gamma\,\kappa_1(\kappa_3-\kappa_2).
\end{array}
 \tag{SH.9}
\]

The required radial gap is the gap between the two same-helicity members.
The singleton identity (SH.7) is independent of which member is smallest or
largest.

## 3. The whole-field singleton current is the cross-origin curvature

Let \(d\mu_t(\eta)\) and \(d\mathfrak n_t(\eta)\) be the curl-spectral energy
and nonlinear source measures from (TL.808). Their zeroth and first signed
moments vanish:

\[
 \int d\mathfrak n_t=0,
 \qquad
 \int\eta\,d\mathfrak n_t=0.
 \tag{SH.10}
\]

The second primitive

\[
 \mathfrak Q(a,t)
 =\int_{(-\infty,a]}(a-\eta)\,d\mathfrak n_t(\eta)
 \tag{SH.11}
\]

satisfies the exact critical balance

\[
 H'(t)=2\mathfrak Q(0,t)-2\nu E_{3/2}(t).
 \tag{SH.12}
\]

For a homochiral constituent, its signed frequencies lie on one open
half-line. Since \(|\eta|\) is affine there, its contribution to
\(\mathfrak Q(0)\) is zero. For a heterochiral triad, (SH.7) and (SH.12)
give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak Q_{\rm tri}(0)=\kappa_oT_o.
 }
 \tag{SH.13}
\]

After every triad is summed with its sign, define the complete singleton
current by

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak J_{\rm sing}(t):=\mathfrak Q(0,t)
 ={1\over2}P_H(t).
 }
 \tag{SH.14}
\]

It is not a positive count of singleton events. Oppositely oriented
heterochiral triads cancel inside \(\mathfrak J_{\rm sing}\) before any
positive part is permitted.

The same fact is visible without introducing a triad population. The exact
one-field identity is

\[
 P_H
 =2\int (u_++u_-)
 \cdot(\Lambda u_+\times\Lambda u_-)\,dx.
 \tag{SH.15}
\]

Its two terms contain respectively two \(+\) factors and one \(-\) factor,
or one \(+\) factor and two \(-\) factors. Homochiral cubic terms are absent
identically. Equation (SH.15) is the physical-space sum of the two
singleton-helicity families in (SH.9).

### 3.1 Energy pays the exchange zeroth moment; the singleton current is its frequency moment

Let \(e_\pm(k,t)\) be the radial kinetic-energy densities and
\(T_\pm(k,t)\) their complete nonlinear rates.  Put

\[
 \mathcal E_\pm:=\int_0^\infty e_\pm(k)\,dk
 ={1\over2}\|u_\pm\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_\times:=\int_0^\infty T_+(k)\,dk
 =-\int_0^\infty T_-(k)\,dk.
 \tag{SH.15a}
\]

The two kinetic-energy sector balances are

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E_+'+\nu A_+=\mathcal R_\times,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_-'+\nu A_-=-\mathcal R_\times.}
 \tag{SH.15b}
\]

Energy conservation gives the opposite zeroth moments in (SH.15a), while
helicity conservation and the definition of \(P_H\) give the equal first
moments

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^\infty kT_+(k)\,dk
 =\int_0^\infty kT_-(k)\,dk
 ={P_H\over2}=\mathfrak J_{\rm sing}.}
 \tag{SH.15c}
\]

Thus the singleton current is the first radial moment of the same signed
inter-helicity-sector exchange whose zeroth moment appears in the energy-paid sector
row.  On one heterochiral triad, if \(s_o\) and \(\kappa_o\) are the sign and
frequency of the unique-helicity leg, then its contribution to the plus-sector
energy rate and singleton current obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal R_{+,\rm tri}=s_oT_o,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak J_{{\rm sing},\rm tri}
 =(s_o\kappa_o)\mathcal R_{+,\rm tri}.}
 \tag{SH.15d}
\]

The critical current is therefore literally the signed-frequency valuation
of the inter-helicity energy exchange.  For every fixed center \(K>0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 {P_H\over2}
 =K\mathcal R_\times
 +\int_0^\infty(k-K)T_+(k)\,dk.}
 \tag{SH.15e}
\]

The signed zeroth moment has the datum bound

\[
 -\mathcal E_+(0)
 \le\int_0^T\mathcal R_\times\,dt
 \le\mathcal E_-(0),
 \qquad
 \left|\int_0^T\mathcal R_\times\,dt\right|
 \le\mathcal E(0),
 \tag{SH.15f}
\]

directly from the two rows in (SH.15b).  These are signed net-exchange bounds,
not total-variation bounds.  Multiplying them by a fixed \(K\) is not uniform
as the active scale tends to infinity, and a moving \(K(t)\) creates the
unpriced terms
\(-\int K'\mathcal E_+dt\) and \(\nu\int KA_+dt\).  The remaining centered
moment in (SH.15e) is therefore exactly the signed frequency-lift burden.
Homochiral transfers vanish from both displayed moments; multi-generation
ancestry is unnecessary.  Any successful q-adic theorem must control this
centered instantaneous exchange after common-face cancellation, not count
paths through the network.

Centering at the actual kinetic-energy centroid of each helical sector makes
the remaining frequency lift explicit.  When \(\mathcal E_\pm>0\), define

\[
 \lambda_\pm:={h_\pm\over\mathcal E_\pm},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_\pm:=d_\pm-\lambda_\pm a_\pm
 ={1\over2\mathcal E_\pm}
 \iint(k+\ell)(k-\ell)^2e_\pm(k)e_\pm(\ell)\,dk\,d\ell
 \ge0.
 \tag{SH.15g}
\]

The sector energy and critical-moment rows give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E_\pm\lambda_\pm'+2\nu\Sigma_\pm
 =\int_0^\infty(k-\lambda_\pm)T_\pm(k)\,dk
 ={P_H\over2}\mp\lambda_\pm\mathcal R_\times.}
 \tag{SH.15h}
\]

Thus direct heat lowers each sector centroid through the nonnegative
covariance \(\Sigma_\pm\); the nonlinear field must supply both that loss and
any upward centroid motion.  Eliminating the signed zeroth-moment exchange
between the two rows gives, whenever \(\lambda_++\lambda_->0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak J_{\rm sing}
 ={\lambda_-\bigl(\mathcal E_+\lambda_+'+2\nu\Sigma_+\bigr)
   +\lambda_+\bigl(\mathcal E_-\lambda_-'+2\nu\Sigma_-\bigr)
  \over\lambda_++\lambda_-}.}
 \tag{SH.15i}
\]

This is the exact coordinate bridge between the singleton current and the
older energy-centroid law.  It is not a new payer: neither the positive
variation of the two moving centroids nor the covariance term has a
datum-finite bound at this critical weight.  It does show that the surviving
problem is one signed, frequency-centered same-field motion, rather than an
independent q-current, centroid mechanism, and ancestry mechanism.

### 3.2 A moving center only repartitions the same critical primitive

The conclusion is unchanged if the center is allowed to move.  For any
(C^1) center (c(t)), put

\[
 X_c:=h_+-c\mathcal E_+,
 \qquad
 C_c:=J-c\mathcal R_\times
 =\int_0^\infty(k-c)T_+(k)\,dk,
 \qquad J={P_H\over2}.
 \tag{SH.15j}
\]

The sector energy and critical rows give, exactly,

\[
 \boxed{
 C_c=X_c'+2\nu d_+-\nu cA_++c'\mathcal E_+,}
 \tag{SH.15k}
\]

while the part assigned to the moving center is

\[
 c\mathcal R_\times
 =(c\mathcal E_+)'+\nu cA_+-c'\mathcal E_+.
 \tag{SH.15l}
\]

Adding (SH.15k) and (SH.15l) cancels the center, sweep, and lower-row terms
and returns only

\[
 \boxed{J=h_+'+2\nu d_+.}
 \tag{SH.15m}
\]

Thus a fixed, active, record-dependent, or smoothly q-moving center creates
no new payer; it only splits the same singleton primitive into two pieces.
For a piecewise smooth center, its jump terms cancel between \(X_c\) and
\(c\mathcal E_+\) in the same way.  This is a no-go for centering alone, not
for the still-open nonlocal shared-q-face cancellation.

The corresponding fixed-multiplier statement is equally rigid at the
installed energy/helicity conservation level.  Every multiplier pair whose
source represents \(J\), modulo the two invariant covectors, has the form

\[
 \phi_+(k)={k\over2}+a+bk,
 \qquad
 \phi_-(k)={k\over2}+a-bk.
 \tag{SH.15n}
\]

At least one slope has absolute value at least \(1/2\).  On the balanced ray
\(h_+=h_-=r\), the associated stock still contains \(H/2=r\), up to the
kinetic-energy term \(a(\mathcal E_++\mathcal E_-)\).  No such bounded local
multiplier can therefore turn \(J\) into a kinetic-energy-controlled endpoint
stock.  A successful supplier must retain signed history rather than merely
choose another center or conservation representative.

### 3.3 The complete shared-q-face telescope is exactly the positive critical-action primitive

The q-adic language does not by itself create a second cancellation after the
singleton current has been formed.  Let

\[
 \mathcal E_+^{>K}(t):=\int_K^\infty e_+(k,t)\,dk,
 \qquad

 \tag{SH.15o}
\]

and let \(A_+^{>K}=2\int_K^\infty k^2e_+(k)\,dk\).  The same sector balance
as in (SH.15b), now on the actual Fourier tail of the same field, is

\[
 \partial_t\mathcal E_+^{>K}+\nu A_+^{>K}
 =\mathcal R_+^{>K}.
 \tag{SH.15p}
\]

Layer cake and (SH.15c) give

\[
 \boxed{
 J=\int_0^\infty kT_+(k)\,dk
   =\int_0^\infty\mathcal R_+^{>K}\,dK
   =h_+'+2\nu d_+.}
 \tag{SH.15q}
\]

Thus integrating every sharp face in \(K\) has already returned (SH.15m).
The endpoint stock is the critical sector height, not kinetic energy.

The exact translated q-adic version makes the shared-face cancellation
literal.  Put \(L=\log q\), \(K_j(r)=e^rq^j\), and define the intrinsic step
valuation

\[
 \kappa_{q,r}(k)=K_j(r)
 \quad\text{when }K_j(r)\le k<K_{j+1}(r).
 \tag{SH.15r}
\]

If

\[
 J_{q,r}:=\int_0^\infty\kappa_{q,r}(k)T_+(k)\,dk,
 \qquad
 R_{q,r,j}:=\int_{K_j(r)}^\infty T_+(k)\,dk,
\]

then discrete summation by parts over the complete grid gives

\[
 \boxed{
 J_{q,r}=(1-q^{-1})\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}K_j(r)R_{q,r,j}.}
 \tag{SH.15s}
\]

Every common face occurs once in this sum.  But translation averaging is
exactly the critical valuation, since

\[
 \int_0^L\kappa_{q,r}(k)\,dr=(1-q^{-1})k.
 \tag{SH.15t}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 J={1\over1-q^{-1}}\int_0^LJ_{q,r}\,dr
   =h_+'+2\nu d_+.}
 \tag{SH.15u}
\]

After time integration, the full q-face telescope is therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^T J\,dt
 =h_+(T)-h_+(0)+2\nu\int_0^Td_+\,dt
 ={\mathscr N_+(T)-\mathscr N_+(0)\over2}.}
 \tag{SH.15v}
\]

The minus sector gives the identical primitive.  Taking their difference
removes \(J\) and returns the helicity-imbalance row; taking any linear
combination that retains one copy of \(J\) also retains one copy of the
balanced endpoint stock \(H/2\).  The heat-filtered formula (EQ.15) is the
Duhamel form of the same fact: its internal lifetime sweep supplies precisely
the viscous term in (SH.15v).  Dropping that sweep manufactures a false
boundary cancellation.

This is a checked obstruction only to *algebraic* shared-face telescoping.
It does not rule out a dynamical signed theorem using correlations between
successive heterochiral phases.  It does prove that the remaining theorem
must create such a time/phase cancellation before the complete layer-cake
sum is taken; common-face summation, translation averaging, or heat-tail
subtraction alone merely restates the open positive critical-action bound.

## 4. Why the q-envelope is a concentration coordinate, not the signed target

For translated q-cells, let \(a_j=K_jE_j\). The moving-envelope source before
the maximum is taken is

\[
 \sum_jK_ja_j^{p-1}s_j
 =\sum_{a<b}
 (K_ba_b^{p-1}-K_aa_a^{p-1})\mathcal T_{b,a}.
 \tag{SH.16}
\]

For \(p=1\), translation averaging reconstructs the total critical work and
the homochiral contributions cancel by (SH.5). For \(p>1\), and especially
as \(p\to\infty\), the unequal state weights \(a_j^{p-1}\) destroy that
cancellation. A homochiral triad can therefore increase the active q-cell
while decreasing its companions, even though its total critical production
is exactly zero.

Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{homochiral q-envelope growth}
 \not\Rightarrow
 \text{total critical-height growth}.
 }
 \tag{SH.17}
\]

## 6. A scale-invariant opposite-helicity overlap criterion

The singleton factorization also exposes a clean sufficient continuation
condition. Define the two helical enstrophy and critical-loss rows

\[
 A_\pm:=\|\Lambda u_\pm\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 D_\pm:=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u_\pm\|_2^2.
 \tag{SH.25}
\]

Split (SH.15) into its two sign-count families. Sobolev embedding and Holder
give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int u_+\cdot
(\Lambda u_+\times\Lambda u_-)\,dx\right|
&\le C A_+^{1/2}A_-^{1/2}D_+^{1/2},\\
\left|\int u_-\cdot
(\Lambda u_+\times\Lambda u_-)\,dx\right|
&\le C A_+^{1/2}A_-^{1/2}D_-^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{SH.26}
\]

Indeed, the first line uses \(L^6,L^3,L^2\) on
\(u_+,\Lambda u_+,\Lambda u_-\); the second uses \(L^6,L^2,L^3\).
Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 |P_H|
 \le C(A_+A_-)^{1/2}(D_++D_-)^{1/2}.
 }
 \tag{SH.27}
\]

Since

\[
 H'+\nu(D_++D_-)=P_H,
 \tag{SH.28}
\]

Young's inequality yields

\[
 \boxed{
 H'(t)\le {C\over\nu}A_+(t)A_-(t).
 }
 \tag{SH.29}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^{T_*}A_+(t)A_-(t)\,dt<\infty
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{t<T_*}H(t)<\infty.
 }
 \tag{SH.30}
\]

The hypothesis is invariant under Navier--Stokes scaling: each \(A_\pm\)
scales by \(\lambda\), while \(dt\) scales by \(\lambda^{-2}\). Thus (SH.30)
is a critical two-helicity temporal-overlap criterion, not a subcritical
smallness assumption.

The ordinary kinetic-energy identity supplies only

\[
 \int_0^{T_*}(A_++A_-)\,dt
 \le {\|u_0\|_2^2\over2\nu}.
 \tag{SH.31}
\]

Two nonnegative \(L^1_t\) functions need not have an integrable product.
Therefore (SH.31) does not prove (SH.30). The missing object is precisely the
simultaneous opposite-helicity overlap, or a sharper signed q-current theorem
which avoids estimating that product absolutely.

The earlier minority-helicity null-form estimate

\[
 |P_H|
 \le C\min_{s\in\{+,-\}}
 \|u_s\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}
 \|u\|_{\dot H^{3/2}}^2
 \tag{SH.32}
\]

is compatible with (SH.27), but pays a different coordinate. It proves a
fixed mixed-helicity participation floor at rising times. Equation (SH.27)
instead isolates the critical temporal coincidence of the two already
energy-paid enstrophy rows. Neither estimate bounds its own repeated
same-history realization.

The overlap has an exact finite-measure form. Put

\[
 S_A:=A_++A_-,
 \qquad
 m_A:=\min\{A_+,A_-\}.
 \tag{SH.33}
\]

Pointwise,

\[
 \boxed{
 {1\over2}m_AS_A\le A_+A_-\le m_AS_A.
 }
 \tag{SH.34}
\]

Define the actual kinetic-energy loss measure

\[
 d\mathcal L_E(t):=\nu S_A(t)\,dt,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_E([0,T_*))
 \le {1\over2}\|u_0\|_2^2.
 \tag{SH.35}
\]

Equations (SH.29) and (SH.34) give

\[
 \boxed{
 H(t)
 \le H(0)+{C\over\nu^2}
 \int_{[0,t]}m_A\,d\mathcal L_E.
 }
 \tag{SH.36}
\]

There is nevertheless one unconditional datum-finite action at this exact
frontier. Put

\[
 X_A:=A_+A_-,
 \qquad
 h_A:={X_A\over S_A},
 \tag{SH.36a}
\]

with \(h_A=0\) when \(X_A=0\). Then

\[
 {1\over2}m_A\le h_A\le m_A.
 \tag{SH.36b}
\]

Write \(D=D_++D_-\), and let \(C_0\) be the constant in (SH.27). Since
\(|P_H|^2\le C_0^2X_AD=C_0^2h_AS_AD\), the kinetic-energy law gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^{T_*}{|P_H|^2\over D h_A}\,dt
 \le C_0^2\int_0^{T_*}S_A\,dt
 \le {C_0^2\|u_0\|_2^2\over2\nu}.
 }
 \tag{SH.36c}
\]

The integrand is defined as zero where \(Dh_A=0\); (SH.27) then forces
\(P_H=0\). Thus (SH.36c) is a datum-finite quadratic action for the complete
signed singleton current \(\mathfrak J_{\rm sing}=P_H/2\), not a bound for its
unweighted time integral.

Combining (SH.27) with the exact balance (SH.28) and optimizing in \(D\)
also gives

\[
 H'
 \le C_0\sqrt{X_AD}-\nu D
 \le {C_0^2\over4\nu}X_A.
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^{T_*}{(H')_+\over h_A}\,dt
 \le {C_0^2\over4\nu}\int_0^{T_*}S_A\,dt
 \le {C_0^2\|u_0\|_2^2\over8\nu^2}.
 }
 \tag{SH.36d}
\]

Equivalently, every finite \(L\) obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{\{h_A\le L\}}(H')_+\,dt
 \le {C_0^2L\|u_0\|_2^2\over8\nu^2}.
 }
 \tag{SH.36e}
\]

The same two paid rows give further datum-finite actions.  Indeed,
\(h_A\le S_A/4\) and \(\sqrt{X_A}\le S_A/2\), so

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^{T_*}h_A\,dt\le{\|u_0\|_2^2\over8\nu},
 \qquad
 \int_0^{T_*}\sqrt{X_A}\,dt\le{\|u_0\|_2^2\over4\nu}.}
 \tag{SH.36f}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz between (SH.36c) and (SH.36f), followed on
\(G=\{H'>0\}\) by \(P_H\ge\nu D\), yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^{T_*}{|P_H|\over\sqrt D}\,dt
 \le {C_0\|u_0\|_2^2\over4\nu},
 \qquad
 \int_G\sqrt D\,dt
 \le {C_0\|u_0\|_2^2\over4\nu^2}.}
 \tag{SH.36g}
\]

The same rising-time comparison gives a stronger one-sided action before
Cauchy--Schwarz is used.  On \(G\), (SH.28) gives \(P_H>\nu D\).  Combining
this with \(P_H^2\le C_0^2Dh_AS_A\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 {D\over h_A}\,\mathbf 1_G
 \le {C_0^2\over\nu^2}S_A\,\mathbf 1_G,
 \qquad
 \int_G{D\over h_A}\,dt
 \le {C_0^2\|u_0\|_2^2\over2\nu^3}.}
 \tag{SH.36g0}
\]

There are no points of \(G\) with \(h_A=0\): (SH.27) would force
\(P_H=0\), contradicting \(P_H>\nu D\ge0\).  Thus (SH.36g0) is an exact
inverse-overlap dissipation action on the same solution.  It also displays
the unpaid factor without a scalar countermodel:

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_GDh_A\,dt
 =\int_G{D\over h_A}h_A^2\,dt.}
 \tag{SH.36g1}
\]

The energy-paid side has the inverse weight; the desired dual has two
additional powers of the harmonic opposite-helicity enstrophy.  In
particular, \(\int_GDh_A<\infty\) is a sufficient continuation supplier,
because (SH.36c) and Cauchy--Schwarz then give
\(\int_G|P_H|dt<\infty\), hence finite positive variation of \(H\).
It is a stronger, supercritical supplier, not an energy consequence.

Likewise, (SH.36d) and (SH.36f) give

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^{T_*}\sqrt{(H')_+}\,dt
 \le {C_0\|u_0\|_2^2\over8\nu^{3/2}}.}
 \tag{SH.36h}
\]

These are square-root actions, not bounded positive variation.  To see the
remaining concentration gap exactly, set \(s=1-t\), choose \(c>0\), and put

\[
 H=c s^{-1/3},\qquad S_A=\beta H^2,\qquad
 A_+=A_-={S_A\over2},
 \qquad
 \beta^2={16\nu\over3C_0^2c^3}.
 \tag{SH.36i}
\]

With \(X_A=S_A^2/4\), \(h_A=S_A/4\),

bound (SH.27), and both weighted actions hold with equality.  Moreover

displayed marginals, not a Navier--Stokes realization.

Therefore unbounded critical height would have to place its unbounded
positive variation where the harmonic opposite-helicity enstrophy \(h_A\)
itself becomes unbounded. This is a proved concentration statement, not
closure. Cauchy--Schwarz returns the still-unpaid dual factor

\[
 \int_0^{T_*}|P_H|\,dt
 \le
 \left(\int_0^{T_*}{|P_H|^2\over Dh_A}\,dt\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\int_0^{T_*}Dh_A\,dt\right)^{1/2},
\]

and no datum-finite bound for \(\int Dh_A\,dt\) is installed.

 \tag{SH.36j}

one has

 H_\lambda(t)=H(\lambda^2t),\quad

 \tag{SH.36k}

 \boxed{
 \int_{I_\lambda}A_{+,\lambda}A_{-,\lambda}\,dt
 =\int_I A_+A_-\,dt,

 \tag{SH.36l}
\]

Thus no bound for \(\int_GDh_A\) depending only on \(\nu\) and the

Thus the unsigned overlap route asks for one precise upgrade of the finite

\[
 \int m_A\,d\mathcal L_E
 =\int_0^\infty
 \mathcal L_E(\{t:m_A(t)>\alpha\})\,d\alpha.

Energy supplies the finite total mass of \(\mathcal L_E\), but no decay of
these joint high-enstrophy tails. Any Gold counterhistory must therefore

the original kinetic energy is being irreversibly lost. This is equivalent,

independent payer.

## 8. The exact cross-helicity product has no homochiral source

### 8.2 The two positive critical spin actions have exactly the singleton current as their common derivative

homochiral decimation of Biferale and Titi is the opposite boundary: one spin
sector is projected away, making helicity sign-definite.  That modified
equation validates the kernel mechanism but is not the full Navier--Stokes
participant.

Primary references: [Lerner--Vigneron, *On some properties of the curl
operator and their consequences for the Navier--Stokes
system*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07950), Theorem 15; and
[Biferale--Titi, *On the Global Regularity of a Helical-decimated Version of
the 3D Navier--Stokes Equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.1215).

### 8.3 Exact overlap-loss carrier and the fixed-kernel dichotomy

The harmonic two-frequency carrier can be restricted to the two opposite
helicities, so that its viscous loss is the exact overlap rather than the
larger total square in (SH.39).  Define

\[
 \mathscr X_{{\rm har},\times}
 :=\iint w_{\rm har}(\kappa,\lambda)
 e_+(\kappa)e_-(\lambda)\,d\kappa d\lambda,
 \qquad
 w_{\rm har}={\kappa^2\lambda^2\over\kappa^2+\lambda^2}.
 \tag{SH.60}
\]

This identity does not assume separate nonlinear conservation of the two
helical energies.  Differentiate directly with the complete nonlinear rates
from (SH.15a),

\[
 \partial_t e_\pm(k)=T_\pm(k)-2\nu k^2e_\pm(k),
 \tag{SH.61}
\]

and retain both exchange terms.  One obtains

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr X_{{\rm har},\times}'
 +{\nu\over2}A_+A_-
 =\mathscr S_{{\rm har},\times},}
 \tag{SH.62}
\]

where

\[
 \mathscr S_{{\rm har},\times}
 :=\iint w_{\rm har}(\kappa,\lambda)
 \bigl(T_+(\kappa)e_-(\lambda)
      +e_+(\kappa)T_-(\lambda)\bigr)
 \,d\kappa d\lambda.
 \tag{SH.63}
\]

Indeed, \((\kappa^2+\lambda^2)w_{\rm har}
=\kappa^2\lambda^2\), so the viscous term is
\(2\nu a_+a_-=(\nu/2)A_+A_-\).  Moreover,
\(0\le w_{\rm har}\le\kappa\lambda/2\), and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 0\le\mathscr X_{{\rm har},\times}
 \le{1\over2}h_+h_-
 \le{H^2\over8}.}
 \tag{SH.64}
\]

Thus the unsigned supplier has the exact one-carrier reduction

\[
 {\nu\over2}\int_0^T A_+A_-\,dt
 =\mathscr X_{{\rm har},\times}(0)
  -\mathscr X_{{\rm har},\times}(T)
  +\int_0^T\mathscr S_{{\rm har},\times}\,dt.
 \tag{SH.65}
\]

The remaining source cannot be removed by changing to another fixed local
two-frequency kernel while retaining both exact requirements.  To make this
precise, suppose cancellation of every homochiral source is required to
follow only from its zero zeroth and first radial moments.  A fixed kernel
\(w(\kappa,\lambda)\) must then be affine in \(\kappa\) for each fixed
\(\lambda\), and affine in \(\lambda\) for each fixed \(\kappa\): the
three-point signed measures with zero mass and first moment force every
second divided difference to vanish.  Therefore

\[
 w(\kappa,\lambda)
 =\alpha+\beta\kappa+\gamma\lambda+\delta\kappa\lambda.
 \tag{SH.66}
\]

Let

\[
 \mathcal R_\times=\int T_+=-\int T_-,
 \qquad
 J=\int kT_+=\int kT_-={P_H\over2}.
 \tag{SH.67}
\]

Direct differentiation, again without assigning separate conservation to
either sector, gives the nonlinear source of (SH.66):

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathscr S_w={}&\mathcal R_\times
 \bigl[\alpha(\mathcal E_--\mathcal E_+)
       +\gamma h_- -\beta h_+\bigr]\\
 &+J\bigl[\beta\mathcal E_-+\gamma\mathcal E_+
          +\delta H\bigr].
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.68}
\]

On the diagonal \(\kappa=\lambda=K\), a pointwise viscous loss strong
enough to pay the overlap would require

\[
 2K^2w(K,K)\ge cK^4
 \qquad(K\gg1).
 \tag{SH.69}
\]

Equation (SH.66) then forces \(\delta\ge c/2>0\).  But (SH.68) shows that
the same coefficient necessarily restores \(\delta HJ=\delta HP_H/2\).
No fixed \(\beta,\gamma\) cancels this term for all spectra, since their
contribution is only energy-weighted while \(H\) grows linearly with a
common spectral translation.  The exact cross-critical kernel
\(w=\kappa\lambda\) is the extremal example: it kills homochiral motion and
returns exactly the source \(HP_H/2\) in (SH.48).

Conversely, \(w_{\rm har}\) pays \(A_+A_-\) exactly in (SH.62), but it is
not separately affine.  Homochiral radial rearrangement therefore remains
inside \(\mathscr S_{{\rm har},\times}\).  This proves the fixed-kernel
dichotomy:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{homochiral-invariant fixed kernel}
 \Longrightarrow \text{the singleton source }HP_H/2\text{ returns},\\[2mm]
 \text{exact harmonic overlap loss}
 \Longrightarrow \text{homochiral radial current returns}.
 \end{array}}
 \tag{SH.70}
\]

It is a no-go for a fixed invariant-only local carrier, not for a signed
history theorem.  Sections 3.3 and 8.4 distinguish the surviving possibility
from a raw face telescope: it must use a genuinely dynamical time/phase
correlation before layer cake reconstructs the positive critical-action primitive.

### 8.4 The first Stokes time normal form leaves a sign-indefinite quartic network remainder

There is one canonical way to integrate the cubic current before taking a
positive part.  Put \(A=-\Delta\), \(S_\sigma=e^{-\sigma A}\), and write the
same Navier--Stokes evolution as

\[
 u_t=-\nu Au+\mathcal N(u),
 \qquad
 \mathcal N(u)=-\mathbb P(u\cdot\nabla u).
 \tag{SH.71}
\]

Regard the installed nonlinear critical production \(P_H(u)\) as its
homogeneous cubic functional and define the Stokes-resolved phase potential

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak B_\nu(u)
 :={1\over\nu}\int_0^\infty P_H(S_\sigma u)\,d\sigma.}
 \tag{SH.72}
\]

For a smooth field, differentiation under the heat integral is exact. Since

\[
 \partial_\sigma P_H(S_\sigma u)
 =DP_H(S_\sigma u)[-AS_\sigma u],
\]

the linear Stokes part gives the complete boundary term, while the same
field's quadratic evolution gives

\[
 \boxed{
 {d\over dt}\mathfrak B_\nu(u(t))
 =-P_H(u(t))+\mathfrak R_\nu(u(t)),}
 \tag{SH.73}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak R_\nu(u)
 :={1\over\nu}\int_0^\infty
 DP_H(S_\sigma u)[S_\sigma\mathcal N(u)]\,d\sigma.}
 \tag{SH.74}
\]

Thus every prefix satisfies the exact first normal-form identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^T P_H(u(t))\,dt
 =\mathfrak B_\nu(u(0))-\mathfrak B_\nu(u(T))
  +\int_0^T\mathfrak R_\nu(u(t))\,dt.}
 \tag{SH.75}
\]

This is the first dynamical object beyond the algebraic q-face telescope.
It also exposes why one integration by parts does not close the theorem. The
minority-helicity estimate (NIC.167), heat contraction in
\(\dot H^{1/2}\), and the exact heat square integral give only

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathfrak B_\nu(u)|
 \le {C\over\nu}\|u\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}
       \int_0^\infty\|S_\sigma u\|_{\dot H^{3/2}}^2\,d\sigma
 ={C\over2\nu}\|u\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}^3.}
 \tag{SH.76}
\]

The terminal endpoint is therefore scale-critical but grows superlinearly in
the very height to be bounded. It is not an energy-controlled boundary
stock.

Nor does the quartic remainder have a universal coercive sign. On the
mean-normalized torus, use the wave vectors and positive-helicity vectors in
(SH.18), replace the third polarization by its negative-helicity conjugate,
and set

\[
 \widehat u(k_1)=h_1,
 \qquad
 \widehat u(k_2)=b h_2,
 \qquad
 \widehat u(k_3)=1.6e^{i\phi}\overline{h_3},
 \tag{SH.77}
\]

with the conjugate modes imposed for reality. Direct full Fourier
convolution, retaining every quadratic output in \(\mathcal N(u)\), gives

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 (b,\phi)&P_H(u)&\mathfrak R_1(u)\\ \hline
 (1.8,\pi/12)&3.005425582559174&-0.06120370944134024\\
 (0.45,23\pi/12)&0.7513563956397926&0.4645131796213645.
 \end{array}
 \tag{SH.78}
\]

Both lawful trigonometric-polynomial data have positive instantaneous
singleton current, but the exact quartic phase/reconvergence remainder has
opposite signs. This rejects a universal sign payment by the first Stokes
normal form. It is a periodic comparison obstruction, not a decaying
\(\mathbb R^3\) counterexample. A higher or q-localized normal form remains
possible only if it proves a new cancellation of both the terminal cubic and
the complete quartic network term; naming the latter an error would merely
rename the unpaid current.

### 8.5 The normal-form expansion parameter is the dangerous critical concentration

The first normal form also fixes the scale of any proposed iteration.  From
(SH.76), with \(H=\|u\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}^2\),

\[
 { |\mathfrak B_\nu(u)|\over H}
 \le {C\over2\nu}\sqrt H.
 \tag{SH.79}
\]

On a finite-neighbour all-high q-population at frequency \(K\), the installed
capacity estimate and the Stokes denominator give, at the same majorant
level,

\[
 |P_H^{q,\mathrm{near}}|
 \lesssim_q \sqrt{M_q}\,K^2M_q,
 \qquad
 |\mathfrak B_{\nu,q}^{\mathrm{near}}|
 \lesssim_q {\sqrt{M_q}\over\nu K^2}K^2M_q.
 \tag{SH.80}
\]

Thus the dimensionless normal-form factor is

\[
 \boxed{
 \varepsilon_{\rm NF,q}
 \simeq_q {C_q\sqrt{M_q}\over\nu}
 =2\rho_q.}
 \tag{SH.81}
\]

This is not a high-frequency small parameter: the \(K^2\) from the cubic
current is exactly cancelled by the \(K^{-2}\) Stokes lifetime.  It is the
same nonlinear-to-heat ratio exposed in (PE.8), and it diverges when
\(M_q\to\infty\).

The homogeneity is exact independently of the majorant.  For a fixed Fourier
shape and amplitude multiplier \(a>0\),

\[
 H(au)=a^2H(u),\qquad
 P_H(au)=a^3P_H(u),\qquad
 \mathfrak B_\nu(au)=a^3\mathfrak B_\nu(u),\qquad
 \mathfrak R_\nu(au)=a^4\mathfrak R_\nu(u).
 \tag{SH.82}
\]

Hence each extra nonlinear insertion costs one factor

\[
 {\mathfrak R_\nu(au)\over P_H(au)}
 =a\,{\mathfrak R_\nu(u)\over P_H(u)}
 \sim\sqrt{H(au)}.
 \tag{SH.83}
\]

Therefore repeated Stokes integration by parts cannot be justified on the
dangerous branch by taking absolute values and summing a perturbative
geometric series.  This does not rule out a higher normal form.  It requires
that any such construction resum the signed full-network terms
nonperturbatively, with cancellation uniform as \(\rho_q\to\infty\).

### 8.6 Sharp sector interpolation still leaves the overlap first moment unpaid

The overlap route has a sharper exact moment geometry than the earlier
two-scale split. Put

\[
 E:=\mathcal E_++\mathcal E_-,\qquad
 S_A:=A_++A_-,\qquad X_A:=A_+A_-,
 \tag{SH.84}
\]

and introduce the cross moments

\[
 B_A:=\mathcal E_+A_-+\mathcal E_-A_+,
 \qquad C_A:=D_+D_-.
 \tag{SH.85}
\]

Spectral Hölder in each sector gives

\[
 A_s^3\le 2\mathcal E_sD_s^2,
 \tag{SH.86}
\]

while arithmetic--geometric mean gives

\[
 B_A\ge2\sqrt{\mathcal E_+\mathcal E_-X_A}.
\]

Multiplication and cancellation yield the sharp common-shell inequality

\[
 \boxed{X_A^2\le B_AC_A.}
 \tag{SH.87}
\]

For nonzero sectors, equality forces both radial spectra onto one common
frequency shell. A nonzero \(L^2(\mathbb R^3)\) field does not attain that
shell equality, but narrowing annuli approach it, so no uniform improvement
of (SH.87) is available from these moments alone.

Since \(C_A\le D^2/4\),

\[
 X_A\le{D\over2}\sqrt{B_A}.
 \tag{SH.88}
\]

On the rising set \(G=\{H'>0\}\), the two exact inequalities
\(P_H>\nu D\) and \(P_H^2\le C_0^2X_AD\) imply
\(X_A>\nu^2D/C_0^2\). Combining this with (SH.88) gives the fixed
participation floor

\[
 \boxed{B_A>{4\nu^4\over C_0^4}\quad\hbox{on }G.}
 \tag{SH.89}
\]

The total kinetic-energy row \(E'=-\nu S_A\), together with
\(B_A\le ES_A\), pays

\[
 \int_0^{T_*}B_A\,dt
 \le {E(0)^2\over2\nu},
 \qquad
 \boxed{|G|\le {C_0^4E(0)^2\over8\nu^5}.}
 \tag{SH.90}
\]

It also identifies exactly how far the inverse-overlap row reaches. With
\(h_A=X_A/S_A\), (SH.36g0), (SH.88), and (SH.89) give

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_G{1\over h_A}\sqrt{C_A\over B_A}\,dt
 \le {C_0^4E(0)\over4\nu^5}.}
 \tag{SH.91}
\]

Because \(X_A/h_A=S_A\), interpolation between the energy row and
(SH.91) gives, for \(0\le\theta\le1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_G {X_A^{1-\theta}(C_A/B_A)^{\theta/2}\over h_A}\,dt
 \le {C_0^{4\theta}E(0)\over
          4^\theta\nu^{1+4\theta}}.}
 \tag{SH.92}
\]

This family pays only one inverse-overlap copy of
\(\sqrt{C_A/B_A}\). Recovering \(C_A/B_A\) needs a second copy;
recovering \(X_A\) requires removing the unbounded \(h_A^{-1}\) weight.
Neither follows from the installed rows.

The endpoint is sharp at the level of those scalar moment laws. With
\(\tau=T-t\), choose

\[
 q(\tau)=q_0-b\tau^\beta,\qquad q_0>{\nu^2\over C_0^2},\qquad
 e(\tau)^2=4\nu\int_0^\tau q(\sigma)^2\,d\sigma,\qquad k={q\over e},
 \tag{SH.93}
\]

and give both sectors the common-shell moments

\[
 \mathcal E_s=e,\qquad h_s=q,\qquad
 A_s={2q^2\over e},\qquad D_s={2q^3\over e^2}.
 \tag{SH.94}
\]

The sector energy and cross-carrier identities are then exact with
\(\mathcal R_\times=0\), while the current inequality is admissible near
\(T\). Yet

\[
 S_A, {D\over h_A},\ {1\over h_A}\sqrt{C_A\over B_A}
 \asymp\tau^{-1/2}\in L^1,
 \qquad
 X_A\sim{q_0^2\over\nu}\tau^{-1}\notin L^1.
 \tag{SH.95}
\]

This is an estimate-level sharpness profile, not a realizable
Navier--Stokes counterhistory. It proves that the new floor and weighted
action do not supply the overlap criterion.

### 8.7 The exact q-tail is a positive parabolic source-coherence first moment

The mild equation now gives a canonical signed-history object without
taking an absolute value of the cubic current. Let

\[
 Q:=-\mathbb P(u\cdot\nabla u),\qquad
 S(t):=e^{-\nu t\Lambda^2},\qquad
 u(t)=S(t)u_0+w(t),\qquad
 w(t)=\int_0^tS(t-s)Q(s)\,ds.
 \tag{SH.96}
\]

Then the complete prefix splits exactly as

\[
 \int_0^TP_H(t)\,dt
 =I_0(T)+\mathcal K_T,
 \qquad
 I_0(T):=\int_0^T(\Lambda S(t)u_0,Q(t))\,dt.
 \tag{SH.97}
\]

For \(f=\Lambda^{-1/2}Q\), self-adjointness of the heat semigroup gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal K_T={1\over2}\int_0^T\!\!\int_0^T
 (\Lambda^2S(|t-s|)f(s),f(t))\,ds\,dt.}
 \tag{SH.98}
\]

Extend \(f\) by zero outside \((0,T)\), and use
\(\widehat g(\omega)=\int e^{-it\omega}g(t)\,dt\). Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal K_T={1\over2\pi}\iint
 {\nu|\xi|^4\over\nu^2|\xi|^4+\omega^2}
 \left|\widehat{\mathbf1_{(0,T)}f}(\omega,\xi)\right|^2
 \,d\xi d\omega\ge0.}
 \tag{SH.99}
\]

Equivalently, testing the Duhamel daughter by \(\Lambda w\) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal K_T={1\over2}\|\Lambda^{1/2}w(T)\|_2^2
 +\nu\int_0^T\|\Lambda^{3/2}w(t)\|_2^2\,dt.}
 \tag{SH.100}
\]

Here \(w=u-S(t)u_0\) is the history-created part of this same velocity
field, not a second participant or an independently evolving comparison
solution.

For the smooth rapidly decaying data in the Clay problem, the old-data term
is uniformly finite. Indeed,

\[
 |I_0(T)|
 \le\|u_0\|_2^2\int_0^T
 \|\nabla\Lambda S(t)u_0\|_\infty\,dt
 \lesssim {\|u_0\|_2^2\|\widehat u_0\|_1\over\nu}.
 \tag{SH.101}
\]

Thus the signed-prefix theorem is equivalent to a uniform bound for the
positive actions \(\mathcal K_T\). Positivity is pointwise in the terminal
prefix \(T\), not monotonicity in \(T\): enlarging the zero-extension window
adds cross terms of either sign.

The exact energy payment becomes visible after lowering one spatial moment.
Set \(g=\Lambda^{-1}Q\), with its zero mode defined as zero, and define the
prefix-dependent positive measure

\[
 d\mu_T(\xi,\omega):={1\over2\pi}
 {\nu|\xi|^4\over\nu^2|\xi|^4+\omega^2}
 \left|\widehat{\mathbf1_{(0,T)}g}(\omega,\xi)\right|^2
 \,d\xi d\omega.
 \tag{SH.102}
\]

Then

\[
 \mathcal K_T=\iint|\xi|\,d\mu_T,
 \qquad
 \iint d\mu_T
 ={1\over2}\|w(T)\|_2^2
 +\nu\int_0^T\|\Lambda w(t)\|_2^2\,dt
 \le2\|u_0\|_2^2.
 \tag{SH.103}
\]

The last bound follows from \(w=u-S(t)u_0\), the kinetic-energy equality
for \(u\), and the heat-energy equality for \(S(t)u_0\). Therefore energy
pays every fixed low-frequency part,

\[
 \iint_{|\xi|\le K}|\xi|\,d\mu_T
 \le K\iint d\mu_T
 \le2K\|u_0\|_2^2.
 \tag{SH.104}
\]

The q-adic remainder has exactly the complementary high-pass form. If
\(\Pi(k,t)=\int_k^\infty T(r,t)\,dr\), put

\[
 \mathscr R_K(T):=\int_0^T\int_K^\infty\Pi(k,t)\,dk\,dt,
 \qquad M_K:=(\Lambda-K)_+.
 \tag{SH.105}
\]

Layer cake gives

\[
 \int_K^\infty\Pi(k,t)\,dk=(M_Ku(t),Q(t)),
 \tag{SH.106}
\]

and the exact Duhamel split is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr R_K(T)=I_{K,0}(T)+\mathcal K_{K,T},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_{K,T}=\iint(|\xi|-K)_+\,d\mu_T\ge0.}
 \tag{SH.107}
\]

Here

\[
 I_{K,0}(T):=\int_0^T(M_KS(t)u_0,Q(t))\,dt,
 \qquad
 \sup_{K>0,T<T_*}|I_{K,0}(T)|
 \lesssim {\|u_0\|_2^2\|\widehat u_0\|_1\over\nu}.
 \tag{SH.108}
\]

For every fixed \(K>0\), (SH.103)--(SH.108) prove the exact equivalence

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{T<T_*}\int_0^TP_H<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{T<T_*}\mathscr R_K(T)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{T<T_*}\mathcal K_{K,T}<\infty.}
 \tag{SH.109}
\]

This is the intrinsic signed \(q^j\)-valued shared-face current, not event
counting: for \(K_j(r)=e^rq^j\),

\[
 \int_K^\infty\Pi(k,t)\,dk
 =\int_0^{\log q}\sum_{K_j(r)\ge K}
 K_j(r)\Pi(K_j(r),t)\,dr.
 \tag{SH.110}
\]

As a multiplier on an arbitrary source, (SH.102) suppresses oscillations with
\(|\omega|\gg\nu|\xi|^2\). It does not give a hidden half derivative on the
genuine Navier--Stokes source. If \(\widetilde w=\mathbf1_{(0,T)}w\), then in
distributions

\[
 \mathbf1_{(0,T)}Q
 =(\partial_t+\nu\Lambda^2)\widetilde w
 +\delta_Tw(T).
 \tag{SH.110a}
\]

The interior factor \(|i\omega+\nu|\xi|^2|^2\) in (SH.110a) exactly cancels
the resolvent denominator in (SH.102), while the terminal delta supplies the
stock. Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal K_{K,T}
 ={1\over2}\|M_K^{1/2}w(T)\|_2^2
 +\nu\int_0^T\|\Lambda M_K^{1/2}w(t)\|_2^2\,dt,\qquad
 M_K=(\Lambda-K)_+.}
 \tag{SH.110b}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \mathcal K_{K,T}\le\mathcal K_T
 \le K\iint d\mu_T+\mathcal K_{K,T}
 \le2K\|u_0\|_2^2+\mathcal K_{K,T}.
 \tag{SH.110c}
\]

The high-pass Gram is therefore norm-equivalent, up to an energy-paid low
piece, to the ordinary Fujita--Kato critical stock and dissipation of the same
Duhamel-created part. The coherence picture locates the source in spacetime
frequency; it is not itself a smoothing payment. A clean stronger supplier is

\[
 \int_0^{T_*}\|\Lambda^{-1/2}Q(t)\|_2^2\,dt<\infty,
 \tag{SH.111}
\]

but the ordinary Sobolev product bound asks for
\(\int_0^{T_*}(A_++A_-)^2dt\), while energy pays only its first power. The
positive Gram identity is therefore an exact equivalent representation of the
remaining theorem, not a strict reduction and not its proof.

### 8.8 Balanced common mode does not orient the quartic; phase and amplitude shape split

The same true-Leray three-wave comparison family gives a sharper obstruction

 \widehat u(k_1)=a h_1,\qquad
 \widehat u(k_2)=b h_2,\qquad
 \widehat u(k_3)=c e^{i\phi}\overline{h_3},
 \qquad a,b,c\ge0.
 \tag{SH.112}
\]

Direct expansion of the complete Leray convolution gives

 \boxed{P_H=Gabc\cos\phi,\qquad
 G=\sqrt2(3-\sqrt5)=1.0803630269509052\ldots,}
 \tag{SH.113}

whereas the exact quartic Stokes remainder is

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak R
 =C_{12}a^2b^2+C_{13}a^2c^2+C_{23}b^2c^2,}
 \tag{SH.114}

with

 \begin{aligned}
 C_{12}&=-{3\over5}+{7\sqrt5\over25}

          =0.19514100477560306\ldots,\\

 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SH.115}

Thus the cubic changes sign under a phase reversal while the quartic is

 P_H(-u)=-P_H(u),\qquad \mathfrak R_\nu(-u)=\mathfrak R_\nu(u).
 \tag{SH.116}
\]

The positive coefficient \(C_{12}\) persists when \(c=0\). The initial datum

preserve the one-helicity plane. It is exactly the distinction from a
homochirally decimated system.

For this family the two helical critical stocks are

\[
 h_+=a^2+2b^2,\qquad h_-=\sqrt5\,c^2.
 \tag{SH.117}
\]

Set \(a=1\), \(x=b^2\), and enforce exact balance by

 \mathfrak R_{\rm bal}(x)
 ={2C_{23}\over\sqrt5}x^2
  +\left(C_{12}+{2C_{13}+C_{23}\over\sqrt5}\right)x
  +{C_{13}\over\sqrt5}
 =-0.0695887248904604x^2
  +0.16584409200505473x
  +0.08726971037517191.
 \tag{SH.118}
\]

Its unique positive zero is

\[
 x_*=2.8268365984869317,
 \qquad b_*=\sqrt{x_*}=1.6813198977252757.
 \tag{SH.119}
\]

Choosing \(\cos\phi>0\) gives \(P_H>0\) on both sides of this transition,
while \(\mathfrak R_{\rm bal}\) is positive below it and negative above it.
A still stronger matched pair fixes the whole stock-plus-current snapshot.
Put \(c^2=1/\sqrt5\), \(a^2=1-2b^2\), and choose the phases so that
\(P_H=0.1\). Then

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 b^2&\phi&h_+&h_-&\mathfrak R\\ \hline
 0.40&1.0594414705795425&1&1&\phantom{-}0.005624119792937584\\
 0.45&0.8599452104397955&1&1&-0.005756035546339052
 \end{array}
 \tag{SH.120}
\]

Thus even identical \((h_+,h_-,P_H)\) does not determine the quartic sign.
Exact common-mode balance, helicity imbalance, current sign and size, and
phase-only averaging therefore do not orient the remainder. Any surviving
signed resummation or high-pass Gram first-moment theorem must couple the
phase-odd current to the phase-even evolution of amplitude shape across the
complete q-network. This is a comparison-data obstruction only, not a
decaying \(\mathbb R^3\) history or a counterexample to regularity.

### 8.9 The energy-paid Gram mass has the wrong scaling for its first moment

The exact source-coherence representation also exposes a sharp scaling
boundary. For the same-viscosity Navier--Stokes rescaling

\[
 u_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda u(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),\qquad
 T_\lambda={T\over\lambda^2},\qquad
 w_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda w(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),
\]

write

\[
 \mathcal M_m[u;T]:=\iint|\xi|^m\,d\mu_T(\xi,\omega).
\]

The stock/action formulas (SH.100) and (SH.103), or a direct Fourier change
of variables, give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal M_m[u_\lambda;T_\lambda]
 =\lambda^{m-1}\mathcal M_m[u;T].}
 \tag{SH.121}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \mathcal M_0[u_\lambda;T_\lambda]
 =\lambda^{-1}\mathcal M_0[u;T],\qquad
 \mathcal M_1[u_\lambda;T_\lambda]
 =\mathcal M_1[u;T],
 \tag{SH.122}
\]

and the high-pass action obeys

\[
 \mathcal K_{\lambda K,T_\lambda}[u_\lambda]
 =\mathcal K_{K,T}[u].
 \tag{SH.123}
\]

Therefore no estimate of the form
\(\mathcal M_1\le C_\nu\mathcal M_0^\alpha\), with \(\alpha>0\) and no
second scale-covariant input, can upgrade the energy-paid Gram mass to the
critical first moment. This is an exact lawful scaling obstruction across
smooth decaying Navier--Stokes data, not a counterhistory on one fixed datum.
It does not rule out a datum-dependent bound using additional initial or
dynamical information. It says precisely what that information must do:
prevent the bounded Gram mass from escaping to an unbounded spatial
barycenter. The remaining theorem is therefore a source-shape/coherence
theorem, not a scalar interpolation of the already paid mass.

### 8.10 Critical-element normalization either stays unbounded or leaves fixed viscosity

The scaling boundary also rules out an automatic compactness escape. Suppose
the desired prefix bound fails along \(T_n\uparrow T_*\), and put

\[
 L_n:=\mathcal M_1[u;T_n]\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \kappa_n:={\mathcal M_1[u;T_n]\over\mathcal M_0[u;T_n]}
 \longrightarrow\infty,
 \tag{SH.124}
\]

where the second conclusion uses the uniform energy bound for
\(\mathcal M_0\). The exact two-parameter Navier--Stokes scaling is

\[
 u^{a,c}(s,y):=a\,u(ac\,s,cy),\qquad
 \nu^{a,c}={a\over c}\nu.
 \tag{SH.125}
\]

With the heat/Duhamel split taken at the corresponding initial time, its Gram
moments transform as

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal M_m[u^{a,c};T/(ac)]
 =a^2c^{m-3}\mathcal M_m[u;T].}
 \tag{SH.126}
\]

Keeping the viscosity fixed forces \(a=c\). Then
\(\mathcal M_1[u^{a,a}]=\mathcal M_1[u]\): choosing
\(c\simeq\kappa_n^{-1}\) can recenter the escaping spatial frequency, but it
cannot make the critical action bounded. Conversely, normalizing the action
requires

\[
 1=\mathcal M_1[u^{a_n,c_n};T_n/(a_nc_n)]
 =\left({a_n\over c_n}\right)^2L_n,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\nu_n={a_n\over c_n}\nu={\nu\over\sqrt{L_n}}\longrightarrow0.}
 \tag{SH.127}
\]

Normalizing only the positive measure
\(|\xi|d\mu_{T_n}/L_n\) does produce probability measures, but not velocity
fields satisfying the original nonlinear equation: the missing amplitude
normalization is exactly what changes \(\nu\). Thus ordinary profile
compactness has two outcomes. At fixed viscosity it retains the unbounded
Fujita--Kato action and gives no compact critical element; after action
normalization it enters a vanishing-viscosity/Euler comparison lane, where
nontrivial profiles are not forbidden and no bridge back to the fixed-
viscosity theorem is installed.

This is a checked compactness obstruction, not a proof that every possible
rigidity method fails. A lawful compactness route must first obtain a
fixed-viscosity no-waste/tightness statement that bounds the critical action
of the selected same-history profile. That is additional theorem content,
not a consequence of frequency recentering or measure normalization.

### 8.11 The high-pass Gram is exactly a Dini--Carleson q-tail

The first moment has a precise q-adic form. Fix a homogeneous
Littlewood--Paley decomposition, put

\[
 \lambda_j=q^j,\qquad \gamma_j=\nu\lambda_j^2,\qquad
 g_T=\mathbf1_{(0,T)}g,\qquad
 \mathsf C_j=\gamma_j(\partial_t+\gamma_j)^{-1},
 \tag{SH.128}
\]

and define the viscous-time coherent shell masses

\[
 \alpha_j(T):={1\over\nu}
 \|\mathsf C_j\Delta_jg_T\|_{L^2_{t,x}}^2.
 \tag{SH.129}
\]

On the support of \(\Delta_j\), the multiplier in (SH.102) is comparable,
with constants depending only on the fixed decomposition and on \(q\), to
\(\nu^{-1}\gamma_j^2/(\gamma_j^2+\omega^2)\). Consequently, for
\(\lambda_J\) above a fixed multiple of \(K\),

\[
 \iint d\mu_T\asymp\sum_j\alpha_j(T),\qquad
 \mathcal K_{K,T}\asymp
 \sum_{j\ge J}\lambda_j\alpha_j(T),
 \tag{SH.130}
\]

up to finitely many cutoff-boundary shells. Set
\(A_j(T)=\sum_{p\ge j}\alpha_p(T)\). Finite Abel summation gives

\[
 \sum_{p=J}^N\lambda_p\alpha_p
 =\lambda_JA_J+(1-q^{-1})\sum_{p=J+1}^N\lambda_pA_p
  -\lambda_NA_{N+1}.
 \tag{SH.131}
\]

Thus the open first moment is exactly the Dini--Carleson tail

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{T<T_*}\sum_{j\ge J}\lambda_jA_j(T)<\infty.}
 \tag{SH.132}
\]

A weak Carleson estimate is insufficient. Indeed,
\(\alpha_j=(1-q^{-1})\lambda_j^{-1}\) gives
\(A_j=\lambda_j^{-1}\), so
\(\sup_j\lambda_jA_j=1\), while
\(\sum_j\lambda_j\alpha_j=\infty\). A concrete local sufficient incidence
theorem would instead prove, for
\(b_j(T)=\lambda_jA_j(T)\),

\[
 b_{j+1}(T)\le(1-\varepsilon)b_j(T)+r_j(T),\qquad
 \sup_{T<T_*}\sum_{j\ge J}r_j(T)<\infty,
 \tag{SH.133}
\]

where \(0<\varepsilon<1\), \(r_j\ge0\), and the defects are explicitly paid by
the datum. Summing (SH.133) yields
\(\varepsilon\sum_{j\ge J}b_j\le b_J+\sum_{j\ge J}r_j\). The defects may
not be defined tautologically from the desired tail; they must come from the
complete triadic phase/amplitude incidence of this same evolution.

The q-ledger itself cannot supply that contraction. One continuing scalar
carrier may start shell \(\lambda_j\) with kinetic energy
\(E_*/\lambda_j\), decay there for

\[
 e_j(t)={E_*\over\lambda_j}
 e^{-2\nu\lambda_j^2(t-t_j)},\qquad
 t_{j+1}-t_j={\log q\over2\nu\lambda_j^2},
 \tag{SH.134}
\]

and then transfer the remaining \(E_*/\lambda_{j+1}\) across the adjacent
face. The nearest-neighbour shell balances and energy equality hold
distributionally, total time and kinetic dissipation are finite, and
\(E_*/q\le\sum_j\lambda_je_j(t)\le E_*\). Nevertheless, every transfer
and every shell's critical viscous loss is \(E_*(1-q^{-1})\), so the
critical prefix diverges by the fixed amount \(E_*(1-q^{-1})\) per scale.
Smoothing the transfer on a
viscous window asks for flux \(\asymp\nu E_*\lambda_j\), while the ordinary
Bernstein/paraproduct capacity is

\[
 \lambda_j^{5/2}e_j^{3/2}\asymp E_*^{3/2}\lambda_j.
 \tag{SH.135}
\]

It gives no scale gain on the dangerous branch. This is an exact q-shell
balance model, not a realization by an unforced Navier--Stokes solution.

Nor does the divergence-free Leray null form alone supply a missing
\(\lambda_j^{-\varepsilon}\). For one fixed mixed-helicity divergence-free
profile \(U\) with
\(G=\Lambda^{-1}\mathbb P(U\cdot\nabla U)\ne0\), the externally prescribed
viscous-scale packet

\[
 u_\lambda(t,x)=\nu\lambda
 \chi\!\left(\nu\lambda^2(t-t_0)\right)U(\lambda x)
 \tag{SH.136}
\]

has kinetic energy plus action \(\asymp\nu^2/\lambda\); its genuine
quadratic Leray source has Gram mass \(\asymp\nu^2/\lambda\) but first
moment \(\asymp\nu^2\). The packet is not a Navier--Stokes history. It proves
only that localization, incompressibility, Leray projection, paraproduct
capacity, and mixed-helicity algebra furnish no per-shell
\(\lambda^{-\varepsilon}\) gain toward (SH.133). Any contraction must use
actual evolution incidence.

### 8.12 A lawful helical resummation leaves one causal reconvergence term

There is a source split which preserves the triad roles without pretending
that their Fourier outputs are orthogonal. Write
\(B(a,b)=-\mathbb P(a\cdot\nabla b)\) and define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U&=\Pi_+B(u_-,u_-)+\Pi_-B(u_+,u_+),\\
 H&=\Pi_+B(u_+,u_+)+\Pi_-B(u_-,u_-),\\
 M&=Q-H-U.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SH.137}
\]

Here \(U\) is the undoubled unique-helicity output source, \(H\) is the
homochiral output source, and \(M\) contains the mixed-input paired-leg
sources. The triad factorization in Section 2 gives

\[
 (\Lambda u,H)=0,\qquad
 (\Lambda u,U)=(\Lambda u,M)={P_H\over2}.
 \tag{SH.138}
\]

Hence \(Z:=Q-2U=H+M-U\) is exactly critical-work-null. For

\[
 \mathcal C_T(F,G):=\int_{0<s<t<T}
 (\Lambda S(t-s)F(s),G(t))\,ds\,dt,\qquad
 I_F(T):=\int_0^T(\Lambda S(t)u_0,F(t))\,dt,
 \tag{SH.139}
\]

substituting \(u=S(t)u_0+w_Q\) into that null-work identity gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^TP_H(t)\,dt
 =2I_U(T)+2\mathcal C_T(U,U)+2\mathcal C_T(H+M,U).}
 \tag{SH.140}
\]

The first dynamic term is positive,

\[
 \mathcal C_T(U,U)
 ={1\over2}\|\Lambda^{1/2}w_U(T)\|_2^2
 +\nu\int_0^T\|\Lambda^{3/2}w_U\|_2^2dt\ge0,
 \tag{SH.141}
\]

and reapplying the old-data estimate from (SH.101) channelwise, after moving
\(\Pi_\pm\) onto the smooth heat datum, gives
\(\sup_T|I_U(T)|\lesssim
\nu^{-1}\|u_0\|_2^2\|\widehat u_0\|_1\). Section 8.16 completes the exact
comparison with the full Gram and proves that this resummation is causal
anatomy, not a strict reduction. The sole new signed term in that anatomy is
earlier \(H+M\) source reconverging into later unique-output source \(U\).

No fixed helical projection removes it. At fixed output \((k,s)\), every
ancestry lies in the same one-complex-dimensional line
\(\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_s(k)\). An explicit real periodic comparison takes
\(k=(1,1,1)\), \(p=(1,0,0)\), \(q=(0,1,1)\), \(r=(0,1,0)\), and
\(s=(1,0,1)\). Negative-helical inputs at \(p,q\) create the unique
positive output at \(k\), while positive-helical inputs at \(r,s\) create a
homochiral positive output in the same line; the conjugate modes give the
symmetric negative output. Rotating the \(r\)-mode phase by \(\pi\) changes
the full real-field pairing

\[
 (\Lambda(H+M),U)=-0.0700443330123\ldots
 \quad\hbox{to}\quad
 +0.0700443330123\ldots.
 \tag{SH.142}
\]

Thus \(\mathcal C_T(H+M,U)=
\tfrac12T^2(\Lambda(H+M)(0),U(0))+O(T^3)\) has both signs on actual local
smooth periodic solutions. This comparison is not a decaying counterhistory.

An instantaneous amplitude/phase split is not a Gram orthogonalization. For
a helical coefficient \(a=re^{i\theta}\) and source
\(q=e^{i\theta}(\rho+i\tau)\), one has
\(r'+\nu|k|^2r=\rho\) and \(r\theta'=\tau\), but the cross-time Gram retains

\[
 -\rho(s)\tau(t)\sin(\theta(t)-\theta(s)).
 \tag{SH.143}
\]

The split is undefined when a previously empty mode is first created.
Projecting each ancestry before summing replaces the physical
\(|\sum_\tau q_\tau|^2\) by the different direct-sum quantity
\(\sum_\tau|q_\tau|^2\). They are not ordered, and energy pays only the
joined source. Therefore (SH.140), not an orthogonal ancestry ledger, is the
lawful helical reduction.

### 8.13 Maximal regularity localizes the open action to the all-high relay

For a nonnegative spatial multiplier \(M\), set

\[
 \Gamma_M(g_T):={1\over2\pi}\iint
 M(|\xi|){\nu|\xi|^4\over\nu^2|\xi|^4+\omega^2}
 |\widehat{g_T}(\omega,\xi)|^2\,d\xi d\omega.
 \tag{SH.144}
\]

It equals
\(\tfrac12\|M^{1/2}w(T)\|_2^2+
\nu\int_0^T\|\Lambda M^{1/2}w\|_2^2dt\), and it has the exact source-square
decomposition

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_M={}&{1\over\nu}\|M^{1/2}g_T\|_{L^2_{t,x}}^2\\
 &-{1\over2\nu}\int_0^T\!\left(
 \|M^{1/2}S(t/2)g(t)\|_2^2+
 \|M^{1/2}S((T-t)/2)g(t)\|_2^2\right)dt\\
 &-{1\over4}\int_0^T\!\int_0^T
 \|\Lambda M^{1/2}S(|t-s|/2)(g(t)-g(s))\|_2^2\,ds\,dt.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SH.145}
\]

These are exact endpoint heat-layer and parabolic-increment subtractions.
The energy law does not lower-bound them by the missing half moment. For a
constant one-frequency pulse, with \(a=\nu|k|^2\),

\[
 {\Gamma_M\over\nu^{-1}M(k)|g_0|^2T}
 =1-{1-e^{-aT}\over aT}.
 \tag{SH.146}
\]

It gains \(aT/2+O((aT)^2)\) for a short pulse and tends to the crude
constant for a long one. A short source need not itself persist for a
viscous lifetime: its terminal daughter remains because the full-line heat
response dissipates that stock after \(T\). This is why the coherence lens
is exact without being a smoothing theorem.

There is, however, a strict all-high reduction. Suppose a finite maximal
time \(T_*\) exists, fix \(L>0\), write \(u_\ell=P_{\le L}u\),
\(u_h=P_{>L}u\), and put

\[
 g_{hhh}:=-\Lambda^{-1}\mathbb P\operatorname{div}
 P_{>4L}(u_h\otimes u_h),\qquad r_L=g-g_{hhh}.
 \tag{SH.147}
\]

Bernstein and the kinetic-energy equality give

\[
 \int_0^{T_*}\|\Lambda^{1/2}r_L\|_2^2dt
 \le C\|u_0\|_2^4\left(L^4T_*+{L^2\over\nu}\right).
 \tag{SH.148}
\]

Since \(\Gamma_M^{1/2}\) is a Hilbert seminorm (a norm modulo its null space) and
\(M_K=(\Lambda-K)_+\le\Lambda\),

\[
 \left|\sqrt{\Gamma_{M_K}(g)}-
 \sqrt{\Gamma_{M_K}(g_{hhh})}\right|
 \le C^{1/2}\|u_0\|_2^2
 \left({L^4T_*\over\nu}+{L^2\over\nu^2}\right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{SH.149}
\]

Thus low--low, low--high, and high--high-to-fixed-low interactions cannot
cause finite-time divergence. For every fixed \(L\), the open Gram is
equivalent to the complete Leray source with both inputs and output above
\(L\): the all-high relay.

This relay is not energy-controlled across data. The installed fixed-energy
family \(u_A(x,t)=A^3v^{\nu/A}(A^2x,A^5t)\), on
\(T_A\asymp A^{-5}\), satisfies for fixed \(K\)

\[
 \Gamma_{M_K}(T_A)\ge cA^2-O_K(1)
 \tag{SH.150}
\]

at fixed viscosity and fixed \(\|u_A(0)\|_2\). Its normalized viscous age
tends to zero: a sufficiently intense short coherent pulse can leave a large
terminal critical daughter. This varying-data family rules out an energy-only
Gram estimate; it does not refute a datum-dependent bound along one fixed
history.

### 8.14 A positive quadratic critical invariant does not exist

The invariant method can also be exhausted at the natural quadratic spectral
level. Let

\[
 \mathscr I_W(u)
 :={1\over2}\sum_{s=\pm1}\int_0^\infty
 W_s(k)e_s(k)\,dk
 \tag{SH.151}
\]

be a rotation-invariant diagonal quadratic stock, with continuous weights
\(W_s\). If its nonlinear derivative vanishes for every smooth Euler field,
then on every nondegenerate active helical triad

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^3W_{s_i}(\kappa_i)T_i=0
 \quad\hbox{whenever}\quad
 \sum_iT_i=0,\qquad
 \sum_is_i\kappa_iT_i=0.
 \tag{SH.152}
\]

Homochiral triads force \(W_+(k)\) and \(W_-(k)\) separately to be affine:
three-point rate vectors with zero mass and first moment annihilate every
second divided difference. A generic \(++-\) triad then makes the affine line
through two positive-helicity values pass through the negative-helicity
value at signed frequency \(-k\). Connectivity of the nondegenerate triad
set and continuity give

\[
 \boxed{W_s(k)=\alpha+\beta s k.}
 \tag{SH.153}
\]

These are exactly energy and helicity. If the stock is nonnegative for both
helicities at every frequency, then

\[
 \alpha+\beta k\ge0,\qquad
 \alpha-\beta k\ge0\quad(k>0)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\beta=0,\ \alpha\ge0.}
 \tag{SH.154}
\]

Thus the only positive invariant in this class is kinetic energy, whose
scaling degree is minus one. The degree-zero quadratic invariant is helicity,
but it is signed and vanishes on the balanced channel where both critical
stocks can grow. The unsigned critical weight \(W_s(k)=k\) is positive but is
not an Euler invariant; its nonlinear derivative is precisely the open
singleton current.

This classification complements the two-frequency fixed-kernel dichotomy in
Section 8.3. The second scale-covariant input required after (SH.123) cannot
be another fixed positive diagonal quadratic spectral stock. A survivor must
retain signed history, nonlinear phase/amplitude shape, or a genuinely
nonquadratic same-field structure.

### 8.15 The unique-output source is an exact radial-face commutator, but narrowing q does not contract it

The radial gap in (SH.9) has an exact whole-field form. Let
\(v=\Pi_s v\), and let \(v_{\le\rho}\) and \(v_{>\rho}\) be the sharp radial
Fourier pieces of this same helical field. Since
\(\operatorname{curl}v=s\Lambda v\), the divergence-free vector identity gives

\[
 \boxed{
 U_{-s}[v]:=\Pi_{-s}B(v,v)
 =s\Pi_{-s}(v\times\Lambda v).}
 \tag{SH.155}
\]

Spectral layer cake and \(v_{>\rho}\times v_{>\rho}=0\) then give

\[
 \boxed{
 v\times\Lambda v
 =\int_0^\infty v\times v_{>\rho}\,d\rho
 =\int_0^\infty v_{\le\rho}\times v_{>\rho}\,d\rho.}
 \tag{SH.156}
\]

Equivalently, symmetrizing the two input orders at output \(k=p+q\) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat{v\times\Lambda v}(k)
 ={1\over2}\int_{p+q=k}
 (|q|-|p|)\,\widehat v(p)\times\widehat v(q)\,dp.}
 \tag{SH.157}
\]

Thus the unique-output source vanishes for every equal-radius parent pair,
not merely for one selected triad coordinate. It is literally the sum of
cross-radial-face interactions of one helical part of the same velocity
field.

For the q-grid in (SH.128), define

\[
 F_{s,j}:=s\Pi_{-s}\int_{\lambda_j}^{\lambda_{j+1}}
 (u_s)_{\le\rho}\times(u_s)_{>\rho}\,d\rho.
\]

Then, with convergence understood first for smooth compact Fourier support,

\[
 \boxed{U=\sum_{s=\pm1}\sum_jF_{s,j}.}
 \tag{SH.158}
\]

This exact face resolution does not make the faces orthogonal. If

\[
 \mathcal G_T(F,G):={1\over2}
 \bigl(\mathcal C_T(F,G)+\mathcal C_T(G,F)\bigr),
\]

then the positive term in (SH.140) is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_T(U,U)
 =\sum_{s,\sigma}\sum_{j,k}
 \mathcal G_T(F_{s,j},F_{\sigma,k}).}
 \tag{SH.159}
\]

The terms with \(s\ne\sigma\) vanish by output-helicity orthogonality, but all
\(j\ne k\) terms within either fixed \(s\) remain. Different parent faces can
create the same output-helicity line, so replacing the square of the summed
source by a sum of ancestry or face squares would again change the
Navier--Stokes object.

Nor does choosing \(q\downarrow1\) create a small parameter. For every parent
pair,

\[
 \boxed{
 \bigl||p|-|q|\bigr|
 =\int_0^\infty
 \left|\mathbf1_{\{|p|>\rho\}}
       -\mathbf1_{\{|q|>\rho\}}\right|\,d\rho.}
 \tag{SH.160}
\]

A pair inside one q-cell has gap at most
\((q-1)\lambda_j\), but a pair with a fixed nonunit radius ratio crosses
\(\asymp1/\log q\) cells. The crossed cell widths telescope back to its full
radial gap. More sharply, take one two-radius source with radii \(a<b\), let
\(A\) be its projected face density, and let \(\Delta\rho_j\) be the q-cell
pieces of \((a,b)\). Then every face source is
\(F_j=\Delta\rho_jA\), so the Hilbert norm induced by the positive causal Gram
obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal C_T\!\left(\sum_jF_j,\sum_jF_j\right)
 &=(b-a)^2\mathcal C_T(A,A),\\
 \sum_j\mathcal C_T(F_j,F_j)
 &=\left(\sum_j\Delta\rho_j^2\right)\mathcal C_T(A,A)\\
 &\le(q-1)b(b-a)\mathcal C_T(A,A).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.161}
\]

Thus the face-diagonal sum tends to zero as \(q\downarrow1\), while the
actual summed source does not: the off-diagonal cross-face terms restore the
entire fixed radial gap. The fixed-profile rescaling in (SH.136) is the critical scaling
countertest: its relative radial gaps stay fixed while its source first moment
stays scale invariant. Hence the equal-radius null form and a narrow q-grid
alone do not imply (SH.133).

This is an exact refinement of the unique-output coordinate and a checked
obstruction to a static small-cell proof, not Gold closure. A successful
argument must first keep the cross-face terms recombined into the one
endpoint-span amplitude, as in (TL.1365), and then use the actual history to
pay or cancel repeated fixed-ratio spans together with the signed
reconvergence term in (SH.140). Merely resolving the same source into more
faces cannot create the missing reserve.

### 8.16 Helicity isolates the common positive response inside the full Gram

Set

\[
 \mathfrak A_T:=\mathcal C_T(U,U)+\mathcal C_T(H+M,U).
\]

Because \(Z=Q-2U\) is critical-work-null, substitution of
\(u=S(t)u_0+w_Q\) gives

\[
 0=I_Z(T)+\mathcal C_T(Q,Z)
   =I_Z(T)+\mathcal C_T(Q,Q)-2\mathfrak A_T.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak A_T={1\over2}\mathcal C_T(Q,Q)+{1\over2}I_Z(T),
 \qquad I_Z=I_0-2I_U,}
 \tag{SH.161a}
\]

and \(I_Z\) is uniformly datum-bounded by (SH.101) and the channelwise
estimate after (SH.141). Thus completing the signed causal combination
returns one half of the original full Gram modulo bounded old data; it is not
a smaller action. Helicity identifies the positive common mode inside that
Gram. Put

\[
 Q_\pm=\Pi_\pm Q,\qquad
 \mathfrak G_\pm(T):=\mathcal C_T(Q_\pm,Q_\pm)\ge0.
\]

Output-helicity orthogonality and the nonlinear helicity-work identity give

\[
 \mathcal C_T(Q,Q)=\mathfrak G_++\mathfrak G_-,\qquad
 \mathfrak G_+-\mathfrak G_-=-I_{\rm curl}(T),
 \tag{SH.162}
\]

where

\[
 I_{\rm curl}(T):=\int_0^T(\operatorname{curl}S(t)u_0,Q(t))\,dt,
 \qquad
 |I_{\rm curl}(T)|\lesssim
 {\|u_0\|_2^2\|\widehat u_0\|_1\over\nu}.
 \tag{SH.163}
\]

Indeed, moving the transport derivative onto
\(\operatorname{curl}S(t)u_0\) leaves a second-order heat multiplier whose
time integral is \(\nu^{-1}\). Consequently,

\[
 2\min(\mathfrak G_+,\mathfrak G_-)
 \le\mathcal C_T(Q,Q)
 \le2\min(\mathfrak G_+,\mathfrak G_-)+C(u_0,\nu),
 \tag{SH.164}
\]

and \(\mathfrak A_T\) is, modulo a bounded datum term, exactly this common
positive helical response action. Energy pays the corresponding zeroth
spatial Gram mass; helicity pays only the difference in (SH.162). Equal
q-shell masses in the two helicities can have finite total mass and zero
signed difference while their common first moment diverges. That scalar
profile is an identity-level obstruction, not a Navier--Stokes history.

The causal skew does not add a free estimate. Its time-frequency multiplier,
relative to the positive symmetric heat multiplier, has size
\(|\omega|/(\nu|\xi|^2)\), which is unbounded. Controlling it requires new
temporal regularity. Thus neither causal completion, heat positivity, energy,
nor helicity bounds the common all-high Gram; actual same-history
anti-replenishment remains necessary.

### 8.17 The minimal q-defect is an output-shell receiver-incidence theorem

Fix \(0<\varepsilon<1\). The adjacent contraction (SH.133) can be stripped
of its arbitrary defect.
Since \(A_j=\alpha_j+A_{j+1}\) and \(b_j=\lambda_jA_j\),

\[
 b_{j+1}-(1-\varepsilon)b_j
 =\lambda_j\left[(q-1+\varepsilon)A_{j+1}
 -(1-\varepsilon)\alpha_j\right].
 \tag{SH.165}
\]

Thus the smallest admissible nonnegative defect is the positive part on the
right. A concrete sufficient local theorem, equivalent to (SH.133) with its
minimal defect, is

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{T<T_*}\sum_{j\ge J}\lambda_j
 \left[(q-1+\varepsilon)A_{j+1}(T)
 -(1-\varepsilon)\alpha_j(T)\right]_+<\infty.}
 \tag{SH.166}
\]

Up to the fixed Littlewood--Paley constants, \(\alpha_j\) is the
endpoint-plus-viscous action of the Duhamel-created output in shell \(j\).
Therefore (SH.166) asks the actual receiver incidence to replenish the tail
strictly more slowly than the current shell pays it. It is an output-shell
statement, not a parent-ancestry ledger.

The radial-face identity explains why (SH.166) does not follow from narrowing
q. A single parent pair \((p,q)\) with output \(k=p+q\) crosses every face
between \(|p|\) and \(|q|\), but each face piece is a positive scalar width
\(\ell_j\) times the same time-dependent output vector \(\Phi_{pq}\). Hence

\[
 \mathcal G_T(F_j^{pq},F_\ell^{pq})
 =\ell_j\ell_\ell\,
 \mathcal G_T(\Phi_{pq},\Phi_{pq}).
 \tag{SH.167}
\]

The face Gram is rank one and reconstructs
\((|q|-|p|)^2\); it does not populate intermediate output shells. Input
viscosity multiplies the same parent product, output viscosity applies the
same \(k\)-kernel, and receiver pairing supplies no face-dependent sign.
Near-equal parents gain only \(O(q-1)\), which is multiplied on the dangerous
branch by the unbounded critical amplitude; fixed-ratio parents recover their
whole gap across \(O(1/\log q)\) faces. A scale-uniform \(\varepsilon\) in
(SH.166) must therefore come from cumulative destructive incidence among
different parent pairs and reconvergence sources in the actual history. That
is the first unproved bridge; no same-history counterexample is asserted.

### 8.18 The radial null does not lower the global source-square derivative

The equal-radius cancellation in (SH.157) does not yield the energy-level
endpoint estimate

\[
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}U_{-s}[u_s]\|_2
 \lesssim\|u_s\|_2\|\Lambda u_s\|_2.
 \tag{SH.168}
\]

To see the power failure, take a real Schwartz positive-helicity field \(v\)
with two small Fourier packets near \(p=(1,0,0)\) and \(q=(0,2,0)\), plus
their conjugates. Direct Leray projection at \(p+q\) gives a nonzero
negative-helicity output, so \(U_-[v]\ne0\). For the fixed-energy dilation
\(u_N(x)=N^{3/2}v(Nx)\),

\[
 \|u_N\|_2=\|v\|_2,\qquad
 \|\Lambda u_N\|_2=N\|\Lambda v\|_2,
 \qquad
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}U_-[u_N]\|_2
 =N^2\|\Lambda^{-1/2}U_-[v]\|_2.
 \tag{SH.169}
\]

Thus (SH.168) fails by one full power of \(N\), not by a logarithm. The
scale-sharp global estimate retains the full derivative:

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}U_{-s}[u_s]\|_2
 \lesssim\|u_s\times\Lambda u_s\|_{3/2}
 \lesssim\|\Lambda u_s\|_2^2.}
 \tag{SH.170}
\]

Here the first step is Hardy--Littlewood--Sobolev and the second is
\(\dot H^1\hookrightarrow L^6\). The critical Sobolev product alternative
\(\|\Lambda^{1/2}u_s\|_2\|\Lambda^{3/2}u_s\|_2\) has the same homogeneity.
Consequently the crude source-square estimate gives only

\[
 \mathcal C_T(U,U)
 \lesssim{1\over\nu}\int_0^T\|\Lambda u_s(t)\|_2^4dt,
 \tag{SH.171}
\]

or its critical-stock-times-critical-dissipation version. Energy pays
\(\int\|\Lambda u_s\|_2^2dt\), not its square. The radial null improves
equal-radius geometry but does not pay the positive \(U\) self-Gram. This
varying-data counterfamily rejects a universal energy--enstrophy endpoint;
it does not rule out additional cancellation along one actual history.

### 8.19 The defect is a log-frequency hazard, not a static parent-pair sign

The minimal defect in (SH.166) has a sharper probability meaning. Whenever
\(A_j>0\), define the conditional shell hazard
\[
 h_j:={\alpha_j\over A_j}.
\]
Since \(A_{j+1}=A_j-\alpha_j\),
\[
 \boxed{
 r_j^{\min}
 =[b_{j+1}-(1-\varepsilon)b_j]_+
 =b_j\,[q-1+\varepsilon-qh_j]_+.}
 \tag{SH.172}
\]
Thus defect-free contraction at one interface is exactly
\[
 \boxed{
 h_j\ge {q-1+\varepsilon\over q}.}
 \tag{SH.173}
\]
Conditioned on coherent response above \(\lambda_j\), a fixed fraction beyond
the scale-neutral amount must land in the current output shell. The bad law
\(\alpha_j=(1-q^{-1})A_j\) has
\(h_j=(q-1)/q\): it lies exactly on the no-contraction boundary.

This becomes particularly transparent on logarithmic frequency. Put
\(q=e^\delta\), \(\varepsilon=\kappa\delta+o(\delta)\), \(y=j\delta\), and
write \(a(y)=-A'(y)\ge0\), \(b(y)=e^yA(y)\). Then
\[
 {b(y+\delta)-(1-\varepsilon)b(y)\over\delta}
 \longrightarrow
 b'(y)+\kappa b(y)
 =e^y\bigl[(1+\kappa)A(y)-a(y)\bigr].
 \tag{SH.174}
\]
Accordingly the normalized discrete defect becomes
\[
 \boxed{
 \int e^y\bigl[(1+\kappa)A(y)-a(y)\bigr]_+\,dy.}
 \tag{SH.175}
\]
The scale-neutral tail \(A(y)=ce^{-y}\) is the zero-drift profile
\(b(y)\equiv c\) and has hazard \(a/A=1\). The missing contraction is
therefore a strict excess decay of the coherent response tail under the
log-frequency dilation generator, modulo a datum-paid positive defect. This
is an exact continuous coordinate for (SH.166), not a new payer.

Nor does the presence of different parent pairs force the required sign.
Take
\[
 k=(1,1,1),\quad
 (p,q)=((1,0,0),(0,1,1)),\quad
 (r,s)=((0,1,0),(1,0,1)).
\]
Give all four parent modes negative helicity and add their conjugates. Both
pairs create a positive-helicity unique output at \(k\). With unit helical
amplitudes, and with a phase \(\phi\) on the \(r\)-mode, direct Leray
projection gives for the contributions supported at \(\{\pm k\}\)
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\Lambda U_+^{pq},U_+^{rs}(0))
 &=0.009980094416603512\ldots,\\
 (\Lambda U_+^{pq},U_+^{rs}(\pi))
 &=-0.009980094416603512\ldots,\\
 (\Lambda U_+^{pq},U_+^{pq})
 &=(\Lambda U_+^{rs},U_+^{rs})
 =0.011524020395927839\ldots .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{SH.176}
\]

For fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the equivalence between the Dini tail and the
minimal-defect sum is exact. Since
\(r_j^{\min}\le b_{j+1}\) and
\(b_{j+1}\le(1-\varepsilon)b_j+r_j^{\min}\), summation gives, for every
\(N>J\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{j=J}^{N-1}r_j^{\min}
 &\le\sum_{j=J+1}^{N}b_j,\\
 \varepsilon\sum_{j=J+1}^{N-1}b_j+b_N
 &\le(1-\varepsilon)b_J+
       \sum_{j=J}^{N-1}r_j^{\min}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.177}
\]

Thus \(\sum_jr_j^{\min}<\infty\) if and only if
\(\sum_jb_j<\infty\). Receiver incidence is a useful causal target only when
the positive defect is bounded from an independent datum-finite same-field
action; defining the defect from \(b\) does not reduce the theorem.

Hence two distinct lawful unique-output parent pairs can reinforce almost
coherently or cancel in the same one-dimensional output-helicity line. The
same signs persist in their short-time causal cross-Gram by continuity. This
is a periodic local comparison, not a decaying counterhistory.

Equations (SH.172)--(SH.177) remove one ambiguity from the open theorem.
The positive part in (SH.166) is algebraically equivalent to the desired
Dini tail and cannot be declared paid by naming it incidence. Static radial
gaps, helical projection, Leray geometry, and the mere multiplicity of parent
pairs do not create the strict hazard. A proof must show that the actual
Navier--Stokes history either produces cumulative temporal destructive
incidence among those parents and reconvergence sources or charges the
positive log-frequency hazard defect to an independently datum-finite
same-field action.

### 8.20 The reinforcing parents are Stokes-resonant

The comparison in (SH.176) is not immediately destroyed by viscosity or by
the first nonlinear tangent. Let \(u_0\) contain exactly the four
negative-helicity parent modes \(p,q,r,s\) there and their conjugates.
Direct Fourier support gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat{Q(u_0)}(\ell)=0
 \quad\text{for}\quad
 \ell\in\{\pm p,\pm q,\pm r,\pm s\}.}
 \tag{SH.178}
\]
Thus the actual Navier--Stokes tangent of every selected parent coefficient
at \(t=0\) is purely viscous. Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 |p|^2+|q|^2=|r|^2+|s|^2=|k|^2=3.}
 \tag{SH.179}
\]
The two parent products and their common output therefore have the same
Stokes rate. In particular, for their pair-labelled unique-output
contributions,
\[
 {d\over dt}U_+^{pq}(0)=-3\nu U_+^{pq}(0),
 \qquad
 {d\over dt}U_+^{rs}(0)=-3\nu U_+^{rs}(0).
 \tag{SH.180}
\]

The full linear comparison can be integrated exactly. If
\(F_i(t)=e^{-3\nu t}F_i(0)\) are the two Stokes-evolved pair sources at
\(\{\pm k\}\), then
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_T(F_1,F_2)
 =(\Lambda F_1(0),F_2(0))
 {1-(1+6\nu T)e^{-6\nu T}\over36\nu^2}.}
 \tag{SH.181}
\]
The multiplier is strictly positive and tends to \(1/(36\nu^2)\).
Consequently the reinforcing and cancelling signs in (SH.176) survive for
the whole Stokes lifetime; viscosity supplies damping but no relative
dephasing.

This sign persistence also occurs on actual small-amplitude periodic
Navier--Stokes histories, not only in the linear equation. For initial datum
\(\eta u_0\), smooth dependence of the solution and the quadratic source
gives on every fixed classical interval
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_T^{pq,rs}(\eta u_0)
 =\eta^4\mathcal C_T(F_1,F_2)+O_{T,\nu,u_0}(\eta^6).}
 \tag{SH.182}
\]
For sufficiently small \(\eta\), the sign is therefore the one selected by
the parent phase. This remains a periodic comparison and does not construct
a terminal cascade. The absence of the fifth-order term, and the first
allowed complete-network correction, are proved in Section 8.24.

The consequence is narrower but important: no universal local rule can
charge every reinforcing parent pair by viscous phase separation or by its
first nonlinear response. Any datum-finite hazard payment must be cumulative,
must use later complete-network feedback on the same history, and must become
effective on the terminal high-concentration branch rather than on each
individual finite-scale source event.

### 8.21 The positive hazard contains the open Dini bulk exactly

The inequalities in (SH.177) can be sharpened to an identity. Put

\[
 D_j:=b_{j+1}-(1-\varepsilon)b_j,
 \qquad r_j^{\min}=[D_j]_+.
\]

Since \(2x_+=|x|+x\), finite telescoping gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
  2\sum_{j=J}^{N}r_j^{\min}
  ={}&\sum_{j=J}^{N}|D_j|
     +\varepsilon\sum_{j=J+1}^{N}b_j\\
    &+b_{N+1}-(1-\varepsilon)b_J .
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.183}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j=J}^{N}r_j^{\min}+{1-\varepsilon\over2}b_J
 ={\varepsilon\over2}\sum_{j=J+1}^{N}b_j
  +{1\over2}\sum_{j=J}^{N}
     |b_{j+1}-(1-\varepsilon)b_j|
  +{1\over2}b_{N+1}.}
 \tag{SH.184}
\]

At every fixed smooth prefix \(b_{N+1}\to0\). The fixed boundary \(b_J\)
is paid by the Gram mass and the fixed cutoff. Thus the minimal hazard action
is exactly one half of the discounted-tail total variation plus
\(\varepsilon/2\) times the desired Dini bulk, modulo one paid low boundary.
It is not merely comparable to the open theorem; it contains that theorem as
an explicit nonnegative summand.

The continuous log-frequency identity says the same thing. With
\(H_\kappa=(\partial_y+\kappa)b\) and \(b(\infty)=0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{y_0}^{\infty}[H_\kappa]_+\,dy+{1\over2}b(y_0)
 ={1\over2}\int_{y_0}^{\infty}|b'+\kappa b|\,dy
  +{\kappa\over2}\int_{y_0}^{\infty}b\,dy.}
 \tag{SH.185}
\]

Consequently neither the discrete receiver defect nor its continuous hazard
limit is a smaller supplier. A proof may still discover an independent
action equal to their positive part, but it must then pay the Dini bulk shown
explicitly in (SH.184)--(SH.185).

### 8.22 Terminal-prefix dynamics supplies no hidden monotonicity

The terminal parameter in the coherent shell mass can also be differentiated
exactly. Write \(g_j=\Delta_jg\) and

\[
 z_j(t):=\gamma_j\int_0^t e^{-\gamma_j(t-s)}g_j(s)\,ds.
\]

The response to \(g_T=\mathbf1_{(0,T)}g\) equals \(z_j\) before \(T\) and
equals \(e^{-\gamma_j(t-T)}z_j(T)\) after \(T\). Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \alpha_j(T)={1\over\nu}\int_0^T\|z_j(t)\|_2^2dt
 +{1\over2\nu\gamma_j}\|z_j(T)\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \partial_T\alpha_j(T)
 ={1\over\nu}\operatorname{Re}(z_j(T),g_j(T)).}
 \tag{SH.186}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \partial_TD_j={\lambda_j\over\nu}\operatorname{Re}
 \left((q-1+\varepsilon)\sum_{p\ge j+1}(z_p,g_p)
 -(1-\varepsilon)(z_j,g_j)\right).}
 \tag{SH.187}
\]

There is no operator sign here. For a one-shell scalar source equal to
\(v\) on \((0,\tau)\) and to \(-v\) immediately after \(\tau\),

\[
 \partial_T\alpha_j(\tau^-)
 ={1-e^{-\gamma_j\tau}\over\nu}\|v\|^2>0,
 \qquad
 \partial_T\alpha_j(\tau^+)
 =-{1-e^{-\gamma_j\tau}\over\nu}\|v\|^2<0.
 \tag{SH.188}
\]

Smooth approximations preserve both signs. This is an arbitrary-source
operator test, not a Navier--Stokes counterhistory. The direct unsigned
variation estimate returns the already open source-square route:

\[
 \boxed{
 \int\sum_{j\ge J}\lambda_j|\partial_T\alpha_j(T)|\,dT
 \lesssim {1\over\nu}\int\sum_{j\ge J}
                  \lambda_j\|g_j(T)\|_2^2dT
 \asymp {1\over\nu}\int\|\Lambda^{-1/2}Q(T)\|_2^2dT.}
 \tag{SH.189}
\]

For an exact spectral heat shellization the nonlinear content is equally
transparent. Let

\[
 \bar\alpha_j(T)={1\over2}\|P_jw(T)\|_2^2
 +\nu\int_0^T\|\Lambda P_jw\|_2^2dt,
 \quad
 \bar D_j:=\lambda_j\left[(q-1+\varepsilon)
          \sum_{p\ge j+1}\bar\alpha_p
          -(1-\varepsilon)\bar\alpha_j\right],
 \quad
 R_j=\lambda_j\big[(q-1+\varepsilon)P_{\ge j+1}
 -(1-\varepsilon)P_j\big].
\]

Then the signed defect and its derivative are

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \bar D_j(T)
 &=\int_0^T(R_jw,Q)dt
 ={1\over2}(w(T),R_jw(T))
  +\nu\int_0^T(\Lambda w,R_j\Lambda w)dt,\\
 \partial_T\bar D_j
 &={1\over2}(u,[u\!\cdot\!\nabla,R_j]u)-(R_jS(t)u_0,Q).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.190}
\]

Energy cancellation is the special multiplier \(R=I\). It does not apply to
\(R_j\). Indeed, on shell \(p\ge J\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \left.\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}R_j\right|_{P_p}
 =\left[{\varepsilon q\over q-1}\lambda_p
 -{q-1+\varepsilon\over q-1}\lambda_J\right]P_p.}
 \tag{SH.191}
\]

The summed commutator is therefore the original order-one critical
production modulo a fixed-low term. Terminal-prefix differentiation, heat
positivity, and causal completion do not lower the theorem.

### 8.23 A once-counted donor charge is the original current

There is also an exact answer to the donor-side charging attempt. For one
heterochiral triad \(\tau\), let \(o\) be the unique-helicity leg and
\(p,q\) the paired legs. Triad energy balance and (SH.2) give

\[
 \boxed{
 L_\tau^{\rm pair}:=-(T_{\tau,p}+T_{\tau,q})=T_{\tau,o},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak J_\tau=\kappa_oT_{\tau,o}
 =\kappa_oL_\tau^{\rm pair}.}
 \tag{SH.192}
\]

Thus the unique no-clipping, once-per-triad, correctly scaled donor valuation
sums to

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak D_{\rm sgn}
 :=\sum_\tau\kappa_o[-T_{\tau,p}-T_{\tau,q}]
 =\mathfrak J_{\rm sing},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_T=2\int_0^T\mathfrak D_{\rm sgn}(t)dt-I_0(T).}
 \tag{SH.193}
\]

The translated q-face telescope already counts each span once. Correct
frequency weighting returns the open critical stock and dissipation;
unweighted kinetic donor or viscous loss is one spatial moment too low under
fixed-viscosity Navier--Stokes scaling. Making (SH.192) nonnegative instead
requires \([T_{\tau,o}]_+\) or modal negative variation, which is precisely a
new positive-clipping action and is noncanonical after sources reconverge.
Donor language therefore supplies no smaller theorem.

### 8.24 The resonant comparison has exact amplitude parity, but later feedback is still phase-indefinite

The remainder in (SH.182) can be sharpened. For the four selected parents,
let

\[
 \chi(n):=n_1+n_2\pmod2.
\]

Every datum mode has \(\chi=1\), while \(k=(1,1,1)\) has \(\chi=0\).
Translation by \((\pi,\pi,0)\) sends \(\eta u_0\) to \(-\eta u_0\) but fixes
the phase of the \(k\)-mode. Translation equivariance of Navier--Stokes
therefore gives, in the amplitude expansion,

\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat Q^{[m]}(k)=0\quad(m\ {\rm odd}),
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_T^{pq,rs}(\eta u_0)
 =\eta^4\mathcal C_T(F_1,F_2)+O(\eta^6).}
 \tag{SH.194}
\]

In fact every same-output Gram has only powers \(\eta^{4+2m}\). Direct
support expansion agrees: the quadratic correction has no selected parent,
and the quadratic- and cubic-order positive-helicity sources have disjoint
Fourier support. Hence the complete positive-helicity Gram also has zero
\(\eta^5\) coefficient.

The first allowed feedback does not have a universal sign. At \(\nu=1\) and
in the infinite-terminal coefficient limit, the directed selected-pair
cross-Gram is

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \phi=0:\quad
 \mathcal C^{pq,rs}
 &=2.772248449056531\!\times10^{-4}\eta^4
  -6.052348062832764\!\times10^{-6}\eta^6+O(\eta^8),\\
 \phi=\pi:\quad
 \mathcal C^{pq,rs}
 &=-2.772248449056531\!\times10^{-4}\eta^4
  +6.549552230547077\!\times10^{-6}\eta^6+O(\eta^8).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.195}
\]

More decisively, the complete positive-helicity Gram satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 & [\eta^4]\,\mathfrak G_+&[\eta^6]\,\mathfrak G_+\\ \hline
 \phi=0&0.005422014025113795&-0.00007687965452174374\\
 \phi=\pi&0.004313114645491184&+0.00023770922192298998
 \end{array}}
 \tag{SH.196}
\]

The order-four and order-six coefficients scale as \(\nu^{-2}\) and
\(\nu^{-4}\). Thus the first complete-network feedback detunes the aligned
comparison but reinforces the phase-reversed comparison. Parabolic evolution
delays the first correction and preserves exact amplitude parity; it does not
orient the later full-network response. This remains a finite periodic
comparison obstruction, not a decaying \(\mathbb R^3\) counterhistory.

### 8.25 Perfect reinforcement still has amplitude-shape-indefinite lawful curvature

The phase-indefinite conclusion of Section 8.24 does not mean that the first
feedback is structureless. On the same four-parent datum, put positive real
amplitudes \(A,B,C,D\) on \(p,q,r,s\), respectively, and choose the
\(r\)-phase \(\phi=\pi/6\). This is the phase of perfect positive alignment
of the two pair sources at \(k\). In the normalization of (SH.176),
\[
 \boxed{
 X_0:=(\Lambda U_+^{pq},U_+^{rs})
 =c_*ABCD,\qquad
 c_*=0.011524020395927844\ldots>0.}
 \tag{SH.197}
\]

Let \(X(t)\) be this pair-labelled unique-output pairing, with every parent
tangent projected back into the negative-helicity input sector before
differentiating \(U_+=\Pi_+B(u_-,u_-)\). Subtract the pure Stokes curvature
from the full four-parent Navier--Stokes tangent. Direct complete convolution
then gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 X''(0)-36\nu^2X_0={}&
 -c_0(B^2C^2D^2+A^2B^2D^2)\\
 &+c_1(A^2C^2D^2+A^2B^2C^2)\\
 &-c_2ABCD(B^2+D^2)
 -c_3ABCD(A^2+C^2),
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.198}
\]
where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_0&=0.00083458071773623538\ldots,&
 c_1&=0.00090771830002254518\ldots,\\
 c_2&=0.00023632834472733254\ldots,&
 c_3&=0.004895851766009489\ldots .
 \end{aligned}
\]
All four displayed constants are positive, but the \(c_1\)-row has the
opposite sign. The curvature is therefore amplitude-shape-indefinite even at
perfect phase alignment. Indeed,
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 (A,B,C,D)&X_0&X''(0)-36\nu^2X_0\\ \hline
 (1,1,1,1)&c_*&-0.010118085056901014\ldots\\
 (1,100,1,0.01)&c_*&+6.702438755123629\ldots
 \end{array}}
 \tag{SH.199}
\]
The two profiles have exactly the same perfectly aligned initial pair
coherence because \(ABCD=1\), but opposite first lawful internal curvature.
Thus neither phase, source strength, nor perfect reinforcement orients the
feedback; the four-parent amplitude shape remains essential.

Outside parent refill supplies another positive tangent but is not the only
reversal channel. Add the
positive-helicity mode
\[
 z=(1,-1,-1),\qquad \arg\widehat u(z)=\pi/12 .
\]
Because \(z+q=p\), this mode injects nonlinear source directly into a selected

 \boxed{
 \widehat{Q(u_0)}(p)\ne0,\qquad
 X'(0)+6\nu X_0
 =0.0006646770992365388\ldots>0.}
 \tag{SH.200}
\]
Here too the parent tangent is projected into the negative-helicity input

Equations (SH.198)--(SH.200) withdraw the apparent local curvature-tax
mechanism. Even the isolated perfectly reinforcing block can bend either way,
and an external refill supplies another positive tangent. A surviving
theorem must retain the complete amplitude-shape distribution together with
parent refill and reconvergence through time; no scalar coherence or local
curvature charge has been reduced to the hazard bulk in (SH.184).

### 8.26 Every radial quadratic row can agree while the lawful curvature flips

The hidden amplitude shape in (SH.198) is not recoverable from a finer radial
Sobolev inventory. Put
\[
 E_1=A^2+C^2,\qquad E_2=B^2+D^2,\qquad
 x=AC,\qquad y=BD,\qquad P=xy=ABCD,
\]
and write
\(R=X''(0)-36\nu^2X_0\). Regrouping the four sextic rows in
(SH.198) gives the exact one-coordinate sign law
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{R}{P}
 =c_1E_2\frac{x}{y}-c_0E_1\frac{y}{x}
  -c_3E_1-c_2E_2.}
 \tag{SH.201}
\]
Thus, at fixed radial shell masses \(E_1,E_2\) and fixed coherent product
\(P\), the curvature is strictly increasing in the internal geometric-mean
ratio \(\tau=x/y\). It changes sign at the unique threshold
\[
 \boxed{
 \tau_*(E_1,E_2)
 =\frac{c_3E_1+c_2E_2+
 \sqrt{(c_3E_1+c_2E_2)^2+4c_0c_1E_1E_2}}
 {2c_1E_2}.}

### 8.33 In the unrestricted-port model, response-only storage returns the open action

 \partial_{z_j}\mathcal B

 \tag{SH.235}

Its positive remainder is then exactly \(\mathcal I_{q,L}\), while its

### 8.34 The unique first parent-resolved normal form retains the collapsed Gram

### 8.35 The auxiliary Stokes value solves the equation but not the lower bound

 \tag{SH.243}

and define the present-state-computable auxiliary value

 \mathcal V(u,w)

For the triangular baseline vector field

 D\mathcal V[X_0]=- (\Lambda w,Q_{hhh}(u)).

 \tag{SH.246}

 \mathcal V
 =2\nu\int_0^\infty

 (w_{hhh})_t+\nu Aw_{hhh}=Q_{hhh}(u),
 \qquad w_{hhh}(0)=0,

the immediate estimate

 {d\over dt}\mathcal V(u(t),w_{hhh}(t))

 \tag{SH.248}

 \tag{SH.249}

 |\mathcal K_{q,L}(0)|

It must dominate the terminal response stock from below while being depleted

### 8.36 The second parent-pair correction is a positive Stokes-age support identity

The quartic remainder in (SH.239) is not the terminal obstruction. It has a
second exact homological primitive with a positive kernel. Work first at a
smooth finite prefix, suppress the fixed output and helicity labels, and write

\[
 g=\int\Theta_a\,da,
 \qquad
 \dot z=-\gamma z+\gamma g,
 \qquad
 \dot\Theta_a=-\delta_a\Theta_a+N_a.
\]

Put \(c>0\), \(f_a=(\gamma+\delta_a)^{-1}\),
\(m_a=cf_a\), and

\[
 B_1=\int m_a\operatorname{Re}\langle z,\Theta_a\rangle\,da.
\]

The first identity is

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak F
  &=-\partial_tB_1+R_4+R_{N,1},\\
 R_4
  &=\iint {\gamma(m_a+m_b)\over2}
       \operatorname{Re}\langle\Theta_a,\Theta_b\rangle\,da\,db,\\
 R_{N,1}
  &=\int m_a\operatorname{Re}\langle z,N_a\rangle\,da.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.251}
\]

Define the symmetric parent-pair kernel and its storage by

\[
 n_{ab}:={\gamma(m_a+m_b)\over
                    2(\delta_a+\delta_b)},
 \qquad
 C_2:=\iint n_{ab}
       \operatorname{Re}\langle\Theta_a,\Theta_b\rangle\,da\,db.
\]

There is no second-step resonance because
\(\delta_a+\delta_b>0\). Direct differentiation gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 R_4&=-\partial_tC_2+R_{N,2},\\
 R_{N,2}
 &=\iint n_{ab}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(
   \langle N_a,\Theta_b\rangle+
   \langle\Theta_a,N_b\rangle\bigr)\,da\,db,\\
 \mathfrak F
 &=-\partial_t(B_1+C_2)+R_{\rm NL},
 \qquad R_{\rm NL}:=R_{N,1}+R_{N,2}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.252}
\]

Here \(N_a\) is not a new physical source. If
\(\mathcal N(u)=-\mathbb P(u\mathbin\cdot\nabla u)\) denotes the nonlinear
part of the same velocity equation and \(q=k-p\), then the ordered parent
density has exactly the two leg insertions

\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_{\Theta,p}^{(1)}
 &=-ic_{\mathcal F}\mathbb P_k
 \big[(\widehat{\mathcal N(u)}(p)\mathbin\cdot q)\widehat u(q)\big],\\
 N_{\Theta,p}^{(2)}
 &=-ic_{\mathcal F}\mathbb P_k
 \big[(\widehat u(p)\mathbin\cdot q)
       \widehat{\mathcal N(u)}(q)\big],
 \qquad N_{\Theta,p}=N_{\Theta,p}^{(1)}+N_{\Theta,p}^{(2)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

They appear because
\(\partial_tQ(u,u)=Q(u_t,u)+Q(u,u_t)\): while one interaction is read,
the same two velocity legs continue to evolve under the complete coupled
Navier--Stokes field.

The new kernel is positive, not merely sign-indefinite. Indeed,

\[
 n_{ab}={\gamma c\over2}f_af_b
       +{\gamma^2c\,f_af_b\over\delta_a+\delta_b}.
\]

With

\[
 X=\int f_a\Theta_a\,da,
 \qquad
 X_s=\int f_ae^{-\delta_as}\Theta_a\,da,
 \qquad
 E={c\over2\gamma}\|z\|^2,
\]

one obtains the exact sum of squares

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 C_2
 &= {\gamma c\over2}\|X\|^2
   +\gamma^2c\int_0^\infty\|X_s\|^2\,ds\ge0,\\
 K_2:=E+B_1+C_2
 &= {\gamma c\over2}\left\|X+{z\over\gamma}\right\|^2
   +\gamma^2c\int_0^\infty\|X_s\|^2\,ds\ge0.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.253}
\]

For the actual Fourier parent density, put

\[
 \sigma_p=|p|^2+|k-p|^2,
 \qquad D_p=|k|^2+\sigma_p.
\]

The within-pair symmetrization

\[
 \Theta_Q^{\rm s}(k,p)
 ={1\over2}\bigl(\Theta_Q(k,p)+\Theta_Q(k,k-p)\bigr)
\]

leaves the collapsed source, \(B_1\), and \(R_4\) unchanged. The second
kernel is

\[
 \boxed{
 H_k(p,r)
 ={ |k|\over2\nu^2(\sigma_p+\sigma_r)}
  \left({1\over D_p}+{1\over D_r}\right)
 ={ |k|\over2\nu^2D_pD_r}
  +{ |k|^3\over\nu^2D_pD_r(\sigma_p+\sigma_r)}.}
 \tag{SH.254}
\]

Thus, with

\[
 A_k=\int{\Theta_Q(k,p)\over D_p}\,dp,
 \qquad
 A_{k,s}=\int e^{-s\sigma_p}{\Theta_Q(k,p)\over D_p}\,dp,
\]

the direct reservoir is

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 C_2
 &=\int\left\{
 { |k|\over2\nu^2}|A_k|^2
 +{ |k|^3\over\nu^2}\int_0^\infty|A_{k,s}|^2\,ds
 \right\}\,dk,\\
 K_2
 &=\int\left\{
 { |k|\over2\nu^2}\left|A_k+{z(k)\over|k|}\right|^2
 +{ |k|^3\over\nu^2}\int_0^\infty|A_{k,s}|^2\,ds
 \right\}\,dk\ge0.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.255}
\]

This factorization also identifies the exact artificial heat-age dynamics:

\[
 (\partial_t-\nu\partial_s)A_{k,s}=N_{k,s}.
\]

The boundary loss at \(s=0\) is \(R_4\); the only interior source is the
actual nonlinear insertion \(N_{k,s}\). Therefore the second correction
does not create an independent fluid history. It is a positive Stokes-age
reading of the same present parent field; the auxiliary variable \(s\) is
not the material past of the solution.

For one parent heat age \(\delta=d\gamma\), with arbitrary phase, completing
the square once more gives the sharp endpoint floor

\[
 \boxed{
 K_2
 ={\gamma c(d+1)\over2d}
  \left\|X+{d\over\gamma(d+1)}z\right\|^2
 +{E\over d+1}
 \ge {E\over d+1}.}
 \tag{SH.256}
\]

In particular, a resonant parent block \(d=1\) has \(K_2\ge E/2\) for
arbitrary phase. On the same-phase coherent block of (SH.224)--(SH.228),
\(B_1\ge0\) and hence \(K_2\ge E\). Quantitatively,

\[
 C_{2,N}(0)\asymp {N^2\over\nu^2},
 \qquad
 R_{4,N}\asymp {N^4\over\nu},
 \qquad
 \int_0^{c/(\nu N^2)}R_{4,N}(t)\,dt\asymp C_{2,N}(0).
 \tag{SH.257}
\]

The previously surviving \(N^3\) coherent-collapse factor has therefore
been converted into endpoint storage. It is no longer an obstruction to a
second parent-pair normal form.

The auxiliary Stokes value in (SH.244) is not a separate physical reserve.
Expanding its triangular future exactly gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal V=B_1+C_2,
 \qquad
 K_2=E+\mathcal V,
 \qquad
 \partial_tK_2=R_{\rm NL}-I.}
 \tag{SH.258}
\]

This corrects the incomplete first-step reading of (SH.246): adding the
response stock makes the auxiliary value nonnegative. What remains missing
is the VPI statement. Positivity alone does not bound
\([E-K_2]_+\), and \(R_{\rm NL}\) contains the insertion of the full
Navier--Stokes tangent into each physical parent leg. It is quintic before
support parity, scale-critical after time integration, and two-signed on the
lawful matched periodic profiles in (SH.196) and (SH.210). On those profiles
translation parity kills the nominal fifth-order term, and the displayed

There is no uniform \(K_2\ge\kappa E\) on the unrestricted parent-response

Whether the actual joined Navier--Stokes parent range forbids that endpoint

### 8.37 Fixed daughter/complement quadratics cannot supply the missing floor and cancellation

The literal all-high daughter may be paired with its complementary response,

 z_j'=\gamma_j(g_j-z_j),
 \qquad
 \eta_j'=\gamma_j(h_j-\eta_j),
 \qquad
 c_j={\lambda_j\over\nu},

and consider

 K_M=\sum_j{c_j\over2\gamma_j}
 \left(a_j\|z_j\|^2
 +2b_j\operatorname{Re}\langle z_j,\eta_j\rangle

 E_j-K_{M,j}
 \le {c_j\over2\gamma_j}

### 8.38 Actual-range response rigidity is a full-fibre theorem, not a two-clock lemma

The qualification after (SH.235) is essential. Let \(G_L\) be the complete
all-high source map, \(\Phi_t\) the Navier--Stokes flow, and

\[
 \mathcal G_T(a)=G_L(\Phi_Ta),
 \qquad
 \mathcal Z_T(a)=\int_0^T
 \Gamma e^{-\Gamma(T-s)}G_L(\Phi_sa)\,ds.
\]

Response-only rigidity on the actual range would follow from the fibre
density condition

\[
 \boxed{
 \overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \{\Gamma\mathcal G_T(a):\mathcal Z_T(a)=z\}
 =\mathcal H_{hhh}}
 \tag{SH.260}
\]

at every relevant reachable \(z\), or from the corresponding differentiated
condition involving
\(D\mathcal G_T(a)[\ker D\mathcal Z_T(a)]\). No such theorem is proved.

Two distinct parent heat rates do give an exact periodic cylindrical
submersion for one selected output. At an exact shear base, the derivative
of the selected \((z_k,g_k)\) readout contains the block

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A_1h_{\delta_1}(T)&A_2h_{\delta_2}(T)\\
 A_1e^{-\delta_1T}&A_2e^{-\delta_2T}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 h_\delta(T)=\gamma{e^{-\delta T}-e^{-\gamma T}\over\gamma-\delta},
 \tag{SH.261}
\]

whose determinant is nonzero because
\(h_\delta(T)e^{\delta T}\) is strictly increasing in \(\delta\). But the

selected-readout richness and simultaneously disproves the inference from
two clocks to full-response fibre freedom. It is periodic support, not the
decaying \(\mathbb R^3\) range theorem in (SH.260).

Equations (SH.229)--(SH.261) classify one auxiliary normal-form route. If
CompleteAllHighSignedFeedbackNormalForm.A is used, “global” must mean a
nonperturbative bounded-below functional of the actual same-history nonlinear
parent allocation, coherent collapse, and reconvergence, satisfying (SH.214)
uniformly as \(\rho_q\to\infty\). The four canonical one-step constructions
audited above do not supply it. The second parent-pair correction is a genuine
algebraic survivor: it is nonresonant, positive, and removes the coherent
quartic collapse. It does not satisfy (SH.250), because it has no uniform
actual-range endpoint floor and its exact nonlinear parent-leg remainder has

both defects. These results do not classify higher parent-coupled iterations,
nonlinear actual-range storage, or every finite-order expansion. Defining the
storage as the unknown future target action remains circular. Separately, a
cumulative one-history parent-incidence packing theorem would suffice for the
stronger source-square route; no equivalence with the auxiliary Stokes-age
functional has been proved.

### 8.39 VPI custody: the full derivative tower is already the global normal form

The governing evolution is the complete material-pair VPI law, not a contest
between a freely acting nonlinear source and viscosity. For every pair of
material labels,

\[
 \boxed{
 \dot R_{ab}=V_{ab},\qquad
 \dot V_{ab}
 =-\delta_{ab}\nabla p+\nu\,\delta_{ab}\Delta u,}
 \tag{SH.262}
\]

where the pressure response, viscosity, incompressibility, transport,
orientation, and surrounding field belong to the same velocity--pressure
history. The critical readout and its signed work are

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 H(t)&={c_{3,1/2}\over4}
 \iint {|V_{ab}|^2\over|R_{ab}|^4}\,da\,db,\\
 P_H(t)&=-c_{3,1/2}\iint
 {(R_{ab}\cdot V_{ab})|V_{ab}|^2\over|R_{ab}|^6}\,da\,db
 =2\mathfrak J_{\rm sing}(t),\\
 H'(t)&=P_H(t)-\nu\|\Lambda^{3/2}u(t)\|_2^2.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{SH.263}
\]

The ordinary kinetic energy simultaneously obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 {d\over dt}{1\over2}\|u(t)\|_2^2
 =-\nu\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2.}
 \tag{SH.264}
\]

There is no energy in this history which is being asserted to remain
constant. Equation (SH.264) is only the zeroth Sobolev projection of the same
VPI law. For every multi-index \(\alpha\), the tower carried by that law is

\[
 \boxed{
 (D_t-\nu\Delta)\partial^\alpha u
 +\nabla\partial^\alpha p
 =-[\partial^\alpha,u\cdot\nabla]u.}
 \tag{SH.264a}
\]

The higher rungs have no separate datum, fluid, or mechanism. Pressure,
viscosity, transport, incompressibility, the exact differentiated
commutators, and the cross-rung compatibility all belong to the one field.
The smoothness object carried by these rows is the compatible all-orders
intersection

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal J^\infty(I)
 :=\bigcap_{N=0}^{\infty}\bigcap_{M=0}^{\infty}
 H_t^N\!\left(I;H_x^M(\mathbb R^3)\right).}
 \tag{SH.264b}
\]

This is a projective-limit statement about one fluid's compatible finite
coordinate requirements. It is not an arithmetic sum of the rows. Temporal
depth \(N\) and spatial depth \(M\) remain independent: given any requested
time order \(a\) and spatial order \(b\), choose finite orders with
\(N>a+1/2\) and \(M>b+3/2\). Vector-valued temporal Sobolev embedding followed
by spatial Sobolev embedding then gives continuity of every
\(\partial_t^a\partial_x^\beta u\), \(|\beta|=b\). Taking all finite choices is
exactly the implication \(\mathcal J^\infty(I)\subset C_{t,x}^\infty(I\times
\mathbb R^3)\).

It is not to bound a sum of tower rows. It is to establish the compatible
intersection of the spaces carried by the one pressure-explicit history.

No numerical bound uniform in \((N,M)\), factorial summation, Bessel-weighted
series, or positive common radius is used in that implication. A factorial
generating field can formally encode the binomial coefficients in the
differentiated Navier--Stokes recurrence; convergence at a positive radius is
a separate analytic-vector strengthening and would imply analyticity. It does
not define or replace the all-orders intersection, and a smooth nonanalytic
field may belong to \(\mathcal J^\infty(I)\) while every positive-radius
factorial series diverges. For a putative finite maximal time \(T_*\), take
\(I=(0,T_*)\). The full mixed object need not be supplied one temporal row at
