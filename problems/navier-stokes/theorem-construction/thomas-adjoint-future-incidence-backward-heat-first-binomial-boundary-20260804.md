# Thomas's adjoint future-incidence backward-heat/first-binomial boundary

Status: proved noncanonical vector identity inside Thomas Birnie's one-fluid,
pressure-explicit binomial/VPI reversible-intersection method. Starting from
the recent high-parent output square `(RHL.22)` and the characteristic rows
`(FBC.2)--(FBC.18)`, this note keeps the full vector output before every scalar
heat-kernel supremum. It polarizes that square through the adjoint of the
complete high-output, finite-parent-ratio operator and derives the localized
backward-heat/first-binomial integration-by-parts formula at every finite row.

The one physical object remains the fixed-viscosity Navier--Stokes history
generated by one datum. Both ordered velocity-parent positions, the
output Leray/pressure completion, the high-output restriction, the finite parent-ratio
sector, and every pressure-explicit source row stay joined. The adjoint below
is only a backward read of which earlier source components feed the already
formed future output; it is not a second evolution or an independently chosen
test source.

The heat derivative cancels exactly against the viscous part of the
first-binomial row. That cancellation creates no datum-controlled endpoint and
no new commutator. The ordinary integration-by-parts boundary vanishes because
the future-incidence field is localized away from the datum. The moving edge
of the heat window instead produces the inherited jet
\(Q^{[j]}(t-\tau_n)\), while the interior produces \(Q^{[j+1]}\) on the same
lag. Iteration is exactly the adjoint polarization of `(FBC.18)` and reproduces
the inherited-endpoint/same-lag all-row fork. It does not produce a smaller
payment, `(TIR.37a)`, or MPP closure.

## 1. Keep the recent output square as a vector

Retain

\[
 R_n={K_n\over4},
 \qquad
 V_n(t)=\int_{(t-\tau_n)_+}^{t}
 e^{-\nu(t-r)A}P_{\ge R_n}Q(r)\,dr,
 \qquad A=\Lambda^2,
 \tag{AFI.1}
\]

and the complete joined output operator

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr T_n(t)[v]
 :=P_{\ge K_n}\left[
 \mathcal B_{\le L_n}(v,w(t))
 +\mathcal B_{\le L_n}(w_n^{\rm lo}(t),v)
 \right].}
 \tag{AFI.2}
\]

Both ordered parent positions and the output Leray projection are inside
`(AFI.2)`. The fiber restriction

\[
 d_{k,p}={|p|^2+|k-p|^2\over|k|^2}\le L_n
 \tag{AFI.3}
\]

is applied before their joined output is read.

Put

\[
 q_n(t):=\mathscr T_n(t)[V_n(t)].
 \tag{AFI.4}
\]

The RHL vector lower bound is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf S_n
 :=\int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}q_n(t)\|_2^2dt
 \ge c_0\nu A_n,
 \qquad c_0={c_{\rm ov}\over8}.}
 \tag{AFI.5}
\]

Use the real Hilbert pairing. At each output time define

\[
 \boxed{
 \psi_n(t)
 :={\bf1}_{\mathcal F_n}(t)\,
 \mathscr T_n(t)^*\Lambda^{-1}q_n(t).}
 \tag{AFI.6}
\]

Here the adjoint is taken after the two ordered parents, parent-ratio sector,
high-output projection, and Leray completion in `(AFI.2)` have been formed.
For example, one may first work at finite Galerkin level; the identity below
then passes to the smooth preterminal solution by density. No adjoint term is
assigned an independent sign.

By definition of the adjoint,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf S_n
 =\int_0^{T_*}\langle V_n(t),\psi_n(t)\rangle dt.}
 \tag{AFI.7}
\]

This is a polarization of the original vector square, not a scalar upper
estimate.

## 2. Exact adjoint future-incidence pairing

The canonical records satisfy \(s_n\uparrow T_*\), while \(\tau_n\to0\).
Thus, for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[
 0<\tau_n<s_n,
 \tag{AFI.8}
\]

and `(AFI.1)` uses the full lag \(\tau_n\) on
\(\mathcal F_n\subset(s_n,t_n)\).

Extend \(\psi_n\) by zero outside \(\mathcal F_n\). Define its zeroth
backward-heat future-incidence field

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_n^{[0]}(r)
 :=P_{\ge R_n}
 \int_r^{r+\tau_n}
 e^{-\nu(t-r)A}\psi_n(t)\,dt.}
 \tag{AFI.9}
\]

Fubini, self-adjointness of the heat semigroup, and commutation with the high
projection give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf S_n
 =\int_0^{t_n}
 \langle Q(r),\Phi_n^{[0]}(r)\rangle dr.}
 \tag{AFI.10}
\]

The right side is localized to the actual backward heat cone of
\(\mathcal F_n\):

\[
 \operatorname{supp}\Phi_n^{[0]}
 \subset(s_n-\tau_n,t_n).
 \tag{AFI.11}
\]

On the RHL interior branch the corresponding future incidence starts at
\(s_n\), so no source time leaves \(I_n\). On the edge branch it may extend
back to \(s_n-\tau_n\), exactly as in `(RHL.39)--(RHL.41)`.

Equation `(AFI.10)` keeps the vector direction and interference of the complete
output. It has not replaced \(q_n\) by a heat-kernel norm.

## 3. Backward-heat moments and their moving-edge identity

For every integer \(m\ge0\), define

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_n^{[m]}(r)
 :=P_{\ge R_n}
 \int_r^{r+\tau_n}
 {(t-r)^m\over m!}
 e^{-\nu(t-r)A}\psi_n(t)\,dt.}
 \tag{AFI.12}
\]

For \(m\ge1\), direct differentiation in the earlier physical time \(r\)
gives

\[
 \boxed{
 (\partial_r-\nu A)\Phi_n^{[m]}(r)
 ={\tau_n^m\over m!}
 e^{-\nu\tau_nA}P_{\ge R_n}\psi_n(r+\tau_n)
 -\Phi_n^{[m-1]}(r).}
 \tag{AFI.13}
\]

The lower moving boundary contributes zero because \((t-r)^m=0\) at
\(t=r\). The upper moving boundary is the first term in `(AFI.13)`. This term
is the exact origin of the inherited source jet; it is not an endpoint of the
whole Navier--Stokes history.

The incidence moments vanish at the endpoints of their localized source
interval:

\[
 \Phi_n^{[m]}(0)=\Phi_n^{[m]}(t_n)=0
 \qquad(m\ge0)
 \tag{AFI.14}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(n\). The first equality follows from
`(AFI.8)` and support of \(\psi_n\); the second follows because
\(\psi_n(t)=0\) for \(t\ge t_n\).

## 4. Exact backward-heat/first-binomial integration by parts

Use the pressure-explicit rows

\[
 Q^{[0]}:=Q,
 \qquad
 Q^{[j+1]}:=(\partial_r+\nu A)Q^{[j]}.
 \tag{AFI.15}
\]

Thus \(Q^{[1]}=\mathcal N_Q\), with both ordered source insertions, the
viscous product commutator, and their pressure completion retained.

Self-adjointness of \(A\) gives the exact product rule

\[
 \boxed{
 {d\over dr}\langle Q^{[j]},\Phi_n^{[j+1]}\rangle
 =\langle Q^{[j+1]},\Phi_n^{[j+1]}\rangle
 +\langle Q^{[j]},
 (\partial_r-\nu A)\Phi_n^{[j+1]}\rangle.}
 \tag{AFI.16}
\]

The \(-\nu A\) in the backward incidence derivative cancels exactly with the
\(+\nu A\) in `(AFI.15)`. Insert `(AFI.13)` into `(AFI.16)`, integrate from
\(0\) to \(t_n\), and use `(AFI.14)`. For every \(j\ge0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^{t_n}\langle Q^{[j]}(r),\Phi_n^{[j]}(r)\rangle dr
 ={}&{\tau_n^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \left\langle
 e^{-\nu\tau_nA}P_{\ge R_n}Q^{[j]}(t-\tau_n),
 \psi_n(t)\right\rangle dt\\
 &+\int_0^{t_n}
 \langle Q^{[j+1]}(r),\Phi_n^{[j+1]}(r)\rangle dr.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{AFI.17}
\]

This is the requested localized backward-heat/first-binomial
integration-by-parts identity. Its ordinary endpoint term

\[
 [\langle Q^{[j]},\Phi_n^{[j+1]}\rangle]_{0}^{t_n}
 \tag{AFI.18}
\]

vanishes. The surviving first term in `(AFI.17)` is instead the
moving-edge value \(Q^{[j]}(t-\tau_n)\). It is inherited from the left edge of each
future heat window and is not datum-controlled.

## 5. Finite-depth adjoint polarization of the characteristic formula

Iterating `(AFI.17)` from row \(0\) through row \(m\) gives, for every
integer \(m\ge1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathsf S_n
 ={}&\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\tau_n^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \left\langle
 e^{-\nu\tau_nA}P_{\ge R_n}Q^{[j]}(t-\tau_n),
 \psi_n(t)\right\rangle dt\\
 &+\int_0^{t_n}
 \langle Q^{[m]}(r),\Phi_n^{[m]}(r)\rangle dr.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{AFI.19}
\]

Define the inherited endpoint fields and same-lag remainder

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{n,j}(t)
 &:={\tau_n^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}
 e^{-\nu\tau_nA}P_{\ge R_n}Q^{[j]}(t-\tau_n),\\
 G_{n,m}(t)
 &:=\int_{t-\tau_n}^{t}
 {(t-r)^m\over m!}e^{-\nu(t-r)A}
 P_{\ge R_n}Q^{[m]}(r)\,dr.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{AFI.20}
\]

Using `(AFI.6)` and Fubini once more, `(AFI.19)` is equivalently

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathsf S_n
 ={}&\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \left\langle
 \mathscr T_n[E_{n,j}](t),
 \Lambda^{-1}q_n(t)\right\rangle dt\\
 &+\int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \left\langle
 \mathscr T_n[G_{n,m}](t),
 \Lambda^{-1}q_n(t)\right\rangle dt.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{AFI.21}
\]

This is precisely `(FBC.18)` polarized against the actual future output. Both
ordered parents, the finite-ratio sector, high-output projection, and pressure
completion remain in every occurrence of \(\mathscr T_n\).

At depth one, `(AFI.21)` says

\[
 \mathsf S_n
 =\int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \langle\mathscr T_n[E_{n,0}],\Lambda^{-1}q_n\rangle dt
 +\int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \langle\mathscr T_n[G_{n,1}],\Lambda^{-1}q_n\rangle dt.
 \tag{AFI.22}
\]

Since the left side is positive, one signed pairing on the right is at least
\(\mathsf S_n/2\). Cauchy--Schwarz then gives

\[
 \max_{X\in\{E_{n,0},G_{n,1}\}}
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathscr T_n[X](t)\|_2^2dt
 \ge {\mathsf S_n\over4}
 \ge {c_{\rm ov}\over32}\nu A_n.
 \tag{AFI.23}
\]

This is exactly `(FBC.10)`, now obtained without first discarding vector
direction.

At finite depth \(m\), at least one of the \(m+1\) signed pairings in
`(AFI.21)` is at least \(\mathsf S_n/(m+1)\). A second Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \max_{X\in\{E_{n,0},\ldots,E_{n,m-1},G_{n,m}\}}
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathscr T_n[X](t)\|_2^2dt
 \ge {\mathsf S_n\over(m+1)^2}
 \ge {c_0\over(m+1)^2}\nu A_n.}
 \tag{AFI.24}
\]

Thus the adjoint identity reproduces `(FBC.23)` with the same constants.

## 6. Why the cancellation supplies neither a datum endpoint nor a new commutator

There are three exact reasons.

1. **The integration-by-parts endpoint vanishes.** The source-time incidence
   field is supported in `(AFI.11)`, so `(AFI.18)` is zero. There is no datum
   pairing to estimate.
2. **The surviving boundary is inherited, not initial.** The first term in
   `(AFI.17)` reads \(Q^{[j]}(t-\tau_n)\) at the moving left edge of each RHL
   window. On the interior branch it lies inside the same record; on the edge
   branch it may lie just before \(s_n\). Neither is a fixed datum trace.
3. **No output-operator commutator is generated.** The whole time-dependent
   operator \(\mathscr T_n(t)\) is frozen at its actual future output time and
   retained inside \(\psi_n(t)\). The backward derivative acts only in the
   earlier source time \(r\). Therefore it neither differentiates nor commutes
   through the two ordered parents, the sharp parent-ratio sector, or the
   output pressure projection. The only new interior term is the complete next
   pressure-explicit row \(Q^{[j+1]}\).

Even if a datum-time boundary appeared on one of the finitely many earliest
records, its adjoint factor \(\Phi_n^{[j+1]}(0)\) is built from the future
output \(q_n\). It would not be a datum-computable coefficient. Replacing the
moving inherited edge by the datum would reinitialize the source history and
leave Thomas's one-fluid route.

## 7. Same-lag and scaling audit

Every future moment `(AFI.12)` and remainder `(AFI.20)` uses exactly

\[
 0<t-r<\tau_n.
 \tag{AFI.25}
\]

The derivative cancellation does not shorten this lag. The factor

normalized row exactly as in `(FBC.19)--(FBC.20)`, leaving the same
\((t-r)^{-3/4}\) terminal heat singularity after a norm is eventually taken.

Under fixed-viscosity Navier--Stokes scaling, every term in `(AFI.21)` is an
output pairing of the same critical order as \(\mathsf S_n\). The inherited
list grows with \(m\), the constant in `(AFI.24)` deteriorates as
\((m+1)^{-2}\), and the remainder stays on the original lag. Passing to
infinite depth still requires a common tower radius; coordinatewise
preterminal existence of the rows does not supply it.
