## 1. Datum-paid rows

Write

\[
 u_t+\nu Au=Q(u),
 \qquad A=\Lambda^2=-\Delta,
 \qquad
 Q(u)=-(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p,
 \tag{HES.1}
\]

with

\[
 -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j).
 \tag{HES.2}
\]

Let

\[
 E_{\rm kin}:=\|u_0\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 Z(t):=\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2.
 \tag{HES.3}
\]

The kinetic-energy identity gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0<t<T_*}\|u(t)\|_2^2\le E_{\rm kin},
 \qquad
 \int_0^{T_*}Z(t)\,dt\le {E_{\rm kin}\over2\nu}.}
 \tag{HES.4}
\]

Retain the complete source quantities

\[
 b(t):=\|\Lambda^{-1/2}Q(u(t))\|_2,
 \qquad
 R_Q(t):={1\over2}\|\Lambda^{-3/2}Q(u(t))\|_2^2.
 \tag{HES.5}
\]

Since \(Q=-\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)\), Sobolev duality and
Gagliardo--Nirenberg give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\Lambda^{-3/2}Q\|_2
 &\le C\|u\otimes u\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}
 \le C\|u\otimes u\|_{3/2}\\
 &\le C\|u\|_3^2
 \le C\|u\|_2\|\nabla u\|_2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{HES.6}
\]

Consequently, for a constant \(C_R\),

\[
 \boxed{
 0\le R_Q(t)\le C_R E_{\rm kin}Z(t),
 \qquad
 \int_0^{T_*}R_Q(t)\,dt
 \le {C_R E_{\rm kin}^2\over2\nu}.}
 \tag{HES.7}
\]

This is a time-mass bound, not a trace bound.

## 2. Rigorous energy/enstrophy bound for the heat-age storage

At one physical time put

\[
 w_s=e^{-\nu sA}u,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_{\rm ha}(u)
 :=\int_0^\infty(Q(w_s),\Lambda w_s)_2\,ds.
 \tag{HES.8}
\]

The global pressure pairing vanishes only after the full field is
recombined, because \(\Lambda w_s\) is divergence free. Thus

\[
 \mathcal B_{\rm ha}(u)
 =-\int_0^\infty\!\int
 (w_s\cdot\nabla)w_s\cdot\Lambda w_s\,dx\,ds.
 \tag{HES.9}
\]

Define the heat maximal and vertical square functions

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal M_Hu(x)
 &:=\sup_{s>0}|e^{-\nu sA}u(x)|,\\
 g_\nabla u(x)
 &:=\left(\nu\int_0^\infty|\nabla e^{-\nu sA}u(x)|^2ds\right)^{1/2},\\
 g_\Lambda u(x)
 &:=\left(\nu\int_0^\infty|\Lambda e^{-\nu sA}u(x)|^2ds\right)^{1/2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{HES.10}
\]

Pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz in heat age, followed by Hölder in space, gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\mathcal B_{\rm ha}(u)|
 &\le {1\over\nu}\int
 \mathcal M_Hu\,g_\nabla u\,g_\Lambda u\,dx\\
 &\le {1\over\nu}
 \|\mathcal M_Hu\|_3
 \|g_\nabla u\|_3
 \|g_\Lambda u\|_3.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{HES.11}
\]

The heat maximal inequality and the heat Littlewood--Paley square-function
inequality, applied componentwise at exponent \(3\), give

\[
 \|\mathcal M_Hu\|_3
 +\|g_\nabla u\|_3
 +\|g_\Lambda u\|_3
 \le C\|u\|_3.
 \tag{HES.12}
\]

Equations `(HES.11)--(HES.12)` and
\(\|u\|_3\le C\|u\|_2^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_2^{1/2}\) prove

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathcal B_{\rm ha}(u(t))|
 \le {C_B\over\nu}
 \|u(t)\|_2^{3/2}Z(t)^{3/4}
 \le {C_B\over\nu}
 E_{\rm kin}^{3/4}Z(t)^{3/4}.}
 \tag{HES.13}
\]

This estimate preserves the full heat-age integral. It does not estimate the
fixed-selector strain and finite-chord pieces separately.

Raising `(HES.13)` to the power \(4/3\) gives the sharp row-level
integrability

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^{T_*}|\mathcal B_{\rm ha}(u(t))|^{4/3}dt
 \le C_B^{4/3}\nu^{-4/3}E_{\rm kin}
 \int_0^{T_*}Z(t)dt
 \le {C_B^{4/3}E_{\rm kin}^2\over2\nu^{7/3}}.}
 \tag{HES.14}
\]

The exponents are forced by amplitude and Navier--Stokes scaling among
monomials built only from \(\|u\|_2\) and \(\|\nabla u\|_2\): cubic
homogeneity requires exponents summing to \(3\), and critical spatial
scaling requires them equal, hence \(3/2\) and \(3/2\). Since the energy row
provides only \(Z\in L_t^1\), the resulting time exponent is \(4/3\).

## 6. A sharp endpoint-spike lemma

Let \(f\) be absolutely continuous on an interval \(I=(s,t)\), let
\(\Delta=t-s\), and suppose

\[
 \int_I|f|^p=\mu,
 \qquad p\ge1.
 \tag{HES.29}
\]

There is \(r\in I\) with
\(|f(r)|\le(\mu/\Delta)^{1/p}\). Hence for either endpoint \(e\),

\[
 |f(e)|
 \le\left({\mu\over\Delta}\right)^{1/p}
 +\int_I|f'|.
 \tag{HES.30}
\]

If \(|f(e)|\ge M\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta\le {2^p\mu\over M^p}
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 \int_I|f'|\ge {M\over2}.}
 \tag{HES.31}
\]

This elementary lemma is sharp at the level of time integrability: a
triangular spike of height \(M\) and width comparable to \(\mu/M^p\)
saturates the first alternative.

## 8. Why time integrability does not eliminate the endpoint branch

An \(L^p\) time-mass bound alone cannot control values at prescribed
endpoints. Given disjoint intervals and heights \(M_n\to\infty\), choose
widths \(w_n\) with

\[
 \sum_n M_n^p w_n<\infty.
 \tag{HES.41}
\]

Continuous triangular functions supported in those widths can take value
\(M_n\) at the selected endpoint while remaining in \(L^p\). This is only
the sharpness model for the inference from \(L^p\); it is not an admitted
Navier--Stokes history.

Therefore `(HES.14)` and `(HES.17)` cannot by themselves eliminate the B or
K endpoint alternatives in `(HES.26)`. The Navier--Stokes equation adds the
non-free consequences `(HES.34)--(HES.40)`: endpoint spikes must be
ultra-thin relative to the local datum-paid mass or generate a stronger
pressure-complete derivative/source pulse.
