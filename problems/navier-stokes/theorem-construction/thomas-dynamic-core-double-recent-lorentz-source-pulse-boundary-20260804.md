## 6. What edge/interior geometry and two simultaneous parents do prove

The receiver pulse \(\mathcal E_n\) lies entirely in the branch
\(\Gamma_n\) selected by CDR.

\[

\tag{LSP.45}
\]

\]

Thus `(LSP.40)` is a genuine earlier pressure-complete high-source pulse

### Edge branch

If

\[
 \Gamma_n=I_n^{\rm edge},
\tag{LSP.47}
\]

then the receiver square is compressed into the first \(\tau_n\) portion of
the record, and \(t-\ell_{n,t}\) may be smaller than \(s_n\).  The pressure
source is still earlier in the same global history, but no same-record claim
is available.

### Why neither branch produces an iterable nested record

Even on the interior branch, the new pulse is a norm statement for
\(P_{\ge r_n}Q(u)\).  The present argument proves none of the following:

1. a signed critical-current lower bound on
   \((t-\ell_{n,t},t)\);
2. a canonical height crossing or a new failed record there;
3. a positive lower bound for \(\ell_{n,t}\) or for the unweighted source mass
   in that interval;
4. two disjoint earlier source intervals, one assigned to each ordered parent;
5. a common modal parent fiber after the scalar source norm is taken; or
6. a prefix-prefix bilinear lower bound after the remaining recent ages are
   removed.

The two slots are simultaneous, but they contain the same recent high field.
One arbitrarily thin source interval may feed both.  The kernel is integrable
but singular at age zero, so `(LSP.40)` gives a diverging average and only an
upper width.  There is no source-mass floor to sum and no positive age gap on
which to iterate.
