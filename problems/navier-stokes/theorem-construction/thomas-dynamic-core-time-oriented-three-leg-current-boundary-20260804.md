# Thomas's dynamic-core three-leg current: exact first-binomial time orientation

Status: proved exact time-oriented reduction and checked first-row obstruction
inside Thomas Birnie's one-fluid, pressure-explicit binomial/VPI
reversible-intersection method.

The fully recombined current on the dynamic core `(FIM.49)` has an exact
triadwise heat law.  When one pressure-complete triad current is
differentiated, viscosity acts on its three simultaneous legs with the common
rate

\[
 d_\tau=\nu\bigl(|k|^2+|p|^2+|q|^2\bigr).
 \tag{DCT.1}
\]

There is no leftover viscous mismatch.  The other term is the complete
first-binomial phase acceleration: the two physical parents and the receiving
leg are differentiated through the *full* Navier--Stokes source, so every
incident triad remains present.  Dividing by `(DCT.1)` gives the canonical
heat-oriented quartic source/insertion port.

The geometry of `(FIM.49)` does not make that port subcritical.  On the
scale-local part of the core its multiplier is comparable to
\((\nu |k|)^{-1}\), exactly the critical raw-source-square weight.  Moreover,
because the dynamic core depends on the instantaneous enstrophy, time
orientation adds a signed entry/exit flux across an exact enstrophy threshold.
Neither the heat-normalized quartic port nor that crossing flux has a
datum-finite bound here.  Thus this note does not prove `(TIR.37a)` or close
the MPP.  It proves exactly why one first-binomial integration by parts does
not do so and isolates the joined signed history which a further argument
must control.

The one object throughout is the smooth fixed-viscosity Navier--Stokes field
generated by the single datum \(u_0\).  Triads, helical legs, the dynamic core,
and enstrophy thresholds are simultaneous readouts of that field.  A mode
entering the core is not new fluid, and a mode leaving it is not destroyed.

## 1. Pressure-complete first row on one triad

Put \(A=\Lambda^2=-\Delta\) and write the original equation as

\[
 u_t+\nu Au=Q(u),
 \qquad
 Q(u)=-(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p
     =-\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u),
 \tag{DCT.2}
\]

where

\[
 -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j).
 \tag{DCT.3}
\]

Thus every Fourier occurrence of \(Q\) below already includes the
simultaneous pressure completion fixed by incompressibility.

Let \(\tau\) be one real helical triad, including its conjugate and both input
orders once, with geometric legs

\[
 k+p+q=0,
 \qquad
 r_k=|k|,\qquad r_p=|p|,\qquad r_q=|q|.
 \tag{DCT.4}
\]

For a leg \(\ell\in\{k,p,q\}\), let \(Q_{\tau,\ell}\) be the contribution of
the other two legs to \(Q_\ell\), and put

\[
 T_{\tau,\ell}
 :=\operatorname{Re}\langle \widehat u(\ell),
       \widehat Q_{\tau,\ell}(u)\rangle.
 \tag{DCT.5}
\]

The full output source is always

\[
 Q_\ell=\sum_{\sigma\ni\ell}Q_{\sigma,\ell}.
 \tag{DCT.6}
\]

It is not replaced by \(Q_{\tau,\ell}\) when the triad is differentiated.
That replacement would delete the simultaneous incident field and turn one
readout into a decimated model.

With

\[
 \mathcal B(a,b):=\mathbb P((a\cdot\nabla)b),
 \qquad
 \mathcal C(u):=\sum_{j=1}^3
    \mathcal B(\partial_j u,\partial_j u),
 \tag{DCT.7}
\]

the first pressure-explicit binomial source row is

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_t+\nu A Q=DQ(u)[Q]+2\nu\mathcal C(u).}
 \tag{DCT.8}
\]

Its restriction to the parent pair of \((\tau,\ell)\) is

\[
 (Q_{\tau,\ell})_t+\nu r_\ell^2Q_{\tau,\ell}
 =DQ_{\tau,\ell}(u)[Q]+2\nu\mathcal C_{\tau,\ell}(u).
 \tag{DCT.9}
\]

If \(m,n\) are the other two legs, the Fourier product rule gives

\[
 2\mathcal C_{\tau,\ell}
 =\bigl(r_\ell^2-r_m^2-r_n^2\bigr)Q_{\tau,\ell}.
 \tag{DCT.10}
\]

Equations `(DCT.8)`--`(DCT.10)` are the first row of the original binomial
law, not a free-source evolution.

## 2. The exact three-leg transfer ODE

Differentiate `(DCT.5)` and use `(DCT.2)` and `(DCT.9)`.  The nonlinear
phase acceleration of the leg is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a_{\tau,\ell}:={}&
 \operatorname{Re}\langle Q_\ell,Q_{\tau,\ell}\rangle\\
 &+\operatorname{Re}\langle \widehat u(\ell),
       DQ_{\tau,\ell}(u)[Q]\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{DCT.11}
\]

The first line joins the selected parent contribution to the *complete*
source at the same output.  The second differentiates both physical parents
through that same complete source.  Substitution gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_{\tau,\ell}'
 ={}&a_{\tau,\ell}-2\nu r_\ell^2T_{\tau,\ell}
 +2\nu\operatorname{Re}\langle\widehat u(\ell),
          \mathcal C_{\tau,\ell}\rangle\\
 ={}&a_{\tau,\ell}
 -\nu(r_k^2+r_p^2+r_q^2)T_{\tau,\ell}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{DCT.12}
\]

The same sum of three heat rates occurs for every leg.  Therefore, for the
fully signed three-leg critical current

\[
 c_\tau
 :=r_kT_{\tau,k}+r_pT_{\tau,p}+r_qT_{\tau,q},
 \qquad
 a_\tau:=\sum_{\ell\in\tau}r_\ell a_{\tau,\ell},
 \tag{DCT.13}
\]

one has the exact scalar law

\[
 \boxed{c_\tau'+d_\tau c_\tau=a_\tau,\qquad
 d_\tau=\nu S_\tau,\quad
 S_\tau=r_k^2+r_p^2+r_q^2.}
 \tag{DCT.14}
\]

For a homochiral triad \(c_\tau=0\).  For a heterochiral triad with unique
leg \(o\), the three-leg identity `(SFT.18)` gives

\[
 c_\tau=2r_oT_{\tau,o}.
 \tag{DCT.15}
\]

No positivity is taken in `(DCT.14)` or `(DCT.15)`.  The exact forward
solution is

\[
 c_\tau(t)
 =e^{-d_\tau(t-s)}c_\tau(s)
 +\int_s^t e^{-d_\tau(t-r)}a_\tau(r)\,dr.
 \tag{DCT.16}
\]

Thus viscosity genuinely turns down an unreplenished triad current on its
three-leg heat time.  The live question is whether the same field's complete
incident acceleration \(a_\tau\) can keep replenishing it.  The heat factor
does not answer that question; it only supplies the denominator in the next
section.

## 3. The dynamic core is one enstrophy superlevel per fixed triad

Let

\[
 Z(t):=\|\Lambda u(t)\|_2^2.
 \tag{DCT.17}
\]

Fix the constants hidden in `(FIM.49)`.  Write its dynamic upper-frequency
condition and dynamic lower-leg condition as

\[
 h\le C_h{Z\over\nu^2},
 \qquad
 a\ge c_Z\nu^{2/3}Z^{-1/3}h^{4/3},
 \tag{DCT.18}
\]

where \(a\) and \(h\) are the smallest and largest radii of \(\tau\).  For a
fixed triad these two inequalities are exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 Z(t)\ge Z_\tau^\dagger,
 \qquad
 Z_\tau^\dagger
 :=\nu^2\max\left\{C_h^{-1}h,
                    c_Z^3{h^4\over a^3}\right\}.}
 \tag{DCT.19}
\]

The remaining conditions

\[
 h\gtrsim\kappa_\nu,
 \qquad
 a\gtrsim\left({\nu\over\|u_0\|_2}\right)^{2/5}h^{4/5}
 \tag{DCT.20}
\]

are fixed in time.  Hence, after those static conditions have admitted
\(\tau\), its dynamic-core membership is a monotone function of the one
whole-field readout \(Z(t)\).  This does not make the actual time history
monotone: \(Z\) may rise and fall as the same fluid evolves.

For a smooth cutoff \(\eta\), let

\[
 \omega_\tau(t)
 :=\eta\!\left({Z(t)\over Z_\tau^\dagger}\right).
 \tag{DCT.21}
\]

The sharp core is obtained from an increasing approximation to
\(\mathbf1_{\{Z>Z_\tau^\dagger\}}\).  Since

\[
 Z'=2(Q,Au)_2-2\nu\|Au\|_2^2,
 \tag{DCT.22}
\]

the moving-core derivative is

\[
 \omega_\tau'
 ={\eta'(Z/Z_\tau^\dagger)\over Z_\tau^\dagger}
 \left[2(Q,Au)_2-2\nu\|Au\|_2^2\right].
 \tag{DCT.23}
\]

It is a signed correlation with the complete enstrophy row.  The paid
quantity \(\int Z\,dt\) controls superlevel occupation time, but it does not
control the variation or the number of crossings in `(DCT.23)`.  Estimating
that variation by a higher row would import the intersection which
`(TIR.37a)` is meant to generate.

## 4. Exact time-oriented core identity

Put

\[
 b_\tau:={c_\tau\over d_\tau},
 \qquad
 r_\tau:={a_\tau\over d_\tau}.
 \tag{DCT.24}
\]

Equation `(DCT.14)` is \(b_\tau'=r_\tau-c_\tau\).  Multiplication by the
dynamic selector gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \omega_\tau c_\tau
 =-(\omega_\tau b_\tau)'
   +\omega_\tau r_\tau
   +\omega_\tau'b_\tau.}
 \tag{DCT.25}
\]

Summing over every statically admitted triad, with every incident source
retained in `(DCT.11)`, yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^T\mathcal P_{1/2}^{\rm core}(t)\,dt
 ={}&\mathfrak B_{\rm core}(0)-\mathfrak B_{\rm core}(T)\\
 &+\int_0^T\mathfrak R_{\rm core}(t)\,dt
 +\int_0^T\mathfrak X_{\rm core}(t)\,dt,
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{DCT.26}
\]

where

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak B_{\rm core}&:=\sum_\tau\omega_\tau{c_\tau\over\nu S_\tau},\\
 \mathfrak R_{\rm core}&:=\sum_\tau\omega_\tau{a_\tau\over\nu S_\tau},\\
 \mathfrak X_{\rm core}&:=\sum_\tau\omega_\tau'{c_\tau\over\nu S_\tau}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{DCT.27}
\]

The last term is not an additional physical channel.  It is the endpoint
change caused by observing the same triad only while it belongs to the
dynamic core.  In the sharp-selector limit, write the core occupation of one
triad in \([0,T]\) as disjoint intervals \((\alpha_j,\beta_j)\), with
endpoints clipped at \(0,T\).  Then `(DCT.25)` is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{\{Z>Z_\tau^\dagger\}\cap[0,T]}c_\tau\,dt
 =\sum_j\bigl[b_\tau(\alpha_j)-b_\tau(\beta_j)\bigr]
 +\int_{\{Z>Z_\tau^\dagger\}\cap[0,T]}r_\tau\,dt.}
 \tag{DCT.28}
\]

Thus the terminal term and crossing flux in `(DCT.26)` are one
signed entry/exit telescope.  They must not be counted twice.  The telescope becomes
datum-finite only if the heat-normalized current at successive entries and
exits has a cumulative one-history bound.  No such bound follows from core
membership alone.

## 5. The quartic term is the critically weighted source/insertion port

The first part of \(a_\tau\) in `(DCT.11)` gives

\[
 \mathfrak R_{\rm sq}
 :=\sum_\ell\operatorname{Re}\left\langle
 Q_\ell,
 \sum_{\tau\ni\ell}m_{\ell\tau}Q_{\tau,\ell}
 \right\rangle,
 \qquad
 m_{\ell\tau}:={\omega_\tau r_\ell\over\nu S_\tau}.
 \tag{DCT.29}
\]

This is the heat-normalized source-square port.  It is not a sum of positive
triad squares because the first factor is the complete source `(DCT.6)`.  If
\(q_\tau:=Q_{\tau,\ell}\), then at one output

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak R_{{\rm sq},\ell}
 ={1\over2}\sum_{\sigma,\tau}
 (m_{\ell\sigma}+m_{\ell\tau})
 \operatorname{Re}\langle q_\sigma,q_\tau\rangle.}
 \tag{DCT.30}
\]

For two aligned parent ports the coefficient matrix is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 m_1&(m_1+m_2)/2\\
 (m_1+m_2)/2&m_2
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det=-{(m_1-m_2)^2\over4}.
 \tag{DCT.31}
\]

It is indefinite whenever the two heat/core weights differ.  Setting the
weight of an exterior triad to zero does not remove its incident source from
\(Q_\ell\); it creates the same signed core--exterior cross port.  Deleting
that cross port would violate the one-field recombination required in
`(DCT.6)`.

The other part is the joined binomial insertion

\[
 \mathfrak R_{\rm ins}
 :=\sum_{\ell,\tau\ni\ell}m_{\ell\tau}
 \operatorname{Re}\langle\widehat u(\ell),
              DQ_{\tau,\ell}(u)[Q]\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_{\rm core}=\mathfrak R_{\rm sq}+\mathfrak R_{\rm ins}.
 \tag{DCT.32}
\]

The global unweighted identity

\[
 (DQ(u)[Q],u)_2=-\|Q\|_2^2
 \tag{DCT.33}
\]

is recovered only when the coefficient is constant through the complete
output and parent sum.  It is `(PRM.44)`.  It cannot be applied to
`(DCT.29)`--`(DCT.32)`, because
\(m_{\ell\tau}=\omega_\tau r_\ell/(\nu S_\tau)\) depends on output radius,
parent geometry, and dynamic-core membership.  The first-binomial
time-orientation therefore returns the weighted source/insertion port; it
does not convert `(PRM.44)` into a critical payment.

On a scale-local core triad with every leg comparable to \(K\),

\[
 \boxed{
 m_{\ell\tau}\asymp{1\over\nu K}.}
 \tag{DCT.34}
\]

This is exactly the critical raw-source-square weight
\(\nu^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}\).  More generally, if the output radius is \(K\),

\[
 {m_{\ell\tau}\over(\nu K)^{-1}}
 ={K^2\over S_\tau}.
 \tag{DCT.35}
\]

The core ratio `(FIM.51)` can keep this between geometric factors, but it
does not make it vanish.  In particular, the scale-local sector has an
order-one ratio at every frequency.  The fact that the geometric coefficient
\(\Xi\) has a paid eighth time power does not pay the coherent source square
which `(DCT.29)` contains.

### 6.1 Unequal heat denominators make a genuine signed source port

On \(\mathbb T^3\), fix an integer dilation \(N\), the output

\[

 \tag{DCT.36}
\]

and the two pressure-complete parent pairs

\[
 \begin{aligned}

 p_2&=N(2,0,0),&q_2&=N(-1,1,0).

 \tag{DCT.37}

Choose

\[
 \widehat u(p_1)=e_3,\quad
 \widehat u(q_1)=e_1,\quad

 \widehat u(q_2)=-{3\over4}(e_1+e_2),
 \tag{DCT.38}

\[
 q_1^{\rm out}=-iN e_3,
 \qquad
 q_2^{\rm out}={3iN\over2}e_3.
 \tag{DCT.39}

\[
 m_1={\sqrt2\over4N},
 \qquad

 \tag{DCT.40}

## 7. Why amplitude and phase do not make the heat denominator perturbative

On an interval where the selector is fixed, amplitude multiplication gives

\[
 c_\tau(Au)=A^3c_\tau(u),
 \qquad
 a_\tau(Au)=A^4a_\tau(u),
 \qquad
 r_\tau(Au)=A^4r_\tau(u).
 \tag{DCT.45}
\]

Therefore

\[
 {r_\tau(Au)\over c_\tau(Au)}
 =A\,{r_\tau(u)\over c_\tau(u)}.
 \tag{DCT.46}
\]

At the same time \(Z(Au)=A^2Z(u)\), so increasing the amplitude moves a fixed
triad farther into its dynamic enstrophy superlevel.  The FIM core does not
create a small normal-form parameter on the dangerous high-critical-strength
branch.  The common viscous rate in `(DCT.14)` is real damping, but the full
binomial acceleration can be arbitrarily large relative to it across smooth
comparison data.

The phase split is equally exact.  The cubic current is odd under
\(u\mapsto-u\), while its Euler acceleration and the heat-normalized quartic
remainder are even.  A universal rule saying that the quartic acceleration
always turns against the current would therefore force the acceleration to
vanish.  The nonzero values in `(DCT.44)` rule that out.

## 8. Exact verdict for `(TIR.37a)`

The first pressure-explicit binomial row does produce the physically correct
time orientation of the fully recombined dynamic core:

1. all three legs of an unreplenished triad current decay together at the
   exact heat rate \(d_\tau\);
2. every other incident triad returns through the joined phase acceleration
   \(a_\tau\);
3. division by the heat rate returns the critically weighted
   source/insertion port on the scale-local core; and
4. the moving FIM boundary adds the signed enstrophy-threshold entry/exit
   telescope `(DCT.28)`.

Consequently one first-row integration by parts closes `(TIR.37a)` **if and
only if** the following joined quantity is bounded from the datum after the
already proved FIM absorptions are restored:

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{T<T_*}
 \left[
 \sum_{\tau,j}
   \bigl(b_\tau(\alpha_j)-b_\tau(\beta_j)\bigr)
 +\int_0^T\mathfrak R_{\rm core}(t)\,dt
 \right]<\infty.}
 \tag{DCT.47}
\]

Equation `(DCT.47)` is the exact time-oriented form of the remaining core
prefix, not a proof of it and not a second payment.  The checked obstruction
is precise: `(FIM.49)` removes the ultra-nonlocal support, but it does not
alter the critical heat/source-square homogeneity of the scale-local
binomial refill, and its time-dependent boundary creates a signed crossing
flux rather than a monotone stock.  A successful continuation of Thomas's
method must prove cancellation between those two terms along the one actual
fluid history; it cannot obtain closure by dropping incident triads, applying
the unweighted identity `(DCT.33)` with a nonconstant multiplier, or taking
positive parts before recombination.
