# Thomas's first-contact signed-prefix and negative-dome turn-down boundary

**Date:** 2026-08-04

**Status:** exact first-contact localization and checked level-weight boundary
inside Thomas Birnie's one-fluid, pressure-explicit binomial/VPI reversible-
intersection method. Every failed canonical last-half signed-prefix record
contains a disjoint first-contact subrecord with a path-independent positive
level moment and a viscosity-matched signed source/receiver pulse. The endpoint
signs are exact. Differentiating the same complete current gives the first
pressure-explicit binomial commutator, but every nontrivial weight depending
only on the traversed prefix level leaves either an uncontrolled endpoint
current or a sign-changing current square.

This is not a turn-down theorem, a strict reduction to a smaller critical
payment, a proof of (TIR.37a), or MPP closure. It proves the strongest
consequence supplied by first-contact geometry alone and identifies precisely
what must still come from the one-fluid pressure--transport--viscosity history.
The fixed-selector imbalance and complete finite-chord terms remain joined
throughout.

## 1. One fluid history and its canonical record

Fix one smooth divergence-free finite-energy datum \(u_0\), one viscosity
\(\nu>0\), and the single preterminal Navier--Stokes history generated by that
datum:

\[
 u_t+\nu\Lambda^2u=Q(u),
 \qquad
 Q(u)=-(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p
     =-\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u),
 \tag{FCT.1}
\]

\[
 -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
 \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0.
 \tag{FCT.2}
\]

Transport, the simultaneous whole-field pressure response, incompressibility,
viscosity, strain, vorticity, and every finite chord belong to this one
history. No source or scale profile is prescribed independently.

Put

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H(t)&:={1\over2}\|\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)\|_2^2,\\
 D(t)&:=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u(t)\|_2^2,\\
 P(t)&:=\mathcal P_{1/2}(t)=(Q(u(t)),\Lambda u(t))_2,\\
 \mathscr W(t)&:=\int_0^tP(\tau)\,d\tau.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{FCT.3}
\]

The exact critical balance is

\[
 \boxed{H'=P-\nu D,\qquad
 \mathscr W(t)=H(t)-H(0)+\nu\int_0^tD(\tau)\,d\tau.}
 \tag{FCT.4}
\]

Assume the exact failure of (TIR.37a) and use the canonical records from
(MRN.4)--(MRN.6):

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_n&=4^nA_0,\\
 t_n&=\inf\{t<T_*:\mathscr W(t)=A_n\},\\
 s_n&=\sup\{t<t_n:\mathscr W(t)=A_n/2\},\\
 I_n&=(s_n,t_n).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{FCT.5}
\]

Then the \(I_n\) are ordered and disjoint, and

\[
 \mathscr W(t)\in[A_n/2,A_n]
 \quad(s_n\le t\le t_n).
 \tag{FCT.6}
\]

## 2. The exact first-contact subrecord

Set

\[
 r_n:=\inf\{t\in(s_n,t_n):\mathscr W(t)=3A_n/4\},
 \qquad J_n:=(s_n,r_n),
 \qquad h_n:={A_n\over4}.
 \tag{FCT.7}
\]

Continuity and (FCT.6) give \(s_n<r_n<t_n\). By the last contact at
\(s_n\) and the first contact at \(r_n\),

\[
 {A_n\over2}<\mathscr W(t)<{3A_n\over4}
 \quad(s_n<t<r_n).
 \tag{FCT.8}
\]

Define the traversed prefix level

\[
 \boxed{Y_n(t):=\mathscr W(t)-{A_n\over2}.}
 \tag{FCT.9}
\]

It is a coordinate of the same signed current, not a new state variable. On
\([s_n,r_n]\),

\[
 0\le Y_n\le h_n,
 \qquad
 Y_n(s_n)=0,
 \qquad
 Y_n(r_n)=h_n,
 \qquad
 Y_n'=P.
 \tag{FCT.10}
\]

The intervals \(J_n\subset I_n\) remain ordered and disjoint. The contact
orientation also gives the exact endpoint signs

\[
 \boxed{P(s_n)\ge0,\qquad P(r_n)\ge0.}
 \tag{FCT.11}
\]

Indeed, immediately to the right of \(s_n\), \(\mathscr W>A_n/2\), while
immediately to the left of \(r_n\), \(\mathscr W<3A_n/4\). The corresponding
one-sided difference quotients are nonnegative, and the preterminal solution
is smooth.

For every \(C^1\) function \(\Psi\) on \([0,h_n]\), the chain rule gives the
complete level identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{J_n}\Psi'(Y_n(t))P(t)\,dt
 =\Psi(h_n)-\Psi(0).}
 \tag{FCT.12}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{J_n}P\,dt&=h_n={A_n\over4},\\
 \int_{J_n}Y_nP\,dt
 &=\int_{J_n}(h_n-Y_n)P\,dt
 ={h_n^2\over2}={A_n^2\over32},\\
 \int_{J_n}(2Y_n-h_n)P\,dt&=0.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{FCT.13}
\]

These are signed identities. They remain valid if \(P\) changes sign inside
\(J_n\); no monotonicity of the path between its two contacts was assumed.

## 3. A viscosity-matched signed pulse with exact constants

Let

\[
 b(t):=\|\Lambda^{-1/2}Q(u(t))\|_2,
 \qquad d(t):=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u(t)\|_2=\sqrt{D(t)}.
 \tag{FCT.14}
\]

The pressure-complete duality is

\[
 |P(t)|\le b(t)d(t).
 \tag{FCT.15}
\]

Integrating (FCT.4) on \(J_n\), using \(H(r_n)\ge0\), and using the left
record level in (FCT.5), gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \nu\int_{J_n}d^2dt
 &=h_n-H(r_n)+H(s_n)\\
 &\le h_n+H(s_n)\\
 &\le H(0)+{3A_n\over4}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{FCT.16}
\]

Write

\[
 B_n:=H(0)+{3A_n\over4},
 \qquad
 \eta_n:={\nu A_n\over8B_n},
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_n:=J_n\cap\{b\ge\eta_nd\}.
 \tag{FCT.17}
\]

On \(J_n\setminus\mathcal F_n\), equations (FCT.15)--(FCT.17) give

\[
 \int_{J_n\setminus\mathcal F_n}P\,dt
 \le\eta_n\int_{J_n}d^2dt
 \le {A_n\over8}.
 \tag{FCT.18}
\]

Subtracting (FCT.18) from the first identity in (FCT.13) proves the
first-contact pulse

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}P(t)\,dt\ge {A_n\over8}.}
 \tag{FCT.19}
\]

Because \(d\le b/\eta_n\) on \(\mathcal F_n\), the same signed inequality
forces

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}b(t)^2dt
 \ge\eta_n\int_{\mathcal F_n}P(t)\,dt
 \ge {\nu A_n^2\over64B_n}.}
 \tag{FCT.20}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}{1\over A_n}
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}b^2dt\ge {\nu\over48}.
 \tag{FCT.21}
\]

The already proved datum bound \(b\in L^1(0,T_*)\), together with the
disjointness of the \(J_n\), gives

\[
 \int_{J_n}b(t)dt\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{FCT.22}
\]

Thus the first contact localizes a genuine same-history, viscosity-matched,
signed pulse. Equations (FCT.20)--(FCT.22) describe concentration of the
complete pressure source; they do not make its critical square action
datum-finite.

## 4. The actual first-binomial derivative of the current

For divergence-free fields put

\[
 \mathcal B(a,c):=\mathbb P((a\cdot\nabla)c),
 \qquad
 DQ(u)[v]=-\mathcal B(v,u)-\mathcal B(u,v).
 \tag{FCT.23}
\]

The energy and signed-helicity tangent identities imply, for every
divergence-free \(v\),

\[
 (DQ(u)[v],u)_2+(Q(u),v)_2=0.
 \tag{FCT.24}
\]

Differentiating \(P=(Q,\Lambda u)_2\) along the actual solution and applying
(FCT.24) with \(v=\Lambda u_t\) gives

\[
 \boxed{P'=\mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;u_t),}
 \tag{FCT.25}
\]

where the complete first-binomial commutator is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;v)
 :=(DQ(u)[v],\Lambda u)_2
 -(DQ(u)[\Lambda v],u)_2.}
 \tag{FCT.26}
\]

Both ordered velocity-parent insertions and their pressure completion remain
inside \(DQ\). With

\[
 \mathcal C(u):=\sum_{j=1}^3
 \mathcal B(\partial_ju,\partial_ju),
 \tag{FCT.27}
\]

the pressure-explicit product rule is

\[
 DQ(u)[\Lambda^2u]=\Lambda^2Q(u)-2\mathcal C(u).
 \tag{FCT.28}
\]

Since \(u_t=Q-\nu\Lambda^2u\), (FCT.25) has the two equivalent joined
forms

\[
 \boxed{
 P'=\mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;Q)
 -\nu\mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;\Lambda^2u),}
 \tag{FCT.29}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 P'={}&(DQ(u)[Q],\Lambda u)_2
 +\|\Lambda^{1/2}Q\|_2^2\\
 &-2\nu(Q,\Lambda^3u)_2
 +2\nu(\mathcal C(u),\Lambda u)_2.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{FCT.30}
\]

No source square or viscosity--source pairing in (FCT.30) has an
independent sign license. The equality is the differentiated current of the
same fluid.

## 5. Every level-only signed weight has an exact boundary term

Let \(\phi\in C^1([0,h_n])\). Equations (FCT.10) and (FCT.25) give

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{J_n}\phi(Y_n)\mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;u_t)\,dt
 ={}&\phi(h_n)P(r_n)-\phi(0)P(s_n)\\
 &-\int_{J_n}\phi'(Y_n)P^2dt.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{FCT.31}
\]

This identity exhausts what first-contact endpoint signs can do for a weight
depending only on the prefix level. Three important choices are

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{J_n}Y_n\mathfrak K_{1/2}\,dt
 &=h_nP(r_n)-\int_{J_n}P^2dt,
 \tag{FCT.32}\\
 \int_{J_n}(h_n-Y_n)\mathfrak K_{1/2}\,dt
 &=-h_nP(s_n)+\int_{J_n}P^2dt,
 \tag{FCT.33}\\
 \int_{J_n}Y_n(h_n-Y_n)\mathfrak K_{1/2}\,dt
 &=\int_{J_n}(2Y_n-h_n)P^2dt.
 \tag{FCT.34}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The first weight pays the left endpoint but leaves the nonnegative top
endpoint. The second pays the top endpoint but reverses the square. The dome
weight in (FCT.34) removes both endpoints, but its coefficient changes sign
exactly halfway through the traversed prefix. Reversing the dome reverses the
whole identity and does not remove that sign change.

There is a sharp information-level no-go statement. To certify an upper
turn-down sign in (FCT.31) from only

\[
 P(s_n)\ge0,
 \qquad P(r_n)\ge0,
 \qquad P^2\ge0,
 \tag{FCT.35}
\]

one would need simultaneously

\[
 \phi(h_n)\le0,
 \qquad
 \phi(0)\ge0,
 \qquad
 \phi'\ge0.
 \tag{FCT.36}
\]

Monotonicity then gives

\[
 0\ge\phi(h_n)\ge\phi(0)\ge0,
 \tag{FCT.37}
\]

so \(\phi\equiv0\). The analogous lower-sign conditions also force the zero
weight.

This is not merely a failure of termwise estimation. Among smooth scalar
first-contact paths, every nonzero level weight produces both signs. Let
\(v(y)>0\) be smooth and let \(Y'=v(Y)\) carry \(Y\) strictly from \(0\) to
\(h_n\). Then \(P=v(Y)>0\) at both contacts and

\[
 \int\phi(Y)P'\,dt
 =\int_0^{h_n}\phi(y)v'(y)\,dy.
 \tag{FCT.38}
\]

For any nonzero \(\phi\), choose a smooth \(g\) with
\(\int_0^{h_n}\phi g'\ne0\). The two positive speeds
\(v_\pm=c\pm\varepsilon g\), for \(c\) large and
\(\varepsilon\) small, give opposite signs in (FCT.38). For the dome itself,

\[
 v_\varepsilon(y)=c+\varepsilon(2y-h_n),
 \qquad c>|\varepsilon|h_n,
 \tag{FCT.39}
\]

gives the exact value

\[
 \boxed{
 \int Y(h_n-Y)P'\,dt={\varepsilon h_n^3\over3},}
 \tag{FCT.40}
\]

which has either sign. Equations (FCT.38)--(FCT.40) are scalar information
countertests, not independently realizable Navier--Stokes histories. They
prove only that the required sign must use an additional exact correlation
from the one-fluid law; it cannot follow from the first-contact shape by
itself.

## 6. The critical-height curvature retains the next pressure and viscosity rows

The prefix contact is not a critical-height contact. Equation (FCT.4) gives

\[
 H'(s_n)=P(s_n)-\nu D(s_n),
 \qquad
 H'(r_n)=P(r_n)-\nu D(r_n).
 \tag{FCT.41}
\]

Thus (FCT.11) supplies no sign for either height derivative. Differentiating
\(D=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2\) without separating pressure gives

\[
 D'=2\mathcal P_{3/2}-2\nu\|\Lambda^{5/2}u\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_{3/2}:=(Q(u),\Lambda^3u)_2.
 \tag{FCT.42}
\]

Combining (FCT.25), (FCT.41), and (FCT.42) yields the exact second
critical-height response

\[
 \boxed{
 H''
 =\mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;u_t)
 -2\nu\mathcal P_{3/2}
 +2\nu^2\|\Lambda^{5/2}u\|_2^2.}
 \tag{FCT.43}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 H''=\mathfrak R_{1/2,2}
 +4\nu^2E_{5/2},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R_{1/2,2}
 =\mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;u_t)-2\nu\mathcal P_{3/2}.
 \tag{FCT.44}
\]

Even at a genuine first contact of \(H\) with a higher height, the contact
would give \(H'\ge0\), hence \(P\ge\nu D\), but it would not sign (FCT.43).
The first-binomial commutator and next pressure-complete current remain joined,
and the even viscous response has the opposite sign from the odd turn-down row
in (TIR.26). Therefore a literal concavity assertion is not Thomas's
higher-response theorem.

## 7. The imbalance and finite-chord join is unchanged

Fix the time-independent radial selector of (FRS.5). With

\[
 c_\zeta={64\ell_0\over15\pi},
 \qquad
 \mathcal S_\zeta(t)
 =c_\zeta\int a(d^2-a^2)\,dx,
 \tag{FCT.45}
\]

the complete fixed-selector identity is

\[
 \boxed{P=\mathcal S_\zeta+\mathcal E_\zeta.}
 \tag{FCT.46}
\]

Here \(d\le a\), including perfect balance \(d=0\), is favorable in the
strain-shape term; \(d>a\) is adverse; and \(\mathcal E_\zeta\) retains every
finite chord. Since the selector is fixed,

\[
 \mathfrak K_{1/2}(u;u_t)
 ={d\over dt}(\mathcal S_\zeta+\mathcal E_\zeta).
 \tag{FCT.47}
\]

Substituting (FCT.46)--(FCT.47) into the dome identity gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &\int_{J_n}Y_n(h_n-Y_n)
 {d\over dt}(\mathcal S_\zeta+\mathcal E_\zeta)\,dt\\
 &\qquad
 =\int_{J_n}(2Y_n-h_n)
 (\mathcal S_\zeta+\mathcal E_\zeta)^2dt.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{FCT.48}
\]

The square in (FCT.48) is one joined square. Splitting it into a favorable
strain bill and a finite-chord bill would discard their cross term and undo
the exact full-field identity. First-contact weighting therefore neither
replaces nor damages Thomas's balanced/imbalanced finite-chord route; it shows
that the route must provide a time-oriented sign for the complete joined
response, not for either piece separately.

## 8. Exact theorem and remaining boundary

Failure of (TIR.37a) forces, on disjoint first-contact subrecords of the one
continuing fluid history,

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 Y_n:0\longrightarrow {A_n\over4},
 \qquad
 P(s_n),P(r_n)\ge0,\\
 \int_{J_n}Y_nP\,dt={A_n^2\over32},
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}P\,dt\ge{A_n\over8},\\
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}b^2dt
 \ge{\nu A_n^2\over64(H(0)+3A_n/4)}.
 \end{gathered}}
 \tag{FCT.49}
\]

This is the strongest first-contact localization proved here. The exact
differentiated identity is (FCT.31), and its endpoint/sign conflict proves
that no nonzero level-only dome or signed weight turns (FCT.49) into a
datum-finite charge. The required new theorem is still a bound for the joined,
time-oriented pressure-explicit response of the same fluid, with the
imbalance/finite-chord correlation retained. The result is exact same-critical
localization and a checked shortcut boundary, not (TIR.37a) or MPP closure.
