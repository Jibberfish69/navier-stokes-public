## 4. Backward pullback represents the divergent squared incidence

Let \(\Psi(r,s)\) be the evolution operator for

\[
 \partial_rY=\mathscr G_u(r)Y.
\tag{IFG.24}
\]

Thus the actual parent satisfies \(F(r)=\Psi(r,s)F(s)\).  At finite cutoff,
define the full backward observability Gramian on the signed source interval

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}(r)
 :=\int_r^{b_n}
 \Psi(s,r)^*\mathsf R_n^{(\ell)}(s)\Psi(s,r)\,ds.}
\tag{IFG.25}
\]

It is positive and satisfies the finite-cutoff operator identity

\[
 \boxed{
 -\partial_r\mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}
 =\mathscr G_u^*\mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}
 +\mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}\mathscr G_u
 +\mathsf R_n^{(\ell)},
 \qquad
 \mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}(b_n)=0.}
\tag{IFG.26}
\]

Evaluation on the same actual parent gives the exact finite-cutoff
forward/backward join

\[
 \boxed{
 (F(a_n),\mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}(a_n)F(a_n))_{\rm par}
 =\int_{a_n}^{b_n}\bigl(c_n^{(\ell)}(r)\bigr)^2\,dr.}
\tag{IFG.27}
\]

The integrated Gramian in `(IFG.25)` generally need not have rank one even
though its point observation `(IFG.16)` has rank at most one.  In the
continuum define the actual-orbit form value directly by

\[
 \mathfrak o_n^{(\ell)}[F(a_n)]
 :=\int_{a_n}^{b_n}\bigl(c_n^{(\ell)}(r)\bigr)^2\,dr.
 \tag{IFG.27a}
\]

The sign is used before the square.  Cauchy--Schwarz and `(IFG.13)` prove the
cutoff-independent scalar conclusion

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak o_n^{(\ell)}[F(a_n)]
 \ge {A_n^2\over4096|S_n^{\rm sgn}|},
 \qquad \ell=1,2.}
\tag{IFG.28}
\]

This is not two payments; by `(IFG.14)` both formal slots give the same
Gramian after the symmetric join.

Since \(|S_n^{\rm sgn}|\to0\) and \(A_n=4^nA_0\), the right side of
`(IFG.28)` diverges.  The actual parent still obeys

\[

 =\|u(a_n)\|_2^4
 \le\|u_0\|_2^4,
\tag{IFG.29}

but the continuum inference
\(\mathfrak o_n[F]\le
\|\mathsf O_n\|_{\rm op}\|F\|_{\rm par}^2\) is unavailable: no bounded

Indeed `(UPG.22)--(UPG.24)` exhibit the unbounded parent-fiber collapse.
The correct conclusion is therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak o_n^{(\ell)}[F(a_n)]
 \ge {A_n^2\over4096|S_n^{\rm sgn}|}
 \longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \text{with only a finite-cutoff/form-domain Gramian representation}.}
\tag{IFG.30}
\]

This is the strongest positive conclusion of the Gramian join.  It is
anti-causal twice over: the point form \(\mathsf R_n^{(\ell)}(s)\) contains the later actual
receiver and the other later actual parent, and `(IFG.25)` then pulls that
future observation backward through the parent evolution.  Equation
`(IFG.30)` is divergence of future-defined squared incidence, not an
unrestricted continuum operator-norm theorem and not growth of a causal stock
computed from the datum at \(a_n\).

## 5. The formal forward storage loses exactly the same future action

At finite cutoff, let \(\mathsf P_{0,n}^{(\ell)}\) be any self-adjoint initial
metric and solve formally forward

\[
 \boxed{
 \partial_r\mathsf P_n^{(\ell)}
 +\mathscr G_u^*\mathsf P_n^{(\ell)}
 +\mathsf P_n^{(\ell)}\mathscr G_u
 =-\mathsf R_n^{(\ell)},
 \qquad
 \mathsf P_n^{(\ell)}(a_n)=\mathsf P_{0,n}^{(\ell)}.}
\tag{IFG.31}
\]

Along the actual parent,

\[
 {d\over dr}{1\over2}
 (F,\mathsf P_n^{(\ell)}F)_{\rm par}
 =-{1\over2}\bigl(c_n^{(\ell)}\bigr)^2.
\tag{IFG.32}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 &(F(b_n),\mathsf P_n^{(\ell)}(b_n)F(b_n))_{\rm par}\\
 &\quad=(F(a_n),\mathsf P_{0,n}^{(\ell)}F(a_n))_{\rm par}
 -\int_{a_n}^{b_n}\bigl(c_n^{(\ell)}\bigr)^2dr\\
 &\quad=(F(a_n),
 (\mathsf P_{0,n}^{(\ell)}-\mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}(a_n))
 F(a_n))_{\rm par}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{IFG.33}
\]

There are two separate boundaries here.

Before either boundary, a continuum passage would require a closed form domain
and cutoff-uniform comparison.  Nothing here supplies that comparison; the
collapse obstruction `(UPG.22)--(UPG.24)` remains in force.

First, `(IFG.31)` is forward only in the direction of integration.  Its
running metric \(\mathsf R_n^{(\ell)}\) is defined by the future receiver, so
`(IFG.31)` is not a datum-computable causal law.  Calling it forward does not
change its information direction.

Second, even if that defect is ignored, the endpoint argument reaches the
continuum only for a cutoff family whose quadratic losses converge to, or
dominate, the actual scalar action `(IFG.27a)`.  Under precisely that additional
comparison, a datum-uniform initial upper bound and terminal lower bound would
give a datum-uniform upper bound for the anti-causal action.  Explicitly, if

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (F(a_n),\mathsf P_{0,n}^{(\ell)}F(a_n))_{\rm par}
 &\le C_{\rm dat},\\
 (F(b_n),\mathsf P_n^{(\ell)}(b_n)F(b_n))_{\rm par}
 &\ge-C_{\rm dat},
\end{aligned}
\tag{IFG.34}
\]

uniformly along such a convergent or dominating cutoff family, then
`(IFG.33)` and passage through that comparison would imply

\[
 \int_{a_n}^{b_n}\bigl(c_n^{(\ell)}\bigr)^2dr
 \le2C_{\rm dat},
\tag{IFG.35}
\]

contradicting `(IFG.28)` for large \(n\).  Thus the exact square-Gramian
conversion cannot yield a datum-computable, uniformly bounded-below causal
storage.  Giving its initial metric the missing size
\(\mathsf O_n^{(\ell)}(a_n)\) merely stores the future action at the earlier
endpoint.

A genuinely causal replacement \(\widetilde{\mathsf R}_n(r)\), computed from
the history only through time \(r\), would need a new comparison on the actual
parent strong enough to recover `(IFG.13)`.  Any comparison that dominates
the square action and has a datum-finite integral is impossible by `(IFG.28)`.
This does not rule out a new nonlinear signed cancellation outside the
positive-Gramian family; it proves the boundary for this conversion.

## 7. The exact survivor is the signed two-edge binomial telescope

Squaring creates positivity but loses the sign and imports the future
receiver.  Keeping the signed cross-pairing and iterating the primitive rows

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}

 ={}&\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\tau_n^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}

 \operatorname{Re}\left\langle
 e^{-\nu\tau_nA}P_{\ge r_n}Q^{[j]}(t-\tau_n),

 &-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\delta_n^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}

 e^{-\nu\delta_nA}P_{\ge r_n}Q^{[j]}(t-\delta_n),

\]

The invariant-tangent contribution is the exact zero binomial telescope

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj

Therefore the projection creates no restoring charge at any finite depth.
The oldest \(\tau_n\)-edge, youngest \(\delta_n\)-edge, and same-window

least \(A_n/[64(2m+1)]\), but no one of those terms is yet datum-paid.
