## 1. The exact finite-chord VPI law

Let \(X(a,t)\) be the volume-preserving material flow of a smooth decaying
solution on \(\mathbb R^3\).  For two labels \(a,b\), put

\[
R_{ab}:=X(a,t)-X(b,t),
\qquad
V_{ab}:=u(X(a,t),t)-u(X(b,t),t),
\tag{JEM.1}
\]

and

\[
A_{ab}:=D_tu(X(a,t),t)-D_tu(X(b,t),t).
\tag{JEM.2}
\]

Write

\[
r:=|R|,
\qquad K:=r^{-1},
\qquad n:=R/r,
\qquad
\alpha:={V\cdot R\over r^2},
\qquad
\beta:={V\over r}-\alpha n.
\tag{JEM.3}
\]

The same labels obey

\[
\dot R=V,
\qquad
\dot V=A,
\qquad
\dot K=-\alpha K,
\qquad
\dot n=\beta.
\tag{JEM.4}
\]

With

\[
\mathsf C:=\nabla D_tu=-\nabla^2p+\nu\Delta\nabla u,
\tag{JEM.5}
\]

the fundamental theorem of calculus on the actual chord gives

\[
\boxed{
A_{ab}
=\int_0^1
\mathsf C\bigl(X(b,t)+sR_{ab},t\bigr)R_{ab}\,ds.}
\tag{JEM.6}
\]

Thus the pair response is not an independently prescribed acceleration.  It
is the joined pressure--viscous VPI curvature of the same whole field sampled
along the chord.  If

\[
\mathfrak c_{ab}:={n\cdot A_{ab}\over r},
\tag{JEM.7}
\]

then direct differentiation gives the finite-chord version of `(MSF.5)`:

\[
\boxed{
\dot\alpha=-\alpha^2+|\beta|^2+\mathfrak c_{ab}.}
\tag{JEM.8}
\]

Variable orientation and wobble are already present through \(\beta\).  The
exterior is already present through the pressure term in \(\mathsf C\).
