# Thomas's RHL binomial generating radius and resummed-characteristic no-gain boundary

Status: proved noncanonical exact conditional resummation and checked
identity-level insufficiency boundary inside Thomas Birnie's one-fluid,
pressure-explicit binomial/VPI reversible-intersection method.

The physical object remains the one smooth fixed-viscosity Navier--Stokes
history generated by \(u_0\).  On a failed RHL record, the recent high parent
\(V_n\) is made by the complete nonlinear-pressure source of that same field
during one shrinking backward heat window.  The rows \(Q^{[j]}\) are
simultaneous differentiated readings of that source history.  They are not
new fluids, successive configurations, or independently prescribed sources.

This note tests the one unpaid passage left by `(FBC.18)`: taking its finite
depth to infinity on the shrinking RHL windows.  The original binomial
recurrence gives the exact exponential generating field.  If an explicit
common mixed-row radius holds, then the remainder vanishes and the endpoint
series sums exactly to \(V_n\).  The sum does not turn the pulse down: it
reconstructs the same recent Duhamel parent.  Moreover the critical row
normalization shows that shrinking the heat lag supplies no all-depth radius;
the two extra spatial derivatives at each row exactly consume the parabolic
lag gain.  A common radius and a datum-paid receiver-weighted majorant are two
different inequalities, and neither follows from coordinatewise existence of
all rows.

The generating series below is used only to test this infinite-depth
representation.  It does not replace Thomas's coordinatewise intersection of
Sobolev spaces by a sum or a weighted tower norm, and temporal/insertion depth
remains distinct from simultaneous spatial depth.

## 1. The generating field comes from the primitive pressure-explicit tower

Write

\[
 J_{N,\alpha}=\partial_t^N\partial_x^\alpha u,
 \qquad
 \Pi_{N,\alpha}=\partial_t^N\partial_x^\alpha p,
 \tag{RBG.1}
\]

so the original recurrence is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 J_{N+1,\alpha}
 &+\sum_{a=0}^{N}\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}
 \binom Na\binom\alpha\beta
 (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)J_{N-a,\alpha-\beta}
 +\nabla\Pi_{N,\alpha}
 =-\nu A J_{N,\alpha},\\
 &\hspace{44mm}\nabla\cdot J_{N,\alpha}=0,
 \qquad A=\Lambda^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{RBG.2}
\]

with the simultaneous pressure row

\[
 -\Delta\Pi_{N,\alpha}
 =\partial_i\partial_j\partial_t^N\partial_x^\alpha(u_i u_j).
 \tag{RBG.3}
\]

At a preterminal time \(a\), let \(V_N=\partial_t^Nu\) and form, first
coefficientwise,

\[
 \mathcal U_a(z)=\sum_{N\ge0}{z^N\over N!}V_N(a),
 \qquad
 \mathcal Q_a(z)=Q(\mathcal U_a(z)),
 \qquad
 Q(v)=-\mathbb P((v\cdot\nabla)v).
 \tag{RBG.4}
\]

The binomial coefficients in `(RBG.2)` give

\[
 \partial_z\mathcal U_a+\nu A\mathcal U_a=\mathcal Q_a,
 \qquad
 \mathcal Q_a(z)
 =\sum_{N\ge0}{z^N\over N!}\partial_t^NQ(u)(a),
 \tag{RBG.5}
\]

as formal identities, and as analytic identities in any product-stable
spatial class in which the series converge.  No pressure contribution has
been removed: the Leray expression in `(RBG.4)` is exactly equivalent to the
joined convection and pressure rows `(RBG.2)--(RBG.3)`.

Put \(D=\partial_t+\nu A\) and retain the FBC rows

\[
 Q^{[j]}=D^jQ.
 \tag{RBG.6}
\]

Since \(A\) commutes with \(\partial_t\), the heat-characteristic generating
field is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr G_a(z)
 :=\sum_{j\ge0}{z^j\over j!}Q^{[j]}(a)
 =e^{\nu zA}\mathcal Q_a(z).}
 \tag{RBG.7}
\]

Equation `(RBG.7)` is coefficientwise before convergence is established.  Its
right side contains backward heat, so its evaluation at nonzero \(z\) is a
mixed temporal-spatial radius statement, not a consequence of the existence
of each coefficient.

## 2. When the radius exists, the infinite FBC series is exactly \(V_n\)

Fix one RHL output time \(t\), and abbreviate

\[
 a=a_n(t),\qquad \ell=t-a=\ell_n(t),\qquad R=R_n.
 \tag{RBG.8}
\]

The recent high parent is

\[
 V_n(t)=\int_a^t e^{-\nu(t-r)A}P_{\ge R}Q(r)\,dr.
 \tag{RBG.9}
\]

If `(RBG.7)` may be integrated on \(0\le z\le\ell\) after multiplication by
\(e^{-\nu\ell A}P_{\ge R}\), then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{j\ge0}{\ell^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}
 e^{-\nu\ell A}P_{\ge R}Q^{[j]}(a)\\
 &\quad=e^{-\nu\ell A}P_{\ge R}
 \int_0^\ell\mathscr G_a(z)\,dz\\
 &\quad=\int_0^\ell
 e^{-\nu(\ell-z)A}P_{\ge R}Q(a+z)\,dz
 =V_n(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{RBG.10}
\]

Thus a legitimate all-depth passage in `(FBC.18)` does not produce a new
quantity.  It is the exact resummed recent parent already present in
`(FBC.2)` and `(RHL.22)`.

Here is a quantitative sufficient radius with all constants exposed.  Suppose
there are \(M=M_{n,t}<\infty\) and \(\rho=\rho_{n,t}>\ell\) such that, for
every \(m\ge0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{a\le\sigma\le t}
 \|P_{\ge R}Q^{[m]}(\sigma)\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}
 \le M\,m!\rho^{-m}.}
 \tag{RBG.11}
\]

Let

\[
 \kappa_R(s)=\sup_{\xi\ge R}\xi^{3/2}e^{-\nu s\xi^2}
 \le C_\kappa\nu^{-3/4}s^{-3/4}
 e^{-\nu R^2s/2}.
 \tag{RBG.12}
\]

The order-\(m\) remainder in `(FBC.18)` then satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\left\|\Lambda\int_a^t{(t-\sigma)^m\over m!}
 e^{-\nu(t-\sigma)A}P_{\ge R}Q^{[m]}(\sigma)\,d\sigma\right\|_2\\
 &\qquad\le {C_\kappa M\over\nu^{3/4}}
 {\ell^{m+1/4}\over(m+1/4)\rho^m}\\
 &\qquad={C_\kappa M\ell^{1/4}\over\nu^{3/4}(m+1/4)}
 \left({\ell\over\rho}\right)^m
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{RBG.13}
\]

For the endpoint terms, `(RBG.11)` gives

\[
 \left\|\Lambda{\ell^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}e^{-\nu\ell A}
 P_{\ge R}Q^{[j]}(a)\right\|_2
 \le\kappa_R(\ell)M\ell{q^j\over j+1},
 \qquad q={\ell\over\rho}<1.
 \tag{RBG.14}
\]

Consequently the series converges absolutely in \(\dot H^1\), with

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Lambda V_n(t)\|_2
 \le\kappa_R(\ell)M\rho
 \log{1\over1-q}.}
 \tag{RBG.15}
\]

Equations `(RBG.11)--(RBG.15)` give a fully sufficient common radius on one
RHL window.  A simple sufficient condition for uniform pointwise summation
over a pulse set is

\[
 \mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{t\in\mathcal F_n}
 {\ell_n(t)\over\rho_{n,t}}\le q_*<1
 \tag{RBG.16}
\]

together with a uniform majorant for the full right side of `(RBG.15)`.
Integrating the summed field against the RHL receiver requires that full
majorant squared to be integrable with weight \(Z_w(t)\).  A bound on
\(M_{n,t}\) alone is not enough because \(\kappa_{R_n}\), \(\rho_{n,t}\),
and the logarithmic boundary factor also move.  None of these estimates is
contained in coordinatewise all-row membership.

## 3. Critical normalization cancels the shrinking-lag gain at all depth

The exact FBC kernel is

\[
 \gamma_{j,R}(s)
 ={s^j\over j!}\sup_{\xi\ge R}
 \xi^{2j+3/2}e^{-\nu s\xi^2}.
 \tag{RBG.17}
\]

With

\[
 y=\nu s\xi^2,\qquad y_0=\nu sR^2,
 \qquad \alpha_j=j+{3\over4},
 \tag{RBG.18}
\]

this becomes the exact formula

\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_{j,R}(s)
 ={\nu^{-j-3/4}s^{-3/4}\over j!}
 \sup_{y\ge y_0}y^{\alpha_j}e^{-y}.}
 \tag{RBG.19}
\]

If \(y_0\le\alpha_j\), the supremum in `(RBG.19)` is attained at
\(y=\alpha_j\), so the high-pass cutoff supplies no exponential gain.  Put

\[
 c_j={\alpha_j^{\alpha_j}e^{-\alpha_j}\over j!}.
 \tag{RBG.20}
\]

The Stirling lower bound gives, for every \(j\ge0\),

\[
 c_j\le(j+1)^{1/4}.
 \tag{RBG.21}
\]

Therefore, with the critical FBC row

\[
 b_{j,R}(a)=
 \|\Lambda^{-(2j+1/2)}P_{\ge R}Q^{[j]}(a)\|_2,
 \tag{RBG.22}
\]

the \(j\)-th endpoint obeys the explicit all-depth bound

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|\Lambda{\ell^{j+1}\over(j+1)!}e^{-\nu\ell A}
 P_{\ge R}Q^{[j]}(a)\right\|_2
 \le {\ell^{1/4}\over\nu^{3/4}}
 {\nu^{-j}b_{j,R}(a)\over(j+1)^{3/4}}.}
 \tag{RBG.23}
\]

The factor \(\ell^j\) from the characteristic Taylor coefficient has been
used up by the \(2j\) spatial derivatives exposed at row \(j\).  Only the
common prefactor \(\ell^{1/4}\) remains.  Hence shrinking
\(\ell_n\to0\) does not by itself produce geometric decay in the row depth.

The RHL choice makes this sharper.  When \(\ell_n=\tau_n\),

\[
 y_n:=\nu\tau_nR_n^2
 ={1+\log(1+X_n)\over2}.
 \tag{RBG.24}
\]

For \(\ell_n(t)\le\tau_n\), one has
\(\nu\ell_n(t)R_n^2\le y_n\).  Thus every row

\[
 j\ge\left\lceil y_n-{3\over4}\right\rceil
 \tag{RBG.25}
\]

lies beyond the high-pass heat-suppressed prefix.  At every fixed record,
the infinite tower therefore has an unsuppressed all-row tail.

A sufficient scalar majorant for that tail is

\[
 \mathscr C_{n,t}
 :=\sum_{j\ge0}{\nu^{-j}b_{j,R_n}(a_n(t))\over(j+1)^{3/4}}.
 \tag{RBG.26}
\]

Its root-test radius is

\[
 \varrho_{n,t}^{\rm crit}
 :=\left(\limsup_{j\to\infty}
 [\nu^{-j}b_{j,R_n}(a_n(t))]^{1/j}\right)^{-1}.
 \tag{RBG.27}
\]

The condition \(\varrho_{n,t}^{\rm crit}>1\) is sufficient for
`(RBG.26)` to converge.  Every individual \(b_{j,R_n}(a_n(t))<\infty\)
places no restriction on `(RBG.27)`.  This is a majorant for a series
representation, not a new Sobolev space and not a replacement for the
intersection theorem.

## 4. A radius sums the identity; it does not pay the pulse

Let

\[
 \mathfrak M_n(t)
 :=\kappa_{R_n}(\ell_n(t))M_{n,t}\rho_{n,t}
 \log{1\over1-\ell_n(t)/\rho_{n,t}}.
 \tag{RBG.28}
\]

Under `(RBG.11)`, equation `(RBG.15)` says
\(\|\Lambda V_n(t)\|_2\le\mathfrak M_n(t)\).  The exact RHL/FBC output lower
bound and sector estimate are

\[
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathscr T_n[V_n](t)\|_2^2dt
 \ge c_0\nu A_n,
 \tag{RBG.29}
\]

and

\[
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathscr T_n[v](t)\|_2^2
 \le4C_{\rm sec}^2Z_w(t)\|\Lambda v(t)\|_2^2.
 \tag{RBG.30}
\]

Therefore every common-radius majorant compatible with a failed record must
itself satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}Z_w(t)\mathfrak M_n(t)^2dt
 \ge {c_0\over4C_{\rm sec}^2}\nu A_n.}
 \tag{RBG.31}
\]

So even a proved radius merely transfers the same critical synchronization
from \(V_n\) to its all-row majorant.  To obtain a datum-paid contradiction one
would additionally have to prove

\[
 \boxed{
 {1\over\nu A_n}
 \int_{\mathcal F_n}Z_w(t)\mathfrak M_n(t)^2dt
 \longrightarrow0,}
 \tag{RBG.32}
\]

or a summable bound over the disjoint records.  Equation `(RBG.31)` shows
that `(RBG.32)` is precisely a turn-down theorem, not a consequence of series
convergence.  The datum-paid row
\(\int_0^{T_*}\|\Lambda^{-1/2}Q\|_2dt<\infty\) controls neither the
all-row amplitudes \(M_{n,t}\) nor their square synchronization with the later
receiver \(Z_w(t)\).

## 5. Two exact characteristic countertests

The following are identity-level insufficiency tests.  They are not claimed
as Navier--Stokes histories and do not refute any theorem using additional
one-fluid structure.  They identify exactly what the binomial characteristic
algebra does not supply by itself.

### 5.1 Every row can exist while the anchored radius misses the window

On one scalar heat channel let \(D=\partial_s+d\), \(d>0\), and choose
\(0<\rho<\ell\).  Put

\[
 q_\rho(s)={e^{-ds}\over1+(s/\rho)^2}.
 \tag{RBG.33}
\]

Then every \(D^jq_\rho(0)\) is finite, while

\[
 D^jq_\rho(0)
 =\left.{d^j\over ds^j}{1\over1+(s/\rho)^2}\right|_{s=0}.
 \tag{RBG.34}
\]

The actual recent-parent integral is finite and equals

\[
 \int_0^\ell e^{-d(\ell-s)}q_\rho(s)\,ds
 =e^{-d\ell}\rho\arctan(\ell/\rho).
 \tag{RBG.35}
\]

But the even endpoint terms in the infinite characteristic series are

\[
 e^{-d\ell}(-1)^k
 {\ell\over2k+1}\left({\ell\over\rho}\right)^{2k},
 \qquad j=2k,
 \tag{RBG.36}
\]

which do not tend to zero because \(\ell>\rho\).  Every finite-depth
characteristic identity remains exact, but its anchored infinite series does
not converge.  Thus a shrinking positive window is not itself a common
radius.

### 5.2 Infinite radius does not bound the amplitude

For any \(M>0\), put

\[
 q_M(s)=Me^{-ds}.
 \tag{RBG.37}
\]

Then \(Dq_M=0\), so the characteristic series terminates after its first
term and has infinite depth radius, yet

\[
 \int_0^\ell e^{-d(\ell-s)}q_M(s)\,ds
 =M\ell e^{-d\ell}.
 \tag{RBG.38}
\]

The amplitude is arbitrary.  Radius information alone therefore cannot be a
datum payment.

## 6. Exact verdict and missing inequality

The pressure-explicit binomial recurrence proves the coefficient identities
`(RBG.5)--(RBG.7)`.  On an RHL window, an actual common mixed-row estimate
such as `(RBG.11)` with `(RBG.16)` proves that the finite FBC remainder tends
to zero.  The resulting infinite series is exactly `(RBG.10)`, the same recent
high Duhamel parent.

The recurrence does not, from coordinatewise row existence, provide either

\[
 \rho_{n,t}>\ell_n(t)
 \quad\hbox{uniformly on the pulse},
 \tag{RBG.39}
\]

or the datum-paid receiver-majorant turn-down `(RBG.32)`.  The sharp kernel
calculation `(RBG.19)--(RBG.25)` explains why the shrinking lag cannot supply
the first inequality for free, and `(RBG.31)` proves why the first inequality
alone cannot supply the second.

Accordingly, this result is a proved exact conditional resummation and a
checked identity-level insufficiency result for deriving closure from radius
alone.  It
is not a strict reduction, `(TIR.37a)`, or MPP closure.  Any continuation
through this infinite-depth resummation must derive a pressure-complete
same-history mixed-jet majorant that includes the receiver-weighted datum
payment; merely summing `(FBC.18)` reconstructs the open critical event.
