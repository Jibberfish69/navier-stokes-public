## 1. The theorem and the one-history ontology

Let

\[
 u_0\in H^\infty_\sigma(\mathbb R^3),\qquad \nu>0,\qquad
 f\in C^\infty([0,\infty);\mathcal S(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)).
\]

The target is the unique pressure-normalized solution of

\[
 \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u+f,
 \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,
 \qquad u(0)=u_0,
 \tag{N1}
\]

with, for every finite \(T\),

\[
 u\in X_T:=\bigcap_{r,m\ge0}H^r(0,T;H^m_\sigma),
 \qquad p\in\bigcap_{r,m\ge0}H^r(0,T;H^m).
 \tag{N2}
\]

Participation means that every local chart, spatial derivative, temporal
row, pressure row, force row and finite rectangle is a coordinate of this
one field.  None may be evolved or assembled as an independent fluid.

## 2. Pressure, projection and incompressibility

With the fixed \(L^2\) normalization,

\[
 p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j)-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot f.
 \tag{N3}
\]

Writing \(\mathbb P\) for the Leray projector gives

\[
 u_t=\nu\Delta u-\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u)+\mathbb Pf.
 \tag{N4}
\]

The pressure is thus slaved to that velocity and force; it is not
an independent amplifier.  Applying divergence to every differentiated row
gives \(\nabla\cdot\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u=0\).  Thus
incompressibility is a law of the complete mixed jet, not only of its
zeroth coordinate.

## 3. The complete forced mixed jet

Set

\[
 V_n=\partial_t^n u,\qquad Q_n=\partial_t^n p,
 \qquad F_n=\partial_t^n f.
\]

Every temporal row satisfies

\[
 V_{n+1}+B_n+\nabla Q_n-\nu\Delta V_n=F_n,
 \qquad \nabla\cdot V_n=0,
 \tag{N5}
\]

where the complete nonlinear row is

\[
 B_n=\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom na(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}.
 \tag{N6}
\]

The corresponding pressure row is

\[
 Q_n=R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom na(V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j
      -(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot F_n.
 \tag{N7}
\]

Spatial differentiation of (N5)--(N7) retains every commutator.  These
identities define the pressure-complete velocity--pressure--force jet
\(\mathcal J_f\).  In particular, the nonlinear term always remains inside
that equation and that mixed-jet estimates.

## 4. Step 23a: the datum generates the compatible initial jet

Put \(A_0=u_0\).  Evaluating (N5) at time zero gives the triangular
recurrence

\[
 A_{n+1}=\nu\Delta A_n
 -\mathbb P\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom na(A_a\cdot\nabla)A_{n-a}
 +\mathbb PF_n(0).
 \tag{N8}
\]

Smooth datum and force jets give every \(A_n\in H^\infty_\sigma\); (N7)
gives every compatible pressure row.  Because (N8) is triangular, all
finite restrictions agree and define one initial mixed jet.

## 5. Step 23b: one local history realizes every finite coordinate

The mild equation

\[
 u(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0
 -\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(s)\,ds
 +\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb Pf(s)\,ds
 \tag{N9}
\]

has one local fixed point in the common \(H^3\)-based class.  Higher-order
energy estimates on that same interval yield, for each \(m\ge3\),

\[
 \frac d{dt}\|u\|_{H^m}
 \le C_m\|\nabla u\|_\infty\|u\|_{H^m}+\|f\|_{H^m}.
 \tag{N10}
\]

Fourier projection and weak compactness identify all higher-order limits
with this fixed point.  The recurrence (N5)--(N7) then realizes every
temporal and pressure row, with the initial values (N8).  Uniqueness joins
overlapping intervals without introducing a second history.

## 7. Step 23b': datum-controlled whole-interval base rows

The kinetic identity gives, with

\[
 K_T=\|u_0\|_2+\int_0^T\|\mathbb Pf(t)\|_2\,dt,
 \qquad D_T=K_T^2/(2\nu),
\]

the whole-interval bounds

\[
 \|u\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}\le K_T,
 \qquad
 \|\nabla u\|_{L^2(0,T;L^2)}^2\le D_T.
 \tag{N13}
\]

The complete nonlinear row satisfies

\[
 \|\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_{L^{4/3}(0,T;H^{-1})}
 \le C K_T^{1/2}D_T^{3/4},
 \tag{N14}
\]

so (N4) supplies \(u_t\in L^{4/3}_tH^{-1}_x\), and (N3) supplies
\(p\in L^{4/3}_tL^2_x\).  These are unconditional datum-controlled base
rows of that mixed jet and agree with the higher intersection
coordinates.

That energy row has a sharper whole-terminal spatial readout.  Let
\(\Delta_j\) be an inhomogeneous Littlewood--Paley decomposition and put
\(g=\mathbb Pf\).  The low--high, high--low and high--high paraproducts give
the endpoint product law

\[
 \|u\otimes u\|_{B^{1/2}_{2,1}}
 \le C\|u\|_{H^1}^2,
 \qquad
 \|u\otimes u\|_{L^1(0,T;B^{1/2}_{2,1})}
 \le C(TK_T^2+D_T).
 \tag{N14a}
\]

Indeed, with \(a_j=2^j\|\Delta_ju\|_2\), the low--high contribution is
bounded by
\(C\sum_ja_j\sum_{k<j}2^{-(j-k)/2}a_k\), and the high--high contribution
has the summable factor \(2^{-2(k-j)}a_k^2\).  Both are bounded by
\(C\sum_ja_j^2=C\|u\|_{H^1}^2\).  Integrating uses only (N13).

Apply the dyadic blocks to the mild equation (N9).  One divergence maps
\(B^{1/2}_{2,1}\) to \(B^{-1/2}_{2,1}\), while the time integral of the heat
kernel on the block \(|\xi|\simeq2^j\) contributes
\(C\nu^{-1}2^{-2j}\).  Summing the high blocks and paying the fixed low block
with \(TK_T\) yields

\[
 \|u\|_{L^1(0,T;B^{3/2}_{2,1})}
 \le CTK_T+C\nu^{-1}
 \left(\|u_0\|_2+\|g\|_{L^1(0,T;L^2)}+TK_T^2+D_T\right)<\infty.
 \tag{N14b}
\]

Since \(B_0=\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)\), the two projections of that
momentum row now give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_0&\in L^1(0,T;B^{-1/2}_{2,1}),\\
 \nabla p&=(I-\mathbb P)(f-B_0)
       \in L^1(0,T;B^{-1/2}_{2,1}),\\
 u_t&=\nu\Delta u-\mathbb PB_0+g
       \in L^1(0,T;B^{-1/2}_{2,1}),\\
 u&\in C([0,T];B^{-1/2}_{2,1}).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N14c}
\]

Thus the datum pays a whole-terminal Besov spatial coordinate, its complete
nonlinear and pressure allocations, and a compatible strong endpoint trace
before the higher spatial column is used in Step 23c.

The two faces of this same base rectangle also interpolate without introducing
another history.  For every (0<\theta\le1), pointwise real interpolation and
(N14b)--(N14c) give

\[
 \|u\|_{L^{1/\theta}(0,T;B^{-1/2+2\theta}_{2,1})}
 \le C
 \left(\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|u(t)\|_{B^{-1/2}_{2,1}}\right)^{1-\theta}
 \left(\int_0^T\|u(t)\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,1}}\,dt\right)^\theta .
 \tag{N14d}
\]

In particular, the datum-controlled rectangle supplies

\[
 u\in L^2(0,T;B^{1/2}_{2,1}).
 \tag{N14e}
\]

This is a spacetime coordinate of the complete base rectangle; its endpoint
representative remains the (B^{-1/2}_{2,1}) trace in (N14c).

That strong trace and the kinetic bound identify a genuine terminal
finite-energy member.  If \(t_j\uparrow T\), weak compactness in \(L^2\)
gives a weakly convergent subsequence, while strong convergence in
\(B^{-1/2}_{2,1}\) identifies its distributional limit with the unique trace
from (N14c).  Hence

\[
 u(T)\in L^2_\sigma,
 \qquad \|u(T)\|_2\le K_T,
 \qquad u(t)\rightharpoonup u(T)\quad\hbox{in }L^2
 \quad(t\uparrow T).
 \tag{N14f}
\]

There is a complete family of stronger negative endpoint traces.  For any
positive sequence \(\omega=(\omega_j)\) satisfying

\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}\omega_j^2,2^{-j}<\infty,

define

\[
 \|v\|_{\mathcal X_\omega}
 :=\sum_{j\ge0}\omega_j2^{-j/2}\|\Delta_jv\|_2.

\[
 u\in C([0,T];\mathcal X_\omega).
 \tag{N14g}
\]

Indeed, finitely many low blocks converge by (N14c), and Cauchy--Schwarz

\[

This includes \(B^{-\varepsilon}_{2,1}\) for every

## 9. Independent critical-height relationship rectangles

The direct intersection and the critical-height construction are independently
derived from that mixed jet.  Independence concerns their construction and
proof order, not logical non-composability.  Differentiating the spatial energy
heights and substituting (N5) produces exact relationships among adjacent
temporal rows and spatial diagonals.  A finite relationship rectangle is a
finite selection of those coupled identities, including every nonlinear,
pressure, and force allocation.  A norm rectangle records the quantitative
readout obtained by pairing, squaring, and integrating those identities on the
common interval.  Once all completed height readouts are controlled, they
recover that spatial column, and the forced recurrence reconstructs the
same mixed intersection.  In the other direction, projecting the independently
constructed intersection supplies the compatible coordinates on which every
finite rectangle relation acts.

The viscous term \(\nu\Delta V_n\) is the geometric tether: two spatial
derivatives on row \(n\) connect to temporal row \(n+1\).  Hence spatial
concentration and temporal acceleration cannot be assigned independently of
the neighboring diagonal.  Every rectangle retains the full force,
pressure and nonlinear allocations.

The exact elementary cell follows directly from (N5).  With
\(\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\), projection and application of \(\Lambda^s\)
give

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda^sV_{n+1}+\nu\Lambda^{s+2}V_n
 =\Lambda^s\mathbb P(F_n-B_n).}
 \tag{N16a}
\]

The viscous sign is positive because \(-\Delta=\Lambda^2\).  If
\(E_{n,s}=\frac12\|\Lambda^sV_n\|_2^2\), pairing (N16a) with
\(\Lambda^sV_n\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 E_{n,s}'+2\nu E_{n,s+1}
 =\langle\Lambda^s\mathbb P(F_n-B_n),\Lambda^sV_n\rangle.}
 \tag{N16b}
\]

Squaring that cell, rather than replacing any coordinate, gives the
viscous diagonal identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Lambda^sV_{n+1}\|_2^2
 +\nu^2\|\Lambda^{s+2}V_n\|_2^2
 +\nu\frac d{dt}\|\Lambda^{s+1}V_n\|_2^2
 =\|\Lambda^s\mathbb P(F_n-B_n)\|_2^2.}
 \tag{N16c}
\]

Consequently, on every interval \([0,S]\) where the selected cell is
classical,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\int_0^S\!\left(
   \|\Lambda^sV_{n+1}\|_2^2
   +\nu^2\|\Lambda^{s+2}V_n\|_2^2\right)dt
   +\nu\|\Lambda^{s+1}V_n(S)\|_2^2\\
 &\qquad=\nu\|\Lambda^{s+1}A_n\|_2^2
   +\int_0^S\|\Lambda^s\mathbb P(F_n-B_n)\|_2^2dt .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N16d}
\]

Before projection, orthogonality of the divergence-free temporal and
viscous faces to the pressure gradient supplies the pressure-complete square

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\|V_{n+1}\|_{\dot H^s}^2
 +\nu^2\|\Delta V_n\|_{\dot H^s}^2
 +\|\nabla Q_n\|_{\dot H^s}^2
 +\nu\frac d{dt}\|\nabla V_n\|_{\dot H^s}^2\\
 &\qquad=\|F_n-B_n\|_{\dot H^s}^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N16e}
\]

Equations (N16b)--(N16e), coupled over a finite set of \((n,s)\) and with
every factor in (N6) retained, are the relationship rectangle.  Their exact
index tether is \((n,s+2)\leftrightarrow(n+1,s)\).  The coordinate
intersection says that all of these entries belong to one history; the
pairing, squaring and integration above are what estimate them.

## 13. The normalized base rectangle forces a critical high-frequency tail

The weighted complete momentum rectangle also gives a quantitative
same-history consequence at the terminal end.  Put

\[
 A=-\Delta,\qquad g=\mathbb P f,\qquad
 y=\|A^{1/2}u\|_2^2,\qquad d=\|Au\|_2^2,

and retain the complete signed enstrophy production

\[
 \mathcal P_1(v):=-\langle\mathbb P((v\cdot\nabla)v),Av\rangle .

The pressure-complete forced row gives

\[
 \frac12y'+\nu d=\mathcal P_1(u)+\langle g,Au\rangle .
 \tag{N21}
\]

The pressure pairing vanishes in this solenoidal contraction, while pressure
itself remains fixed by (N6) and is retained in the weighted complete square.
Suppose the terminal reciprocal from that square is zero.  Then
\(y(t)\to\infty\) as \(t\uparrow T_*\).  On a terminal interval where
\(y\ge a\), the weighted bounds imply

\[
 \int\frac{d}{y^2}<\infty,
 \qquad
 \int\frac{|\mathcal P_1(u)|}{y^2}<\infty .
\]

For the second assertion, use
\(|\mathcal P_1(u)|\le\|\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_2\|Au\|_2\)
and Cauchy--Schwarz with the two corresponding terms of the weighted complete
square.  Dividing (N21) by \(y^2\) and passing to the terminal endpoint gives

\[
 \frac1{2y(t)}+\nu\int_t^{T_*}\frac{d}{y^2}\,ds
 =\int_t^{T_*}\frac{\mathcal P_1(u)+\langle g,Au\rangle}{y^2}\,ds .
 \tag{N22}
\]

Fix a frequency threshold \(\kappa>0\), let
\(\ell=\mathbf 1_{\{|\xi|\le\kappa\}}(D)u\), and let
\(h=u-\ell\).  The gradient form of the incompressible cubic current shows

\[
 |\mathcal P_1(u)-\mathcal P_1(h)|
 \le C\|\nabla\ell\|_\infty\|\nabla u\|_2^2
 \le C\kappa^{5/2}K_{T_*}\,y .
 \tag{N23}
\]

Moreover,
\(d=\|A\ell\|_2^2+\|Ah\|_2^2\),
\(\|A\ell\|_2^2\le\kappa^4K_{T_*}^2\), and

\[
 |\langle g,Au\rangle|
 =|\langle\nabla g,\nabla u\rangle|
 \le\|\nabla g\|_2\sqrt y .
 \tag{N24}
\]

Choose \(t_j\uparrow T_*\) so that, with \(y_j=y(t_j)\),
\(y(s)\ge y_j/2\) for \(s\in[t_j,T_*)\).  Such a tail-minimum sequence
exists because \(y(t)\to\infty\).  Equations (N22)--(N24) then give

\[
 \frac1{2y_j}
 +\nu\int_{t_j}^{T_*}\frac{\|Ah\|_2^2}{y^2}\,ds
 =\int_{t_j}^{T_*}\frac{\mathcal P_1(h)}{y^2}\,ds
 +o(y_j^{-1}).
 \tag{N25}
\]

All discarded terms in this passage are explicit: the fixed low-frequency
current is at most
\(C\kappa^{5/2}K_{T_*}(T_*-t_j)y_j^{-1}\), the low viscous term is
at most \(4\kappa^4K_{T_*}^2(T_*-t_j)y_j^{-2}\), and the force term is
at most a prescribed constant times
\((T_*-t_j)y_j^{-3/2}\).

Finally, Sobolev embedding gives the critical high-frequency estimate

\[
 |\mathcal P_1(h)|
 \le \|h\|_3\|\nabla h\|_6\|Ah\|_2
 \le C_*\|h\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}\|Ah\|_2^2 .
 \tag{N26}
\]

If \(\|h(t)\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}<\nu/(2C_*)\) eventually, substitution in
(N25) would give

\[
 \frac1{2y_j}

 \le o(y_j^{-1}),
\]

which is impossible after multiplication by \(y_j\).  Thus terminal

\[
 \boxed{

 \|\mathbf 1_{\{|\xi|>\kappa\}}(D)u(t)\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}

 \quad\text{for every fixed }\kappa>0 .}
 \tag{N27}
\]

the normalized whole-equation estimate, the viscous diagonal, the complete

Under the favorable scale-matched roof

 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N29}
\]

The minimizing lifetime is proportional to
\((\nu\lambda^2)^{-1}\).  This order is sharp under the exact forced
scaling chart

\[
 u_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda U(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),\quad
 p_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda^2P(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),\quad
 f_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda^3G(\lambda^2t,\lambda x).
\]

Indeed (y_\lambda=\lambda y_U\), while
\(q_\lambda,d_\lambda,\pi_\lambda,b_\lambda\) are each
\(\lambda^3\) times their chart value, and hence

\[
 \int\frac{q_\lambda+\nu^2d_\lambda+\pi_\lambda+b_\lambda}
 {(a+y_\lambda)^2}\,dt
 =\lambda^{-1}
 \int\frac{q_U+\nu^2d_U+\pi_U+b_U}{(a/\lambda+y_U)^2}\,ds.
 \tag{N30}
\]

Every mixed row remains covariant in this same chart:
\(V_n\) scales as \(\lambda^{1+2n}\), while
\(V_{n+1}\), \(\nu AV_n\), the complete (B_n\),
\(\nabla Q_n\), and (F_n\) all scale as
\(\lambda^{3+2n}\).  Thus the mixed rectangles retain every term and every
row, while the normalized base-rectangle cost of a parabolic critical-tail
event is proportional to \(\lambda^{-1}\).  A dyadic sequence of those costs
is summable, so a further inter-scale relationship must carry an
octave-independent charge before this terminal branch can be excluded.

## 14. The scale-free adjacent rectangle and first-arrival charge

That temporal--viscous tether has a scale-free readout when its two faces
are measured at the critical dual orders.  Let \(P_\lambda\) be an annular
Fourier projector, put

\[
 w_\lambda=P_\lambda u,
 \qquad H_\lambda=\|\Lambda^{1/2}w_\lambda\|_2^2 .
\]

Because \(P_\lambda\) and \(\Lambda\) commute with the mixed-jet rows,

\[
 H_\lambda'
 =2\langle\Lambda^{-1/2}P_\lambda u_t,
              \Lambda^{3/2}w_\lambda\rangle .
\]

Young's inequality thus gives, on every interval \([s,t]\) contained
in the original classical history,

\[
 \boxed{
 |H_\lambda(t)-H_\lambda(s)|
 \le\int_s^t\left(
   \nu^{-1}\|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_\lambda u_t\|_2^2
   +\nu\|\Lambda^{3/2}P_\lambda u\|_2^2
 \right)\,dr .}
 \tag{N31}
\]

Thus either direction of a passage between \(\eta^2/4\) and \(\eta^2\)
costs at least \(3\eta^2/4\) in this adjacent critical rectangle,
independently of \(\lambda\).  This is the unweighted scale-free form of the
two estimates in (N28); it uses that temporal and viscous faces rather
than a separate regularity criterion.

There is also an exact source form.  Write

\[
 R=\mathbb P(f-(u\cdot\nabla)u).
\]

The annular projection of the complete forced equation is

\[
 (w_\lambda)_t+\nu\Lambda^2w_\lambda=P_\lambda R.
\]

Duhamel's formula followed by pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz in time gives

\[
 \|\Lambda^{1/2}w_\lambda(t)\|_2
 \le e^{-c\nu\lambda^2(t-s)}
       \|\Lambda^{1/2}w_\lambda(s)\|_2
 +(2\nu)^{-1/2}
 \left(\int_s^t
   \|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_\lambda R(r)\|_2^2\,dr
 \right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{N32}
\]

Consequently, if a first upward passage satisfies
\(H_\lambda(s)\le\eta^2/4\) and
\(H_\lambda(t)\ge\eta^2\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_s^t
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_\lambda
       \mathbb P(f-(u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_2^2\,dr
 \ge\frac{\nu\eta^2}{2}.}
 \tag{N33}
\]

Pressure is retained in this statement.  Since

\[
 \nabla p=(I-\mathbb P)(f-(u\cdot\nabla)u),
\]

the two orthogonal allocations satisfy

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_\lambda(f-(u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_2^2
 ={}&\|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_\lambda R\|_2^2\\
 &+\|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_\lambda\nabla p\|_2^2 .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N34}
\]

For pairwise disjoint annuli, the charges in (N33) add by Fourier
orthogonality even when their first-arrival intervals overlap.  Smooth initial
data has vanishing critical annular tail, so every fixed-size event at a new
high annulus has such a first passage.  Hence infinitely many fixed-size new
events force divergence of the complete critical source square in (N34).
This identifies the exact rectangle coordinate that must be paid to exclude
the terminal critical cascade.

## 15. What positive inter-scale telescoping can pay

The high-frequency kinetic rows determine the full class of positive linear
frequency telescopes.  Let \(Q_\kappa\) project to
\(\{|\xi|>\kappa\}\), and write

\[
 E_\kappa=\frac12\|Q_\kappa u\|_2^2,
 \quad D_\kappa=\|\Lambda Q_\kappa u\|_2^2,
 \quad \Pi_\kappa=-\langle Q_\kappa u,Q_\kappa (u\cdot\nabla u)\rangle,
 \quad W_\kappa=\langle Q_\kappa u,Q_\kappa g\rangle .
\]

The pressure-complete row gives

\[
 E_\kappa'+\nu D_\kappa=\Pi_\kappa+W_\kappa .
\]

For any positive measure \(\mu\) on the frequency thresholds, set
\(m(r)=\mu([0,r])\).  Tonelli's theorem yields the exact one-history
identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac d{dt}\frac12\int m(|\xi|)|\widehat u(\xi)|^2\,d\xi
 +\nu\int m(|\xi|)|\xi|^2|\widehat u(\xi)|^2\,d\xi
 ={}&-\langle m(\Lambda)u,(u\cdot\nabla)u\rangle\\
 &+\langle m(\Lambda)u,g\rangle .
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N35}
\]

The pressure pairing vanishes only in this solenoidal contraction; its value
remains fixed by (N3).  A universal comparison of the positive stock and
dissipation in (N35) with the kinetic stock and dissipation is possible
exactly when \(m\) is bounded.  Such a multiplier changes by only \(O(1)\)
across an octave.  A critical packet at frequency \(\lambda\) and critical
height \(\eta^2\) carries kinetic mass \(O(\eta^2/\lambda)\), so every
kinetic-paid octave charge produced by (N35) remains
\(O(\eta^2/\lambda)\).

An octave-independent charge would instead require

\[
 m(2\lambda)-m(\lambda)\gtrsim\lambda .
\]

The minimal positive frequency order with this growth is \(m(r)=r\).  In
that case (N35) is exactly the critical relationship row

\[
 \frac12\frac d{dt}\|\Lambda^{1/2}u\|_2^2
 +\nu\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2
 =-\langle\Lambda u,(u\cdot\nabla)u\rangle
   +\langle\Lambda u,g\rangle .
 \tag{N36}
\]

Thus the relationship rectangles do contain an octave-independent charge:
it is (N31)--(N34), equivalently the critical first-moment row (N36).  The
kinetic row alone pays only its \(\lambda^{-1}\)-weighted version.  The
datum-to-terminal construction must thus control the critical
source/trace square through a further complete-equation relationship rather
than by a bounded positive recombination of kinetic shell rows.

## 16. Full-row scaling and the positive-reservoir boundary

That conclusion holds for every positive fixed-weight combination of
mixed rectangles, not only the kinetic shell telescope.  For a temporal row
\(n\) and spatial index \(s\), set

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_{n,s}&=\|\Lambda^{s+1}V_n\|_2^2,
 &T_{n,s}&=\|\Lambda^sV_{n+1}\|_2^2,\\
 D_{n,s}&=\|\Lambda^{s+2}V_n\|_2^2,
 &\Pi_{n,s}&=\|\Lambda^s\nabla Q_n\|_2^2,\\
 R_{n,s}&=\|\Lambda^s(F_n-B_n)\|_2^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The exact pressure-complete cell gives

\[
 T_{n,s}+\nu^2D_{n,s}+\Pi_{n,s}
 +\nu H_{n,s}'=R_{n,s}.
 \tag{N37}
\]

Under the forced scaling used in (N30), put
\(\gamma_{n,s}=4n+2s+1\).  Then

\[
 H_{n,s}^{\lambda}=\lambda^{\gamma_{n,s}}H_{n,s},
 \qquad
 (T_{n,s}^{\lambda},D_{n,s}^{\lambda},
   \Pi_{n,s}^{\lambda},R_{n,s}^{\lambda})
 =\lambda^{\gamma_{n,s}+2}(T_{n,s},D_{n,s},\Pi_{n,s},R_{n,s}).
 \tag{N38}
\]

After parabolic time integration every face scales as
\(\lambda^{\gamma_{n,s}}\).  The critical cell in (N31) is precisely
\((n,s)=(0,-1/2)\), for which \(\gamma_{0,-1/2}=0\).

Let \(\rho\ge0\) be an own-height normalizer and
\(\Phi(H)=\int_0^H\rho(r)\,dr\).  Multiplication of (N37) gives the exact
normalized rectangle

\[
 \rho(H_{n,s})(T_{n,s}+\nu^2D_{n,s}+\Pi_{n,s})
 +\nu\frac d{dt}\Phi(H_{n,s})
 =\rho(H_{n,s})R_{n,s}.
 \tag{N39}
\]

For \(\rho(H)=(a+H)^{-p}\), a multiplicative passage between \(H\) and
\(\theta H\), \(0<\theta<1\), has boundary charge

\[
 \nu\int_{\theta H}^{H}(a+r)^{-p}\,dr.
\]

If \(p>1\), the total saturated storage \(\Phi(\infty)\) is finite but this
charge tends to zero as \(H\to\infty\).  If \(p=1\), the charge tends to
\(\nu\log(1/\theta)\), while the logarithmic storage is unbounded.  If
\(p<1\), both the charge and the storage grow.

More generally, let \((c_j,\rho_j)\) be a finite or countable family with
\(c_j\ge0\), fixed after arbitrary dependence on the prescribed data, and
suppose its total saturated storage is finite:

\[
 \nu\sum_jc_j\int_0^\infty\rho_j(r)\,dr<\infty.
\]

For scale-copy passages
\(H_j(\lambda)=h_j\lambda^{\gamma_j}\) with \(\gamma_j>0\), the guaranteed
total boundary charge is

\[
 \nu\sum_jc_j
 \int_{\theta_jH_j(\lambda)}^{H_j(\lambda)}\rho_j(r)\,dr
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\lambda\to\infty).
 \tag{N40}
\]

Each term is a tail of an integrable normalizer and is dominated by the
summable saturated reservoir, so (N40) follows by dominated convergence.
The critical cell escapes the vanishing exponent, but its first-frequency
moment and nonlinear recharge are exactly the unsigned payment isolated in
(N31)--(N36).  Thus finite datum storage in positive normalized rectangles
cannot replace the critical signed whole-equation relationship.

## 17. Exact generating-field cancellation and its scope

The binomial temporal jet does possess one all-order nonlinear cancellation.
The coefficient relation required for

\[
 \sum_nc_nB_n
 =\left(\sum_nc_nV_n\cdot\nabla\right)\left(\sum_nc_nV_n\right)
\]

is
\(c_ac_b=\binom{a+b}{a}c_{a+b}\), whose nondegenerate solutions are
\(c_n=z^n/n!\).  Hence the unique multiplicative generating field is

\[
 U(t,z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{n!}V_n(t),
 \quad P(t,z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{n!}Q_n(t),
 \quad \mathcal F(t,z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{n!}F_n(t).
 \tag{N41}
\]

Where these series converge they satisfy the complete forced equation, and
\(U_z=U_t\).  For real \(z\), uniqueness identifies
\(U(t,z)=u(t+z)\), and kinetic pairing cancels the complete binomial
nonlinearity:

\[
 \frac12\partial_t\|U(t,z)\|_2^2
 +\nu\|\nabla U(t,z)\|_2^2
 =\langle\mathcal F(t,z),U(t,z)\rangle .
 \tag{N42}
\]

A positive real measure in \(z\) thus gives an exact positive Hankel
cross-term energy.  It is an average of the already bounded kinetic energies
of real time translates; derivative evaluation at \(z=0\) is not coercive in
that average.  Arbitrary smooth data and force also need not have any nonzero
Taylor radius.

Phase averaging at \(z=re^{i\vartheta}\) would produce the coercive diagonal
quantity

\[
 \frac12\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{r^{2n}}{(n!)^2}\|V_n\|_2^2,
\]

but the generated field is then complex and the transport cancellation is
lost.  Its exact leading nonlinear term is

\[
 r^2\langle V_1,(V_1\cdot\nabla)u\rangle+O(r^4),
 \tag{N43}
\]

which has either sign on exact smooth forced histories.  A finite Taylor
selection retains the corresponding top-degree boundary current; it vanishes
only in the convergent all-order real-shift limit.  Thus the generating-field
identity preserves either complete transport cancellation or coercive jet
measurement, but does not supply both by this downstream readout alone.  No
such supply is required for closure because Section 6 supplies (N11a)
directly.

## 18. Forced critical source-square descent with a paid force insertion

The August 4 first-binomial source row has a direct forced conversion which
separates the prescribed force without changing the velocity history.  Put
\(A=\Lambda^2=-\Delta\) and, on the one forced history,

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 g=\mathbb P f,\qquad
 Q(u)=-\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u),\qquad
 \mathcal R=Q(u)+g,\\
 u_t+\nu Au=\mathcal R,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R
 =\mathbb P(f-(u\cdot\nabla)u)
 =f-(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p,\\
 \mathcal E_T
 :=\|u_0\|_2^2
   +\nu^{-1}\int_0^T\|\Lambda^{-1}g(t)\|_2^2\,dt,\\
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|u(t)\|_2^2\le\mathcal E_T,
 \qquad
 \nu\int_0^T\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2\,dt\le\mathcal E_T.
 \end{gathered}}
 \tag{N44}
\]

Thus \(\mathcal R\) is exactly the complete source in (N32)--(N34).  The
gradient part of the force remains in that normalized pressure \(p\);
it has not been discarded by writing \(g=\mathbb P f\).  The force norms in
(N44) are finite under the source admissibility in Section 2.  In particular,
the prescribed \(L_x^1\) control handles the low frequency in
\(\Lambda^{-1}g\).

Let

\[
 H={1\over2}\|\Lambda^{1/2}u\|_2^2,
 \qquad d=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2,
 \qquad b_X=\|\Lambda^{-1/2}X\|_2,
 \qquad P_X=(X,\Lambda u)_2.
\]

The critical row is
\(H'+\nu d^2=P_{\mathcal R}=P_Q+(g,\Lambda u)_2\), and
\((g,\Lambda u)=(\Lambda^{-1/2}g,\Lambda^{3/2}u)\).  Hence
\(H'+(\nu/2)d^2\le P_Q+b_g^2/(2\nu)\): the external critical work is
paid inside that row.

Suppose a finite maximal time \(T_*\) exists and the complete forced prefix
\(\mathscr W_f(t)=\int_0^tP_{\mathcal R}(s)\,ds\) is unbounded above.
Choose \(A_n=4^nA_0\), let \(t_n\) be the first time
\(\mathscr W_f=A_n\), let \(s_n<t_n\) be the last preceding time
\(\mathscr W_f=A_n/2\), and put \(I_n=(s_n,t_n)\).  The \(I_n\) are
pairwise disjoint.  Directly from the critical row, duality, and
Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{I_n}P_{\mathcal R}\,dt&={A_n\over2},\\
 \nu\int_{I_n}d^2\,dt
 &={A_n\over2}-H(t_n)+H(s_n)
 \le A_n+H(0),\\
 \int_{I_n}b_{\mathcal R}^2\,dt
 &\ge {\nu A_n^2\over4(A_n+H(0))},\\
 \int_{I_n}b_Q^2\,dt
 &\ge {\nu A_n^2\over8(A_n+H(0))}
      -\int_{I_n}b_g^2\,dt,\\
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}{1\over A_n}
 \int_{I_n}b_Q^2\,dt&\ge {\nu\over8}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N45}
\]

For the second line,
\(H(s_n)=H(0)+A_n/2-\nu\int_0^{s_n}d^2\le H(0)+A_n/2\).
The fourth line uses
\(b_{\mathcal R}^2\le2b_Q^2+2b_g^2\); the last uses the disjointness of
the records and \(b_g\in L^2(0,T_*)\).  Thus the prescribed force cannot
carry the divergent part of the complete source square.  Every quantity in
(N45), including \(Q(u)\), is evaluated on the forced solution itself.

For divergence-free fields write

\[
 \mathcal B(a,b)=\mathbb P((a\cdot\nabla)b),\qquad
 DQ(u)[h]=-\mathcal B(h,u)-\mathcal B(u,h),\qquad
 \mathcal C(u)=\sum_{j=1}^3
 \mathcal B(\partial_ju,\partial_ju).
\]

The product identity
\(DQ(u)[Au]=AQ(u)-2\mathcal C(u)\), together with
\(u_t=Q+g-\nu Au\), gives the complete forced first-binomial source row
and its signed storage identity:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 Q_t+\nu AQ
 =DQ(u)[Q]+2\nu\mathcal C(u)+DQ(u)[g],\\
 \mathscr R_Q={1\over2}\|\Lambda^{-3/2}Q\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 J_{\rm int}
 =\bigl(DQ(u)[Q]+2\nu\mathcal C(u),\Lambda^{-3}Q\bigr)_2,\\
 J_g=\bigl(DQ(u)[g],\Lambda^{-3}Q\bigr)_2,\\
 \mathscr R_Q'+\nu b_Q^2=J_{\rm int}+J_g.
 \end{gathered}}
 \tag{N46}
\]

The Leray projections in (N46) retain the nonlinear pressure response, and
\(g=\mathbb P f\) retains the simultaneous force--pressure allocation.
Unlike the intrinsic term, the new force insertion is paid by (N44).
Hardy--Littlewood--Sobolev, Hölder, and Sobolev interpolation give

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\Lambda^{-3/2}DQ(u)[g]\|_2
 &\le C\|gu\|_{3/2}
 \le C\|g\|_6\|u\|_2,\\
 \|\Lambda^{-3/2}Q\|_2
 &\le C\|u\otimes u\|_{3/2}
 =C\|u\|_3^2
 \le C\|u\|_2\|\nabla u\|_2,\\
 |J_g(t)|
 &\le C\|g(t)\|_6\|u(t)\|_2^2\|\nabla u(t)\|_2,\\
 \int_0^T|J_g(t)|\,dt
 &\le C\mathcal E_T\|g\|_{L^2(0,T;L^6)}
 \left(\int_0^T\|\nabla u\|_2^2dt\right)^{1/2}\\
 &\le C\nu^{-1/2}\mathcal E_T^{3/2}
 \|g\|_{L^2(0,T;L^6)}=:\mathcal F_T.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N47}
\]

Thus \(J_g\in L^1(0,T_*)\), with a bound determined only by
\((u_0,\nu,f)\) on that horizon.  Integrating (N46) from the datum to
\(t_n\), retaining \(\mathscr R_Q(t_n)\ge0\), and using (N45) proves

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^{t_n}J_{\rm int}(t)\,dt
 \ge{}&{\nu^2A_n^2\over8(A_n+H(0))}
 -\nu\int_{I_n}b_g^2\,dt
 -\mathscr R_Q(0)-\mathcal F_{T_*},\\
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}{1\over A_n}
 \int_0^{t_n}J_{\rm int}(t)\,dt
 \ge{}&{\nu^2\over8}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N48}
\]

This is the forced composition.  A failed complete critical prefix forces
linearly divergent pressure-complete intrinsic first-binomial work on that
same forced history; prescribed forcing cannot supply the divergence after
its exact insertion is paid by (N47).  Conversely, an input-dependent upper
bound

\[
 \sup_{0<T<T_*}\int_0^T J_{\rm int}(t)\,dt<\infty
\]

would bound \(\int_0^{T_*}b_Q^2\) by (N46)--(N47), hence also
\(\int_0^{T_*}b_{\mathcal R}^2\).  The direct estimate

\[
 H(t)+{\nu\over2}\int_0^t d^2ds
 \le H(0)+{1\over2\nu}\int_0^t b_{\mathcal R}^2ds
\]

would then bound the complete forced critical prefix and rule out the
first-arrival cascade in (N31)--(N34).  Equation (N48) proves the exact
contrapositive leading to that signed inequality; it does not prove the
upper bound itself.

There is no universal pointwise turn-down sign for \(J_{\rm int}\).  The
exact pressure-complete \(2D3C\) calibration from the August 4 source, on
the normalized periodic cell with

\[
 V=(\cos y+\sin(x+y),\ \cos x-\sin(x+y),
       \ \cos x+\cos y+\sin(x+y)),
\]

and datum \(u(0)=A V\), \(f=0\), gives

\[
 J_{\rm int}(0;A)
 =A^4(AK_E+2\nu K_C),\qquad
 K_E={2\over5}-{1\over4\sqrt2}+{1\over40\sqrt5}>0,
 \quad
 K_C=-{3\over4}+{11\over100\sqrt5}<0.
\]

It is negative for \(0<A<-2\nu K_C/K_E\) and positive for larger
\(A\).  The zero-force choice shows that the added force insertion is not
responsible for either sign, and the pressure is the exact pressure-Poisson
response.  Because this calibration is periodic, it retires only a
domain-independent algebraic claim \(J_{\rm int}\le0\) or
\(J_{\rm int}\ge0\); it does not rule out the cumulative datum-dependent
\(\mathbb R^3\) upper bound isolated above.

## 19. Exact terminal energy defect and critical source residue

Let \(T=T_*<\infty\) be a hypothetical maximal time.  The strict-interval
kinetic equality, (N13), and the integrability of the prescribed force work
show that the left kinetic energy has a limit.  With the terminal member from
(N14f), define

\[
 e_T^-:=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{1\over2}\|u(t)\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak d_E(T):=e_T^- -{1\over2}\|u(T)\|_2^2\ge0.
 \tag{N50}
\]

This is not only weak lower semicontinuity.  Let
\(S_J={\bf1}_{\{|\xi|\le2^J\}}(D)\) and \(Q_J=I-S_J\).  For fixed \(J\),
(N14g), or already (N14c), gives
\(S_Ju(t)\to S_Ju(T)\) strongly in \(L^2\).  Orthogonality thus gives

\[
 \mathfrak d_E(T)
 =\lim_{t\uparrow T}
   \left({1\over2}\|Q_Ju(t)\|_2^2
              -{1\over2}\|Q_Ju(T)\|_2^2\right)
 =\lim_{J\to\infty}\lim_{t\uparrow T}
   {1\over2}\|Q_Ju(t)\|_2^2 .
 \tag{N51}
\]

The first equality in (N51) holds for every fixed \(J\).  The last expression
is a different, nested limit: first \(t\uparrow T\) at a fixed cutoff, then
\(J\to\infty\), using \(\|Q_Ju(T)\|_2\to0\).  Thus the negative traces remove
every fixed-frequency defect, but they leave exactly a frequency-escape defect.
Indeed, if a unit \(L^2\) packet is placed in shell \(j\), its
\(\mathcal X_\omega\) norm is proportional to
\(\omega_j2^{-j/2}\), which tends to zero under the summability hypothesis
in (N14g).

For \(s<t<T\), the full forced equation gives the exact high-pass balance

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 E_J(t)-E_J(s)+\nu\int_s^tD_J(r)\,dr
   =\int_s^t\bigl(\Pi_J(r)+W_J(r)\bigr)\,dr,\\
 E_J={1\over2}\|Q_Ju\|_2^2,\qquad
 D_J=\|\Lambda Q_Ju\|_2^2,\\
 \Pi_J=-\langle Q_Ju,Q_J((u\cdot\nabla)u)\rangle,
 \qquad W_J=\langle Q_Ju,Q_Jg\rangle,\\
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \mathfrak d_E(T)
   =\lim_{J\to\infty}
      \left(\lim_{t\uparrow T}\int_s^t\Pi_J(r)\,dr\right).}
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{N52}
\]

For each fixed \(J\), the inner improper integral exists by the first line:
the other three terms have limits.  Only afterwards is \(J\) sent to
infinity.  At that stage \(E_J(s)\to0\),
\(\int_s^TD_J\to0\) by (N13), and
\(\int_s^T|W_J|\to0\) because
\(g\in L^1(0,T;L^2)\).  The pressure pairing is identically zero because
\(Q_Ju\) is solenoidal.  Hence the minimal kinetic defect that survives is
the signed nonlinear flux in (N52); neither viscosity nor the smooth force
has a remaining high-frequency term that can cancel it.

The corresponding critical law has an exact signed form.  Let \(P_A\) be
an orthogonal Fourier projection onto any band away from zero, and put

\[
 w_A=P_Au,\quad H_A=\|\Lambda^{1/2}w_A\|_2^2,\quad
 a_A=\Lambda^{-1/2}P_A\mathcal R,\quad
 z_A=\Lambda^{3/2}w_A.
\]

Since \((w_A)_t+\nu\Lambda^2w_A=P_A\mathcal R\), completing the square,
without taking a terminal limit, gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 H_A(t)-H_A(s)
 ={}&{1\over2\nu}\int_s^t\|a_A\|_2^2\,dr
   -2\nu\int_s^t
       \left\|z_A-{a_A\over2\nu}\right\|_2^2\,dr\\
 ={}&{1\over2\nu}\int_s^t
   \left(
    \|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_A(f-(u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_2^2
    -\|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_A\nabla p\|_2^2
   \right)dr\\
 &\hspace{2cm}
   -2\nu\int_s^t
       \left\|z_A-{a_A\over2\nu}\right\|_2^2\,dr .
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N53}
\]

The second equality is exactly the orthogonal pressure allocation (N34).
Thus the raw complete-source square is not itself the defect: the pressure
allocation and the nonnegative viscous matching square must be subtracted.
The resulting signed residue is precisely the change of critical band
height.  In particular, a first upward passage from
\(H_A\le\eta^2/4\) to \(H_A\ge\eta^2\) forces

\[
 \int_s^t\|a_A\|_2^2\,dr\ge {3\nu\eta^2\over2}.
\]

Here is the finite-band selection needed to use (N34).  Put
\(\eta_0=\nu/(2C_*)\), the threshold in (N27), and fix
\(0<\eta<\eta_0/4\).  Having chosen the upper edge of \(A_{n-1}\), choose
\(\kappa_n\) above it and so large that the initial critical tail beyond
\(\kappa_n\) is below \(\eta^2/4\).  Equation (N27) supplies
\(t_n>T_*-1/n\) for which the critical tail beyond \(\kappa_n\) has norm
at least \(3\eta\).  Since the single preterminal field \(u(t_n)\) is smooth,
its critical Fourier tail tends to zero at infinity; hence a finite
\(M_n>\kappa_n\) captures critical height at least \(\eta^2\) in
\(A_n=\{\kappa_n<|\xi|\le M_n\}\).  Choose
\(\kappa_{n+1}>M_n\).  Continuity gives a first upward passage of \(H_{A_n}\)
from \(\eta^2/4\) to \(\eta^2\).  Uniform smoothness on every compact
subinterval \([0,T_*-\delta]\) forces these passages to approach \(T_*\).
Thus the bands are finite and pairwise disjoint even though the tail in
(N27) may initially be spread across arbitrarily many shells.

Applying the last inequality on the resulting first-arrival intervals
\(I_n\uparrow T_*\), and using Fourier orthogonality even when the intervals
overlap, gives

\[
 \sum_n\int_{I_n}
   \|\Lambda^{-1/2}P_{A_n}\mathcal R\|_2^2\,dt=\infty,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R\notin L^2(0,T_*;\dot H^{-1/2}).
 \tag{N54}
\]

That conclusion holds for the full pressure-complete source by (N34).
Because the prescribed \(f\) is smooth, its critical source square is finite;
the divergence in (N54) belongs to the nonlinear solenoidal source, with its
simultaneous pressure allocation retained.  Conversely, a proof that
\(\mathcal R\in L^2(0,T_*;\dot H^{-1/2})\) would close this branch: (N32)
applied to \(Q_J\) gives

\[
 \sup_{t<T_*}\|\Lambda^{1/2}Q_Ju(t)\|_2
 \le \|\Lambda^{1/2}Q_Ju_0\|_2
 +(2\nu)^{-1/2}
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}Q_J\mathcal R\|_{L^2_tL^2_x}
 \longrightarrow0,
\]

contradicting (N27).  The established rows give only
\(\mathcal R\in L^1_tB^{-1/2}_{2,1}\); they do not give this square.
Moreover, zero kinetic defect would not by itself close the proof: a critical
packet of fixed height \(\eta^2\) at frequency \(\lambda\) has only
\(O(\eta^2/\lambda)\) kinetic energy.

There is a same-history countertest to any attempt to manufacture the missing
square from the paid base rows.  Choose
\(U(s,x)=\chi(s)V(x)\), where
\(\chi\in C_c^\infty(0,1)\) and
\(V\in\mathcal S_\sigma\) has Fourier support in one annulus.  For any
\(P\in C_c^\infty((0,1);\mathcal S)\), set

\[
 G=U_s+(U\cdot\nabla)U+\nabla P-\nu\Delta U.
\]

Let \(\lambda_j=8^j\) and choose disjoint parabolic time intervals inside
one fixed finite horizon.  For every finite \(N\), define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 u^{(N)}(t,x)&=\sum_{j=1}^N
   \lambda_jU(\lambda_j^2(t-a_j),\lambda_jx),\\
 p^{(N)}(t,x)&=\sum_{j=1}^N
   \lambda_j^2P(\lambda_j^2(t-a_j),\lambda_jx),\\
 f^{(N)}(t,x)&=\sum_{j=1}^N
   \lambda_j^3G(\lambda_j^2(t-a_j),\lambda_jx).
 \end{aligned}
\]

The time supports are disjoint, so this is one exact smooth solution
generated by the single prescribed triple \((0,\nu,f^{(N)})\); there are no
cross-pulse nonlinear terms and no second history.  Its pressure is exactly
the displayed normalized pressure.  Standard forced scaling gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_t\|u^{(N)}(t)\|_2^2&\le C,\\
 \int\|\nabla u^{(N)}\|_2^2dt
   +\int\|\Lambda^{-1}\mathbb Pf^{(N)}\|_2^2dt
   &\le C\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^{-1},\\
 \int\|\mathbb Pf^{(N)}\|_2dt
   &\le C\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^{-1/2},\\
 \int\|u^{(N)}\|_{\dot B^{3/2}_{2,1}}dt
   +\int\|\mathcal R^{(N)}\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}dt
   &\le C\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^{-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

All these paid quantities are thus bounded uniformly in \(N\).  But

\[
 \int\|\mathcal R^{(N)}\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2dt
 =N\int_0^1
   \|\Lambda^{-1/2}(U_s-\nu\Delta U)\|_2^2ds.
\]

Each pulse has that critical height and that scale-free source-square
charge.  Every \(f^{(N)}\) is source-admissible and every term belongs to its
one forced history.  This does not construct a terminal singularity; it
proves that no bound using the currently paid kinetic, dissipation, Besov,
negative-order force, and qualitative smooth-force inputs can supply the missing
critical square.  Higher force seminorms in this family are allowed to grow,
so the test does not rule out a new estimate that genuinely uses such a
seminorm.  An additional signed nonlinear estimate, such as the cumulative
bound left open after (N48), would be required only for that alternate route;
it is not a prerequisite of the direct construction in Section 6.

## 20. Critical-source geometry and the signed payment boundary

The full pressure law gives an exact geometric form of the nonlinear source,
but it does not give that source a sign.  Let

\[
 \omega=\nabla\times u,\qquad
 q=-\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u)=\mathbb P(u\times\omega),
 \qquad g=\mathbb P f,
\]

so that the source in (N32)--(N34) is \(\mathcal R=q+g\).  The gradient in
\((u\cdot\nabla)u=\nabla|u|^2/2-u\times\omega\) is absorbed into that
normalized pressure.  Since both \(u\) and \(\omega\) are solenoidal,

\[
 \boxed{
 \langle q,u\rangle=0,
 \qquad \langle q,\omega\rangle=0,
 \qquad
 J:=\langle q,\Lambda u\rangle
   =-\langle\Lambda u,(u\cdot\nabla)u\rangle .}
 \tag{N60}
\]

Thus kinetic energy and helicity see two exact nonlinear tangencies, whereas
the critical first moment sees the signed current \(J\).

This distinction is especially transparent in the helical decomposition.
On nonzero frequencies let

\[
 P_\pm={1\over2}\bigl(I\pm\Lambda^{-1}\nabla\times\bigr),
 \quad u_\pm=P_\pm u,\quad g_\pm=P_\pm g,
\]

and set

\[
 R_\pm=\|\Lambda^{1/2}u_\pm\|_2^2,
 \qquad D_\pm=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u_\pm\|_2^2.
\]

The second tangency in (N60) says that the two helical projections of the
nonlinear source have equal critical work.  Pairing their two projected
equations thus gives the exact forced common-mode rows

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 R_+'+2\nu D_+&=J+2\langle g_+,\Lambda u_+\rangle,\\
 R_-'+2\nu D_-&=J+2\langle g_-,\Lambda u_-\rangle,\\
 (R_+-R_-)'+2\nu(D_+-D_-)
 &=2\langle g,\omega\rangle .
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N61}
\]

The nonlinear current is absent from the difference row and enters both
positive critical stocks in that direction.  Consequently a helicity
or helical-imbalance estimate alone cannot pay positive common-mode critical
work.

The vorticity--strain pair gives no additional global signed account.  Write
\(S=(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T})/2\),
\(Y=\|\omega\|_2^2\), \(Z=\|\nabla\omega\|_2^2\), and
\(\mathcal V=\int\omega\cdot S\omega\,dx\).  The vorticity equation and the
strain equation, with the pressure Hessian retained before integration, give

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 {1\over2}Y'+\nu Z
   &=\mathcal V+\langle\nabla\times g,\omega\rangle,\\
 {1\over2}{d\over dt}\|S\|_2^2+\nu\|\nabla S\|_2^2
   &=-\int\operatorname{tr}(S^3)\,dx-{1\over4}\mathcal V
     +\langle\operatorname{sym}\nabla g,S\rangle,\\
 \int\operatorname{tr}(S^3)\,dx&=-{3\over4}\mathcal V,
 \qquad \int S:\nabla^2p\,dx=0,\\
 \|S\|_2^2&={1\over2}Y,\qquad
 \|\nabla S\|_2^2={1\over2}Z,\qquad
 \langle\operatorname{sym}\nabla g,S\rangle
 ={1\over2}\langle\nabla\times g,\omega\rangle .
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N62}
\]

The third line is the Betchov identity.  Substitution into the strain row
makes it exactly one half of the vorticity row.  Incompressibility cancels the
global pressure-Hessian pairing, but the pressure field and its positive
source-square allocation in (N34) remain present.  Hence these two rows do
not furnish a second signed relation with which to bound \(J_+\).

The material-pair calculation identifies the missing geometry rather than a
hidden cancellation.  With \(L_u=u\cdot\nabla\), skew-adjointness of \(L_u\)
gives

\[
 \boxed{
 J=-{1\over2}\langle[\Lambda,L_u]u,u\rangle
   =-c_\Lambda\iint_{\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^3}
     {|u(x)-u(y)|^2\,(u(x)-u(y))\cdot(x-y)
      \over |x-y|^6}\,dx\,dy,\qquad c_\Lambda>0 .}
 \tag{N63}
\]

The second equality follows from the symmetric difference formula for
\(\Lambda\) and one integration by parts in \((x,y)\).  Positive critical
work is the net compressive longitudinal part of this nonlocal material-pair
current.  Trace-free strain permits both signs; taking a positive part before
the full integral destroys the cancellation in (N63).

An exact divergence-free triad checks all of these sign boundaries.  On the
unit-volume normalized torus take

\[
 k=(1,0,0),\quad \ell=(0,2,0),\quad m=(-1,-2,0),
\]

\[
 \widehat v(k)=(0,-i,0),\quad
 \widehat v(\ell)=(-1,0,0),\quad
 \widehat v(m)=(-2,1,0),\quad
 \widehat v(-r)=\overline{\widehat v(r)}.
\]

Every coefficient is perpendicular to its wave vector.  Direct evaluation of

\[
 \widehat B(r)=i\sum_{a+b=r}(\widehat v(a)\cdot b)\widehat v(b),
 \qquad \widehat q(r)=-\mathbb P_r\widehat B(r),
\]

gives, on the three displayed modes,

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 r&\widehat B(r)&\widehat q(r)&
 \operatorname{Re}(\widehat q(r)\cdot\overline{\widehat v(r)})\\ \hline
 k&(4i,-i,0)&(0,i,0)&-1\\
 \ell&(4,-4,0)&(-4,0,0)&4\\
 m&(-2,-1,0)&(6/5,-3/5,0)&-3
 \end{array}
\]

and the negative modes give the conjugate contributions.  Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 J(v)&=14-6\sqrt5>0,
 &\int v\cdot\omega\,dx&=0,
 &\int\omega\cdot S\omega\,dx&=0,\\
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}q(v)\|_2^2
   &=18+4\sqrt2+{36\over5\sqrt5}+{2\over17\sqrt{17}},
 &\|\Lambda^{-1/2}\nabla p(v)\|_2^2
   &=48+4\sqrt2+{64\over5\sqrt5}+{32\over17\sqrt{17}},\\
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}B(v)\|_2^2
   &=66+8\sqrt2+4\sqrt5+{2\over\sqrt{17}},
 &\|\Lambda^{3/2}v\|_2^2&=18+50\sqrt5 .
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N64}
\]

The field is planar and independent of the third coordinate, so
\(\omega=(0,0,\omega_3)\) and \(S\omega=0\) pointwise.  The convolution also
has output modes outside the three occupied legs.  Grouping every output and
its conjugate by squared radius gives

\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 \rho^2&1&4&5&8&17\\ \hline
 \sum_{|r|^2=\rho^2}|\widehat q(r)|^2
   &2&32&36/5&16&2/17\\
 \sum_{|r|^2=\rho^2}|(I-\mathbb P_r)\widehat B(r)|^2
   &32&32&64/5&16&32/17
 \end{array}
\]

Multiplication of these rows by \(\rho^{-1}\) gives the two source-square
values in (N64), and their sum is the displayed full square, verifying (N34)
exactly.  Replacing
\(v\) by \(-v\) reverses \(J\) and leaves both source squares unchanged;
replacing it by \(Av\) makes \(J\) cubic and the viscous row quadratic.
Thus neither helicity, vortex stretching, pressure completion, nor viscosity
has a universal pointwise sign capable of paying the positive critical
current.

For the whole-space sign test, replace the six Fourier points by sufficiently
narrow conjugate-paired smooth bumps, apply the Leray multiplier on their
supports, and keep the supports disjoint from every unintended zero-sum
triple.  Continuity of the cubic and quadratic Fourier multipliers preserves
the strict sign and the pressure-complete allocations, producing a real
Schwartz divergence-free field.  Its zero-force local Navier--Stokes history
has \(J>0\) on a nonempty initial interval.  Under the exact scaling

\[
 u_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda u(\lambda^2t,\lambda x)
\]

both \(\int J_+dt\) and
\(\int\|\Lambda^{-1/2}q\|_2^2dt\) are invariant, whereas the initial kinetic
energy and the integrated kinetic dissipation are multiplied by
\(\lambda^{-1}\).  Since the force is zero, no estimate whose right-hand side
contains only the kinetic-order stock and prescribed-force seminorms that
vanish with the force can pay either critical quantity.  This is a scaling
and sign obstruction to that estimate family, not an obstruction to a
different datum-sensitive critical bound.

There is nevertheless a strictly smaller signed target which would pay the
source square.  Define on strict prefixes

\[
 \mathfrak J_+(T)
 :=\sup_{0<S<T}\int_0^S[J(t)]_+\,dt,
 \qquad
 M_T:={1\over2}\|\Lambda^{1/2}u_0\|_2^2
      +\mathfrak J_+(T)
      +{1\over2\nu}\int_0^T\|\Lambda^{-1/2}g\|_2^2dt .
\]

The critical row (N36), with only the prescribed force absorbed by Young's
inequality, and the fractional product estimate

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathbb P((u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_2
 &\le C\|\Lambda^{1/2}u\|_2\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2,\\
 \|\Lambda^{-1/2}((u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_2
 &\le C\|\Lambda^{1/2}u\|_2\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2,
 \end{aligned}
\]

(the second estimate is that fractional product estimate before applying
the Leray contraction),

give the exact conditional payment

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{0<S<T}\left\{
 {1\over2}\|\Lambda^{1/2}u(S)\|_2^2
 +{\nu\over2}\int_0^S\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2dt\right\}
 &\le M_T,\\
 \int_0^T\|\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathcal R\|_2^2dt
 &\le 2\int_0^T\|\Lambda^{-1/2}g\|_2^2dt
       +{8C^2\over\nu}M_T^2,\\
 \int_0^T\|\Lambda^{-1/2}(f-(u\cdot\nabla)u)\|_2^2dt
 &\le 2\int_0^T\|\Lambda^{-1/2}f\|_2^2dt
       +{8C^2\over\nu}M_T^2.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N65}
\]

The last line retains both orthogonal allocations in (N34).  Consequently a
datum-dependent proof that \(\mathfrak J_+(T_*)<\infty\) would bound the
critical action and the complete critical source square, excluding the
first-arrival cascade in (N31)--(N34).  The helical and strain identities above
reduce the search to the positive balanced common-mode, compressive current;
they do not prove its cumulative datum bound.  Thus (N65), rather than a sign
claim, is the exact smaller outstanding target supplied by this geometry.

## 21. Parabolic record clock, normalized (H^1) exclusion, and analyticity scope

The finite disjoint bands in (N54) admit a useful quantitative continuation
test, but their lower edges must not be identified with a continuation scale
without proof.  Write the first-arrival intervals as (I_n=(s_n,t_n)), with

\[
 H_{A_n}(s_n)={\eta^2\over4},\qquad
 H_{A_n}(t_n)=\eta^2,
 \qquad
 A_n=\{\kappa_n<|\xi|\le M_n\},
\]

where \(\kappa_n\to\infty\) and (t_n\uparrow T_*).  Put

\[
 \delta_n=T_*-t_n,
 \qquad
 \lambda_n=\min\{\kappa_n,\delta_n^{-1/4}\}.
\]

This \(\lambda_n\) is an auxiliary rescaling scale, not the lower edge of the
band.  It satisfies

\[
 \lambda_n\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad \lambda_n\le\kappa_n,
 \qquad
 \Theta_n:=\lambda_n^2(T_*-t_n)le\delta_n^{1/2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{N66}
\]

On the future part of that prescribed-force history define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U_n(\tau,y)
   &=\lambda_n^{-1}u(t_n+\lambda_n^{-2}\tau,\lambda_n^{-1}y),\\
 P_n(\tau,y)
   &=\lambda_n^{-2}p(t_n+\lambda_n^{-2}\tau,\lambda_n^{-1}y),\\
 G_n(\tau,y)
   &=\lambda_n^{-3}g(t_n+\lambda_n^{-2}\tau,\lambda_n^{-1}y),\\
 \widetilde{\mathcal R}_n(\tau,y)
   &=\lambda_n^{-3}\mathcal R(t_n+\lambda_n^{-2}\tau,
                                      \lambda_n^{-1}y).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Then (U_n) obeys the full forced equation with viscosity \(\nu\), pressure
\(P_n\), and projected force \(G_n\) on (0\le\tau<\Theta_n\).  If
\(\widetilde A_n=\lambda_n^{-1}A_n\), then
\(\widetilde A_n\subset\{|\zeta|\ge1\}\), critical height and critical
source action are invariant, and the prescribed force has the exact scaling

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\Lambda_y^{1/2}P_{\widetilde A_n}U_n\|_2^2
   &=\|\Lambda_x^{1/2}P_{A_n}u\|_2^2,\\
 \|\Lambda_y^{-1/2}P_{\widetilde A_n}
       \widetilde{\mathcal R}_n\|_2^2
   &=\lambda_n^{-2}
      \|\Lambda_x^{-1/2}P_{A_n}\mathcal R\|_2^2,\\
 \|G_n(\tau)\|_2^2
   &=\lambda_n^{-3}
      \|g(t_n+\lambda_n^{-2}\tau)\|_2^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N67}
\]

Consequently (N53) gives the scale-free record charge

\[
 \boxed{
 {3\nu\eta^2\over2}
 \le
 \int_{\lambda_n^2(I_n-t_n)}
 \|\Lambda_y^{-1/2}P_{\widetilde A_n}
       \widetilde{\mathcal R}_n\|_2^2\,d\tau .}
 \tag{N68}
\]

The force part of these charges is summable.  Indeed, Fourier orthogonality
of the disjoint (A_n), even when the (I_n) overlap, gives

\[
 \sum_n\int_{\lambda_n^2(I_n-t_n)}
   \|\Lambda_y^{-1/2}P_{\widetilde A_n}G_n\|_2^2d\tau
 =\sum_n\int_{I_n}
   \|\Lambda_x^{-1/2}P_{A_n}g\|_2^2dt
 \le\int_0^{T_*}\|\Lambda^{-1/2}g\|_2^2dt.
 \tag{N69}
\]

Thus the prescribed force still cannot carry infinitely many record charges
after rescaling.

There is also an exact normalized (H^1) consequence.  Set

\[
 Y_n(\tau)=\|\nabla_yU_n(\tau)\|_2^2,
 \qquad D_n(\tau)=\|\Delta_yU_n(\tau)\|_2^2.
\]

The complete enstrophy row, with the nonlinear and force terms both retained,
obeys

\[
 Y_n'+\nu D_n
 \le C_1\nu^{-3}Y_n^3+C_2\nu^{-1}\|G_n\|_2^2.
 \tag{N70}
\]

This follows from

\[
 |\langle (U_n\cdot\nabla)U_n,\Delta U_n\rangle|
 \le C Y_n^{3/4}D_n^{3/4}
 \le {\nu\over4}D_n+C\nu^{-3}Y_n^3
\]

and

\[
 |\langle G_n,-\Delta U_n\rangle|
 \le {\nu\over4}D_n+\nu^{-1}\|G_n\|_2^2.
\]

The standard (L^2\cap\dot H^1) strong construction uses (N70) as its
continuation clock.  Its lower lifespan depends on the homogeneous quantity
\(Y_n(0)\) and on \(G_n\in L^2_\tau L^2_y\); the possibly growing rescaled
kinetic norm does not enter this clock.  More explicitly, if
\(Y_n(0)\le L\) along a subsequence, put (R=L+1) and choose

\[
 0<\tau_0\le {\nu^3\over16C_1R^2}.
\]

Because the prescribed force is continuous into (L^2) across (T_*),
(N67) gives, for every fixed \(\tau_0\),

\[
 \int_0^{\tau_0}\|G_n(\tau)\|_2^2d\tau
 \le \tau_0\lambda_n^{-3}
   \sup_{|t-T_*|\le1}\|g(t)\|_2^2\longrightarrow0.
\]

A direct bootstrap in (N70) then keeps (Y_n\le2R) on
\([0,\tau_0]\) for all sufficiently large (n), and the strong solution
exists there.  Since \(\Theta_n\to0\), uniqueness glues this solution to the
original history and extends it beyond (T_*), a contradiction.  Thus
every finite-terminal first-arrival sequence satisfies the Type-II
scale-separation law

\[
 \boxed{
 {\|\nabla u(t_n)\|_2^2\over\lambda_n}
 =Y_n(0)\longrightarrow\infty .}
 \tag{N71}
\]

The distinction between \(\lambda_n\) and \(\kappa_n\) is essential:
(N54) proves (t_n\uparrow T_*) and \(\kappa_n\uparrow\infty\), but supplies
no rate relation between those two limits.  Thus (N71) is a genuine
same-history restriction on a surviving cascade, not a proof that its
band-edge clock is uniformly bounded.

Spatial or temporal analyticity does not strengthen (N71) for the admitted
force class.  If a Gevrey norm happens to be finite, then the record itself
gives only

\[
 \eta^2\le H_{A_n}(t_n)
 \le e^{-2\rho\kappa_n}
       \|e^{\rho\Lambda}\Lambda^{1/2}u(t_n)\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \rho\kappa_n
 \le\log {\|e^{\rho\Lambda}\Lambda^{1/2}u(t_n)\|_2\over\eta}.
 \tag{N72}
\]

Excluding the records through (N72) would require a uniform positive radius
and a uniform terminal Gevrey norm, neither of which is supplied by the mixed
jet.  In fact analyticity is false for the full admissible forced class.
Choose a nonzero \(\psi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\), put
\(V=(\partial_2\psi,-\partial_1\psi,0)\), and let
\(\chi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)\) be nonanalytic.  Then

\[
 u(t,x)=\chi(t)V(x),\qquad p=0,
 \qquad
 f=\chi'V+\chi^2(V\cdot\nabla)V-\nu\chi\Delta V
 \tag{N73}
\]

is one exact global forced history with smooth Schwartz datum and force.
It need be analytic neither in space nor in time.  The normalized pressure is
zero because the divergence of the nonlinear term is already included in
the displayed force.

Finally, (P_{A_n}u) satisfies an inhomogeneous projected equation with
source (P_{A_n}\mathcal R), and (N68) says that this source does not vanish.
The bands move with (n), their terminal traces are not zero, and the
rescaled endpoint carries critical height \(\eta^2\).  A backward-uniqueness
theorem for a fixed homogeneous equation thus has no object to which it
can be applied here.  Parabolic analyticity, backward uniqueness, and the
ordinary local lifespan theorem leave the finite-time cascade compatible
with the exact parabolic scaling; their rigorous new output is the
scale-separation law (N71), not a no-Zeno theorem.

## 22. Forced material-pair evolution and the aggregate signed response

The commutator current in (N63) has an exact evolution on the actual forced
material flow.  Let

\[
 \dot X(a,t)=u(X(a,t),t),\qquad X(a,0)=a,
 \qquad \mathcal D_a(t):=\det D_aX(a,t).
\]

Incompressibility retains the Jacobian rather than silently discarding it:

\[
 \dot{\mathcal D}_a=(\nabla\!\cdot u)(X(a,t),t)\mathcal D_a=0,
 \qquad \mathcal D_a(t)=1.
 \tag{N74}
\]

For a pair of labels put

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 R=X(a,t)-X(b,t),\qquad V=u(X(a,t),t)-u(X(b,t),t),
 \qquad r=|R|,\quad n=R/r,\\
 \alpha={V\cdot R\over r^2},\qquad
 \beta={V\over r}-\alpha n,\\
 A=(D_tu)(X(a,t),t)-(D_tu)(X(b,t),t).
 \end{gathered}
\]

The complete forced equation gives, without separating the pair from its
field,

\[
 \boxed{
 A=\delta_{ab}(-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u+f)
   =\int_0^1
   [-\nabla^2p+\nu\nabla\Delta u+\nabla f](X_b+\theta R,t)R\,d\theta .}
 \tag{N75}
\]

Write

\[
 \mathfrak c={n\cdot A\over r},\qquad
 \chi={V\cdot A\over |V|^2},\qquad
 e={c_\Lambda\over4}{|V|^2\over r^4},\qquad
 j=-4e\alpha .
\]

Changing variables from Eulerian points to the two label variables retains
both flow Jacobians and gives

\[
 J(t)=\iint j_{ab}(t)\mathcal D_a(t)\mathcal D_b(t)\,da\,db.
\]

Every product containing \(\chi\) is understood division-free through
\(e\chi=(c_\Lambda/4)(V\cdot A)/r^4\).  Direct differentiation along the
same pair gives

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \dot r=\alpha r,\qquad \dot n=\beta,\qquad
 \dot\alpha=-\alpha^2+|\beta|^2+\mathfrak c,
 \qquad {\dot e\over e}=2\chi-4\alpha,\\
 \boxed{
 {d\over dt}(j\mathcal D_a\mathcal D_b)
 =4e\mathcal D_a\mathcal D_b
   (5\alpha^2-|\beta|^2-\mathfrak c-2\alpha\chi)
 +j\mathcal D_a\mathcal D_b
  [\nabla\!\cdot u(X_a)+\nabla\!\cdot u(X_b)].}
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{N76}
\]

The last line displays the Jacobian contribution before (N74) makes it zero.
The acceleration in (N75), and hence both response contractions in (N76),
retain pressure, viscosity and the prescribed force.

There is a useful exact recombination hidden in (N76).  For any vector field
\(h\), set \(H_{ab}=h(X_a)-h(X_b)\) and

\[
 \mathfrak c_h={n\cdot H_{ab}\over r},\qquad
 \chi_h={V\cdot H_{ab}\over|V|^2},\qquad
 \mathscr L_u[h]
 :=\iint e(\mathfrak c_h+2\alpha\chi_h)
       \mathcal D_a\mathcal D_b\,da\,db .
\]

Again \(e\chi_h\) is the division-free expression.  Differentiating (N63)
at fixed Eulerian points proves the Fr\'echet identity

\[
 \boxed{DJ(u)[h]=-4\mathscr L_u[h].}
 \tag{N77}
\]

That result follows wholly in material coordinates.  With
\(B=(u\cdot\nabla)u\), one has
\(A=\delta_{ab}B+\delta_{ab}u_t\).  Volume-preserving relabelling gives the
kinematic cancellation

\[
 \boxed{
 \iint e(5\alpha^2-|\beta|^2-\mathfrak c_B-2\alpha\chi_B)
       \mathcal D_a\mathcal D_b\,da\,db=0.}
 \tag{N78}
\]

Thus all apparent radial self-amplification left by a pairwise reading
recombines with the advective acceleration before a sign is assigned, and
(N76) reduces exactly to the full forced row

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 J'&=-4\mathscr L_u[u_t]\\
   &=4\mathscr L_u[B]+4\mathscr L_u[\nabla p]
      -4\nu\mathscr L_u[\Delta u]-4\mathscr L_u[f]\\
   &=-4\mathscr L_u[q]-4\mathscr L_u[g]
      +4\nu\mathscr L_u[\Lambda^2u],
 \qquad q=-\mathbb PB,\quad g=\mathbb Pf.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N79}
\]

The second line retains the physical pressure and force separately; the
third is its pressure-complete orthogonal recombination.  A gradient moved
between \(f\) and the normalized pressure cancels between the corresponding
terms in the second line.  No individual pressure, viscous or force
contraction in that line has been assigned a sign.

On every strict compact interval the smooth forced history makes \(J\)
continuously differentiable.  The ordinary positive-part chain rule applies
only after the all-pair integral has been taken:

\[
 {d\over dt}[J(t)]_+
 =-4\mathbf 1_{\{J(t)>0\}}\mathscr L_u[u_t]
 \quad\hbox{for a.e. }t.
\]

Its derivative is zero almost everywhere on the zero set of \(J\), so a
crossing of \(J=0\) carries no atom.  A second integration gives the exact
causal identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^T[J(t)]_+\,dt
 =T[J(0)]_+
 -4\int_0^T(T-t)\mathbf 1_{\{J(t)>0\}}
       \mathscr L_u[u_t](t)\,dt .}
 \tag{N80}
\]

This is strictly smaller than selecting positive material pairs: by (N63),

\[
 [J(t)]_+=\left[\iint j_{ab}\,da\,db\right]_+
 \le \iint(j_{ab})_+\,da\,db,
\]

and (N80) preserves every cancellation inside both \(J\) and its complete
response before applying the one scalar sign selector.  It is noncircular as
an identity.  On a finite terminal interval, however, an upper bound for its
future-weighted response is exactly equivalent, up to the datum term
\(T[J(0)]_+\), to the cumulative-current bound required in (N65).  Thus
(N80) narrows the carrier and removes the larger pairwise-positive target;
it does not by itself supply the datum payment.

The torus triad in (N64) checks the signs and constants.  If \(v\) is that
field and the initial field is \(av\), direct evaluation of all convolution
outputs gives, for the zero-force Navier--Stokes row,

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 J(av)&=a^3(14-6\sqrt5),\\
 J'(0)&=a^4\Gamma_4+\nu a^3\Gamma_\nu,\\
 \Gamma_4
 &= {3402\over85}+32\sqrt2-{3428\over85}\sqrt5
    +{2\over17}\sqrt{17}<0,\\
 \Gamma_\nu&=-140+60\sqrt5<0.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N81}
\]

Hence for \(a>0\) the exact positive current initially turns down, while
replacing \(v\) by \(-v\) reverses the viscous contribution and the current.
The pressure-complete nonlinear coefficient \(\Gamma_4\) is even under that
amplitude reversal.  The force response has either sign on that triad:
at \(u=v\), prescribing

\[
 g_c=cv-q(v)+\nu\Lambda^2v
\]

gives \(u_t=cv\) and, by homogeneity,

\[
 J'=DJ(v)[cv]=3cJ(v).
 \tag{N82}
\]

The choice \(c=0\) is the exact stationary forced history; all nonzero
pairwise geometric, pressure, viscous and force responses then recombine to
zero in (N79).  Thus dropping any of those responses would fail an exact
forced solution.

Finally, under

\[
 u_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda u(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),\quad
 p_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda^2p(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),\quad
 f_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda^3f(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),
\]

the pair density \(e\), pair current density \(j\), aggregate current \(J\),
and aggregate response \(-\mathscr L_u[u_t]\) scale respectively as
\(\lambda^6,\lambda^8,\lambda^2,\lambda^4\), while the pair-label measure
scales as \(\lambda^{-6}\).  Consequently

\[
 \int_0^{T/\lambda^2}[J_\lambda(t)]_+\,dt
 =\int_0^T[J(t)]_+\,dt,
\]

and both terms on the right of (N80) are invariant.  The aggregate response
identity is thus at the required critical scale; it cannot be paid by
a only kinetic-order quantity that scales as \(\lambda^{-1}\).

## 23. The datum-paid Besov base does not pay the positive critical current

The whole-terminal base rows (N13)--(N14g) give two finite time integrals
which enter the critical current, but their separate finiteness is not a
bound for their product.  Indeed, the embedding
\(B^{3/2}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow L^\infty\) gives the scale-sharp estimate

\[
 \boxed{
 |J(t)|
 \le \|\Lambda u\|_2\,\|(u\cdot\nabla)u\|_2
 \le C\|u\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,1}}\|\nabla u\|_2^2 .}
 \tag{N83}
\]

Equation (N14b) pays the first factor in \(L^1_t\), and (N13) pays the
second factor in \(L^1_t\).  There is no Hölder pairing with those two
time exponents.  Interpolation between \(L^2\) and
\(B^{3/2}_{2,1}\) makes the missing improvement explicit:

\[
 \|\nabla u\|_2
 \le C\|u\|_2^{1/3}\|u\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,1}}^{2/3},
 \qquad
 |J(t)|
 \le CK_T^{2/3}\|u\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,1}}^{7/3}.
 \tag{N84}
\]

Thus \(u\in L^{7/3}_tB^{3/2}_{2,1}\) would be one sufficient entry, but
the datum-paid row (N14b) supplies only \(L^1_tB^{3/2}_{2,1}\).

This time-exponent boundary is realized by exact one-history forced
solutions, rather than by unrelated scalar functions.  Take a real
Schwartz divergence-free field \(V\) with \(J(V)>0\), supplied by the
whole-space realization following (N64), and choose a nonnegative
\(\chi\in C_c^\infty(0,1)\).  Put

\[
 U(s,x)=\chi(s)V(x),
 \qquad
 F=U_s+(U\cdot\nabla)U-\nu\Delta U.
 \tag{N85}
\]

Then \((U,0,F)\) is an exact smooth forced velocity--pressure--force
history.  Its normalized pressure is zero: the divergence of the prescribed
force contains exactly the divergence of the nonlinear term.  Let
\(\lambda_j=8^j\), choose pairwise disjoint intervals
\(I_j=a_j+\lambda_j^{-2}(0,1)\) inside one fixed finite horizon, and for
each finite \(N\) set

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 u^{(N)}(t,x)
   &=\sum_{j=1}^N
       \lambda_jU(\lambda_j^2(t-a_j),\lambda_jx),\\
 f^{(N)}(t,x)
   &=\sum_{j=1}^N
       \lambda_j^3F(\lambda_j^2(t-a_j),\lambda_jx),
 \qquad p^{(N)}=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N86}
\]

The time supports are disjoint, so there are no cross-pulse nonlinear
terms.  For every \(N\), (N86) is one exact source-admissible forced
history generated by the single triple \((0,\nu,f^{(N)})\).  Parabolic
scaling gives, with constants independent of \(N\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_t\|u^{(N)}(t)\|_2^2&\le C,\\
 \int\|\nabla u^{(N)}\|_2^2dt
  +\int\|u^{(N)}\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,1}}dt
  +\int\|\Lambda^{-1}\mathbb Pf^{(N)}\|_2^2dt
  &\le C\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^{-1}\le C,\\
 \int\|\mathbb Pf^{(N)}\|_2dt
  &\le C\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^{-1/2}\le C.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{N87}
\]

On the other hand, \(J\) is cubic in the amplitude and has parabolic
weight two.  Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \int [J(u^{(N)}(t))]_+dt
 =N J(V)\int_0^1\chi(s)^3ds\longrightarrow\infty .}
 \tag{N88}
\]

Consequently the already paid kinetic, dissipation, negative-force and
\(L^1_tB^{3/2}_{2,1}\) rows cannot by themselves supply the cumulative
bound in (N65).  Higher force seminorms in (N86) grow with \(N\), so this
countertest does not exclude an estimate that genuinely uses them, nor an
intrinsic signed-response estimate for that history.  It does show that
the next terminal-producer argument must use a scale-critical source or
response relation beyond the base-row composition; multiplying the two
available \(L^1_t\) bounds is not such a relation.

## 24. A coercive first-binomial rectangle normal form

The critical rectangle and the first-binomial source row can be combined
without separating the nonlinear term from the equation.  Retain the
notation of (N44)--(N47) and make the aliases

\[
 q:=Q(u),\qquad
 D:=d^2=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2,\qquad
 J:=P_Q=(q,\Lambda u)_2,\qquad
 Z:=\|\Lambda^{-1/2}(u_t-g)\|_2.
\]

Because \(u_t-g=Q-\nu Au\), the complete square in likewise forced row is

\[
 \boxed{
 Z^2=b_Q^2+\nu^2D-2\nu J,
 \qquad
 2\nu J=b_Q^2+\nu^2D-Z^2.}
 \tag{N89}
\]

The critical identity

\[
 H'+\nu D=J+(g,\Lambda u)_2
\]

is a coordinate relation in that same rectangle.  Multiply it by
\(2\nu^2\), substitute (N89), and add the pressure-complete first-binomial
identity (N46).  The \(\nu b_Q^2\) terms then recombine exactly, giving

\[
 \boxed{
 (\mathscr R_Q+2\nu^2H)'
 +\nu^3D
 +\nu Z^2
 =J_{\rm int}+J_g+2\nu^2(g,\Lambda u)_2.}
 \tag{N90}
\]

This is a coercive relationship of the whole equation:
\(\mathscr R_Q+2\nu^2H\ge2\nu^2H\), while both terms contributed by the
prescribed force are datum-paid.  In addition to (N47), duality and Young's
inequality give

\[
 \boxed{
 2\nu^2|(g,\Lambda u)_2|
 \le {\nu^3\over2}D+2\nu b_g^2.}
 \tag{N91}
\]

For \(0<T\le T_*\), define the upper intrinsic prefix

\[
 \mathfrak B_T
 :=\sup_{0<S<T}\int_0^S J_{\rm int}(t)\,dt.
\]

Integrating (N90), using (N91), and retaining every nonnegative left-hand
term yields, for every \(S<T\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathscr R_Q(S)+2\nu^2H(S)
 +{\nu^3\over2}\int_0^S D\,dt
 +\nu\int_0^S Z^2\,dt\\
 &\qquad\le
 \mathscr R_Q(0)+2\nu^2H(0)+\mathfrak B_T
 +\int_0^T|J_g|\,dt
 +2\nu\int_0^T b_g^2\,dt .
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N92}
\]

Consequently, if \(\mathfrak B_{T_*}<\infty\), the datum and the single
upper-prefix bound give

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0<S<T_*}H(S)<\infty,
 \qquad
 \int_0^{T_*}D\,dt<\infty,
 \qquad
 \int_0^{T_*}Z^2\,dt<\infty.}
 \tag{N93}
\]

The first-binomial identity (N46) now pays the nonlinear source directly,
and critical duality pays the current:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \nu\int_0^{T_*}b_Q^2\,dt
 &\le \mathscr R_Q(0)+\mathfrak B_{T_*}
       +\int_0^{T_*}|J_g|\,dt<\infty,\\
 \int_0^{T_*}[J(t)]_+\,dt
 &\le\int_0^{T_*}|J(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left(\int_0^{T_*}b_Q^2dt\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\int_0^{T_*}Ddt\right)^{1/2}<\infty.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{N94}
\]

Thus the upper intrinsic prefix in (N48) is itself a direct coercive entry
to the critical action and the cumulative current in (N65).  From (N93),
the spatial induction, temporal-pressure recurrence, finite compatible
rectangles, endpoint jet and same-force restart follow in their established
order.  Within this downstream normal form, a bound
\(\mathfrak B_{T_*}<\infty\) would independently recover that terminal
conclusion.  The direct datum-generated construction in Section 6 already
supplies (N11a), so (N89)--(N94) are not used as a gate for closure.
