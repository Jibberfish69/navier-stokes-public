## 2. The exact kinetic and fixed-label input

The sources use the unforced whole-space history

\[
u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,\qquad
\nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad K_0:=\|u_0\|_2^2.
\tag{BPA.1}
\]

Here \(K_0\) is the **squared** datum norm, as BRC.2 explicitly defines. Put \(S=(\nabla u+\nabla u^T)/2\), \(\omega=\nabla\times u\). BRC.3–5 gives

\[
\|u(t)\|_2^2+2\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla u\|_2^2=K_0,
\quad\|S\|_2^2=\tfrac12\|\nabla u\|_2^2,
\quad\|\omega\|_2^2=\|\nabla u\|_2^2.
\tag{BPA.2}
\]

The nonlinear transport and pressure pair to zero in the complete kinetic row; viscosity remains. Consequently \(\int_0^t\|S\|_2^2\le K_0/(4\nu)\), \(\int_0^t\|\nabla u\|_2^2\le K_0/(2\nu)\).

For the actual material map \(X\), \(F=D_aX\), \(\det F=1\), BRC.9 defines

\[
\mathfrak a(a,t)=\int_0^t|S(X(a,s),s)|\,ds.
\tag{BPA.3}
\]

Cauchy in time and the change of variables \(x=X(a,s)\) give **for any fixed finite-volume material set** \(A\)

\[
\int_A\mathfrak a(a,t)^2da
 \le t\int_0^t\!\int_{X(A,s)}|S|^2
 \le\frac{tK_0}{4\nu}.
\tag{BPA.4 / BRC.11}
\]

The same calculation applies on \(I=(s,t)\) with the strain action integrated from \(s\) and coefficient \(|I|\). It charges the actual fixed labels and does not select a record-carrying cohort.

The exact material-vector identity is \(d\log|Fv|/dt=e\cdot Se\), \(e=Fv/|Fv|\). Thus \(\log\sigma_{\max}(F)\le\mathfrak a\), \(-\log\sigma_{\min}(F)\le\mathfrak a\). For a fixed cohort of volume \(m\) with two transverse singular values at most \(\rho<1\), \(\det F=1\) forces \(\sigma_{\max}\ge\rho^{-2}\). BPA.4 therefore gives BRC.14–15 exactly:

\[
m\le\frac{tK_0}{16\nu[\log(1/\rho)]^2},
\qquad\rho\ge\exp[-\sqrt{tK_0/(16\nu m)}].
\tag{BPA.5}
\]

Physical displacement and its centered cohort version both have bound \(t^2K_0\), by time Cauchy and orthogonal subtraction of the cohort mean. Thus this total strain action is finite for Lebesgue-almost every material label in each finite-volume set. ## 3. Independent check of BRP.21: real Hardy pressure, with exact hypotheses

The analytic input is the standard **Coifman–Lions–Meyer–Semmes div–curl theorem** in its \(L^2\)-\(L^2\) form: if \(E,B\in L^2(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)\), \(\operatorname{div}E=0\) and \(\operatorname{curl}B=0\) in distributions, then

\[
E\cdot B\in\mathcal H^1(\mathbb R^3),\qquad
\|E\cdot B\|_{\mathcal H^1}\le C_{\rm dc}\|E\|_2\|B\|_2.
\tag{BPA.6}
\]

Here \(\mathcal H^1\) denotes the **real Hardy space**, not the Sobolev space \(H^1\). The second standard input is boundedness of Riesz transforms and their compositions on this Hardy space, together with its inclusion in \(L^1\). These two analytic theorems are used as standard inputs; this audit checks their precise application rather than re-proving them. No general strong \(L^1\to L^1\) Calderón–Zygmund assertion is used.

For the actual divergence-free \(u\in\dot H^1_\sigma\), take

\[
E_i=\partial_i u,\qquad B_i=\nabla u_i.
\tag{BPA.7}
\]

Then \(\operatorname{div}E_i=\partial_i\operatorname{div}u=0\), \(\operatorname{curl}B_i=0\), and both fields belong to \(L^2\). The exact unforced Poisson source is

\[
q=-\Delta p=\operatorname{tr}[(\nabla u)^2]
 =\sum_i E_i\cdot B_i
 =|S|^2-\tfrac12|\omega|^2.
\tag{BPA.8}
\]

Summing BPA.6 and applying finite-dimensional Cauchy gives

\[
\|q\|_{\mathcal H^1}
 \le C_{\rm dc}\sum_i\|\partial_i u\|_2\|\nabla u_i\|_2
 \le C_{\rm dc}\|\nabla u\|_2^2.
\tag{BPA.9}
\]

The pressure is the source's **canonical decaying/Riesz pressure**: \(p=(-\Delta)^{-1}q=R_iR_j(u_i u_j)\). With \(R_i\) multiplier \(i\xi_i/|\xi|\), its complete Hessian and traceless Hessian are

\[
(\nabla^2p)_{ij}=R_iR_jq,
\qquad
(\nabla^2p)^\circ_{ij}=(R_iR_j+\delta_{ij}/3)q.
\tag{BPA.10}
\]

The trace is \(-q\), since \(\sum_iR_i^2=-I\). Hardy-space boundedness therefore yields

\[
\|(\nabla^2p)^\circ\|_{L^1_x}\le C_H\|\nabla u\|_2^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\int_0^T\|(\nabla^2p)^\circ\|_1dt
 \le C_H K_0/(2\nu).}
\tag{BPA.11 / BRP.21}
\]

The full Hessian also has this bound with another universal constant. Smooth strict prefixes permit the identities directly; the resulting time budget is uniform as \(T<T_*\) increases. The spatial div–curl estimate needs the global divergence-free field and its canonical pressure. A bare Poisson equation with an unrestricted harmonic addition would not determine this Hessian. BRP's infinite-energy affine calibration is outside these hypotheses and is not used to test the datum budget.
