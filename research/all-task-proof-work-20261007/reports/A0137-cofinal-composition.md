## One history, full nonlinear rows, initial responses

Fix $\nu>0$, a finite horizon $0<T<\infty$, divergence-free $u_0\in H^\infty_\sigma$, and prescribed $f\in C^\infty([0,\infty);\mathcal S(\mathbb R^3))$. Let the actual classical history exist on $[0,T)$. Set $V_n=\partial_t^nu$, $F_n=\partial_t^nf$, $Q_n=\partial_t^np$, and $J_{n,\alpha}=\partial_x^\alpha V_n$. Write $\mathbb P$ for the Leray projection and $\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}$.

The source uses the complete binomial row

\[
B_n=\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a},\qquad
V_{n+1}+B_n+\nabla Q_n=\nu\Delta V_n+F_n.
\]

Its exact pressure and projected forms are

\[
\nabla Q_n=(I-\mathbb P)(F_n-B_n),\quad
V_{n+1}=\nu\Delta V_n-\mathbb PB_n+\mathbb PF_n,
\]

\[
Q_n=R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na(V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j
-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot F_n.
\]

Thus the force-pressure sign is negative in the scalar potential and positive in `(I-P)(F_n-B_n)`. Pressure has not been discarded by projecting: the scalar formula and gradient are reconstructed concurrently.

Spatial differentiation retains every factor ordering:

\[
\partial_tJ_{n,\alpha}
+\sum_{a=0}^n\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}
\binom na\binom\alpha\beta
(J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
+\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
=\nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha}+F_{n,\alpha}.
\]

At time zero these equations generate A_0=u0 and

\[
A_{n+1}=\nu\Delta A_n
-\mathbb P\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na(A_a\cdot\nabla)A_{n-a}
+\mathbb PF_n(0).
\]

Each finite A_n belongs to every finite H^m: the right side uses previously generated rows, two additional spatial derivatives, continuous Sobolev multiplication, and the prescribed force jet. This is a finite recursion; no convergence of an infinite Taylor series is used. Material responses can be recovered by the triangular conversion in framework (18)–(20). Material rows need not be solenoidal, so pressure cancellation is applied to Eulerian mixed jets, whose divergence vanishes.

## Mixed Gram and scalar height relationships

For a finite mixed index set split the full nonlinear sum as `(u·grad)J_A+T̃_A`, with T̃_A retaining every other mixed term. Define

\[
K_{AB}=\langle J_A,J_B\rangle,
D_{AB}=\langle\nabla J_A,\nabla J_B\rangle,
\]

\[
\mathcal R_{AB}=\langle\widetilde T_A,J_B\rangle
+\langle J_A,\widetilde T_B\rangle,
\quad
\mathcal W_{AB}=\langle\mathbb PF_A,J_B\rangle
+\langle J_A,\mathbb PF_B\rangle.
\]

Differentiating both factors, the two pressure pairings vanish because both test jets are solenoidal. The two base transport terms sum to the integral of u·grad(J_A·J_B), which is zero. The two Laplacian pairings integrate to -2nu D_AB. This proves the source's exact finite matrix identity

\[
K'+2\nu D=-\mathcal R+\mathcal W.
\tag{1}
\]

K and D are Gram matrices: z^T K z=||sum z_A J_A||_2^2 and z^T D z=||grad sum z_A J_A||_2^2. For the source Gram F^-_(AB)=<Lambda^-1 PF_A,Lambda^-1 PF_B>,

\[
z^T\mathcal Wz
=2\langle\Lambda^{-1}\sum_Az_A\mathbb PF_A,
\Lambda\sum_Az_AJ_A\rangle
\le\epsilon z^TDz+\epsilon^{-1}z^TF^-z.
\]

Hence W<=epsilon D+epsilon^-1 F^- in Loewner order. With epsilon=nu, integrating (1) gives

\[
K(t)+\nu\int_0^tD+\int_0^t\mathcal R
\preceq K(0)+\nu^{-1}\int_0^tF^-.
\tag{2}
\]

These calculations hold on strict classical [0,S], S<T. Constants and the finite force budgets can be chosen on the prescribed whole force horizon. The complete intrinsic matrix R remains in (1)–(2). No entrywise sign of the gradient pairing or of R is needed or asserted: positive semidefiniteness is the quadratic-form statement for each finite matrix.

The fractional-height temporal Gram is a different, related pairing. Set

\[
E_{r,s}=\tfrac12\|\Lambda^sV_r\|_2^2,
\quad C_{a,b;s}=\langle\Lambda^sV_a,\Lambda^sV_b\rangle,
\]

\[
\mathcal P_{r,s}=-\langle\Lambda^sV_r,\Lambda^s(B_r+\nabla Q_r)\rangle,
\quad\mathcal W_{r,s}=\langle\Lambda^sV_r,\Lambda^sF_r\rangle,
\quad\mathcal G=\mathcal P+\mathcal W.
\]

Pressure again pairs to zero because Lambda commutes with divergence. At fractional height the full B terms are retained; the unweighted base-transport cancellation above must not be applied after commuting Lambda through transport. Direct substitution yields

\[
E'_{r,s}=-2\nu E_{r,s+1}+\mathcal G_{r,s},
\tag{3}
\]

\[
\partial_tC_{a,b;s}=-2\nu C_{a,b;s+1}
-\langle\Lambda^sB_a,\Lambda^sV_b\rangle
-\langle\Lambda^sV_a,\Lambda^sB_b\rangle
+\langle\Lambda^sF_a,\Lambda^sV_b\rangle
+\langle\Lambda^sV_a,\Lambda^sF_b\rangle.
\tag{4}
\]

Leibniz differentiating E gives precisely the anti-diagonal contraction

\[
\partial_t^kE_{r,s}
=\tfrac12\sum_{a=0}^k\binom ka C_{r+a,r+k-a;s}.
\tag{5}
\]

The finite C matrix is positive semidefinite, but (5) is a signed contraction, rather than a diagonal norm. Differentiating (3) and substituting it at each next height proves by finite induction

\[
\partial_t^kE_{r,s}
=(-2\nu)^kE_{r,s+k}
+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-2\nu)^j
\partial_t^{k-1-j}\mathcal G_{r,s+j}.
\tag{6}
\]

The work derivatives remain complete. For example

\[
\partial_t^q\mathcal G_{r,s}
=\sum_{a=0}^q\binom qa
\langle\Lambda^sV_{r+a},
\Lambda^s(F_{r+q-a}-B_{r+q-a}-\nabla Q_{r+q-a})\rangle.
\]

Here partial_t^q B_r=B_(r+q): the coefficient of `(V_c·grad)V_(r+q-c)` is sum_a binom(r,a)binom(q,c-a)=binom(r+q,c). For r=0 the nonlinear work expansion is the full cubic sum over a+b+c=q with coefficient q!/(a!b!c!), and the force work is sum_a binom(q,a)<Lambda^s F_a,Lambda^s V_(q-a)>.

Consequently the source's completed critical coordinate is exactly

\[
\mathcal C^f_{r,k}
=\frac{\partial_t^kE_{r,1/2}
-\sum_{j<k}(-2\nu)^j\partial_t^{k-1-j}\mathcal G_{r,j+1/2}}
{(-2\nu)^k}
=E_{r,k+1/2}\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]

This is the bridge between temporal Gram contractions and spatial quadratic-form domains. Its positivity belongs to the completed expression including every nonlinear and force work term. It does not require derivatives of the critical height to have a prescribed sign.

Repeated integration of (3) also retains all endpoints. With phi in C^k([0,S]), a=2nu,

\[
a^k\int_0^S\phi E_{r,s+k}
=\int_0^S\phi^{(k)}E_{r,s}
+\sum_{j<k}a^j\int_0^S\phi^{(k-1-j)}\mathcal G_{r,s+j}
-\sum_{j<k}a^j[\phi^{(k-1-j)}E_{r,s+j}]_0^S.

The signs follow from a int(phi E_(j+1))=int(phi' E_j)+int(phi G_j)-[phi E_j]. Equation (8) has no implicit removal of the upper endpoint.
