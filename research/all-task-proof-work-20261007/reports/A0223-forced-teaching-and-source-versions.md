## 1. What the argument constructs

The datum is the triple

\[
u_0\in H^\infty_\sigma(\mathbb R^3),\qquad \nu>0,\qquad
f\in C^\infty([0,\infty);\mathcal S(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)).
\tag{FT1}
\]

The force is prescribed for all absolute times, with no amplitude restriction. Every finite time interval has finite seminorms of every selected time and space derivative of this force. No bound uniform in the order of differentiation is assumed.

The evolving object is one velocity and its normalized pressure, together with all their derivatives. A maximal history \((T_*,u,p)\) satisfies the full equation and the initial datum; on every strict interval \([0,T]\), \(T<T_*\), all its temporal rows are continuous in every finite Sobolev space. Maximality concerns continuation of this same history generated by the same triple (FT1).

Pressure is fixed by the whole-space \(H^0\) normalization:

\[
p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j)-(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}f,\qquad
u_t=\nu\Delta u-\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)+g,\quad g=\mathbb P f.
\tag{FT2}
\]

In particular,
\[
(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p
=\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)+(I-\mathbb P)f.
\]
The gradient part of the prescribed force is in pressure; its solenoidal part is in the velocity equation. The pressure normalization is decisive: two \(L^2\) pressures with the same Poisson equation differ by an \(L^2\) harmonic function, whose Fourier transform is supported at the origin and therefore vanishes.

The force potential belongs to every selected inhomogeneous \(H^m\). For a selected derivative \(h\in L^1\cap H^m\), the multiplier of \((-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}h\) has size at most \(|\xi|^{-1}\). At low frequency use \(|\widehat h|\le (2\pi)^{-3/2}\|h\|_1\) and
\(\int_{|\xi|<1}|\xi|^{-2}\,d\xi=4\pi\); at high frequency its negative order is bounded by one. This is the paper's explicit payment of the pressure-source coordinate.

Sources: [analytic definitions and multipliers](../COVERAGE.md#A0204), especially LF256–323; [pressure and history](../COVERAGE.md#A0207), LF4–88. PDF pages 19–20.

## 2. The full mixed graph and its initial rows

Set
\[
V_n=\partial_t^n u,\quad Q_n=\partial_t^n p,\quad F_n=\partial_t^n f,\quad
J_{n,\alpha}=\partial_x^\alpha V_n,\quad
\Pi_{n,\alpha}=\partial_x^\alpha Q_n,\quad F_{n,\alpha}=\partial_x^\alpha F_n.
\]
Every mixed velocity row is divergence-free. The temporal recurrences are
\[
Q_n=R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na V_{a,i}V_{n-a,j}
-(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}F_n,
\]
\[
V_{n+1}=\nu\Delta V_n-\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
-\nabla Q_n+F_n.
\tag{FT3}
\]
The complete spatial allocation is
\[
\Pi_{n,\alpha}
=R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^n\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}
\binom na\binom\alpha\beta
J_{a,\beta,i}J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta,j}
-\partial_x^\alpha(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}F_n,
\]
\[
J_{n+1,\alpha}
+\sum_{a=0}^n\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}\binom na\binom\alpha\beta
(J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
+\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
=\nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha}+F_{n,\alpha}.
\tag{FT4}
\]

These identities are differentiated equations of one history, with both ordered nonlinear parents and the force derivative in every row. They do not define independent solutions for separate derivative indices.

The initial jet is determined before any endpoint is considered. Put \(A_n=V_n(0)\), \(P_n^0=Q_n(0)\), and \(F_n^0=F_n(0)\). Starting with \(A_0=u_0\), use the pressure and velocity recurrences (FT3) at time zero. Induction in \(n\), the Sobolev product estimate, and the normalized force-potential estimate give \(A_n,P_n^0\in H^\infty\); spatial differentiation supplies all mixed initial coordinates. This is a finite-order triangular computation. It imposes no convergence of an infinite Taylor series.

For a selected finite collection of rows, the paper carries the adjacent temporal rows, viscous derivatives, every nonlinear allocation, pressure coordinate and prescribed source coordinate needed by those selected equations. The buffer is a dependency list for the selected record; the stated object is not a collection of autonomous truncated Navier–Stokes solutions.

Sources: [mixed graph](../COVERAGE.md#A0207), LF91–175; [initial jet](../COVERAGE.md#A0207), LF214–266; finite buffer LF540–572. PDF pages 20–21, 24–25.

## 3. How the local history is genuinely constructed

The analytic framework first gives a shifted forced mild class. At an absolute start time \(s\), datum \(a\), and endpoint \(b\), it requires
\[
w(t)=S_{t-s}a+\int_s^t S_{t-r}g(r)\,dr
-\int_s^t S_{t-r}\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(w\otimes w)(r)\,dr,
\quad S_t=e^{\nu t\Delta},
\tag{FT5}
\]
and its pressure is the expression (FT2) evaluated using \(w(t)\) and \(f(t)\). The force argument remains \(r\), the absolute time.

Write \(A=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}\), \(C_q=\sqrt3\,C_3^{\rm alg}\), and
\[
L_\nu=4(1+\nu+\nu^{-1})e^{(\nu+\nu^{-1})/2},\qquad
c_\nu=(8C_qL_\nu^2)^{-2},\quad
\Gamma_s=\|g\|_{L^2(s,s+1;H^2)}.
\]
The local time is
\[
\delta_{\nu,f}(s,a)
=\min\{1,c_\nu(1+\|a\|_{H^3}+\Gamma_s)^{-2}\}.
\tag{FT6}
\]
The fixed-point space has norm from
\(C H^3\), \(L^2H^4\), and \(w_t\in L^2H^2\).
The linear estimate is
\(\|z\|_X\le L_\nu(\|a\|_{H^3}+\|F\|_{L^2H^2})\);
the quadratic estimate is
\(\|\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes z)\|_{H^2}
\le C_q\|v\|_{H^3}\|z\|_{H^3}\).
For \(B_0=\|a\|_{H^3}+\Gamma_s\), the ball radius is \(2L_\nu B_0\).
The chosen time makes the difference-map factor at most
\(B_0/[2(1+B_0)]\le1/2\), and the map sends this ball to itself. If \(B_0=0\), the zero trajectory supplies the zero-radius case.

Higher Sobolev persistence is proved on this same local time. The paper derives the integer transport commutator by expanding the inhomogeneous Sobolev pairing into weighted ordinary derivatives, cancelling the divergence-free leading transport term, and estimating every remaining Leibniz term. Its explicit constant is
\[
K_m=\sum_{\ell=1}^m3^\ell\binom m\ell
\sum_{k=1}^\ell\binom\ell k\Gamma_{\ell,k},\quad
\Gamma_{\ell,k}=(2\ell-4+\sqrt3)^{(k-1)(\ell-k)}
\]
for \(\ell\ge2\), with \(\Gamma_{1,1}=1\).
The two complementary interpolation exponents for the nonlinear parents have reciprocal sum \(1/2\). Thus the full commutator is bounded by
\(K_m\|\nabla w\|_\infty\|w\|_{H^m}^2\).
Fourier truncation, this bound, the common \(H^3\) ball bound, and the admitted force norms give higher-order energy bounds independent of the truncation. The truncated fixed points converge to the original fixed point in its contraction norm, identifying the higher-order weak limits with that same solution. Adjacent-row trace continuity and (FT3) then give all temporal and pressure rows.

The uniqueness proof compares trajectories with the same force, partitions a finite overlap into short intervals, and uses (FT5). Local solutions therefore glue into the maximal history appearing in §1. This construction supplies local existence and strict-subinterval smoothness before the whole-terminal theorem is used.

Sources: [commutator](../COVERAGE.md#A0204), LF388–600; [shifted local class and fixed point](../COVERAGE.md#A0204), LF608–1071. PDF Theorem 3.8 begins on page 14.

## 4. The finite whole-terminal construction used by the published proof

Theorem 4.10 assumes (FT1), its maximal history, and a proposed finite \(T_*\). Its time domain is the entire interval \(I=(0,T_*)\). For every finite pair \(N,M\), it asserts
\[
\mathfrak P^f_{N,M}
=\sum_{n=0}^N\left(
\|V_n\|_{L^2(I;H^{M+1})}^2+
\|V_{n+1}\|_{L^2(I;H^{M-1})}^2\right)<\infty.
\tag{FT7}
\]
It also asserts a complete compatible velocity–pressure–force record: every selected \(J\) has its finite \(L^2\) norm, every selected pressure coordinate its finite \(L^1\) norm, and every prescribed source coordinate its continuous finite Sobolev norm.

The published proof begins by citing **[3, §6, (N11a)–(N11d)]** as the supplier of (FT7). It then identifies the initial jet, complete finite graph and pressure projection used by that supplier. It does not begin by proving a bound for a late selected high-frequency current.

The new forced faces have explicit finite payments. The 81-page source uses homogeneous spatial energy:
\[
2|\langle F_{n,\alpha},J_{n,\alpha}\rangle_{\dot H^M}|
\le \nu\|J_{n,\alpha}\|_{\dot H^{M+1}}^2
+\nu^{-1}\|F_{n,\alpha}\|_{\dot H^{M-1}}^2.
\tag{FT8}
\]
This follows from pairing Fourier weights \(|\xi|^{M-1}\) and \(|\xi|^{M+1}\), followed by Young's inequality. At \(M=0\), the prescribed \(\dot H^{-1}\) force norm is finite by the same radial low-frequency integral \(4\pi\). The text separately retains the velocity's low-frequency face inside the construction. The pressure-source budget is
\[
\|\partial_x^\alpha(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}F_n\|_{L^1(I;H^M)}
+\|\nabla\partial_x^\alpha(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}F_n\|_{L^1(I;H^{M-1})}<\infty.
\tag{FT9}
\]
These statements admit arbitrary prescribed force amplitude. They pay the affine source faces at each selected finite order. The proof explicitly says that this payment alone does not replace the coupled block construction supplying (FT7).

Pressure membership follows from (FT7), using the full product row and time Cauchy–Schwarz. For example,
\[
\|Q_n\|_{L^1H^M}+\|\nabla Q_n\|_{L^1H^{M-1}}
\le C_M^{\rm p}\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na
\|V_a\|_{L^2H^{M+1}}\|V_{n-a}\|_{L^2H^{M+1}}
+\mathfrak F^{\rm p}_{n,0,M}.
\tag{FT10}
\]
For mixed pressure the sum includes every \(\beta\le\alpha\) with both binomial coefficients. The involution
\((a,\beta)\mapsto(n-a,\alpha-\beta)\) preserves the weights, whose total is \(2^{n+|\alpha|}\). This proves the compressed estimate without dropping an ordered parent.

The records restrict projectively because they are coordinates of the same history and the same normalized pressure. Increasing \(N\), \(M\), or the selected spatial derivatives changes the retained coordinates, not their values.

Sources: [Theorem 4.10 and supplier](../COVERAGE.md#A0207), LF487–608; pressure estimates LF268–400; [bibliographic reference 3](../COVERAGE.md#A0219). PDF pages 24–25, equation 60.

## 5. The two exhaustions preserve the same object

The mixed exhaustion chooses a sufficiently large finite pair in (FT7) for each fixed temporal depth \(r\) and spatial order \(m\). Its output is
\[
u\in\bigcap_{r,m\ge0}H^r(I;H^m_\sigma).
\tag{FT11}
\]
Here \(H^r\) in time contains the actual weak derivatives \(V_0,\ldots,V_r\). Spatial differentiation selects higher spatial columns. Every individual coordinate norm is finite on \(I\); no single constant uniform over all \(r,m\) is required. Conversely, restricting the nonnegative integrals to \(0<T<T_*\) gives bounds uniform over strict cutoffs for each fixed \(r,m\), and monotone convergence recovers the whole-\(I\) norm.

The paper also gives a complete spatial exhaustion through recovered heights. Set
\[
E_{r,s}=\tfrac12\|\Lambda^sV_r\|_2^2,\quad
B_r=\sum_{a=0}^r\binom ra(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{r-a},
\]
\[
P_{r,s}=-\operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda^sV_r,\Lambda^s(B_r+\nabla Q_r)\rangle,\quad
W_{r,s}=\operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda^sV_r,\Lambda^sF_r\rangle.
\]
The complete energy law is
\[
E_{r,s}'=P_{r,s}+W_{r,s}-2\nu E_{r,s+1}.
\]
Iterating gives the exact identity
\[
\partial_t^kE_{r,s}
=(-2\nu)^kE_{r,s+k}
+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-2\nu)^j
\partial_t^{k-1-j}(P_{r,s+j}+W_{r,s+j}).
\tag{FT12}
\]
The recovered nonnegative height at the critical starting spatial index is therefore
\[
R_k=
\frac{\partial_t^kE_{0,1/2}
-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-2\nu)^j
\partial_t^{k-1-j}(P_{0,j+1/2}+W_{0,j+1/2})}{(-2\nu)^k}
=\tfrac12\|\Lambda^{k+1/2}u\|_2^2.
\tag{FT13}
\]
All nonlinear, pressure and force corrections remain in its numerator. Temporal derivatives of a positive quadratic norm can be indefinite; the expression corrected in (FT13) is the nonnegative spatial height.

Its \(L^1(I)\) payment follows from the already supplied rectangle:
\(2\int_I R_k\le\mathfrak R^*_{0,k}\).
The low spatial frequencies are paid by the forced kinetic estimate. With
\[
K_f(T)=\|u_0\|_2+\int_0^T\|g(t)\|_2\,dt,
\]
the kinetic equation gives
\[
\sup_{0<t<T}\|u(t)\|_2\le K_f(T),\qquad
\int_0^T\|\nabla u\|_2^2\,dt\le K_f(T)^2/(2\nu).
\tag{FT14}
\]
The proof uses square-root regularization of the kinetic norm, retaining \(\int f\cdot u=\int g\cdot u\). The identity
\(\int K_f\|g\|_2=\tfrac12(K_f(T)^2-\|u_0\|_2^2)\)
pays its work contribution.

Splitting at \(|\xi|=1\), the recovered height supplies the high frequencies and (FT14) supplies the low frequencies:
\[
\|u\|_{L^2(I;H^m)}^2
\le2^m\left(T_*K_f(T_*)^2+2\int_I R_m\right)<\infty.
\tag{FT15}
\]
Thus the spatial intersection is the zeroth temporal projection of the same record. Its reconstruction of the mixed intersection uses the full equation:
\[
\|u_t\|_{L^1H^m}
\le\nu\sqrt{T_*}\|u\|_{L^2H^{m+2}}
+C_m\|u\|_{L^2H^{m+2}}^2+\|g\|_{L^1H^m}.
\]
This gives \(u\in W^{1,1}(I;H^m)\) and a continuous spatial curve. Then (FT3), with all its force and pressure rows, recursively controls each \(V_n\) in \(C H^m\). For example the next velocity bound contains
\(\nu M_{n,m+2}+C_m\sum_a\binom naM_{a,m+2}M_{n-a,m+2}
+\sup_t\|\mathbb PF_n(t)\|_{H^m}\).
It is a transfer from complete spatial membership to complete mixed membership.

Sources: [mixed exhaustion and rectangle norms](../COVERAGE.md#A0207), LF616–733; [kinetic and heights](../COVERAGE.md#A0208), LF13–186, 441–569. PDF pages 25–33.

## 6. What nonlinear control the rectangles yield

The paper retains the full source in the positive-square identity. For a temporal row, projection gives
\[
\Lambda^sV_{n+1}+\nu\Lambda^{s+2}V_n
=\Lambda^s\mathbb P(F_n-B_n).
\]
The full unprojected identity is
\[
\|\Lambda^sV_{n+1}\|_2^2+\nu^2\|\Lambda^{s+2}V_n\|_2^2
+\|\Lambda^s\nabla Q_n\|_2^2
+\nu\,\partial_t\|\Lambda^{s+1}V_n\|_2^2
=\|\Lambda^s(F_n-B_n)\|_2^2.
\tag{FT16}
\]
The orthogonality is between divergence-free velocity/viscous rows and the pressure-gradient row. Integration retains the terminal norm, its initial value and all three action terms.

Given a sufficiently high rectangle, adjacent-row continuity supplies the \(L^\infty\) factor in each product:
\[
\|B_n\|_{L^2\dot H^s}
\le C_s\sum_a\binom na
\|V_a\|_{L^\infty H^{s+2}}\|V_{n-a}\|_{L^2H^{s+2}}.
\tag{FT17}
\]
For the finite sum of rows, the displayed bound has the form
\(\sum_n\|B_n\|_{L^2H^{M-1}}^2
\le C_{N,M}(\mathfrak I_{N,M}+\mathfrak R^*_{N,M})\mathfrak R^*_{N,M}\),
where \(\mathfrak I_{N,M}\) is the finite initial-jet sum.
The full square is bounded using
\(\|F_n-B_n\|^2\le2\|F_n\|^2+2\|B_n\|^2\).
This is substantive nonlinear control produced from the whole-terminal rectangles. It is not a replacement upstream premise for the datum theorem.

Sources: [positive squares and nonlinear faces](../COVERAGE.md#A0208), LF188–381. PDF pages 29–31.

## 7. The common endpoint and restart

The endpoint proof uses both adjacent coordinates from (FT7):
\[
V_n\in L^2(I;H^{m+1}),\qquad
\partial_tV_n=V_{n+1}\in L^2(I;H^{m-1}).
\]
Their exact pairing is
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\|V_n\|_{H^m}^2
=2\operatorname{Re}\langle A^{m-1}V_{n+1},A^{m+1}V_n\rangle.
\]
Fourier truncation and Cauchy–Schwarz give a continuous representative on the full closed interval and
\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}\|V_n(t)\|_{H^m}^2
\le (1+T_*^{-1})\mathfrak R^*_{N,m}\quad(n\le N).
\tag{FT18}
\]
Representatives at different spatial orders agree on the same preterminal distribution; continuity of the Sobolev embeddings makes their endpoint values agree. Thus \(V_n^*\in H^\infty\) is one endpoint row, and
\(J_{n,\alpha}^*=\partial_x^\alpha V_n^*\).

The Banach-valued fundamental identity
\(V_n(t)-V_n(s)=\int_s^tV_{n+1}(r)\,dr\)
identifies the temporal derivatives, rather than merely constructing separate limits. Taking the endpoint and using the bound for the next row gives
\[
\|V_n(t)-V_n^*\|_{H^m}
\le(T_*-t)(1+T_*^{-1})^{1/2}
\bigl(\mathfrak R^*_{n+1,m}\bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
All pressure endpoints then follow from their actual product and prescribed force-potential formula:
\[
Q_n^*=R_iR_j\sum_a\binom naV_{a,i}^*V_{n-a,j}^*
-(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}F_n(T_*).
\tag{FT19}
\]
Passing to the limit in (FT3) retains viscosity, both nonlinear parents, pressure, and \(F_n(T_*)\). Consequently the root \(u_*=V_0^*\) determines every other endpoint row by the same triangular initial-jet recurrence.

Apply the actual local theorem at absolute start time \(s=T_*\), with datum \(u_*\). It uses the existing prescribed \(f(t)\). In translated restart coordinates \(r=t-T_*\), the force is \(f(T_*+r)\). The right-hand jet has the same root and the same recurrence (FT19), including all force derivatives at \(T_*\); induction gives equality of every left and right jet. The velocity and pressure glue smoothly in every finite Sobolev order. They form a continuation of the same maximal history, contradicting finite maximality.

Thus the endpoint/restart composition has the precise input (FT7), rather than an unforced restart or an unrelated right-hand branch.

## 8. What the matrix forms mean

The matrix section places the same equations in finite mixed-index arrays. For \(\kappa=(n,\alpha)\), \(\lambda=(a,\beta)\le\kappa\), the lower triangular coefficient is
\[
\mathcal L(J)_{\kappa\lambda}
=\binom\kappa\lambda\,\mathbb P[(J_{\kappa-\lambda}\cdot\nabla)\,\cdot\,].
\]
After summing and changing \(\lambda\leftrightarrow\kappa-\lambda\), its action is the exact projected mixed nonlinear row (FT4). It is a matrix depending on the jet \(J\).

The factorial diagonal \(D_{\kappa\kappa}=n!\alpha!\) removes the binomial weights:
\(D^{-1}\mathcal L(D\widehat J)D=\widehat{\mathcal L}(\widehat J)\).
The normalized temporal derivative retains the factor \(n+1\):
\[
(n+1)\widehat J_{n+1,\alpha}
+\sum_{\lambda\le\kappa}
(\widehat J_\lambda\cdot\nabla)\widehat J_{\kappa-\lambda}
+\nabla\widehat\Pi_\kappa
=\nu\Delta\widehat J_\kappa+\widehat F_\kappa.
\tag{FT20}
\]
The pressure has the normalized quadratic sum and the normalized prescribed force potential. For each selected row the number of parent slots is finite. Factorial normalization is an exact finite algebraic change of coordinates; no analytic generating-series convergence is used.

The object matrix has velocity, adjacent velocity, pressure and force columns. The norm matrix keeps their respective velocity \(L^2\), pressure \(L^1\) and source norms. The velocity part of its Frobenius norm equals the selected rectangle's quadratic norm. Entrywise endpoint estimates are consequences of the existing finite coordinate norms and (FT18).

Source: [matrix forms](../COVERAGE.md#A0209), LF12–340. PDF pages 42–45.
