## 2. The ontology: what exists, what is a coordinate, and what is a measurement

The physical state is the velocity field \(u(t,x)\) of one incompressible fluid in \(\mathbb R^3\), together with its pressure \(p(t,x)\). The viscosity \(\nu>0\) is fixed. In the forced problem the entire prescribed field \(f(t,x)\) is also fixed. “One history” means that every later object is built from the same solution generated by these data. Increasing the number of recorded derivatives does not create another fluid.

The actual equation is
\[
u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u+f,\qquad
\nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad u(0)=u_0.                       \tag{T1}
\]
The unforced equation sets \(f=0\). The datum class is
\[
H^\infty_\sigma=\bigcap_{m\ge0}H^m_\sigma.
\]
It is a Sobolev intersection, with a separate finite norm at each order. It neither says that the solution is analytic nor supplies one common bound over all orders. Schwartz solenoidal data are a subclass.

The [analytic definitions](../SOURCES.md#S136) use the unitary Fourier transform. \(A=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}\) measures inhomogeneous \(H^m\); \(\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\) measures homogeneous height. The former includes low frequencies and kinetic \(L^2\); the latter calibrates scale.

The following objects must remain distinct:

| Object | Definition and role |
|---|---|
| Maximal classical history | \((T_*,u,p)\), smooth with every finite Sobolev derivative on each closed strict interval \([0,S]\), \(S<T_*\). Maximality forbids an extension agreeing with it before \(T_*\). |
| Temporal rows | \(V_n=\partial_t^nu\), \(Q_n=\partial_t^np\). These are fields, not energies. |
| Mixed jet | \(J_{n,\alpha}=\partial_x^\alpha V_n\), \(\Pi_{n,\alpha}=\partial_x^\alpha Q_n\); forced rows also retain \(F_{n,\alpha}=\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^nf\). |
| Initial jet | The values of these actual rows at \(t=0\), recursively fixed by the datum and equation. |
| Finite rectangle or block | A finite pressure-complete record of selected rows, adjacent time rows, and the finite dependency cover needed to write their complete equations. |
| Rectangle norm | \(\mathfrak R_{N,M}\), a scalar sum of squared velocity coordinate norms. It is not the record itself. |
| Critical height | \(H=E_{0,1/2}=\frac12\|\Lambda^{1/2}u\|_2^2\), a scalar quadratic measurement of the velocity. |
| Completed height | A combination of derivatives of \(H\) and complete production/work terms that equals a higher spatial energy. |
| All-orders intersection | Membership of the one history in every finite \(H^r_tH^m_x\); equivalently, a compatible record containing every mixed row with the stated domains. |
| Endpoint jet | Limits of those same fields at \(T_*\), compatible across all finite Sobolev orders. |

The [maximal-history definition](../SOURCES.md#S139) has strict-cutoff quantifiers. Whole-terminal membership on \(I=(0,T_*)\), when \(T_*<\infty\), is a further theorem claim. Compatibility identifies overlapping coordinates; membership specifies their function spaces. Those are different operations.

Incompressibility has several exact roles. It converts transport into tensor divergence, determines pressure elliptically, cancels the global pressure pairing against solenoidal velocity rows, cancels the undifferentiated transport energy term, and preserves material volume. It does not mean that all derivative norms have the same size.

The recovered cited-spine witnesses use “column” for four coordinate descriptions: the all-spatial half-integer column, the temporal scalar critical-height column together with its complete velocity–pressure–production graph, the \(H^m_x\)-valued all-temporal velocity column, and the complete mixed velocity–pressure record. Each has its own Sobolev embedding implication. Current TIR.2 explicitly says its two-way arrows are simultaneous readouts of one compatible solution, and that the scalar height/VPI readout is not an injective reconstruction of velocity. Reversibility means the complete equation relates these same-history descriptions; a scalar energy sequence alone does not specify the vector field. The [unforced teaching note](A0288-unforced-published-argument-and-source-comparison.md) gives the exact witnesses, four columns and tags.

Here that \(Q\) belongs to the earlier ontology, not the pressure row \(Q_n\). Its classification is not installed as the status or premise of the later TIR argument. ## 3. Pressure and every nonlinear parent stay in the construction

Let \(\mathbb P\) be the Leray projection with symbol \(I-\xi\otimes\xi/|\xi|^2\), and \(R_j\) the Riesz transform with symbol \(i\xi_j/|\xi|\). Taking divergence of (T1) gives
\[
-\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j)-\nabla\cdot f,
\quad
p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j)-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot f.          \tag{T2}
\]
The [pressure normalization proof](../SOURCES.md#S117) fixes \(p\) in \(L^2\): an \(L^2\) harmonic difference has Fourier support only at zero and therefore vanishes. Higher Sobolev regularity supplies the continuous decaying representative. The projected equation is
\[
u_t-\nu\Delta u+\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=g,
\qquad g=\mathbb Pf.
\]
The complementary projection is retained through
\[
\nabla p=(I-\mathbb P)\bigl[f-(u\cdot\nabla)u\bigr].
\]
Thus projection is a decomposition of the complete equation, not an omission of pressure.

For \(F_n=\partial_t^nf\), put
\[
B_n=\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}.
\]
The complete temporal row and pressure row are
\[
V_{n+1}+B_n+\nabla Q_n=\nu\Delta V_n+F_n,
\quad
Q_n=R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na V_{a,i}V_{n-a,j}
      -(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot F_n.                 \tag{T3}
\]
Spatial differentiation produces
\[
J_{n+1,\alpha}
+\sum_{a=0}^n\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}
 \binom na\binom\alpha\beta
 (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
+\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
=\nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha}+F_{n,\alpha}.                  \tag{T4}
\]
Every binomial and multi-index allocation comes from the ordinary product rule. Both ordered velocity factors remain. The pressure coordinate has the same full product sum and the corresponding force potential. These formulas are [the exact mixed recurrences](../SOURCES.md#S117).

At time zero, \(A_0=u_0\), and
\[
P_n=R_iR_j\sum_a\binom na(A_a)_i(A_{n-a})_j
       -(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot F_n(0),
\quad
A_{n+1}=\nu\Delta A_n-\sum_a\binom na(A_a\cdot\nabla)A_{n-a}
       -\nabla P_n+F_n(0).                            \tag{T5}
\]
Finite Sobolev multiplication and multiplier bounds put each fixed initial row in every finite spatial order. This identifies the initial face of every larger finite record.

The prescribed force class is \(C^\infty([0,\infty);\mathcal S)\). On every fixed compact time interval all required force norms are finite. Its pressure potential has a genuine low-frequency estimate:
\[
\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot h\|_{H^m}
\le C_m(\|h\|_1+\|h\|_{H^m}).
\]
Inside \(|\xi|\le1\), use the \(L^1\) Fourier bound and integrability of \(|\xi|^{-2}\) in dimension three; outside, the order \(-1\) multiplier is bounded. This is the [force-potential proof](../SOURCES.md#S114).

## 4. The initial local realization and its uniqueness

The [local restart theorem](../SOURCES.md#S114) constructs the full nonlinear fluid, not independent jet entries. At absolute time \(s\), let
\[
\Gamma_s=\|g\|_{L^2(s,s+1;H^2)},\quad
\delta_{\nu,f}(s,a)=
\min\{1,c_\nu(1+\|a\|_{H^3}+\Gamma_s)^{-2}\},
\]
where
\[
L_\nu=4(1+\nu+\nu^{-1})e^{(\nu+\nu^{-1})/2},
\qquad c_\nu=(8\sqrt3 C_3^{\rm alg}L_\nu^2)^{-2}.
\]
On \(0<\delta\le\delta_{\nu,f}(s,a)\), translate time while retaining \(f(s+r)\). The fixed-point map is
\[
\Phi(v)(r)=e^{\nu r\Delta}a+
\int_0^r e^{\nu(r-\rho)\Delta}g(s+\rho)\,d\rho
-\int_0^r e^{\nu(r-\rho)\Delta}
       \mathbb P\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)(\rho)\,d\rho.  \tag{T6}
\]
Its Banach space is
\[
X_\delta=CH^3_\sigma\cap L^2H^4_\sigma,\qquad v_r\in L^2H^2_\sigma,
\]
with the sum of those three norms. The linear heat estimate is bounded by \(L_\nu\) times the initial \(H^3\) norm plus the \(L^2H^2\) source norm. The full quadratic estimate is
\[
\|\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes z)\|_{H^2}
\le\sqrt3 C_3^{\rm alg}\|v\|_{H^3}\|z\|_{H^3}.
\]
The radius \(2L_\nu(\|a\|_{H^3}+\Gamma_s)\) and displayed \(\delta\) give a self-map and contraction factor at most \(1/2\).

Fourier truncations are used to prove higher persistence on this same interval. Their \(H^3\) norms are uniformly bounded by that radius. The integer transport commutator bounds each higher \(H^m\) energy using \(\|\nabla v\|_\infty\), which the fixed \(H^3\) bound controls. The forced estimate also retains the prescribed \(\|g\|_{L^2H^m}^2\) term; Gronwall gives the displayed fixed-order bound with that term and the Lipschitz coefficient. Weak higher-order limits are identified with the original fixed point by convergence in \(X_\delta\). The full recurrence then identifies all \(W_n\) with actual \(\partial_t^nv\), and all pressure rows with actual pressure derivatives. The interval is common to every finite order; the higher constants may depend on that order.

Restriction of the Duhamel equation preserves the same absolute-time source. On a finite interval, two members of the same shifted mild class have a difference estimate on short pieces; the common force cancels, and a finite partition proves equality. Their normalized pressures agree. Compatible local solutions therefore form one maximal history.

This is the local entry and the eventual restart operation. The papers distinguish it from their whole-terminal finite-block theorem.

## 5. The native whole-terminal theorem and its two readings

For proposed finite \(T_*\), set \(I=(0,T_*)\). The finite record includes \(V_0,\ldots,V_N\), their adjacent rows \(V_1,\ldots,V_{N+1}\), the normalized pressure rows, and, in the forced problem, the prescribed source rows. Spatial height \(M\) specifies the adjacent domains
\[
V_n\in L^2(I;H^{M+1}),\qquad
V_{n+1}\in L^2(I;H^{M-1}).                            \tag{T7}
\]
At \(M=0\), the second space is \(H^{-1}\), not \(L^2\). Larger records retain every common coordinate.

The scalar measured on a strict cutoff is
\[
\mathfrak R_{N,M}(S)=
\sum_{n=0}^N\left(
\|V_n\|_{L^2(0,S;H^{M+1})}^2+
\|V_{n+1}\|_{L^2(0,S;H^{M-1})}^2\right).
\]
The native theorem's promised output is a finite norm on \(I\), equivalently a finite supremum of these norms for \(S<T_*\). Constants may depend on the fixed record and the prescribed data, but not on the strict cutoff. There is no supremum over all derivative orders.

The actual deposited unforced argument invokes its author proof-construction record, TIR.11a-pre–TIR.11a, before restricting time. Its equation (58), page 29, names the finite whole-\(I\) output \(\mathfrak P_{N,M}[u_0,\nu;I]<\infty\). The [forced archive's realization](../COVERAGE.md#A0207) invokes N11a–N11d and names the corresponding forced output \(\mathfrak P^f_{N,M}<\infty\). Initial rows are fixed by (T5); interior rows obey (T3)–(T4). The finite cover buffers the adjacent time and viscous coordinates while preserving all transport splits and pressure products.

The combined [unforced realization](../SOURCES.md#S139) and [forced realization](../SOURCES.md#S117) present this finite graph in the manuscript. The forced construction is applied to the complete forced graph of the same prescribed history; it does not import an unforced velocity or pressure value.

The archived forced proof pays each prescribed affine source row by the actual homogeneous pairing
\[
2|\langle F_{n,\alpha},J_{n,\alpha}\rangle_{\dot H^M}|
\le\nu\|J_{n,\alpha}\|_{\dot H^{M+1}}^2
  +\nu^{-1}\|F_{n,\alpha}\|_{\dot H^{M-1}}^2.
\]
The force's gradient part has the separate finite pressure-potential budget. These fixed-order budgets permit arbitrary finite source amplitude; their constants increase with the prescribed force norms. They retain the intrinsic nonlinear Leibniz graph rather than identifying its velocity factors with prescribed sources. The [archived payment and realization](../COVERAGE.md#A0207) and [forced teaching note](A0223-forced-teaching-and-source-versions.md) give their exact domains.

Several recovered local spine witnesses differ. A local record can explain the intended object without automatically being the exact version used by the deposit. Their mathematical definitions and version distinctions are recorded in the unforced and forced teaching notes.

Given the stated output (T7), the next operations are explicit:

**Mixed-index reading.** Fix \(r,m\). Select a record deep and high enough that every \(V_n\), \(0\le n\le r\), belongs to \(L^2(I;H^m)\). These are the actual weak derivatives of \(u\), so
\[
\|u\|_{H^r(I;H^m)}^2=\sum_{n=0}^r\|V_n\|_{L^2(I;H^m)}^2<\infty.
\]
Taking every finite \(r,m\) yields
\[
u\in\bigcap_{r,m\ge0}H^r(I;H^m_\sigma).               \tag{T8}
\]
The inverse limit is the compatible tuple representation of these same rows. It does not introduce independently chosen fields.

**Completed-height reading.** Derive the height identities first, then evaluate them on the same whole-terminal blocks. Their positive spatial-energy readouts recover every spatial Sobolev row. The original momentum recurrence then reconstructs every temporal and pressure row, reaching (T8) again. The two readings are consequences of one family, not separate fluids or new sources.

Restriction decreases each nonnegative integral, and monotone convergence reads its terminal value. It is the prior whole-\(I\) membership that makes this value finite. The ontology explicitly distinguishes that membership operation from its restriction/exhaustion readout.

## 6. Viscosity, completed critical heights, and the full square

The guide calibrates the critical height by the actual Navier–Stokes scaling
\[
u_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda u(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),\quad
p_\lambda=\lambda^2p(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),\quad
f_\lambda=\lambda^3f(\lambda^2t,\lambda x).
\]
Every momentum term scales by \(\lambda^3\) at fixed viscosity. The homogeneous norm scales by \(\lambda^{s-1/2}\), making \(s=1/2\) invariant, whereas kinetic energy scales by \(\lambda^{-1}\). This explains why finite kinetic energy alone can coexist with concentration. The scaling contraction and the Banach contraction in local theory are distinct operations. Scaling compares transformed data and their corresponding fields; the finite-record proof keeps the original prescribed data and history throughout.

For each temporal row \(r\in\mathbb N_0\) and nonnegative spatial height \(s\) on a strict classical interval, define
\[
E_{r,s}=\tfrac12\|\Lambda^sV_r\|_2^2,\quad
\mathcal P_{r,s}=-\operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda^sV_r,\Lambda^s(B_r+\nabla Q_r)\rangle,
\quad
\mathcal W_{r,s}=\operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda^sV_r,\Lambda^sF_r\rangle.
\]
Pressure's global pairing vanishes by solenoidality, while its coordinate remains in the full graph. Pairing the actual row gives
\[
E_{r,s}'=-2\nu E_{r,s+1}+\mathcal P_{r,s}+\mathcal W_{r,s}. \tag{T9}
\]
On each strict classical interval, induction gives
\[
\partial_t^k E_{r,s}
=(-2\nu)^kE_{r,s+k}
+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-2\nu)^j
 \partial_t^{k-1-j}(\mathcal P_{r,s+j}+\mathcal W_{r,s+j}). \tag{T10}
\]
Differentiate the \(k\) identity and substitute (T9) at its highest height to obtain \(k+1\). The sign and coefficient are fixed by viscosity.

At critical height \(H=E_{0,1/2}\), the completed readout is
\[
R_k=
\frac{H^{(k)}-
\sum_{j<k}(-2\nu)^j\partial_t^{k-1-j}
   (\mathcal P_{0,j+1/2}+\mathcal W_{0,j+1/2})}
{(-2\nu)^k}
=E_{0,k+1/2}\ge0.                                    \tag{T11}
\]
The unforced case deletes \(\mathcal W\). Raw \(H^{(k)}\) is not the energy of \(V_k\), and is not the completed \(R_k\).

The scalar derivative itself is the full quadratic cross-pairing
\[
H^{(k)}=\tfrac12\sum_{a=0}^k\binom ka
\operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda^{1/2}V_a,\Lambda^{1/2}V_{k-a}\rangle.
\]
These signed cross terms are another reason to distinguish the derivative of the critical energy from the energy of a higher velocity row.

For explaining that identity, its finite velocity-jet Gram array is
\(G_{ab}(t)=\operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda^{1/2}V_a(t),\Lambda^{1/2}V_b(t)\rangle\).
For real coefficients, \(c^TGc=\|\sum_a c_a\Lambda^{1/2}V_a\|_2^2\ge0\).
The displayed derivative takes the weighted anti-diagonal \(a+b=k\), including signed off-diagonal entries. This explanatory Gram notation is distinct from the printed transport-operator array and rectangle-norm table.

The full production derivative retains all three factors:
\[
\partial_t^\ell\mathcal P_{r,s}
=-\sum_{a=0}^r\binom ra
 \sum_{b+c+d=\ell}\frac{\ell!}{b!c!d!}
 \operatorname{Re}\langle
 \Lambda^sV_{r+b},
 \Lambda^s((V_{a+c}\cdot\nabla)V_{r-a+d})\rangle.       \tag{T12}
\]
Work derivatives likewise retain both velocity and force:
\[
\partial_t^\ell\mathcal W_{r,s}
=\sum_{b=0}^\ell\binom\ell b
 \operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda^sV_{r+b},
                          \Lambda^sF_{r+\ell-b}\rangle.
\]
The pressure part of the differentiated production is
\(-\sum_{b=0}^{\ell}\binom\ell b\operatorname{Re}
\langle\Lambda^sV_{r+b},\Lambda^s\nabla Q_{r+\ell-b}\rangle\).
Each term is zero by the actual solenoidality of the velocity row. Thus (T12) is the exact remaining transport sum, with the complete pressure graph still retained. Spatial differentiation adds the complete multi-index allocations. The [height and square derivations](../SOURCES.md#S118) preserve these terms.

Finiteness of every completed mass \(\int_I R_k\) gives the all-spatial \(L^2_tH^m_x\) column. On low frequencies kinetic \(L^2\) controls the Bessel weight; on high frequencies select \(k\) with \(k+\frac12\ge m\). Thus
\[
L^2_x\cap\bigcap_k\dot H^{k+1/2}_x=\bigcap_mH^m_x,
\]
and its integrated version has the same finite-order interpretation.

For \(n\in\mathbb N_0\), the temporal-to-spatial diagonal is first an identity on a strict classical interval. Project (T3):
\[
\Lambda^sV_{n+1}+\nu\Lambda^{s+2}V_n
=\Lambda^s\mathbb P(F_n-B_n).
\]
Squaring, using \(V_{n+1}=\partial_tV_n\), and recombining the complementary pressure projection gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Lambda^sV_{n+1}\|_2^2+\nu^2\|\Lambda^{s+2}V_n\|_2^2
+\|\Lambda^s\nabla Q_n\|_2^2
+\nu\partial_t\|\Lambda^{s+1}V_n\|_2^2
=\|\Lambda^s(F_n-B_n)\|_2^2.                          \tag{T13}
\end{aligned}
\]
Integrating this identity on \(0<S<T_*\) retains the initial and final \(\nu\|\Lambda^{s+1}V_n\|_2^2\) terms. The right side is the entire nonlinear-and-force source. It is never replaced by the force alone.

The printed completed-height estimates and square estimates are evaluated on the finite blocks already supplied in the preceding order. Their role is to expose and estimate relations among those same coordinates.

## 7. Spatial-to-temporal reconstruction and the common endpoint

The original spatial-column reconstruction is concrete. Assume every fixed \(u\in L^2(I;H^{m+2})\). Product and multiplier estimates give
\[
\|u_t\|_{L^1H^m}
\le \nu\sqrt{T_*}\|u\|_{L^2H^{m+2}}
+C_m\|u\|_{L^2H^{m+2}}^2+\|g\|_{L^1H^m}.             \tag{T14}
\]
Also \(u\in L^1H^m\) because \(T_*<\infty\). The Banach-valued fundamental theorem gives \(u\in W^{1,1}(I;H^m)\subset C([0,T_*];H^m)\). The full binomial recurrence (T3), Sobolev multiplication and prescribed force derivatives now inductively give continuous \(V_n,Q_n\) at every finite order, and hence the same mixed intersection. Each induction uses finitely many higher spatial orders.

The [detailed height/rectangle note](A0226-producer-rectangles-heights-and-matrices.md) also works out the finite-order conditional conversion \(\mathfrak R_{0,M+2N}(I)<\infty\Rightarrow\mathfrak R_{N,M}(I)<\infty\) on the same \(I\), retaining every binomial allocation and the \(M=0\) negative-order face. It is a worked consequence of the actual adjacent trace and full equation, explicitly distinguished from the printed all-spatial reconstruction and from the native operation that supplies whole-terminal membership.

There is also the direct adjacent trace route. If
\[
v\in L^2(I;H^{m+1}),\qquad v_t\in L^2(I;H^{m-1}),
\]
then
\[
\|v(t)\|_{H^m}^2-\|v(s)\|_{H^m}^2
=2\operatorname{Re}\int_s^t
 \langle A^{m-1}v_t,A^{m+1}v\rangle\,d\rho,
\]
\[
\sup_{[0,T_*]}\|v\|_{H^m}^2
\le T_*^{-1}\|v\|_{L^2H^m}^2+
2\|v_t\|_{L^2H^{m-1}}\|v\|_{L^2H^{m+1}}.             \tag{T15}
\]
Fourier truncation proves the identity first and the continuous limit afterward. This is the [adjacent Hilbert-scale trace](../SOURCES.md#S136).

Apply it to every \(V_n\). Representatives at different \(m\) equal the same preterminal distribution under Sobolev inclusions, so their endpoints agree. They define one
\[
V_n^*\in H^\infty_\sigma,\qquad u_*=V_0^*.
\]
Continuity of the pressure products gives
\[
Q_n^*=R_iR_j\sum_a\binom na V_{a,i}^*V_{n-a,j}^*
      -(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot F_n(T_*),
\]
\[
V_{n+1}^*=\nu\Delta V_n^*
-\sum_a\binom na(V_a^*\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}^*
-\nabla Q_n^*+F_n(T_*).                              \tag{T16}
\]
Thus the complete terminal jet is determined by \(u_*\), \(\nu\), and the actual force jet at \(T_*\). It is not freely prescribed.

Restart local theory at absolute time \(T_*\) from \(u_*\). In translated coordinates the force is \(f(T_*+r)\). Its right-hand initial jet obeys exactly (T16), so induction matches every right derivative to the incoming left derivative. Pressure matches with the same normalization. Gluing yields a pressure-complete classical extension agreeing with the incoming history before the proposed finite maximal time. The final contradiction is therefore with the definition of maximality.

## 8. What the matrices and quantitative consequences mean

The publication's main matrices are three different bookkeeping objects: a tuple of actual mixed fields, a table of nonnegative coordinate norms, and a row-finite lower-triangular array of transport operators. They are not interchangeable with a Gram matrix.

For mixed indices \(\kappa=(n,\alpha)\) and \(\lambda=(a,\beta)\preceq\kappa\), the transport array has blocks
\[
\mathbb L(J)_{\kappa\lambda}
=\binom\kappa\lambda\mathbb P[(J_{\kappa-\lambda}\cdot\nabla)\,\cdot].
\]
Its action, after reindexing, is exactly the full projected Leibniz sum in (T4). The forced array additionally retains the prescribed source coordinate and force-pressure potential. Each row has finitely many entries. Ordering by \(n+|\alpha|\) makes it lower triangular.

Dividing each jet coordinate by \(\kappa!=n!\alpha!\) removes binomial coefficients in each finite downward-closed truncation, because
\[
\binom\kappa\lambda\lambda!(\kappa-\lambda)!=\kappa!.
\]
The normalized temporal shift is \((n+1)\widehat J_{n+1,\alpha}\), not merely \(\widehat J_{n+1,\alpha}\). This finite algebraic conjugacy does not assert convergence of an infinite analytic generating series.

The velocity columns of the norm matrix are
\[
G_{n,M+1}=\|V_n\|_{L^2H^{M+1}},\qquad
G_{n+1,M-1}=\|V_{n+1}\|_{L^2H^{M-1}}.
\]
Their squared Frobenius norm is \(\mathfrak R_{N,M}\). The pressure column uses \(L^1\) norms. A fixed high rectangle supplies frequency tails, Sobolev embeddings, pressure products, time moduli, vorticity estimates and material flow estimates.

That separate argument preserves the nonlinear source \(\mathcal B(u)=\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)\). With \(a=\|\Lambda^{1/2}u\|_2\), \(b=\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2\), its exact unforced square is

The source bound is

\]
For Schwartz datum with \(a(0)<\sqrt2\pi\nu\), a first-exit argument preserves this strict critical threshold. The positive reserve \(\nu^2-a(0)^2/(2\pi^2)\) bounds the full action; higher commutators give each fixed higher energy bound, and the uniform \(H^3\) local restart excludes a finite maximal time.
