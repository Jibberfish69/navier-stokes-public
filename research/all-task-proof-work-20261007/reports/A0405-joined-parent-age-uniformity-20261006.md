## 2. Uniform domination of every complete joined prefix

Use the Cauchy-kernel Hilbert seminorm
\[
\|\Theta\|_K^2
=\iint K(\delta_a,\delta_b)
\operatorname{Re}\langle\Theta_a,\Theta_b\rangle\,da\,db.

\[
\frac{1-r_xr_y}{x+y}
=\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma+x)(\gamma+y)}
+\frac{\gamma^2}{(x+y)(\gamma+x)(\gamma+y)}
\succeq0.
\]
The first summand is rank one and the second is a weighted Cauchy Gram kernel. Iteration gives \(\|R^N\Theta\|_K\le\|\Theta\|_K\), independently of \(N,\gamma\), or a finite prefix.

Keeping all signed cross terms,
\[
\overline C_N
=c\bigl(\|\Theta\|_K^2
-\operatorname{Re}\langle\Theta,R^N\Theta\rangle_K\bigr).
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz in this positive metric yields
\[
\boxed{0\le\overline C_N\le2C_\infty,\qquad
|\overline C_N-C_\infty|\le C_\infty.}
\]
This is an operator-metric argument, not pointwise domination of a signed parent integrand. It does not assert that \(\overline C_N\) is monotone in \(N\); the individual increments can be indefinite.

For each actual nonzero output \(k\), the physical rates obey
\[

Here \(\widehat u(p)\cdot(k-p)=\widehat u(p)\cdot k\) uses the original incompressibility relation, and the outer pressure projector remains contractive. Since
\((\delta_p+\delta_q)^{-1}\le\gamma_k^{-1}\), finite parent approximations, including finite atoms, approximate this density in the heat metric. On such a finite prefix \(R^N\to0\); the contractions extend convergence to the actual continuum density. Equivalently, dominated convergence in the complete double-parent integral gives \(\|R^N\Theta\|_K\to0\) directly.

The zero output has the source's zero-mode interpretation and measure zero. Output integration uses the already integrable \(C_\infty\) bound. Thus \(\overline C_N(t)\to C_\infty(t)\) for each strict time without introducing a first-parent-rate moment.
