## 2. The actual shared linearization, including pressure

Let \(\mathscr H=L^2_\sigma(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)\), let \(\mathbb P\) be the orthogonal Leray projection, and use the real \(L^2\) inner product. Fix an actual smooth forced solution on \([a,b]\), with \(b<T_*\). Put

\[
D_j=\partial_j,\qquad A=-\Delta=\sum_jD_j^*D_j,
\]
\[
L_u h=\mathbb P\big[(u\cdot\nabla)h+(h\cdot\nabla)u\big],
\qquad B=\nu A+L_u.
\]

Incompressibility and spatial integration by parts give, on solenoidal test fields,

\[
L_u^* k=\mathbb P\big[-(u\cdot\nabla)k+(\nabla u)^Tk\big],
\]
\[
L_u+L_u^*=2\mathbb P S_u\mathbb P,
\qquad S_u=\tfrac12\big(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^T\big).
\tag{O1}
\]

There are exactly four adjoining rows in this calculation:

\[
Y=(v,a_1,a_2,a_3),\qquad v=u_t,\quad a_j=\partial_ju,
\]
\[
Y_t+(B\oplus B\oplus B\oplus B)Y
=g=(G_1,\partial_1G_0,\partial_2G_0,\partial_3G_0),
\quad G_r=\mathbb P\partial_t^rf.
\tag{O2}
\]

The term \((a_j\cdot\nabla)u\) is the spatial commutator and remains in \(L_u\). The corresponding unprojected pressures are \(p_t\) and \(\partial_jp\). In general a row with unprojected force \(F_h\) has pressure \(\pi_h\) determined by

\[
-\Delta\pi_h
=\partial_i\partial_j(u_ih_j+h_iu_j)-\nabla\cdot F_h,
\]
\[
\pi_h=R_iR_j(u_ih_j+h_iu_j)
-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot F_h.
\tag{O3}
\]

Thus projection here retains the complete pressure response. A nonlocal metric acting on \(\mathscr H\) tests against a solenoidal vector; the pressure pairing vanishes for that reason. Its nonlocal nonlinear contribution is included in \(L_u\), rather than discarded as in a weighted multiplication test.

At the initial datum,

\[
v(0)=-\nu Au_0-\mathbb P[(u_0\cdot\nabla)u_0]+G_0(0).
\tag{O4}
\]

Consequently \(Y(0)\) is generated by the actual datum and force. Higher temporal or spatial rows are not asserted to obey the homogeneous equation (O2); their additional Leibniz source allocations would have to be retained separately.

## 3. Ordinary terminal Gramian versus an initial metric

For any selfadjoint operator metric \(H\) whose quadratic forms permit the following calculation,

\[
\frac d{dt}\langle h,Hh\rangle
=\langle h,(H_t-B^*H-HB)h\rangle
+2\langle Hh,g_h\rangle.
\tag{O5}
\]

Hence the equation

\[
H_t=B^*H+HB-C^*C
\tag{O6}
\]

gives exactly

\[
\frac d{dt}\langle h,Hh\rangle+\|Ch\|_2^2
=2\langle Hh,g_h\rangle.
\]

Let \(U(t,s)\) be the propagator of the actual homogeneous linearized equation \(h_t+Bh=0\). For terminal \(H(b)=H_b\ge0\), the positive solution is

\[
H(s)=U(b,s)^*H_bU(b,s)
+\int_s^b U(t,s)^*C(t)^*C(t)U(t,s)\,dt.
\tag{O7}
\]

This is the observation energy of the actual future history from \(s\) to \(b\). The time-dependent coefficients and the full projected linearization occur inside \(U\). In particular, with terminal value zero it vanishes at \(b\); positivity alone gives no lower bound uniform through that terminal time. Output order matters: one cannot infer the same spatial coercivity for an output \(C=I\) as for an output containing derivatives.

An initial prescription in (O6) has positive \(\nu(AH+HA)\) in forward time. It therefore has the inverse diffusion direction; a positive terminal formula is not automatically a causal bounded evolution from a prescribed initial metric.

## 4. A stronger shared metric cancels the diffusion port too

Instead choose the same \(H\) for all four actual rows and impose

\[
H_t=B^*H+HB-2\nu\sum_jD_j^*HD_j,
\qquad H(b)=I.
\tag{O8}
\]

Direct substitution in (O5) proves

\[
E_H'+\nu\mathcal D_H=\langle HY,g\rangle,
\tag{O9}
\]
\[
E_H=\tfrac12\sum_{h=v,a_1,a_2,a_3}\langle h,Hh\rangle,
\qquad
\mathcal D_H=\sum_{h,j}\langle D_jh,HD_jh\rangle.
\]

Thus this operator equation cancels the stretching, the metric diffusion remainder and the weighted-pressure port in one exact identity. It does so by retaining their full Leray-projected operator, not by declaring any of those ports zero in the source's multiplication-metric calculation.

The diffusion part can also be written exactly as

\[
\nu(AH+HA)-2\nu\sum_jD_j^*HD_j
=-\nu\sum_j[D_j,[D_j,H]].
\]

For multiplication by a matrix this is \(-\nu\Delta H\); for the present metric it is the corresponding operator commutator. This verifies the forward inverse-diffusion sign and its positive reverse-time direction without substituting a separate model for the actual operator.

The sign and positivity can be verified on the actual \(\mathbb R^3\) domain. Let \(\Pi_R=1_{\{|\xi|\le R\}}\) on \(\mathscr H\). This is an infinite-dimensional spectral range; no finite-rank Fourier projection or torus change is used. On its range put

\[
A_R=\Pi_RA\Pi_R,\quad D_{j,R}=\Pi_RD_j\Pi_R,
\quad L_R=\Pi_RL_u\Pi_R.
\]

These are bounded operators on a fixed strict classical interval; \(D_j\), \(A\), \(\mathbb P\) and \(\Pi_R\) commute. In particular

\[
A_R=\sum_jD_{j,R}^*D_{j,R},\qquad
L_R+L_R^*=2\Pi_R\mathbb P S_u\mathbb P\Pi_R.
\tag{O10}
\]

In reverse time \(s=b-t\), write \(K_R(s)=H_R(b-s)\). Its generator is

\[
K_R'=-L_R^*K_R-K_RL_R
-\nu(A_RK_R+K_RA_R)
+2\nu\sum_jD_{j,R}^*K_RD_{j,R}.
\tag{O11}
\]

At each reverse time this has the form

\[
\mathcal L_s(K)=G_s^*K+KG_s+\sum_jJ_{j,s}^*KJ_{j,s},
\quad G_s=-\nu A_R-L_R(b-s),\quad J_{j,s}=\sqrt{2\nu}D_{j,R}.
\]

Positivity does not require an external assertion: the homogeneous propagator for \(K'=G^*K+KG\) is \(X\mapsto W^*XW\), where \(W'=WG\). Duhamel iteration inserts only maps \(X\mapsto J_j^*XJ_j\) between such conjugations. Every Dyson term is positive on positive \(X\), and the bounded-operator series converges on compact time intervals. The same argument after matrix amplification proves complete positivity.

The diffusion terms annihilate the identity, leaving

\[
\mathcal L_s(I)=-(L_R^*+L_R)
=-2\Pi_R\mathbb P S_u(b-s)\mathbb P\Pi_R.
\tag{O12}
\]

Set \(\mu(t)=\|S_u(t)\|_{L^\infty_x}\), with matrix operator norm. Comparing the positive evolution with the scalar subsolution and supersolution gives

\[
e^{-2\int_t^b\mu(r)\,dr}I
\preceq H_R(t)
\preceq e^{2\int_t^b\mu(r)\,dr}I.
\tag{O13}
\]

For example the lower comparison difference has nonnegative forcing \(\alpha(-2\Pi_R\mathbb P S_u\mathbb P\Pi_R+2\mu I)\), where \(\alpha=e^{-2\int_0^s\mu(b-r)dr}\); the upper difference has forcing \(\beta(2\mu I+2\Pi_R\mathbb P S_u\mathbb P\Pi_R)\). This proves the bounds with constants independent of \(R\).

For the original row \(h\), its cutoff equation is exactly

\[
(\Pi_Rh)_t+(\nu A_R+L_R)\Pi_Rh
=\Pi_Rg_h+\delta_{R,h},
\quad \delta_{R,h}=-\Pi_RL_u(1-\Pi_R)h.
\tag{O14}
\]

The defect is not omitted. On the fixed strict interval, \(h\in C([a,b];H^\infty)\) and \(L_u:H^1\to L^2\) is uniformly bounded, so \(\delta_{R,h}\to0\) uniformly in \(L^2\). The action inequality below passes to the original history by these strong convergences and the uniform bounds (O13). If an actual full operator is desired, weak-operator Arzelà extraction on a countable dense set of \(H^2_\sigma\) test pairs also yields a terminal-I metric satisfying (O8) in quadratic-form distributions on this strict interval: every occurrence of the unbounded operators can be placed on the fixed test fields. No full terminal metric at a possibly singular \(T_*\) is obtained by this compact-interval extraction alone.

## 5. Complete source payment and actual rectangle bound

Write

\[
J_{a,b}=\int_a^b\mu(t)\,dt,
\quad m_H=e^{-2J_{a,b}},\quad M_H=e^{2J_{a,b}},
\]
\[
F=\|g\|_{L^1(a,b;\mathscr H^4)},\qquad
B_0=\|Y(a)\|_{\mathscr H^4}+F.
\tag{O15}
\]

At each cutoff the force in (O9) is the complete bundle plus (O14)'s defect. For readability the limiting source is written below. Cauchy–Schwarz in the positive metric gives

\[
\langle HY,g\rangle
\le\sqrt{2E_H}\,\sqrt{M_H}\,\|g\|_{\mathscr H^4}.
\]

Put \(x=\sqrt{2E_H}\). The standard \(E_H+\varepsilon\) regularization justifies the same differential inequality at zeros of \(x\). Since diffusion is nonnegative,

\[
x(t)\le x(a)+\sqrt{M_H}\int_a^t\|g(r)\|\,dr.
\]

The source integral has the precise bound

\[
\int_a^b\langle HY,g\rangle
\le\sqrt{M_H}\,x(a)F+\tfrac12M_HF^2.
\tag{O16}
\]

Indeed, the integral of the accumulated source \(F(t)\) against \(F'(t)\) is \(F^2/2\); replacing it by \(F^2\) would unnecessarily lose a factor. Combining (O9), (O13) and (O16), and retaining the nonnegative terminal energy, gives

\[
\sup_{a\le t\le b}\|Y(t)\|_2^2
\le\frac{M_H}{m_H}B_0^2
=e^{4J_{a,b}}B_0^2,
\tag{O17}
\]
\[
\int_a^b\|\nabla Y\|_2^2\,dt
\le\frac{M_H}{2\nu m_H}B_0^2
=\frac{e^{4J_{a,b}}}{2\nu}B_0^2.
\tag{O18}
\]

No derivative of the prescribed force has been removed: \(F\) contains \(G_1\) and all \(\partial_jG_0\). All force norms are finite for the stipulated smooth force on a fixed finite interval. At \(a=0\), (O4) supplies the initial row norm from the datum and force.

With the Bessel Sobolev convention, the native rectangle is

\[
\mathfrak R_{0,1}(a,b)
=\int_a^b\big(\|u\|_{H^2}^2+\|u_t\|_2^2\big)\,dt,
\]
\[
\|u\|_{H^2}^2
=\|u\|_2^2+2\|\nabla u\|_2^2+\|\Delta u\|_2^2,
\quad \sum_{j,k}\|\partial_k\partial_ju\|_2^2=\|\Delta u\|_2^2.
\]

If \(K\ge\sup_{[a,b]}\|u\|_2\) is the actual known kinetic budget, then (O17)–(O18) imply the explicit unweighted bound

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\mathfrak R_{0,1}(a,b)
\le(b-a)K^2
+\left(2(b-a)+\frac1{2\nu}\right)
e^{4J_{a,b}}B_0^2.}
\tag{O19}
\]

Other equivalent integer Sobolev norms change only fixed constants. The simultaneous-row metric also pays \(\nabla v\), which is stronger than the temporal component requested in this rectangle.

For completeness, integrating the projected source by parts instead of using (O16) gives the exact nonlocal version of the source's (52):

\[
\langle Hv,G_1\rangle
=\frac d{dt}\langle u,HG_1\rangle
-\langle u,H_tG_1+HG_2\rangle,
\]
\[
\sum_j\langle H\partial_ju,\partial_jG_0\rangle
=-\left\langle u,H\Delta G_0
+\sum_j[D_j,H]D_jG_0\right\rangle.
\tag{O20}
\]

Thus native force integration by parts does not turn this metric into a prescribed-source payment: \(H_t\) and the operator commutators depend on the actual evolving history. Formula (O16) avoids omitting those terms by paying the entire original source directly, with the verified metric upper bound.

## 6. Verified scope and orientation

The stronger nonlocal operation is valid on every strict classical interval. It genuinely eliminates all three ports left in the multiplication metric: intrinsic stretching, metric diffusion and weighted pressure. The bounded-operator positivity construction, cutoff defect, complete four-row source, and resulting unweighted rectangle bound have all been accounted for.

The operation has terminal value \(H(b)=I\) and is defined backwards through the actual history. Its available coercivity is \(e^{-2J_{a,b}}\), its upper bound is \(e^{2J_{a,b}}\), and its rectangle estimate contains \(e^{4J_{a,b}}\). These are history-dependent quantities. No datum-only bound for \(J_{0,b}\), uniform as \(b\uparrow T_*<\infty\), has been derived here. Consequently this calculation alone does not produce whole-terminal \(\mathfrak R_{0,1}\) from arbitrary admitted data.

An initial prescription reverses the diffusion direction in the metric equation and has not been supplied with a bounded positive forward construction. Neither terminal positivity nor finite-cutoff positivity licenses replacing that missing initial evolution or the missing uniform terminal bound by assumption. This is a precise limitation of the independently executed operation; it is not a verdict about an independent intersection construction or an uninspected source.
