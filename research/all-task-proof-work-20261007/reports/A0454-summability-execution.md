## 1. Source operation and time domains

Fix \(\nu>0\), \(u_0\in H^\infty_\sigma\), and either \(f=0\) or the prescribed \(f\in C^\infty([0,\infty);\mathcal S)\). Let \((u,p)\) be the pressure-complete classical history generated by these data on its current maximal interval \([0,T_*)\). Fix a finite target horizon \(T\), and work only at times less than \(\min\{T,T_*\}\). This does not presume existence beyond \(T_*\).

Put \(g=\mathbb P f\) and \(N(u)=(u\cdot\nabla)u\). The original complete row and its normalization are
\[
u_t-\nu\Delta u=g-\mathbb P N(u),\qquad
p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j)-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot f,
\qquad \nabla p=(I-\mathbb P)(f-N(u)).
\tag{NS1}
\]
Thus a gradient part of the prescribed force is retained in pressure. Every construction below uses the original absolute-time force \(f(t)\), with the same viscosity.

The local theorem, at any attained time \(s\), uses
\[
\Gamma_s=\|g\|_{L^2(s,s+1;H^2)},\qquad
\delta_s=\min\{1,c_\nu(1+\|u(s)\|_{H^3}+\Gamma_s)^{-2}\},
\tag{NS2}
\]
where \(L_\nu=4(1+\nu+\nu^{-1})e^{(\nu+\nu^{-1})/2}\), \(C_q=\sqrt3 C_3^{\rm alg}\), \(c_\nu=(8C_qL_\nu^2)^{-2}\). Its fixed point obeys
\[
\|u\|_{C H^3}+\|u\|_{L^2H^4}+\|u_t\|_{L^2H^2}
\le 2L_\nu(\|u(s)\|_{H^3}+\Gamma_s)
\tag{NS3}
\]
on the constructed interval. All finite higher velocity and pressure derivatives persist on that same interval. Their complete recurrence is
\[
Q_n=R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^n\binom naV_{a,i}V_{n-a,j}
-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot F_n,
\quad
V_{n+1}=\nu\Delta V_n-\sum_{a=0}^n\binom na(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}-\nabla Q_n+F_n,
\tag{NS4}
\]
with \(V_n=\partial_t^nu\), \(F_n=\partial_t^nf\). No higher-order lifespan is separately selected. Overlap uniqueness identifies each local fixed point with the same generated history.

Direct source: [forced analytic framework, LF680–717 and LF832–1023](../SOURCES.md#S114); [forced setting, LF116–149](../SOURCES.md#S117). The unforced theorem is the same local operation with the prescribed-force terms zero, [unforced analytic framework, LF662–1018](../SOURCES.md#S136).

## 2. The actual kinetic budget is paid before terminal membership

On every strict classical interval the energy identity gives
\[
\tfrac12\|u(S)\|_2^2+\nu\int_0^S\|\nabla u\|_2^2
=\tfrac12\|u_0\|_2^2+\int_0^S(g,u)\,dt.
\tag{NS5}
\]
Let
\[
K=\|u_0\|_2+\int_0^T\|g(t)\|_2\,dt,
\qquad D_T=\frac{\|u_0\|_2^2/2+K\int_0^T\|g(t)\|_2\,dt}{\nu}.
\tag{NS6}
\]
Then \(\sup_{t<\min(T,T_*)}\|u(t)\|_2\le K\), and the nonnegative kinetic action satisfies \(\int_0^S Z\le D_T\), where \(Z=\|\nabla u\|_2^2\). These constants depend only on the prescribed data and the fixed finite target. They do not assume a whole-terminal high-order norm. In the unforced case \(K=\|u_0\|_2\), \(D_T=\|u_0\|_2^2/(2\nu)\).

NS5 is the complete equation's kinetic pairing: the nonlinear transport and pressure pairings vanish because \(u\) is solenoidal; the work \((g,u)=(f,u)\) remains. Sources: [forced endpoint global proof, LF570–657](../SOURCES.md#S121), [unforced endpoint global proof, LF648–733](../SOURCES.md#S143).

## 3. Two new Fourier estimates on the actual velocity

Write \(a_3(t)=\|\Lambda^3u(t)\|_2\), \(\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\). The unitary Fourier convention of the sources gives
\[
\|u\|_\infty\le(2\pi)^{-3/2}
\left[\sqrt{4\pi/3}\,R^{3/2}\|u\|_2
+\sqrt{4\pi/3}\,R^{-3/2}a_3\right].
\]
The first term is the integral over \(|\xi|\le R\); the second uses \(\int_{|\xi|>R}|\xi|^{-6}\,d\xi=4\pi/(3R^3)\). Choose \(R^3=a_3/\|u\|_2\), with zero cases handled by approximation. Hence
\[
\|u\|_\infty^2\le C_\infty^2 K a_3,
\qquad C_\infty^2=\frac{2}{3\pi^2}.
\tag{NS7}
\]

Similarly,
\[
\|\nabla u\|_\infty\le(2\pi)^{-3/2}
\left[\sqrt{4\pi/5}\,R^{5/2}\|u\|_2
+\sqrt{4\pi}\,R^{-1/2}a_3\right].
\]
Optimizing at \(R^3=a_3/(\sqrt5\|u\|_2)\) gives the genuine improved gradient bound
\[
\|\nabla u\|_\infty\le C_G K^{1/6}a_3^{5/6},
\qquad C_G=\frac{3\sqrt2}{\pi5^{11/12}}.
\tag{NS8}
\]
These estimates apply to the full vector and ordered gradient tensor by Euclidean Fourier contraction. They are newly derived applications of the source Fourier convention, rather than quotations from the manuscript. In particular \(a_3\le A(t):=\|u(t)\|_{H^3}\).

## 4. Execute the common local intervals

Choose the fixed known budget \(\Gamma^*=\|g\|_{L^2(0,T+1;H^2)}\), and define at an attained start \(t_j\)
\[
A_j=\|u(t_j)\|_{H^3},\quad B_j=1+A_j+\Gamma^*,
\quad h_j=\tfrac12\min\{1,c_\nu B_j^{-2}\}.
\tag{NS9}
\]
Because \(\Gamma_{t_j}\le\Gamma^*\), the original theorem constructs the pair at least to \(t_j+2h_j\), with
\[
\sup_{[t_j,t_j+2h_j]}\|u\|_{H^3}\le R_j,
\qquad R_j\le2L_\nu B_j,
\qquad h_jB_j^2\le c_\nu/2.
\tag{NS10}
\]
Take \(t_{j+1}=t_j+h_j\) for complete steps before the finite target. If this step crosses \(T\), stop the tested partition at \(T\); all estimates involving \(K,D_T\) are applied only through \(T\). Any original strict-window history agrees with these constructed patches on overlap by the source's mild uniqueness. Every retained finite mixed coordinate follows the complete NS4 on the same intervals.

If this iteration has a finite accumulating time below or at the target, its \(B_j\) must tend to infinity: otherwise there is a positive lower bound on a subsequence of the \(h_j\), inconsistent with summability. An accumulation before the original maximal time is also excluded, because the old history is smooth there and its attained \(H^3\) stocks are bounded. Thus genuine finite accumulation of this full-length native iteration could only be at the maximal time. This deduction uses the actual constructed intervals, not a freely selected scalar sequence.

## 5. Improved attained-stock growth

The exact \(H^3\) transport commutator printed in the source yields, with its finite explicit constant \(K_3\),
\[
\tfrac12(A^2)' +\nu\|\nabla u\|_{H^3}^2
\le K_3\|\nabla u\|_\infty A^2+\|g\|_{H^3}A.
\tag{NS11}
\]
Pressure is orthogonal in this global Sobolev pairing; its canonical row has not been altered. Regularizing the norm at zero gives
\(A'\le K_3\|\nabla u\|_\infty A+\|g\|_{H^3}\).

For a native patch, NS8–10 give
\[
\eta_j:=K_3\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}\|\nabla u\|_\infty
\le q B_j^{-7/6},\quad
q=\frac{c_\nu}{2}K_3 C_G K^{1/6}(2L_\nu)^{5/6}.
\tag{NS12}
\]
Let \(G_j=\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}\|g\|_{H^3}\). Gronwall, with \(e^x-1\le e^q x\) for \(0\le x\le q\), gives
\[
B_{j+1}\le B_j+\kappa B_j^{-1/6}+e^qG_j,
\qquad\kappa=e^q q.
\tag{NS13}
\]
The force increment is actually summable: \(\sum G_j\le\|g\|_{L^1(0,T;H^3)}\), a prescribed finite quantity.

Here is a closed quantitative growth bound, including that force. Put
\[
F_j=e^q\int_{t_j}^T\|g\|_{H^3},\quad x_j=B_j+F_j,
\quad\widetilde\kappa=\kappa(1+F_0)^{1/6}.
\]
Then \(F_j-F_{j+1}=e^qG_j\), \(x_j/B_j\le1+F_0\), and NS13 implies
\[
x_{j+1}\le x_j+\widetilde\kappa x_j^{-1/6}.
\]
As \(x_j\ge1\), the mean value theorem gives
\[
x_{j+1}^{7/6}-x_j^{7/6}
\le C_{\rm step}:=\tfrac76\widetilde\kappa(1+\widetilde\kappa)^{1/6},
\]
also when the left difference is negative. Consequently
\[
B_j\le x_j\le(x_0^{7/6}+C_{\rm step}j)^{6/7},
\quad
h_j\ge\tfrac12\min\{1,c_\nu(x_0^{7/6}+C_{\rm step}j)^{-12/7}\}.
\tag{NS14}
\]
This is a genuine datum-controlled improvement over the direct radius recursion. The lower duration envelope has exponent \(12/7>1\), so its series is finite. The estimate supplies a positive quantitative initial coverage and controls every finite attained stock; it does not force coverage to an arbitrary finite target. No actual Navier–Stokes accumulation or failure is inferred from that fact.

Source operation executed: [forced analytic framework, LF388–414 and LF929–988](../SOURCES.md#S114). NS12–14 are new consequences, with all force increments retained.

## 6. A stronger local lifespan follows from the same operation

On the currently existing history, NS8 and NS11 imply
\[
A'\le K_3 C_G K^{1/6}A^{11/6}+\|g\|_{H^3}.
\]
For \(B(t)=1+A(t)+\Gamma^*\ge1\), set
\(a_T=K_3C_GK^{1/6}+\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|g(t)\|_{H^3}\). Then
\[
B'\le a_TB^{11/6},\quad
B(t)^{-5/6}\ge B(s)^{-5/6}-\tfrac56 a_T(t-s).
\tag{NS15}
\]
If \(a_T>0\), this bounds the stock on every closed interval strictly shorter than
\(6B(s)^{-5/6}/(5a_T)\). Iterating the original local theorem while that stock is bounded cannot accumulate there: NS2 has a positive lower lifespan and the common higher-order persistence remains available. Therefore the maximal generated domain reaches at least
\[
\min\{T,\ s+6B(s)^{-5/6}/(5a_T)\}.
\tag{NS16}
\]
This is a newly derived lifespan improvement, using the original construction rather than a new mild-function space. If a smaller finite maximal time existed while the stock stayed bounded, one could restart at a strict incoming time less than the common positive local lifespan from that maximal time; uniqueness would identify the restart on its incoming overlap and extend the history past it. No terminal trace is assumed in this argument. Equality at the limiting comparison time is a domain-length bound, not a claim that its endpoint stock is bounded.

If \(T_*\le T\) were finite, bounded attained stocks near \(T_*\) would contradict the original restart theorem. Thus NS15 implies the exact necessary lower growth
\[
B(s)\ge\left[\frac6{5a_T(T_*-s)}\right]^{6/5},\qquad s<T_*.
\tag{NS17}
\]
This gives a quantitative terminal alternative, but no upper bound at the terminal time. If \(a_T=0\), NS15 makes \(B\) nonincreasing, so the original local iteration covers the target directly.

## 7. The complete source square telescopes the restart faces

At row \(n=0,s=0\), the original unprojected square is exactly
\[
\|u_t\|_2^2+\nu^2\|\Delta u\|_2^2+\|\nabla p\|_2^2
+\nu Z'=\|f-N(u)\|_2^2.
\tag{NS18}
\]
Its two orthogonal projections are NS1 and the canonical pressure gradient. The right side is the entire source,
\[
\|f-N\|_2^2=\|f\|_2^2-2\operatorname{Re}(f,N)+\|N\|_2^2,
\tag{NS19}
\]
including the force–nonlinearity cross term. It has not been replaced by \(\|f\|^2\).

Integrate NS18 over consecutive native patches and add. Every internal \(\nu Z(t_j)\) cancels between the terminal face of the preceding patch and the initial face of the next. Thus, for every attained strict time \(S\),
\[
\nu Z(S)+\int_0^S\left(\|u_t\|_2^2+\nu^2\|\Delta u\|_2^2+\|\nabla p\|_2^2\right)
=\nu Z(0)+\int_0^S\|f-N\|_2^2.
\tag{NS20}
\]
Only the original initial enstrophy remains. Summing a separate initial-stock cost at every restart would lose this exact telescope and would manufacture an extra requirement.

Source: [forced rectangle cell, LF189–251](../SOURCES.md#S118). Its printed use on already supplied whole-terminal blocks, LF254–307, is a separate consumer; NS18–20 here are executed on strict constructed intervals without invoking those memberships.

## 8. A precise native weighted kinetic payment of the first rectangle

Pointwise on the actual velocity, NS7 gives
\[
\|N(u)\|_2^2\le\|u\|_\infty^2 Z
\le\frac{2K}{3\pi^2}a_3 Z.
\tag{NS21}
\]
This identifies an intrinsic complete-history upper payment
\(\int a_3Z\), independent of how overlap times are labeled. On a native patch, \(a_3\le R_j\le2L_\nu B_j\). Let \(D_j=\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}Z\). Estimating the exact NS19 by \(\|f-N\|^2\le2\|f\|^2+2\|N\|^2\) gives
\[
\int_0^S\|f-N\|_2^2
\le2\|f\|_{L^2(0,T;L^2)}^2
+\frac{8L_\nu K}{3\pi^2}\sum_{j:\,t_j<S}B_jD_j,
\tag{NS22}
\]
where the final partial interval uses its actual partial \(D_j\). The full force term is prescribed and finite, including its gradient part.

Therefore a finite upper bound on the actual weighted kinetic moment \(\sum B_jD_j\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak R_{0,1}(S)
&=\|u\|_{L^2(0,S;H^2)}^2+\|u_t\|_{L^2(0,S;L^2)}^2\\
&\le TK^2+2D_T+\max\{1,\nu^{-2}\}
\left[\nu Z(0)+2\|f\|_{L^2(0,T;L^2)}^2
+\frac{8L_\nu K}{3\pi^2}\sum_{j:\,t_j<S}B_jD_j\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{NS23}
\]
Here \(\|u\|_{H^2}^2=\|u\|_2^2+2Z+\|\Delta u\|_2^2\) exactly. NS20 simultaneously pays the normalized pressure-gradient action and the terminal enstrophy stock.

By NS14 a sufficient quantitative native payment is
\[
\sum_j(x_0^{7/6}+C_{\rm step}j)^{6/7}D_j<\infty.
\tag{NS24}
\]
The source kinetic identity supplies \(\sum_jD_j\le D_T\). The execution above has not established NS24 or the weaker actual \(\sum B_jD_j\) from that unweighted budget. This statement concerns precisely the residual weighted action in this attempted producing operation; it is not an assertion that a fixed actual history fails NS24, or that a signed cancellation in NS19 cannot give a better payment.

There is a separate useful velocity-only bound on each actual patch. Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\|\Lambda^2u\|_2^2\le\|\Lambda u\|_2 a_3\), hence
\[
\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}\|\Lambda^2u\|_2^2
\le2L_\nu B_j\sqrt{h_jD_j}
\le\sqrt{2c_\nu}L_\nu\sqrt{D_j}.
\tag{NS25}
\]
This removes the attained-stock factor from this one viscous action price, but its summation requires \(\sum\sqrt{D_j}\), which the present kinetic calculation also does not bound. It does not pay the adjacent \(u_t\) action by itself.

More generally, Fourier interpolation between spatial orders 1 and 3 gives, for \(1\le s\le3\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}\|\Lambda^s u\|_2^2
&\le R_j^{s-1}h_j^{(s-1)/2}D_j^{(3-s)/2}\\
&\le(2L_\nu)^{s-1}(c_\nu/2)^{(s-1)/2}D_j^{(3-s)/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NS25a}
\]
At the critical order \(s=3/2\), this is a genuine stock-independent patch payment by \(D_j^{3/4}\). For the first \(J\) complete patches, Hölder consequently gives
\[
\int_0^{t_J}\|\Lambda^{3/2}u\|_2^2
\le(2L_\nu)^{1/2}(c_\nu/2)^{1/4}J^{1/4}D_T^{3/4}.
\tag{NS25b}
\]
The bound is uniform in the patch's attained stock, without an upper derivative-order parameter, but retains the number of patches. Hence this executed interpolation does not provide a whole-terminal critical action bound when that number grows without bound. It gives a useful exact alternative payment, \(\sum D_j^{3/4}<\infty\), without claiming that the kinetic total already supplies it.

For context on the exact target, first-rectangle membership is equivalent to finite complete source action under the already paid finite-horizon kinetic/force budget and finite initial enstrophy. NS20 proves one direction. In the other direction, \(u\in L^2H^2\), \(u_t\in L^2L^2\) give \(u\in CH^1\) by the original adjacent trace theorem; the printed \(H^1\times H^1\to L^2\) product estimate then gives \(N(u)\in L^2L^2\), using \(u\in L^\infty H^1\) and \(\nabla u\in L^2H^1\). Canonical pressure supplies \(\nabla p=(I-\mathbb P)(f-N)\). Thus the full action in NS20 is finite. This equivalence does not produce its finite value from the datum; NS22–24 record the attempted payment. The exact auxiliary source passages are [the product estimate, LF192–211](../SOURCES.md#S114) and [the adjacent trace proof, unforced LF330–374](../SOURCES.md#S136).

## 9. A lawful overlap selection, and exactly what it buys

The construction is available on \([t_j,t_j+2h_j]\), so the next start may be selected in \([t_j+h_j/2,t_j+h_j]\). If
\(\widehat D_j=\int_{t_j}^{t_j+h_j}Z\), continuity and averaging give a point \(s_j\) in that last half with
\[
Z(s_j)\le2\widehat D_j/h_j.
\tag{NS26}
\]
Restart from the actual \(u(s_j)\); the canonical pressure and complete source/time jet stay on the same history by uniqueness. The selected duration lies between \(h_j/2\) and \(h_j\), so this introduces no artificially vanishing duration at a point with bounded \(H^3\) stock. NS12–14 remain valid with this shorter interval and the corresponding force tail; the lower duration envelope is only changed by a factor two.

NS26 selects a low-order endpoint stock. It provides no additional \(H^3\) bound for the restart beyond the original \(R_j\). The full source identity still telescopes over the selected partition, and NS21 still contains the same actual \(a_3Z\). Thus good kinetic times preserve compatibility and can improve a low-order face, but this executed selection has not converted the paid kinetic mass into a paid whole-terminal source square. No scalar model or independent jet assignment is used in this conclusion.

## 10. Exact outcome of the attempted promotion

The native datum construction has been advanced concretely: all finite orders share the original local interval; the kinetic budget gives the two explicit Fourier estimates; the original commutator yields the improved \(j^{6/7}\) stock bound and \(H^3\)-based \(B^{-5/6}\) lifespan; the full nonlinear/forcing/pressure square telescopes restart faces; and its actual weighted kinetic payment gives the explicit first-rectangle bound NS23. The good-time overlap selection is lawful and its limits are quantified.

For a whole finite-terminal rectangle through this route, the remaining producing step is a finite upper bound for \(\int_0^S\|f-N(u)\|_2^2\), uniformly in strict \(S<T_*\), or a bound sufficient to pay it, such as the actual weighted action in NS22. The analysis has not proved that payment from kinetic action alone. It also has not proved that it is impossible for the full actual Navier–Stokes coupling to supply it. No previous audit conclusion or claimed obstruction has been used as mathematical evidence.
