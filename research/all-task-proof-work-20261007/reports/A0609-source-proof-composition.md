## Completing the actual Gram and pressure equations

Define the original nonlinear generator and its derivative by
\[
K(u)=\nu\Delta u-B(u,u),\qquad
\mathcal A_uh=\nu\Delta h-B(u,h)-B(h,u),
\quad B(v,w)=\mathbb P[(v\cdot\nabla)w].
\]
The actual forced temporal rows satisfy
\[
V_1=K(u)+g,\qquad
V_2=\mathcal A_uK(u)+S_2,\qquad
S_2=\mathcal A_ug+g_t.
\tag{8}
\]
All terms of \(S_2\) have a whole-\(I\) L2 budget from the kinetic coordinate and prescribed source:
\[
\|S_2\|_{L^2L^2}\le
\nu\|\Delta g\|_{L^2L^2}+\|g_t\|_{L^2L^2}
+\sqrt{T_*}K_T\sup_I\|\nabla g\|_\infty
+\sup_I\|g\|_\infty\frac{K_T}{\sqrt{2\nu}}.
\tag{9}
\]
Incompressibility, self-adjoint projection and integration by parts give the exact new contraction
\[
\langle u,\mathcal A_uK(u)\rangle
=\langle\nu\Delta u+B(u,u),K(u)\rangle
=\nu^2\|\Delta u\|_2^2-\|B(u,u)\|_2^2.
\tag{10}
\]
The omitted cross-allocation would change this equation: both \(B(u,K)\) and \(B(K,u)\) have been retained. Also
\[
\langle u,S_2\rangle
=\frac{d}{dt}\langle u,g\rangle+2\langle B,g\rangle-\|g\|_2^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (8)–(11) with the actual kinetic Gram anti-diagonal
\(E_0''=\|V_1\|_2^2+\langle u,V_2\rangle\)
returns precisely the full squared momentum equation. Thus the completed second-row Gram contraction retains the nonlinear square with its actual sign; it does not isolate a positive terminal entry.

Specifically, with \(X(S)=\int_0^S(\|u_t\|_2^2+\nu^2\|\Delta u\|_2^2)\),
\[
X(S)+\int_0^S\|\nabla p\|_2^2+\nu\|\nabla u(S)\|_2^2
=\nu\|\nabla u_0\|_2^2+\int_0^S\|f-b\|_2^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Orthogonality, using the actual pressure equation, gives equivalently
\[
X(S)+\nu\|\nabla u(S)\|_2^2
=\nu\|\nabla u_0\|_2^2+\int_0^S\|g-B\|_2^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Moreover \(\langle B,g\rangle=-\int u_i u_j\partial_jg_i\), so its absolute time integral is bounded by \(K_T^2\int_I\|\nabla g\|_\infty\). The source and its cross term are paid at arbitrary amplitude. The intrinsic term \(\int\|B\|_2^2\) remains in this actual budget.

The simultaneous contraction supplies a real quantitative improvement over the paper's first-rectangle estimate. Write \(y_0=\|\nabla u_0\|_2^2\), \(\Phi=\int_I\|g\|_2^2\), and let \(\mathcal S_2\) denote the known upper bound in (9). Equations (11) and (13) give exactly
\[
X(S)+\nu\|\nabla u(S)\|_2^2
=\nu y_0+\int_0^S\|B\|_2^2+\mathcal R_g(S),
\]
\[
\mathcal R_g(S)=
\langle u(S),g(S)\rangle-\langle u_0,g(0)\rangle
-\int_0^S\langle u,S_2\rangle.
\]
The entire source remainder has the known upper bound
\[
A_g=\min\left\{
K_T\sup_I\|g\|_2-\langle u_0,g(0)\rangle+
K_T\sqrt{T_*}\mathcal S_2,\quad
\Phi+2K_T^2\int_I\|\operatorname{Sym}\nabla g\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}
\right\}.
\]
The Fourier \(H^2\) embedding has squared constant \(1/(8\pi)\). Also
\(\sup_{t\le S}\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2\le y_0+X(S)/\nu\), directly from the differentiated gradient energy and \(2ab\le a^2+b^2\). The kinetic budget gives
\(\int_0^S\|u\|_{H^2}^2\le L+X(S)/\nu^2\), with \(L=T_*K_T^2+K_T^2/\nu\). Therefore
\[
X(S)\le\nu y_0+A_g+
\frac{(y_0+X(S)/\nu)(L+X(S)/\nu^2)}{8\pi}.
\tag{17}
\]
Define
\[

b=\frac{y_0/\nu^2+L/\nu}{8\pi},\quad
c=\nu y_0+A_g+\frac{y_0L}{8\pi}.

If \(b<1\) and \((1-b)^2>4ac\), continuity from \(X(0)=0\) traps \(X(S)\) below
\[
