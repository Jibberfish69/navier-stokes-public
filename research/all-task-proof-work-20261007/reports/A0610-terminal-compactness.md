This is a signed datum-only prefix bound on the actual whole-terminal history. It does not require $\int_I|p\,\partial u|$.

### The same operation pays additional fractional time regularity

For any fixed bounded matrix test $\Psi$ define $A_\Psi(t)=\int\Psi_{ij}u_i u_j$. The exact quadratic difference and the kinetic bound give

\[
 |A_\Psi(t+h)-A_\Psi(t)|
 \le C K\|\Psi\|_\infty\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_2 .
\]

Thus the datum-paid G9 norm constructs $A_\Psi\in H^{2/5}(I)$, with its Slobodeckij seminorm bounded by $C K^2\|\Psi\|_\infty^2[u]_{H^{2/5}(I;L^2)}^2$. This retains the same actual two-time evaluations and interval.

Write $W_\Psi(S)=\int_0^S L_\Psi(t)\,dt$, where the component calculation above is contracted with $\Psi$. Its total variation is datum-paid. For $0<h<T$,

\[
 \int_0^{T-h}|W_\Psi(t+h)-W_\Psi(t)|^2\,dt
 \le h\|L_\Psi\|_{L^1(I)}^2 .
\]

Consequently $W_\Psi\in H^\alpha(I)$ for each $\alpha<1/2$; its seminorm is at most

\[
 \|L_\Psi\|_{L^1}^2\,\frac{T^{1-2\alpha}}{1-2\alpha}.
\]

It follows directly that the signed pressure-strain prefix is datum-paid in $H^{2/5}(I)\cap L^\infty(I)$, in addition to the explicit signed bound above.

There is also an actual vector-valued pressure gain. For every $s>3/2$, the Fourier $L^1_x\to H^{-s}_x$ bound and the full pressure normalization give

\[
 \|p(t+h)-p(t)\|_{H^{-s}}
 \le C_s K\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_2+
                  \|\Phi_f(t+h)-\Phi_f(t)\|_{H^{-s}}.
\]

The force potential is smooth in these spaces. Hence the same G9 operation constructs

\[
 p\in H^{2/5}(I;H^{-s})\cap L^\infty(I;H^{-s}),\qquad s>3/2 .
\]

This is a new datum-paid whole-interval pressure/time domain. It is weaker than a pointwise canonical terminal pressure trace, and it has not been relabeled as one.

### Execute the next compatible time-pressure row and keep its endpoint quantifier

The original [native pressure row (20)](../SOURCES.md#S152) differentiated once is

\[

\]

For a fixed scalar $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$, let $\Psi_{ij}=R_iR_j\varphi$. These functions and their needed spatial derivatives are bounded. Testing the tensor identity with this matrix is justified first on each strict interval by spatial cutoffs, then by their removal: all terms have the spatial integrability used above on a strict classical interval. The datum payments of the remaining terms persist uniformly for $S<T$.

Since Riesz products are self-adjoint,

\[
 \langle p(t),\varphi\rangle=A_\Psi(t)-\langle\Phi_f(t),\varphi\rangle .
\]

Substituting the exact tested tensor evolution into this next pressure row therefore gives

\[
 \int_0^S\langle Q_1(t),\varphi\rangle\,dt
 =\mathcal Z_\Psi(S)+\int_0^S L_\Psi(t)\,dt
          -\langle\Phi_f(S)-\Phi_f(0),\varphi\rangle

\]

Its signed prefix is uniformly datum-bounded, and it retains every pressure-strain allocation and the smooth prescribed source derivative. This is a producing calculation, not an invocation of a pressure endpoint theorem.

The exact convergence quantifier remains visible. For $S_1<S_2<T$,

\[

 =A_\Psi(S_2)-A_\Psi(S_1)-\int_{S_1}^{S_2}L_\Psi(t)\,dt .

The last integral tends to zero uniformly as both times approach $T$, because $L_\Psi\in L^1(I)$.
