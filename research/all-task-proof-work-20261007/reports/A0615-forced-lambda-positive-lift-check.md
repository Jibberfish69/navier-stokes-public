Let \(A=-\Delta\), \(N(a,b)=(a\cdot\nabla)b\), \(g=\mathbb Pf\), and

\[
\chi=-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot f,\quad p_0=p-\chi,
\quad h=u_t-g,\quad k=h_t=u_{tt}-g_t.
\]

For a known solenoidal force row \(a\), define the **raw unprojected** source

\[
J_a=-\nu Aa-N(u,a)-N(a,u).
\]

The exact field equations are

\[
u_t+\nu Au+N(u,u)+\nabla p=f,
\]
\[
h_t+\nu Ah+N(u,h)+N(h,u)+\nabla p_{0,t}=J_g,
\]
\[
k_t+\nu Ak+N(u,k)+N(k,u)+2N(h,h)+\nabla p_{0,tt}=F_k,
\tag{L1}
\]
\[
\boxed{F_k=J_{g_t}-2N(h,g)-2N(g,h)-2N(g,g).}
\]

The pressure signs follow by canceling exactly \(f_t-g_t=\nabla\chi_t\) in the first differentiated original row, then differentiating that raw equation. In the latter step \(u_t=h+g\); its two contributions to the raw source each supply one copy of \(-N(h,g)-N(g,h)\). The canonical pressures in (L1) are correct with the displayed raw sources. Replacing those sources by their Leray projections while retaining the same unprojected pressure would require the corresponding gradient adjustment. The physical gradient force has been canceled by its actual potential, not discarded.

Put \(e=\|u\|_2^2\), \(Y=\|\nabla u\|_2^2\), \(b=e+\delta\), \(\lambda=\nu Y/b\), \(w=h+\lambda u\), \(\delta>0\). Choose fixed \(a,c>0\) with \(ac>1/4\). The field-dependent matrix is

\[
Q_\lambda=
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^2+a&\lambda&1/2\\
\lambda&1&0\\1/2&0&c\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&1&0\end{pmatrix}
+\begin{pmatrix}a&0&1/2\\0&0&0\\1/2&0&c\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{L2}
\]

The second summand is positive on the \((u,k)\) block. Thus \(Q_\lambda\) is positive, and

\[
\frac12\sum (Q_\lambda)'_{rs}\langle H_r,H_s\rangle
=\lambda'\langle w,u\rangle=-\delta\lambda\lambda'.
\tag{L3}
\]

Adding the derivative of \(\delta\lambda^2/2\) gives the exact positive energy

\[
E_\lambda=R_w+\frac a2e+\frac12\langle u,k\rangle
+\frac c2\|k\|_2^2,
\qquad R_w=\frac12(\|w\|_2^2+\delta\lambda^2),
\]
\[
D_\lambda=\|\nabla w\|_2^2+aY
+\langle\nabla u,\nabla k\rangle+c\|\nabla k\|_2^2.
\tag{L4}
\]

Let \(\theta=c-1/(4a)>0\). Completion of the actual \((u,k)\) block gives

\[
E_\lambda=R_w+\frac a2\|u+k/(2a)\|_2^2
+\frac\theta2\|k\|_2^2\ge R_w+\frac\theta2\|k\|_2^2,
\]
\[
D_\lambda=\|\nabla w\|_2^2
+a\|\nabla u+\nabla k/(2a)\|_2^2
+\theta\|\nabla k\|_2^2.
\tag{L5}
\]

The temporal-block algebra has \(Q_{11}-2Q_{02}=0\) and \(Q_{12}=0\). It therefore cancels the intrinsic first-response strain exactly and retains only the complete highest current

\[
R_k=S(u,k,k)+2\langle k,N(h,h)\rangle,
\qquad S(u,k,k)=\langle k,\operatorname{Sym}(\nabla u)k\rangle.
\]

The resulting exact law, including the coefficient source, is

\[
\boxed{E_\lambda'+\nu D_\lambda=-cR_k+W_G,}
\tag{L6}
\]
\[
\boxed{W_G=\langle w,J_g\rangle+\lambda\langle w,g\rangle+aF
+\tfrac12\langle k,g\rangle+\tfrac12\langle u,F_k\rangle
+c\langle k,F_k\rangle,\qquad F=\langle u,g\rangle.}
\]

These pairings may use projected sources in a global solenoidal energy test; the full raw \(F_k\) remains as displayed in (L1), with its physical pressure companion. No pressure norm or flux is deleted from the field or square identities.

For the known kinetic bound \(\|u\|_2\le K\), (L5) gives

\[
\|w\|_2^2\le2E_\lambda,\quad
\lambda^2\le2E_\lambda/\delta,\quad
\|k\|_2^2\le2E_\lambda/\theta,
\quad\|h\|_2^2\le(4+4K^2/\delta)E_\lambda.
\tag{L7}
\]

The complete force can be paid with this energy and known kinetic action. The only transports requiring gradient absorption have the exact integrations by parts

\[
\langle w,N(g,u)\rangle=-\langle N(g,w),u\rangle,
\quad\langle k,N(g_t,u)\rangle=-\langle N(g_t,k),u\rangle,
\]
\[
\langle k,N(g,h)\rangle=-\langle N(g,k),h\rangle,
\quad\langle u,N(g,h)\rangle=-\langle N(g,u),h\rangle,
\quad\langle u,N(g_t,u)\rangle=0.
\tag{L8}
\]

For example, the first three are bounded by \(\|g\|_\infty K\|\nabla w\|\), \(\|g_t\|_\infty K\|\nabla k\|\) and \(\|g\|_\infty\|h\|\|\nabla k\|\), with their actual coefficients from (L6). Young absorption into the positive terms in (L5) leaves known force factors times \(E_\lambda\) or known data. The fourth is bounded by \(\|g\|_\infty\sqrt Y\|h\|\), leaving linear energy plus known kinetic \(Y\). Transports with \(g\) or \(g_t\) in the differentiated receiving slot use their prescribed \(\|\nabla g\|_\infty\), \(\|\nabla g_t\|_\infty\) directly; \(N(g,g)\), \(Ag\) and \(Ag_t\) are known force rows. Finally \(|\lambda\langle w,g\rangle|\le2\|g\|_2E_\lambda/\sqrt\delta\).

Consequently, for known nonnegative coefficients \(C_1(t),C_2(t),C_3(t)\) depending only on \(\nu,a,c,\delta,K\) and the stipulated norms of \(g,g_t\), all actual terms in \(W_G\) satisfy a payment of the form

\[
|W_G|\le\frac\nu2D_\lambda+C_1(t)E_\lambda+C_2(t)Y+C_3(t).
\tag{L9}
\]

On any finite force horizon these coefficients are bounded or integrable using its smooth force norms, and \(\int Y\) is the known kinetic action. This payment leaves the intrinsic signed \(R_k\) intact; it establishes no datum-only bound for that current. Thus the first-response cancellation and complete forced positive lift are verified, while the highest temporal nonlinear current remains the exact input for any further proof operation.
