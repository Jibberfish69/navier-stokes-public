\section{Derivative and Finite-Record Calculus}\label{app:DerivativeAndFiniteRecordCalculus}\label{sec:FromCriticalHeightToCompatibleFiniteViews}

If one response becomes unbounded before a finite time, its next time derivative cannot remain integrable in the same fixed space.  Applying that fact to Navier--Stokes produces temporal rows containing transport, pressure, and viscosity.  Spatial differentiation opens the corresponding columns, and adjacent Sobolev pairs give those rows a common time trace.

Every whole-terminal rectangle contains derivatives of the same velocity and normalized pressure.  The restriction maps make that agreement exact; the adjacent trace then produces one endpoint, the differentiated equation produces its complete jet, and local uniqueness joins the restarted solution to the history on the left.
