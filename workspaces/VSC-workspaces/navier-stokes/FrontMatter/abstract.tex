We prove global existence and smoothness for the unforced three-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equations on \(\R^3\), for every viscosity \(\nu>0\) and every divergence-free Schwartz initial velocity.
Assume that the maximal classical time \(T_*\) is finite.
The energy identity still bounds kinetic energy, but continuation theory forces both the critical \(L^3\) norm and the half-derivative height
\[
  H(t)=\frac12\|\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)\|_2^2
\]
to escape every finite bound.
A perfectly balanced stretching vortex gives that statement its physical form: the same fluid would have to complete infinitely many critical-height first passages before \(T_*\).
Repeated integration in time then forces every fixed temporal derivative of the velocity to lose the same critical control.

Differentiating the original equation generates the complete time-and-space derivative Tower, including the pressure response.
Viscosity opens a spatial Sobolev column inside each temporal row, so the very rows forced toward critical escape also contain stronger spatial regularity.
The whole-terminal rectangle estimate controls every finite piece of this Tower, and compatibility places all of those pieces in one intersection belonging to the same fluid history.
Its lowest rectangles bound the \(L^3\) norm, critical height, stretching action, and high-frequency tail; its higher rectangles give one common terminal velocity in \(H^\infty\).
Local well-posedness and uniqueness continue that velocity beyond \(T_*\), contradicting maximality.
Thus \(T_*=\infty\), the velocity and normalized pressure are smooth for all time, and kinetic energy remains bounded by its initial value.
