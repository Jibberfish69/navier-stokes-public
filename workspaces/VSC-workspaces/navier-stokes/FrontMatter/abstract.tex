We prove that, for every viscosity \(\nu>0\) and every divergence-free Schwartz initial velocity \(u_0\) on \(\R^3\), the maximal classical solution of the unforced three-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equations is global, with velocity and pressure smooth for all time.
If its lifespan ended at a finite \(T_*\), every fixed finite block of temporal and spatial derivatives of that same solution would remain uniformly controlled over all cutoffs \(T<T_*\).
The equation supplies the time regularity needed to extend the solution to \(T_*\) in every finite Sobolev space; compatibility on overlaps identifies these extensions with one terminal velocity \(u_*\in H^\infty\).
Local well-posedness continues the original solution from \(u_*\) beyond \(T_*\), contradicting maximality.
The global solution obeys the energy identity, which bounds kinetic energy by its initial value.
