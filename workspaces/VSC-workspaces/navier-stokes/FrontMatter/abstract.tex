In three dimensions, the kinetic-energy law does not see how narrow a
sharpening velocity field has become.  Let \(u\) be the maximal classical
solution of the unforced incompressible Navier--Stokes equations on \(\R^3\),
generated by a divergence-free Schwartz datum \(u_0\) at viscosity \(\nu>0\),
and suppose its maximal interval ends at \(T_*<\infty\).  The first adjacent
Sobolev rectangle reaches that endpoint exactly when the critical dissipation
is finite:

\[
  D_*:=\int_0^{T_*}\norm{\Lambda^{3/2}u(t)}_2^2\,dt<\infty
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \begin{cases}
    u\in L^2(0,T_*;H^2),\\
    u_t\in L^2(0,T_*;L^2).
  \end{cases}
\]
The critical-height balance makes this equivalent to keeping the signed
critical production accumulated before \(T_*\) bounded above.

The direct enstrophy route shows why the energy law does not supply this
control.  With \(Z=\norm{\Lambda u}_2^2\) and
\(Y=\norm{\Lambda^2u}_2^2\), it stops at
\[
  Z'+\nu Y\leq C\nu^{-3}Z^3,
\]
while energy gives only \(\int_0^{T_*}Z(t)\,dt<\infty\).  When
\(D_*<\infty\), the adjacent trace gives
\(u\in C([0,T_*];H^1)\subset L^\infty(0,T_*;L^3)\), so the
Escauriaza--Seregin--\v{S}ver\'ak endpoint criterion excludes
\(T_*<\infty\).  The lowest rectangle is already enough for this
contradiction.

If the corresponding whole-terminal bound holds for every finite rectangle,
their agreement on overlaps carries the differentiated flow to one
\(H^\infty\) terminal state.  Local existence launches a smooth solution from
that state, and uniqueness identifies it as the continuation of the same
history beyond \(T_*\).  At the smooth endpoint the critical height has a
finite roof:
it cannot pass through infinitely many doublings, distinct fixed material
labels remain apart, and the local scale-invariant content required by a
singular point tends to zero.  No estimate proved here controls the cubic
enstrophy term from the kinetic-energy bound, so this manuscript's
datum-to-global conclusion through the lowest rectangle remains conditional
on \(D_*<\infty\).
