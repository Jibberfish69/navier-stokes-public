The three-dimensional Navier--Stokes regularity problem asks whether a smooth
solution can lose its smoothness in finite time.  Fix smooth, divergence-free,
finite-energy initial data \(u_0\), fix \(\nu>0\), and let \((u,p)\) be the
corresponding maximal classical solution on \([0,T_*)\).
We want to prove that \(T_*\) cannot be finite.
Classical local theory supplies this solution on a
nonzero interval and makes it unique there
\parencite{FujitaKato1964,Kato1984}.  In symbols, the target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GlobalTimeTarget}
  T_*=\infty.
\end{equation}

Fefferman's formulation of the whole-space Millennium Prize Problem takes
\(u_0\) to be smooth and divergence-free and imposes a decay estimate on every
spatial derivative at every polynomial order \parencite{Fefferman2000}.  We
write this class as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FeffermanDatumClass}
  \Ssigma
  :=
  \left\{
  a\in C^\infty(\R^3;\R^3):
  \begin{array}{l}
    \nabla\cdot a=0,\\[0.3em]
    \forall\alpha\in\Nzero^3\;
    \forall K\in\Nzero\;
    \exists C_{\alpha,K}<\infty\;
    \forall x\in\R^3,\\[0.3em]
    |\partial_x^\alpha a(x)|
    \leq C_{\alpha,K}(1+|x|)^{-K}
  \end{array}
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
We take \(u_0\in\Ssigma\).
Here \(\alpha\) is a spatial multi-index and \(K\) is any prescribed polynomial
decay order.  The constant \(C_{\alpha,K}\) may change when either one changes.
Because the estimate holds for every \(K\), the field and each of its spatial
derivatives decay faster than every polynomial power.

For finite \(m\in\Nzero\), write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DivergenceFreeSobolevClass}
  H_\sigma^m(\R^3)
  :=
  \left\{
    v\in H^m(\R^3;\R^3):\nabla\cdot v=0
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
Fix \(m\) and choose an integer \(K>3/2\).  For every
\(|\alpha|\leq m\), the decay estimate gives
\[
  \norm{\partial_x^\alpha u_0}_2^2
  \leq
  C_{\alpha,K}^2
  \int_{\R^3}(1+|x|)^{-2K}\,dx
  <\infty.
\]
Summing over the finitely many multi-indices with \(|\alpha|\leq m\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FeffermanDatumSobolevRows}
  \Ssigma\subset H_\sigma^m(\R^3)
  \qquad\text{for every finite }m.
\end{equation}

The problem asks for velocity and pressure fields satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NavierStokesSystem}
\begin{aligned}
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p&=\nu\Delta u,
  \\
  \nabla\cdot u&=0,
  \\
  u(\cdot,0)&=u_0
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{on }\R^3.
\end{equation}
We use the decaying whole-space normalization for the pressure.  The required
conclusion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<\infty.
\end{equation}
Smoothness here includes the endpoint of every finite interval.  It is stronger
than being smooth on every shorter interval \([0,T]\) with \(T<T_*\), because a
derivative can remain finite on each such interval and still have no finite
trace at \(T_*\).
