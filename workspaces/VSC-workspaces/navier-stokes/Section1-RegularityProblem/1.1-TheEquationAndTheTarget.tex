\subsection{The whole-space regularity problem}\label{sub:TheEquationAndTheTarget}

Fefferman's whole-space formulation fixes a positive viscosity, no external force, and a smooth divergence-free initial velocity on \(\R^3\) whose derivatives decay faster than every power at infinity \parencite{Fefferman2000}.
It asks whether the velocity and pressure generated by every such datum remain smooth for all time while kinetic energy stays bounded.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AdmissibleDatumClass}
  \Ssigma
  :=
  \left\{
    a\in\mathscr S(\R^3;\R^3):
    \nabla\cdot a=0
  \right\},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathscr S\) is the Schwartz class.
Fix \(u_0\in\Ssigma\) and \(\nu>0\).
The unforced incompressible Navier--Stokes equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NavierStokesSystem}
\begin{aligned}
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p&=\nu\Delta u,
  \\
  \nabla\cdot u&=0,
  \\
  u(\cdot,0)&=u_0
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{on }\R^3.
\end{equation}
The required conclusion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<\infty.
\end{equation}

Classical local theory gives a unique smooth solution on a nonzero interval \parencite{FujitaKato1964,Kato1984}.
Continue that solution for as long as classical regularity permits, and denote its maximal interval by \([0,T_*)\), where \(0<T_*\leq\infty\).
The problem is to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GlobalTimeTarget}
  T_*=\infty.
\end{equation}

If \(T_*\) were finite, the solution would still be smooth at every earlier time.
Any failure would be confined to the approach \(t\uparrow T_*\), where a bound strong enough to continue the same solution would have to escape.
