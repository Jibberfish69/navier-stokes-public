The three-dimensional Navier--Stokes regularity problem asks whether a
smooth solution can lose its smoothness in finite time. Fix smooth,
divergence-free, finite-energy initial data \(u_0\), fix \(\nu>0\), and let
\((u,p)\) be the corresponding maximal classical solution on \([0,T_*)\).
We want to prove that \(T_*\) cannot be finite.

The whole-space formulation sets the external force to zero and takes
\parencite{Fefferman2000}
\[
  \Ssigma
  :=
  \left\{
    a\in\mathscr S(\R^3;\R^3):
    \nabla\cdot a=0
  \right\},
  \qquad
  u_0\in\Ssigma,
\]
where \(\mathscr S\) denotes the Schwartz class. The velocity and pressure
satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NavierStokesSystem}
\begin{aligned}
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p&=\nu\Delta u,
  \\
  \nabla\cdot u&=0,
  \\
  u(\cdot,0)&=u_0
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{on }\R^3.
\end{equation}
The required global conclusion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<\infty.
\end{equation}

Classical local theory supplies the maximal history named above, with
\(0<T_*\leq\infty\) \parencite{FujitaKato1964,Kato1984}. The global problem is
exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GlobalTimeTarget}
  T_*=\infty.
\end{equation}
