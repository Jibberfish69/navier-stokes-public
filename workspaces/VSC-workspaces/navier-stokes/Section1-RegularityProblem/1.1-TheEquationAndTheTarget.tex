Fefferman's whole-space problem fixes \(\nu>0\), starts from a smooth divergence-free
velocity \(u_0\) on \(\R^3\) whose derivatives decay faster than every power at
infinity, and asks whether the Navier--Stokes evolution from that datum stays smooth
for all time \parencite{Fefferman2000}.
In the notation used here,
\[
  \Ssigma
  :=
  \left\{
    a\in\mathscr S(\R^3;\R^3):
    \nabla\cdot a=0
  \right\},
  \qquad
  u_0\in\Ssigma,
\]
where \(\mathscr S\) is the Schwartz class.
For the unforced equation, the velocity and pressure satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NavierStokesSystem}
\begin{aligned}
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p&=\nu\Delta u,
  \\
  \nabla\cdot u&=0,
  \\
  u(\cdot,0)&=u_0
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{on }\R^3.
\end{equation}
The nonlinear term transports velocity through its own field, pressure enforces the divergence constraint across that field, and viscosity diffuses spatial variation.
We take the pressure with its decaying whole-space normalization.
The target is the existence of this one pair for every \(t\geq0\), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<\infty.
\end{equation}

Classical local theory gives a unique classical solution on a nonzero interval \parencite{FujitaKato1964,Kato1984}.
Let \([0,T_*)\), with \(0<T_*\leq\infty\), be its maximal classical interval.
The global conclusion above is exactly the statement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GlobalTimeTarget}
  T_*=\infty.
\end{equation}
