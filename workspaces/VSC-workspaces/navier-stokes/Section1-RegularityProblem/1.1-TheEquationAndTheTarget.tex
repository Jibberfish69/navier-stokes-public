Local theory already gives the motion a beginning.  Fix a smooth,
divergence-free, finite-energy velocity \(u_0\) and a viscosity \(\nu>0\).
There is then a unique maximal classical velocity--pressure pair \((u,p)\) on
an interval \([0,T_*)\) \parencite{FujitaKato1964,Kato1984}.  The regularity
problem asks whether this interval can have a finite right endpoint.  We shall
prove that it cannot:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GlobalTimeTarget}
  T_*=\infty.
\end{equation}

Fefferman's whole-space formulation rules out any difficulty coming from rough
initial data or a slowly decaying tail.  Every spatial derivative of \(u_0\)
must decay faster than every polynomial power \parencite{Fefferman2000}.  Write
the resulting divergence-free class as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FeffermanDatumClass}
  \Ssigma
  :=
  \left\{
  a\in C^\infty(\R^3;\R^3):
  \begin{array}{l}
    \nabla\cdot a=0,\\[0.3em]
    \forall\alpha\in\Nzero^3\;
    \forall K\in\Nzero\;
    \exists C_{\alpha,K}<\infty\;
    \forall x\in\R^3,\\[0.3em]
    |\partial_x^\alpha a(x)|
    \leq C_{\alpha,K}(1+|x|)^{-K}
  \end{array}
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
We take \(u_0\in\Ssigma\).  The quantifiers in
\eqref{eq:FeffermanDatumClass} matter.  After a spatial derivative \(\alpha\)
has been chosen, any polynomial decay order \(K\) may be demanded; only the
constant \(C_{\alpha,K}\) is allowed to change.  Thus the datum arrives with
every finite derivative and with more decay than any one later estimate will
need.

For finite \(m\in\Nzero\), write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DivergenceFreeSobolevClass}
  H_\sigma^m(\R^3)
  :=
  \left\{
    v\in H^m(\R^3;\R^3):\nabla\cdot v=0
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
This pointwise condition immediately supplies every finite Sobolev row.  Fix
\(m\), choose an integer \(K>3/2\), and apply the decay estimate to each
\(|\alpha|\leq m\):
\[
  \norm{\partial_x^\alpha u_0}_2^2
  \leq
  C_{\alpha,K}^2
  \int_{\R^3}(1+|x|)^{-2K}\,dx
  <\infty.
\]
There are only finitely many such multi-indices, so summing gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FeffermanDatumSobolevRows}
  \Ssigma\subset H_\sigma^m(\R^3)
  \qquad\text{for every finite }m.
\end{equation}

The equation must carry this all-orders datum through time:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NavierStokesSystem}
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot u=0,
  \qquad
  u(\cdot,0)=u_0
  \quad\text{on }\R^3.
\end{equation}
Incompressibility also fixes the pressure once the velocity is known.  Taking
the divergence of \eqref{eq:NavierStokesSystem} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:OpeningPressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j).
\end{equation}
and the decaying whole-space normalization selects the unique pressure in this
class.  The required conclusion is therefore the global smoothness of the
entire pair, together with the finite-energy bound:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<\infty.
\end{equation}
The endpoint is the whole issue.  Classicality already makes every derivative
finite on each shorter interval \([0,T]\) with \(T<T_*\).  Those bounds may
still deteriorate as \(T\uparrow T_*\), leaving no finite trace at the last
time.  Global regularity requires estimates that survive that approach.
