The three-dimensional Navier--Stokes regularity problem asks whether a smooth solution can lose its smoothness in finite time.
Fefferman formulates the whole-space problem for initial velocities in the class \(\Ssigma\) \parencite{Fefferman2000}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FeffermanDatumClass}
  \Ssigma
  :=
  \left\{
  a\in C^\infty(\R^3;\R^3):
  \begin{array}{l}
    \nabla\cdot a=0,\\[0.3em]
    \forall\alpha\in\Nzero^3\;
    \forall K\in\Nzero\;
    \exists C_{\alpha,K}<\infty\;
    \forall x\in\R^3,\\[0.3em]
    |\partial_x^\alpha a(x)|
    \leq C_{\alpha,K}(1+|x|)^{-K}
  \end{array}
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
We fix \(u_0\in\Ssigma\) and a viscosity \(\nu>0\).
Here \(\alpha\) is a spatial multi-index, \(K\) is the chosen decay order, and \(C_{\alpha,K}\) may depend on both.
Since the estimate is available for every \(K\), each spatial derivative of \(u_0\) decays faster than any polynomial.
Taking \(\alpha=0\) and \(K>3/2\) gives \(u_0\in L^2(\R^3)\), so the datum has finite kinetic energy.

The velocity \(u\) and pressure \(p\) must satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NavierStokesSystem}
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot u=0,
  \qquad
  u(\cdot,0)=u_0
  \quad\text{on }\R^3\times(0,\infty).
\end{equation}
The required conclusion is that, for every \(u_0\in\Ssigma\) and every \(\nu>0\), such fields exist for all nonnegative time and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx<\infty.
\end{equation}
Smoothness in \eqref{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget} means that every spatial and temporal derivative of both fields exists and is continuous for all \(t\geq0\).
Regularity is the assertion that this smoothness persists throughout the evolution.
A continuation past a finite time \(T\) extends the original velocity to \([0,T+\varepsilon)\), for some \(\varepsilon>0\), while solving the same initial-value problem there.
Finite-time loss of regularity occurs when this smooth extension fails at some finite \(T\).
