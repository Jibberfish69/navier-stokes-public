\subsection{The perfectly balanced rip-cord vortex}\label{sub:ThePerfectlyBalancedRipCordVortex}

Picture one smooth vortex held exactly on its axis.
The surrounding fluid pulls it lengthwise.
Incompressibility makes that extension narrow the cross-section at the same time, so the spiral around the centre winds tighter and the velocity changes more sharply across the core.
Now remove the ordinary ways that this alignment can be lost: no wobble, no buckling, no fragmentation, and no off-centre drift.
Each narrower state becomes the starting state for the next stretch.
The question is whether the same fluid can complete infinitely many such tightenings before one finite time.

This is the perfectly balanced rip-cord vortex.
It is not asserted to be an exact special solution.
It is the hardest clean version of the stretching picture: the geometry is kept favourable to concentration, and the fixed-viscosity equation is left responsible for stopping it.

Write \(\omega:=\nabla\times u\) and
\(S:=\tfrac12\bigl(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)\).
Taking the curl of \eqref{eq:NavierStokesSystem} and using incompressibility
gives the exact competition inside the drawing:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VorticityEquation}
  D_t\omega
  :=\partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega
  =S\omega+\nu\Delta\omega.
\end{equation}
The term \(S\omega\) can amplify vorticity aligned with an expanding strain direction.
The Laplacian acts on the spatial curvature created by the tightening.
Pressure does not appear after curl is taken, but it still shapes the velocity and strain that enforce incompressibility around the core.
Stretching, transverse contraction, pressure response, and diffusion are simultaneous actions of one fluid.

The vortex becomes a singularity scenario only when the whole solution loses continuation control.
At the critical endpoint, the theorem of Escauriaza, Seregin, and \v{S}ver\'ak says that a bounded \(L^\infty_tL^3_x\) norm would carry the solution regularly through \(T_*\) \parencite{EscauriazaSereginSverak2003}.
Hence a finite maximal time forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FiniteTimeLThreeEscape}
  T_*<\infty
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}\norm{u(t)}_3=\infty.
\end{equation}
A radius alone cannot distinguish genuine growth from a change of scale.
The narrowing now needs a quantity unchanged by Navier--Stokes rescaling and strong enough to feel the global escape in \eqref{eq:FiniteTimeLThreeEscape}.
