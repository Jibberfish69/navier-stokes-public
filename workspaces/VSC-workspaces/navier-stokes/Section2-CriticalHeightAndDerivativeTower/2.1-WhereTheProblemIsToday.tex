\subsection{Where the Problem Is Today}\label{sub:ThePerfectlyStretchingVortex}

The hard case developed here is a vortex that remains aligned with a positive extensional direction of the strain.  The strain amplifies its vorticity; incompressibility contracts the cross-section of the material filament; and that contraction sharpens precisely the gradients on which viscosity acts.  Bending, wobble, loss of alignment, or fragmentation would break the repetition.  The case we are testing gives us none of those exits: the vortex stretches, narrows, and remains aligned to stretch again.

Let
\[
  \omega:=\nabla\times u,
  \qquad
  S:=\frac12\bigl(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bigr).
\]
Taking the curl of Navier--Stokes puts the two sides of that motion in the same equation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VorticityEquation}
  \partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega
  =S\omega+\nu\Delta\omega.
\end{equation}
When \(\omega\) points along an eigenvector of \(S\) with positive eigenvalue, \(S\omega\) amplifies it.  The corresponding extension contracts the filament's cross-section and concentrates the vorticity.  Viscosity responds through \(\nu\Delta\omega\), diffusing the gradients created by that same concentration.  The question is whether this contest changes when the concentration moves to a smaller scale.

Going to a smaller scale might have broken that balance by giving viscosity a relative advantage.  Navier--Stokes does not give it one.  Replace a characteristic length \(\ell\) by \(\ell/\lambda\), with \(\lambda>1\), through
\[
  u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \qquad
  p_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2p(\lambda x,\lambda^2t).
\]
The four terms in the momentum equation transform together:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \partial_tu_\lambda
  &=\lambda^3(\partial_tu)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  &
  (u_\lambda\cdot\nabla)u_\lambda
  &=\lambda^3((u\cdot\nabla)u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \\
  \nabla p_\lambda
  &=\lambda^3(\nabla p)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  &
  \nu\Delta u_\lambda
  &=\lambda^3\nu(\Delta u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t).
\end{aligned}
\]
The vortex variables make the physical consequence even sharper:
\[
  \omega_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2\omega(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \qquad
  S_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2S(\lambda x,\lambda^2t).
\]
Both \(S_\lambda\omega_\lambda\) and \(\nu\Delta\omega_\lambda\) acquire the factor \(\lambda^4\).  Scaling does not show that the alignment persists, nor does it equate the magnitudes of the two terms.  It shows that, if the aligned vortex does persist to a smaller scale, scale alone gives viscosity no relative advantage: stretching and diffusion carry the same homogeneity.  That is the critical balance.

A balance that survives concentration needs a quantity capable of seeing the concentrated profile as the same size.  The homogeneous Sobolev scale tells us exactly where that happens.  Since
\[
  \widehat{u_\lambda}(\xi,t)
  =\lambda^{-2}\widehat u(\xi/\lambda,\lambda^2t),
\]
the change of variables \(\eta=\xi/\lambda\) gives
\begin{align*}
  \norm{u_\lambda(t)}_{\dot H^s}^2
  &=\int_{\R^3}
  |\xi|^{2s}\lambda^{-4}
  \left|\widehat u(\xi/\lambda,\lambda^2t)\right|^2
  \,d\xi
  \\
  &=\lambda^{2s-1}\int_{\R^3}
  |\eta|^{2s}
  \left|\widehat u(\eta,\lambda^2t)\right|^2
  \,d\eta
  \\
  &=\lambda^{2s-1}\norm{u(\lambda^2t)}_{\dot H^s}^2,
  \\
  \norm{u_\lambda(t)}_{\dot H^s}
  &=\lambda^{s-1/2}\norm{u(\lambda^2t)}_{\dot H^s}.
\end{align*}
There is one exponent, and only one, at which the scale factor disappears: \(s=\tfrac12\).  With \(\Lambda:=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightDefinition}
  H(t)
  :=\frac12\norm{u(t)}_{\dot H^{1/2}}^2
  =\frac12\norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)}_2^2.
\end{equation}
This is the critical height.  Concentrating a profile by the Navier--Stokes rescaling does not make this measurement smaller.  The danger has changed scale, but \(H\) assigns the rescaled profile the same size.

Kinetic energy sees something else.  Pairing Navier--Stokes with \(u\) removes the transport and pressure terms by incompressibility, while integration by parts fixes the viscous sign.  Hence
\[
  \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\norm{u(t)}_2^2
  +\nu\norm{\nabla u(t)}_2^2
  =0,
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEnergyIdentity}
  \frac12\norm{u(t)}_2^2
  +\nu\int_0^t\norm{\nabla u(s)}_2^2\,ds
  =\frac12\norm{u_0}_2^2.
\end{equation}
This law is exact, but it lives below the critical scale.  At \(s=0\), the same concentration gives
\[
  \norm{u_\lambda(t)}_2^2
  =\lambda^{-1}\norm{u(\lambda^2t)}_2^2.
\]
Under the concentration being tested, kinetic energy acquires the factor \(\lambda^{-1}\) while critical height stays invariant and gradients acquire positive powers of \(\lambda\).  The rescaled profile therefore fits more easily inside the energy bound even as its critical-scale gradient danger sharpens.  The energy law does not rule out that route.  This is why the problem sits at critical height: ordinary energy gets smaller while the scale-invariant danger survives.

The smaller state still has to be produced.  At length \(\ell\), the Laplacian contributes \(\ell^{-2}\), so viscosity changes a structure at the rate \(\nu/\ell^2\).  Its natural response time is the reciprocal:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeatComparisonTime}
  \delta t_{\mathrm{heat}}(\ell)\asymp\frac{\ell^2}{\nu}.
\end{equation}
If the vortex contracts from \(\ell\) to \(\ell/\lambda\), the viscous clock contracts by \(\lambda^{-2}\).  This is the same time compression already built into \(u_\lambda(x,t)\).

For a geometric family of active lengths,
\[
  \ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0,
  \qquad 0<\rho<1.
\]
With scale-uniform comparison constants, their heat times satisfy
\[
  \delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp\frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k},
  \qquad
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\frac{1}{1-\rho^2}<\infty.
\]
The sum is the opening for a Zeno cascade: infinitely many smaller-scale transitions can fit inside a finite amount of comparison time.  It is not yet the cascade itself.  A geometric clock says what the equation permits at those scales; the evolving solution must still make the passages.

Scale invariance also shows why this schedule is not enough by itself: repeating one scale-normalized profile at smaller lengths leaves \(H\) unchanged.  To describe the branch in which critical height itself escapes, fix \(H_0>H(0)\) and mark its first passages by
\[
  L_k:=2^kH_0,
  \qquad
  t_k:=\inf\{t>0:H(t)=L_k\},
\]
and suppose these first passages exist for arbitrarily large \(k\).  Continuity gives
\[
  t_0<t_1<t_2<\cdots.
\]
If the \(k\)-th passage is carried by an active length \(\ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0\) and the transitions obey the scale-uniform comparison
\[
  0<t_{k+1}-t_k
  \leq C\,\delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp C\frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k},
\]
then
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t_{k+1}-t_k)<\infty,
  \qquad
  t_k\uparrow\tau<\infty.
\]
Only here do the two clocks become one event.  The heat scale supplies the shrinking schedule; the first passages say that the solution actually uses it.  Critical height crosses every chosen level while the active region and the time between successive changes both collapse.  That is the Zeno route.

Its spatial limit is the singularity direction of the same vortex.  For every \(m\in\Nzero\),
\[
  \nabla^m u_\lambda(x,t)
  =\lambda^{m+1}(\nabla^m u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t).
\]
At the \(k\)-th scale, \(\lambda_k:=\ell_0/\ell_k=\rho^{-k}\), so the first gradient carries the factor \(\lambda_k^2\).  If the realized states retain a nonzero scale-normalized gradient profile, their gradients diverge while their passage times converge to \(\tau\).  The singularity is not a separate disaster appended to the cascade.  It is what the Zeno tightening becomes in space: the scales collapse, the gradients sharpen, and the perfectly stretching vortex has nowhere left to go.
