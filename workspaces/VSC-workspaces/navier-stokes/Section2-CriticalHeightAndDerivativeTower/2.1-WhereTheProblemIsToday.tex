\subsection{Where the problem is today}\label{sub:ThePerfectlyStretchingVortex}

The problem at critical height is the perfectly stretching vortex: its vorticity remains aligned with an extensional direction of the strain while viscosity acts on the gradients that the stretching creates.
With
\[
  \omega:=\nabla\times u,
  \qquad
  S:=\frac12\bigl(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bigr),
\]
the vorticity equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VorticityEquation}
  \partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega
  =S\omega+\nu\Delta\omega.
\end{equation}
The stretching term amplifies vorticity under persistent extensional alignment; the same concentration sharpens the gradients on which diffusion acts.
In generic motion the alignment changes.
The perfectly stretching case is the limiting one in which it does not: every sharper state remains positioned for the next stretch while diffusion acts on the field produced by the last one.

That stretching--diffusion balance is critical under the Navier--Stokes scaling.
For \(\lambda>0\), the rescaling
\[
  u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \qquad
  p_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2p(\lambda x,\lambda^2t)
\]
preserves the equation, and
\[
  \norm{u_\lambda(t)}_{\dot H^s}
  =\lambda^{s-1/2}\norm{u(\lambda^2t)}_{\dot H^s}.
\]
The invariant exponent is \(s=\tfrac12\).
With \(\Lambda:=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\), define the critical height
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightDefinition}
  H(t)
  :=\frac12\norm{u(t)}_{\dot H^{1/2}}^2
  =\frac12\norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)}_2^2.
\end{equation}

The energy law acts below that height:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEnergyIdentity}
  \frac12\norm{u(t)}_2^2
  +\nu\int_0^t\norm{\nabla u(s)}_2^2\,ds
  =\frac12\norm{u_0}_2^2.
\end{equation}
It keeps the kinetic energy finite and controls one spatial derivative after integration in time.
It does not prevent concentration from passing into shorter scales while the critical height rises.

If \(H\) is unbounded before a finite candidate time \(\tau\), choose \(H_0>H(0)\), set \(L_k:=2^kH_0\), and let \(t_k\) be the first passage through \(L_k\) after \(t_{k-1}\).
Continuity gives
\[
  t_0<t_1<t_2<\cdots<\tau,
  \qquad
  H(t_k)=2^kH_0,
  \qquad
  t_k\uparrow\tau.
\]
The height need not be monotone between passages.
The Zeno demand is that every finite critical level be crossed before the same finite time.

A finite singular time forces exactly that demand.
Suppose the maximal classical solution has a finite last time \(T_*<\infty\).
The endpoint criterion of Escauriaza, Seregin, and \v{S}ver\'ak says that a uniform \(L^3\) bound would continue the solution through \(T_*\) \parencite{EscauriazaSereginSverak2003}.
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FiniteTimeLThreeEscape}
  T_*<\infty
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}\norm{u(t)}_3=\infty.
\end{equation}
Since \(\dot H^{1/2}(\R^3)\hookrightarrow L^3(\R^3)\), finite maximality also forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CriticalHeightMustEscape}
  T_*<\infty
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}H(t)=\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus \(\tau=T_*\), and the first passages satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ZenoFirstPassageTimes}
  t_0<t_1<t_2<\cdots<T_*,
  \qquad
  H(t_k)=2^kH_0,
  \qquad
  t_k\uparrow T_*.
\end{equation}

This is the Zeno form of the singularity problem.
The remaining time collapses while the required critical-height response keeps increasing.
If the velocity reaches that loss of control at \(T_*\), no fixed temporal response can remain uniformly bounded behind it; repeated integration would keep every lower response, and finally \(u\), bounded on the finite interval.
Velocity, acceleration, jerk, and the later responses have to be considered together.
The singularity has become a derivative Tower.
