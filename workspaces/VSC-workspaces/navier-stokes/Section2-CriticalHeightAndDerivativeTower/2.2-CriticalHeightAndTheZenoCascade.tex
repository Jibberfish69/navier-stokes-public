\subsection{Critical height and the Zeno cascade}\label{sub:CriticalHeightAndTheZenoCascade}

The core in \cref{fig:BalancedVortexCriticalRoute} tightens by moving velocity variation to shorter distances.
For \(\lambda>0\), the Navier--Stokes rescaling is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyNavierStokesScaling}
  u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \qquad
  p_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2p(\lambda x,\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
Each term in the equation transforms with the same homogeneity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyBalancedEquationScaling}
\begin{aligned}
  \partial_tu_\lambda
    &=\lambda^3(\partial_tu)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  &
  (u_\lambda\cdot\nabla)u_\lambda
    &=\lambda^3((u\cdot\nabla)u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \\
  \nabla p_\lambda
    &=\lambda^3(\nabla p)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  &
  \nu\Delta u_\lambda
    &=\lambda^3(\nu\Delta u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Narrowing gives neither nonlinear deformation nor viscous smoothing a scaling advantage.
They meet again at each finer scale with the same relative homogeneity.
This is the critical balance behind the perfectly centred picture.

The familiar norms move in different directions under the same rescaling:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyScalingNormComparison}
  \begin{aligned}
    \norm{u_\lambda(t)}_2
      &=\lambda^{-1/2}\norm{u(\lambda^2t)}_2,\\
    \norm{u_\lambda(t)}_\infty
      &=\lambda\norm{u(\lambda^2t)}_\infty,\\
    \norm{\nabla u_\lambda(t)}_2
      &=\lambda^{1/2}\norm{\nabla u(\lambda^2t)}_2.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
A finer copy can carry less kinetic energy while its amplitude and gradients become larger.
That is why the energy ceiling in \eqref{eq:BodyEnergyIdentity} does not settle the narrowing.

For homogeneous Sobolev norms,
\[
  \norm{u_\lambda(t)}_{\dot H^s}
  =\lambda^{s-1/2}\norm{u(\lambda^2t)}_{\dot H^s}.
\]
The neutral height is \(s=1/2\).
Write \(\Lambda:=(-\Delta)^{1/2}\) and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightDefinition}
  H(t)
  :=\frac12\norm{u(t)}_{\dot H^{1/2}}^2
  =\frac12\norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)}_2^2.
\end{equation}
The embedding \(\dot H^{1/2}(\R^3)\hookrightarrow L^3(\R^3)\), together with \eqref{eq:FiniteTimeLThreeEscape}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CriticalHeightMustEscape}
  T_*<\infty
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}H(t)=\infty.
\end{equation}

There are two different facts here.
A rescaled copy of one fixed profile keeps the same critical height while it moves to finer frequencies.
A genuine finite-time singularity would need the actual solution's critical height to pass beyond every finite level.
The first explains why concentration can hide from the energy scale; the second is the continuation threat that the proof has to stop.

Because the solution is classical at every strict preterminal time, \(H\) is continuous on \([0,T]\) for each \(T<T_*\).
Choose \(H_0>H(0)\) and set \(L_k=2^kH_0\).
Let
\[
  t_0:=\inf\{t\in(0,T_*):H(t)=H_0\},
\]
and, once \(t_k\) is fixed, let
\[
  t_{k+1}:=\inf\{t\in(t_k,T_*):H(t)=L_{k+1}\}.
\]
The unboundedness in \eqref{eq:CriticalHeightMustEscape} makes every set nonempty, and continuity places the stated level at its first-passage time.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ZenoFirstPassageTimes}
  t_0<t_1<t_2<\cdots<T_*,
  \qquad
  H(t_k)=2^kH_0,
  \qquad
  t_k\uparrow T_*.
\end{equation}
The last limit is forced because an earlier accumulation time would lie inside the smooth interval, where \(H\) is locally bounded.

This is the Zeno cascade.
The same fluid would have to cross infinitely many critical-height levels before one finite last time, with no assumption that \(H\) rises monotonically between crossings.
The next question is what that finite-time escape demands from the successive responses of the velocity itself.
