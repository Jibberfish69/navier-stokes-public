\subsection{The heat clock}\label{sub:BodyHeatClock}

Making the vortex smaller is only half of the proposed motion.
The smaller state still has to be produced.

At length \(\ell\), the Laplacian contributes a factor of order \(\ell^{-2}\).
Viscosity therefore acts at the rate \(\nu/\ell^2\), and the corresponding response time is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeatComparisonTime}
  \delta t_{\mathrm{heat}}(\ell)
  \asymp\frac{\ell^2}{\nu}.
\end{equation}
This is the heat clock.
It is the temporal half of the same scaling that preserved critical height: replacing \(\ell\) by \(\ell/\lambda\) replaces the clock by \(\lambda^{-2}\delta t_{\mathrm{heat}}(\ell)\).
The concentrated state is harder in space and faster in time.

Now let the active length contract geometrically,
\[
  \ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0,
  \qquad
  0<\rho<1.
\]
The heat times then satisfy
\[
  \delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k},
\]
so
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^{2k}
  =
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu(1-\rho^2)}
  <\infty.
\]
Infinitely many comparison intervals fit inside a finite amount of heat time.
That is the opening for a Zeno cascade.

The geometric sum is not yet a cascade made by the fluid.
It tells us how quickly each scale can respond; the solution still has to pass through the corresponding states.
An exact rescaled copy also leaves \(H\) unchanged.
The critical-height blow-up branch must therefore do more than replay one normalized profile at smaller scales.
It must climb through the critical levels while the active scale contracts.

Choose \(H_0>H(0)\), set
\[
  L_k:=2^kH_0,
\]
and, whenever the level is reached, let
\[
  t_k:=\inf\{t>0:H(t)=L_k\}.
\]
Continuity orders the first passages:
\[
  t_0<t_1<t_2<\cdots.
\]
Suppose these passages continue to arbitrarily high levels, the \(k\)-th passage is carried at an active length \(\ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0\), and the time required for the next passage obeys the scale-uniform comparison
\[
  0<t_{k+1}-t_k
  \leq
  C\frac{\ell_k^2}{\nu}
  =
  C\frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k}.
\]
Then
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t_{k+1}-t_k)<\infty,
  \qquad
  t_k\uparrow\tau
\]
for some \(\tau<\infty\).
The heat clock and the critical-height clock have become the same event: the field crosses higher levels while its active scale and the time between successive passages both collapse.
That is the Zeno route.

The same contraction has an opposite effect on spatial derivatives.
For every \(m\in\Nzero\),
\[
  \nabla^m u_\lambda(x,t)
  =\lambda^{m+1}(\nabla^m u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t).
\]
At the \(k\)-th active scale, \(\lambda_k:=\ell_0/\ell_k=\rho^{-k}\), so the first gradient carries the factor \(\lambda_k^2\).
If the realized states retain a nonzero gradient after normalization to the active scale, their physical gradients diverge as \(k\to\infty\).
The times converge while the gradients move the other way.
That limiting motion is the singularity direction of the critical-height vortex.

The vortex has now turned a spatial concentration problem into a temporal one.
Between \(t_k\) and \(t_{k+1}\), the velocity must keep producing distinguishable states across intervals that are disappearing.
A nontrivial change over a vanishing interval has a diverging difference quotient.
That is where acceleration enters.
