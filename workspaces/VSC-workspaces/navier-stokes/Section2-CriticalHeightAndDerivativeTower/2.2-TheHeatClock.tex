\subsection{The heat clock}\label{sub:BodyHeatClock}

Viscosity has no single clock.
It responds to each active length on that length's own time.
To isolate that time, let \(v_t=\nu\Delta v\) and suppose the Fourier mass of \(v\) is localized in a fixed band \(|\xi|\simeq\ell^{-1}\).
Under this linear heat flow, each such frequency decays according to
\[
  \widehat v(\xi,t+\delta t)
  =e^{-\nu|\xi|^2\delta t}\widehat v(\xi,t).
\]
The heat multiplier makes an order-one change when \(\nu|\xi|^2\delta t\) is of order one.
At scale \(\ell\), this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeatComparisonTime}
  \delta t_{\mathrm{heat}}(\ell)
  \asymp\frac{\ell^2}{\nu}.
\end{equation}
The comparison constants depend on the fixed localization band, not on \(\ell\).
If the active length changes from \(\ell\) to \(\ell/\lambda\), the viscous response time is divided by \(\lambda^2\).
Halving the length quarters the clock.
This is the temporal side of the same scaling that kept stretching and viscosity level in space.

The heat clock is not yet the duration of an actual vortex contraction.
It is the time on which diffusion at that scale produces an order-one response.
Any proposed cascade has to create its next stage while this faster response is already under way.

To see what the shrinking clocks permit, prescribe only as a scale comparison the geometric sequence
\[
  \ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0,
  \qquad
  0<\rho<1.
\]
With comparison constants independent of \(k\), the heat time attached to the \(k\)-th scale is
\[
  \delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k},
\]
and the whole sequence satisfies
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^{2k}
  =
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu(1-\rho^2)}
  <\infty.
\]
Each scale carries only \(\rho^2\) of the previous heat clock, and the comparison clocks have a finite total.
This calculation does not show that Navier--Stokes realizes the contractions or completes them on those clocks.
It shows why diffusion supplies no scale-independent minimum waiting time that would rule out an accelerating cascade by timing alone.

Scale collapse by itself is not yet a singularity.
An exact rescaled copy leaves \(H\) unchanged, while
\(\dot H^{1/2}(\R^3)\hookrightarrow L^3(\R^3)\) and bounded critical \(L^3\) control rules out singularity \parencite{EscauriazaSereginSverak2003}.
An actual singular route must strengthen while it concentrates: the active length must fall while the critical height rises.
The embedding and the critical \(L^3\) criterion mean that such a route must cross arbitrarily high values of \(H\).
Suppose this happens along a classical interval \([0,\tau)\), with \(H(t)\) unbounded as \(t\uparrow\tau\).

Let \(H_0>H(0)\) and set
\[
  L_k:=2^kH_0.
\]
For each level that the solution reaches, let
\[
  t_k:=\inf\{t\in(0,\tau):H(t)=L_k\}.
\]
Continuity orders these first passages:
\[
  t_0<t_1<t_2<\cdots.
\]
Now the heat clock can be posed as a dynamical question.
Can the passage from \(L_k\) to \(L_{k+1}\) occur on the viscous response time of the active scale?
Suppose the solution has an active length \(\ell(t_k)=\ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0\) at each first passage.
If one constant \(C\), independent of \(k\), bounds completion of the next passage by the corresponding heat time, then
\[
  0<t_{k+1}-t_k
  \leq
  C\frac{\ell_k^2}{\nu}
  =
  C\frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k}.
\]
The first-passage times are then Cauchy.
Their limit cannot lie below \(\tau\), because \(H(t_k)=L_k\to\infty\) while \(H\) is finite and continuous on every compact subinterval of \([0,\tau)\).
Their only possible limit is \(\tau\), and
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t_{k+1}-t_k)<\infty,
  \qquad
  t_k\uparrow\tau<\infty.
\]
Under this hypothesis, the crossings accumulate while the critical height climbs and the active scale collapses.
That conditional accumulation is the Zeno cascade attached to critical height.

The same rescaling sharpens spatial derivatives:
\[
  \nabla^m u_\lambda(x,t)
  =\lambda^{m+1}(\nabla^m u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \qquad
  m\in\Nzero.
\]
At the \(k\)-th active scale, let \(\lambda_k:=\ell_0/\ell_k=\rho^{-k}\).
If a localized active core can be centred at \(x_k\), define its normalized profile by
\[
  u\!\left(x_k+\ell_k y,t_k\right)
  =\lambda_k U_k(y),
  \qquad y\in B_1.
\]
This is a change of coordinates at each passage, not an assertion that the profiles \(U_k\) agree.
It gives
\[
  \nabla_xu\!\left(x_k+\ell_k y,t_k\right)
  =\frac{\lambda_k}{\ell_k}\nabla_yU_k(y)
  =\frac{\lambda_k^2}{\ell_0}\nabla_yU_k(y).
\]
If these normalized profiles do not flatten,
\[
  \inf_k\norm{\nabla U_k}_{L^\infty(B_1)}\geq c>0,
\]
then
\[
  \norm{\nabla u(t_k)}_{L^\infty}
  \geq \frac{c}{\ell_0}\lambda_k^2
  \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Under the scale, timing, and non-flattening hypotheses, the Zeno route has its spatial consequence: the active length collapses while critical height and the physical gradients escape every finite bound.

The level crossings also give an exact increment in the critical norm:
\[
  \norm{u(t_{k+1})-u(t_k)}_{\dot H^{1/2}}
  \geq
  \left|\sqrt{2L_{k+1}}-\sqrt{2L_k}\right|
  =
  (\sqrt2-1)\sqrt{2L_k}.
\]
The fundamental theorem of calculus then gives
\[
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t_k<t<t_{k+1}}
  \norm{u_t(t)}_{\dot H^{1/2}}
  \geq
  \frac{(\sqrt2-1)\sqrt{2L_k}}{t_{k+1}-t_k}.
\]
The first Eulerian response \(u_t\) must grow on any such sequence of collapsing passages.
The next question is whether later responses are activated in the same way.
