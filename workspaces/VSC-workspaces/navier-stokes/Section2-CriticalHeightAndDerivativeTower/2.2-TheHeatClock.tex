\subsection{The heat clock}\label{sub:BodyHeatClock}

Every contraction has to happen while viscosity is already acting on the vortex.
That gives the smaller scale a shorter clock.

A structure of width \(\ell\) is carried by frequencies of order \(|\xi|\sim\ell^{-1}\).
Under the linear heat flow, those frequencies decay according to
\[
  \widehat v(\xi,t+\delta t)
  =e^{-\nu|\xi|^2\delta t}\widehat v(\xi,t).
\]
Viscosity makes an order-one change when \(\nu|\xi|^2\delta t\) is of order one.
At scale \(\ell\), this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeatComparisonTime}
  \delta t_{\mathrm{heat}}(\ell)
  \asymp\frac{\ell^2}{\nu}.
\end{equation}
When the vortex narrows from \(\ell\) to \(\ell/\lambda\), its viscous response time shrinks by \(\lambda^{-2}\).
Halving the width quarters the clock.
The same scaling that preserves the critical balance is compressing the time available for the next stage.

After repeated contractions at a fixed ratio,
\[
  \ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0,
  \qquad
  0<\rho<1.
\]
The heat time attached to the \(k\)-th scale is
\[
  \delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k},
\]
and the whole sequence satisfies
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta t_k^{\mathrm{heat}}
  \asymp
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho^{2k}
  =
  \frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu(1-\rho^2)}
  <\infty.
\]
The sum converges.
So an infinite sequence of smaller scales does not require an infinite amount of time; every stage can fit inside a shorter interval than the one before it.
That is the opening left by the heat clock.

But scale collapse by itself is not yet a singularity.
An exact rescaled copy leaves \(H\) unchanged, while
\(\dot H^{1/2}(\R^3)\hookrightarrow L^3(\R^3)\) and bounded critical \(L^3\) control rules out singularity \parencite{EscauriazaSereginSverak2003}.
The actual route would have to strengthen while it concentrates: the active length must fall while the critical height rises.

Let \(H_0>H(0)\) and set
\[
  L_k:=2^kH_0.
\]
For every level the solution reaches, let
\[
  t_k:=\inf\{t>0:H(t)=L_k\}.
\]
Along a singular route, arbitrarily high levels are reached and continuity orders the first passages:
\[
  t_0<t_1<t_2<\cdots.
\]
The Zeno question is whether the passage from \(L_k\) to \(L_{k+1}\) can occur on the heat clock of the active scale.
If the \(k\)-th passage occurs at \(\ell_k=\rho^k\ell_0\) and the next passage is completed within a fixed multiple of that heat time, then
\[
  0<t_{k+1}-t_k
  \leq
  C\frac{\ell_k^2}{\nu}
  =
  C\frac{\ell_0^2}{\nu}\rho^{2k}.
\]
Then
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t_{k+1}-t_k)<\infty,
  \qquad
  t_k\uparrow\tau
\]
for some \(\tau<\infty\).
The crossings accumulate while the critical height climbs and the active scale collapses.
This is the Zeno cascade attached to critical height.

The same rescaling sharpens spatial derivatives:
\[
  \nabla^m u_\lambda(x,t)
  =\lambda^{m+1}(\nabla^m u)(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
  \qquad
  m\in\Nzero.
\]
At the \(k\)-th active scale, let \(\lambda_k:=\ell_0/\ell_k=\rho^{-k}\) and write the vortex in normalized coordinates as
\[
  u\!\left(x_k+\frac{y}{\lambda_k},t_k\right)
  =\lambda_k U_k(y),
  \qquad y\in B_1.
\]
Then
\[
  \nabla_xu\!\left(x_k+\frac{y}{\lambda_k},t_k\right)
  =\lambda_k^2\nabla_yU_k(y).
\]
If the normalized vortex profiles do not flatten,
\[
  \inf_k\norm{\nabla U_k}_{L^\infty(B_1)}\geq c>0,
\]
then
\[
  \norm{\nabla u(t_k)}_{L^\infty}
  \geq c\lambda_k^2
  \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The Zeno route has now reached its spatial consequence: the scale collapses while critical height and the physical gradients escape every finite bound.

What does the fluid have to do in time to cross those levels on intervals that keep collapsing?
It has to keep changing faster.
