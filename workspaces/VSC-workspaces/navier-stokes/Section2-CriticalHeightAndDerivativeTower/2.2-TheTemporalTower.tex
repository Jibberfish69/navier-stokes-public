\subsection{What finite time demands of the next response}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

If velocity escapes in a fixed spatial norm \(X\) before a finite time, its local acceleration cannot remain bounded in that norm.
Otherwise
\[
  \norm{u(t)}_X
  \leq
  \norm{u(0)}_X
  +
  \int_0^t\norm{\partial_su(s)}_X\,ds
  \leq
  \norm{u(0)}_X
  +
  T_*\sup_{0\leq s<T_*}\norm{\partial_su(s)}_X,
\]
would keep the velocity finite all the way to \(T_*\).
Apply the same argument to acceleration: if its next time derivative were bounded, acceleration could not escape either.
The implication repeats at every order,
\[
  u,
  \qquad
  \partial_tu,
  \qquad
  \partial_t^2u,
  \qquad
  \partial_t^3u,
  \qquad
  \ldots,
\]
with each row the time derivative of the row above it at the same spatial coordinate.
This repeated finite-time necessity is the temporal Tower of the one evolving velocity field.
Write its velocity and pressure rows as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
We differentiate only finitely many times and only on strict intervals where the solution is classical.

The equation determines how the rows are joined.
The first derivative is built from the spatial structure of \(u\); differentiating that identity produces the next rows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  V_0
  &:=u,
  \\
  V_1
  &=\nu\Delta V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -\nabla Q_0,
  \\
  V_2
  &=\nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1,
  \\
  V_3
  &=\nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The visible pattern is the product rule, and at order \(n\) it becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}

Pressure stays inside every response, but it is fixed by the same velocity rows.
Taking the divergence of the original equation and using the decaying whole-space normalization gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j),
\end{equation}
where \(R_j:=\partial_j(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\); the decay condition removes the otherwise arbitrary function of time.
After \(n\) derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
Thus the velocity rows determine \(Q_n\), and \(\nabla Q_n\) returns to the equation for \(V_{n+1}\).
The Tower cannot remain purely temporal: viscosity contributes \(\Delta V_n\), transport differentiates velocity products, and pressure is recovered by spatial singular integrals of those same products.
Record the resulting mixed coordinates as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
  \qquad
  \alpha\in\Nzero^3.
\end{equation}
Already one spatial derivative divides across the two transport factors:
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
For a general multi-index \(\alpha\), the temporal and spatial product rules give
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
The matching pressure coordinate satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}

The recurrence that generates each response also contains
\(\nu\Delta V_n\).  Finite time has exposed the temporal rows; viscosity now
asks how much spatial height is carried inside each one, with pressure still
attached.
