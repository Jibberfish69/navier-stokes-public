\subsection{Velocity, acceleration, jerk}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

A Zeno cascade has to be produced by the time evolution.
The shorter its transition intervals become, the larger the responses producing those transitions have to be.
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
Let \(B\) be a fixed Banach space.
Whenever \(V_n\) is absolutely continuous from \([s,t]\) into \(B\),
\[
  V_n(t)-V_n(s)
  =\int_s^tV_{n+1}(\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyResponseEscalation}
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s<\tau<t}\norm{V_{n+1}(\tau)}_B
  \geq
  \frac{\norm{V_n(t)-V_n(s)}_B}{t-s}.
\end{equation}
Suppose a sequence of intervals \([s_k,t_k]\) satisfies
\[
  t_k-s_k\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad
  \norm{V_n(t_k)-V_n(s_k)}_B\geq c_n>0.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:BodyResponseEscalation} forces the \((n+1)\)-st response to diverge along those intervals.
The conclusion is conditional at each rung: the estimate converts an actual increment of \(V_n\) into a lower bound for \(V_{n+1}\).
At the first rung, distinguishable velocity states produced on the shrinking Zeno intervals force a large first response.
The same implication is available again whenever that first response makes distinguishable changes on a still shorter interval, and so on through every finite order.
This is the precise temporal pressure behind the acceleration--jerk picture.

For a fluid particle following \(X'(t)=u(X(t),t)\),
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}u(X(t),t)
  =D_tu(X(t),t),
  \qquad
  D_tu:=\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u.
\]
The Navier--Stokes equation already gives this material acceleration:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
Its next material response \(D_t(D_tu)\) is the jerk.
For the Tower we keep the Eulerian rows \(V_n=\partial_t^nu\), evaluated at fixed \(x\), because each differentiation then exposes exactly how transport, pressure, and viscosity create the next response.
The first row is
\[
  V_1
  =\nu\Delta V_0-(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0-\nabla Q_0.
\]
Thus material acceleration and the first Eulerian row are related by
\[
  D_tu=V_1+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0,
\]
while \(V_1\) itself remains the object that enters the fixed-point time tower.

Differentiate the first row once.
The time derivative can land on either velocity in the transport term:
\[
  V_2
  =\nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1.
\]
Differentiate again and those placements split once more:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  V_3={}&
  \nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2,
  \\
  V_4={}&
  \nu\Delta V_3
  -(V_3\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -3(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -3(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_3
  -\nabla Q_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficients count the ways the time derivatives divide between the transporting velocity and the velocity being transported.
At order \(n\), the product rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}
This is the temporal Tower inside the equation.
The \((n+1)\)-st response is built from every transport split among the earlier rows, the pressure response at depth \(n\), and two spatial derivatives of \(V_n\).

Pressure advances through the same temporal rows.
Taking the divergence of the original momentum equation and using \(\nabla\cdot u=0\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j),
\end{equation}
where decay at spatial infinity fixes the additive constant.
Applying \(\partial_t^n\) produces the same binomial split:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
The velocity rows determine \(Q_n\) at the same temporal depth, and \(\nabla Q_n\) returns to \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} as part of the next response.
The Tower is pressure-complete at every rung.

The recurrence also reveals why a temporal tower cannot stay purely temporal.
The viscous term contains \(\Delta V_n\), transport contains one spatial derivative, and pressure is reconstructed nonlocally from spatial derivatives of products of the velocity rows.
Expose those derivatives by setting
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
  \qquad
  \alpha\in\Nzero^3.
\end{equation}
For one spatial derivative,
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
At a multi-index \(\alpha\), each spatial derivative divides between the two factors just as the time derivatives do.
Applying \(\partial_x^\alpha\) to \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} gives
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
The pressure coordinates satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}

The one temporal index has now become a two-dimensional mixed jet.
Moving down changes the temporal response; moving across differentiates that same response in space.
Critical scaling will tell us where these two directions line up, and the height equation will show what viscosity places on that diagonal.
