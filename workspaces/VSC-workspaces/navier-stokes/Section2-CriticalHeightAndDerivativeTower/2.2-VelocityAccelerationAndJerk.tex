\subsection{Velocity, acceleration, jerk}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

The field has to make the shrinking passages from the previous section.
Each smaller scale is a new state that the velocity has to produce.
When a definite change is made in less time, the next temporal response grows.
Carry the same demand upward: a nontrivial change in the first response over a shorter interval makes the second response grow, and the implication repeats at every later rung.
This is the sense in which a Zeno singularity would have to be led by acceleration, then jerk, and then the later responses.
The condition matters at every step: the shrinking interval magnifies a change that the cascade actually carries.

Now write that statement in Eulerian time, holding the spatial coordinates fixed.
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
Let \(B\) be a fixed Banach space.
Whenever \(V_n\) is absolutely continuous from \([s,t]\) into \(B\),
\[
  V_n(t)-V_n(s)
  =\int_s^tV_{n+1}(\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyResponseEscalation}
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s<\tau<t}\norm{V_{n+1}(\tau)}_B
  \geq
  \frac{\norm{V_n(t)-V_n(s)}_B}{t-s}.
\end{equation}
If a sequence of intervals \([s_k,t_k]\) satisfies
\[
  t_k-s_k\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad
  \norm{V_n(t_k)-V_n(s_k)}_B\geq c_n>0,
\]
then the \((n+1)\)-st response diverges along those intervals.

Now return to Navier--Stokes.
The first physical response is already in the equation.
For a fluid particle following \(X'(t)=u(X(t),t)\),
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}u(X(t),t)
  =D_tu(X(t),t),
  \qquad
  D_tu:=\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u,
\]
and the momentum equation gives its material acceleration:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
The next material response \(D_t(D_tu)\) is the jerk.
The fixed-position Tower uses the Eulerian rows \(V_n\), so \(V_1\) is the Eulerian part of acceleration and \(V_2\) is the second fixed-position response that enters the jerk calculation.
Their relation begins with
\[
  D_tu=V_1+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0.
\]

Solve Navier--Stokes for the first Eulerian row:
\[
  V_1
  =\nu\Delta V_0-(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0-\nabla Q_0.
\]
Differentiate it once.
The time derivative can land on either velocity in the transport term:
\[
  V_2
  =\nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1.
\]
Differentiate again and those placements split again:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  V_3={}&
  \nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2,
  \\
  V_4={}&
  \nu\Delta V_3
  -(V_3\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -3(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -3(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_3
  -\nabla Q_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficients count every way the time derivatives divide between the transporting velocity and the velocity being transported.
At order \(n\), the product rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}
This is the temporal Tower inside Navier--Stokes.
The next response contains every transport split among the earlier rows, the pressure response at the same depth, and two spatial derivatives of \(V_n\).

Pressure comes up the Tower with the velocity.
Taking the divergence of the original momentum equation and using \(\nabla\cdot u=0\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j),
\end{equation}
where decay at spatial infinity fixes the additive constant.
Applying \(\partial_t^n\) produces the same binomial split:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
The velocity rows determine \(Q_n\), and \(\nabla Q_n\) returns through \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} as part of the next response.
Every rung is pressure-complete.

By this point a second direction is already present in every line.
Viscosity contributes two spatial derivatives at each rung, transport contributes one, and pressure returns through spatial singular integrals of the velocity products.
Expose that direction by setting
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
  \qquad
  \alpha\in\Nzero^3.
\end{equation}
For one spatial derivative,
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
At a multi-index \(\alpha\), the spatial derivatives divide between the factors just as the temporal derivatives did.
Applying \(\partial_x^\alpha\) to \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} gives
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
The pressure coordinates satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}

The single temporal ladder has become a mixed jet.
Moving down changes the temporal response; moving across differentiates that response in space.
The next question is what these two directions do at critical height.
