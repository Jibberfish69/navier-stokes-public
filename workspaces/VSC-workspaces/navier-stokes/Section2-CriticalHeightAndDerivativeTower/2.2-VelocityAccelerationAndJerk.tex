\subsection{Velocity, acceleration, jerk}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

The shrinking heat clock tells us what the next derivative has to do.
Let \(B\) be a fixed Banach space and write
\[
  V_n(t):=\partial_t^nu(\cdot,t).
\]
Whenever \(V_n\) is absolutely continuous as a \(B\)-valued function,
\[
  V_n(t)-V_n(s)
  =\int_s^tV_{n+1}(\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyResponseEscalation}
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s<\tau<t}\norm{V_{n+1}(\tau)}_B
  \geq
  \frac{\norm{V_n(t)-V_n(s)}_B}{t-s}.
\end{equation}
This is the missing temporal side of the Zeno picture.
If the fluid has to produce a comparable change in \(V_n\) across successively shorter intervals, \(V_{n+1}\) must become successively larger.
The heat clock passes the same demand from velocity to its first response, from that response to the next, and onward through every finite temporal order.

For a moving fluid particle, the first response is the material acceleration
\[
  D_tu:=\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u.
\]
The Navier--Stokes equation already contains it:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
Acceleration is the combined pressure and viscous response of the velocity field after transport has been included.
Jerk differentiates this response again, so it differentiates transport, pressure, and viscosity together.

To keep particle and field derivatives distinct, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
Then \(V_1\) is the Eulerian time response at fixed \(x\), while the material acceleration is \(D_tu=V_1+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0\).
Solving Navier--Stokes for that first response gives
\[
  V_1
  =\nu\Delta V_0-(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0-\nabla Q_0.
\]
Differentiate once in time.
The derivative can fall on either factor in transport, which gives
\[
  V_2
  =\nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1.
\]
Differentiate again and the two transport placements split once more:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  V_3={}&
  \nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2,
  \\
  V_4={}&
  \nu\Delta V_3
  -(V_3\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -3(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -3(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_3
  -\nabla Q_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The binomial coefficients count the ways the time derivatives can divide between the transporting velocity and the velocity being transported.
At arbitrary order the same product rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}
This is the temporal Tower in the equation itself.
Every new response contains all earlier transport splits, the next pressure response, and the Laplacian of the row immediately below it.

Pressure advances through those same rows.
Taking the divergence of the original equation and using \(\nabla\cdot u=0\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j),
\end{equation}
where decay at spatial infinity fixes the additive constant.
Applying \(\partial_t^n\) and using the same binomial split gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
The pressure row is reconstructed from the velocity rows at the same temporal depth, then returns to the momentum recurrence through \(\nabla Q_n\).

The Tower now opens horizontally as well.
Each row in \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} already contains two additional spatial derivatives through \(\Delta V_n\), one spatial derivative inside transport, and the nonlocal spatial reconstruction of pressure.
To expose those derivatives, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
  \qquad
  \alpha\in\Nzero^3.
\end{equation}
For one spatial derivative,
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
At multi-index \(\alpha\), every spatial derivative divides between the two factors just as the time derivatives did.
Applying \(\partial_x^\alpha\) to the temporal recurrence gives
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
The pressure coordinate satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}

The initial datum supplies the bottom edge of this entire construction.
Because \(u_0\in\Ssigma\), the formula for \(Q_0(0)\) places the first pressure row in every finite Sobolev space, and the formula for \(V_1(0)\) does the same for the next velocity row.
If \(V_0(0),\ldots,V_n(0)\) are known in every finite Sobolev space, \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence} gives the same property for \(Q_n(0)\), and \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} then gives it for \(V_{n+1}(0)\).
Induction yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyInitialPressureCompleteMixedJet}
  J_{n,\alpha}(0)\in H_\sigma^m(\R^3),
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}(0)\in H^m(\R^3)
\end{equation}
for every finite \(n\), \(\alpha\), and \(m\).

The temporal Tower has now opened a spatial direction inside every row.
The recurrence has established the time--space coupling.
We can now ask what that coupling does at the critical height where the vortex problem began.
