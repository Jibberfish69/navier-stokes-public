\subsection{Velocity, acceleration, jerk}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

The singularity cannot be built by temporal responses that go quiet.  Every Zeno passage is an actual change in the velocity field, and the passages arrive on shorter intervals.  If velocity makes a definite change in less time, its first temporal response has to grow.  If that response makes the next definite change on a still shorter interval, its derivative has to grow as well.  Velocity leads to acceleration; acceleration leads to jerk; and the same demand continues upward.  The shrinking clock matters because none of these responses can stagnate while continuing to carry a nontrivial change.

The acceleration of the fluid is already in Navier--Stokes.  Along a particle path \(X'(t)=u(X(t),t)\),
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}u(X(t),t)
  =D_tu(X(t),t),
  \qquad
  D_tu:=\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u,
\]
and the momentum equation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
Applying \(D_t\) again produces the material jerk.  But repeated material differentiation also keeps differentiating the particle path and the transport field.  The clean way to expose the response hierarchy is to hold the spatial coordinate fixed and separate its Eulerian rows:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
The distinction is exact.  Material acceleration is not \(V_1\); rather,
\[
  D_tu=V_1+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0.
\]
Thus \(V_1\) is the fixed-position time response inside acceleration, and \(V_2\) is the next fixed-position response that enters the jerk calculation.

The velocity--acceleration--jerk pressure can now be stated without metaphor.  Let \(B\) be a fixed Banach space.  Whenever \(V_n\) is absolutely continuous from \([s,t]\) into \(B\),
\[
  V_n(t)-V_n(s)
  =\int_s^tV_{n+1}(\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyResponseEscalation}
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s<\tau<t}\norm{V_{n+1}(\tau)}_B
  \geq
  \frac{\norm{V_n(t)-V_n(s)}_B}{t-s}.
\end{equation}
If intervals \([s_k,t_k]\) collapse while the \(n\)-th response still carries a nonzero increment,
\[
  t_k-s_k\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad
  \norm{V_n(t_k)-V_n(s_k)}_B\geq c_n>0,
\]
then \(V_{n+1}\) must become unbounded along those intervals.  The conclusion is rungwise and conditional: the shorter interval amplifies a change only when the cascade actually carries that change at the current rung.  This is exactly what it means for the later responses to lead rather than stagnate.

Navier--Stokes generates those rows from the start.  Solving the momentum equation for the first Eulerian response gives
\[
  V_1
  =\nu\Delta V_0-(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0-\nabla Q_0.
\]
Differentiate once and the time derivative can land on either velocity in the transport term:
\[
  V_2
  =\nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1.
\]
Differentiate again and the two placements split again:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  V_3={}&
  \nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2,
  \\
  V_4={}&
  \nu\Delta V_3
  -(V_3\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -3(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -3(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_3
  -\nabla Q_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficients are not decoration.  They count every way the time derivatives can divide between the velocity doing the transporting and the velocity being transported.  At depth \(n\), all of those divisions close into one recurrence:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}
This is the temporal Tower inside the equation.  Its next row contains every transport split among the earlier responses, two new spatial derivatives through viscosity, and the pressure response at the same depth.

Pressure has not disappeared into a forcing term; it is determined by the same rows and returns at every rung.  Taking the divergence of the momentum equation and using \(\nabla\cdot u=0\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j),
\end{equation}
where decay at spatial infinity fixes the additive constant.  After \(n\) time derivatives, the pressure inherits the same binomial split:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
The velocity rows determine \(Q_n\); its gradient then returns through \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} as part of \(V_{n+1}\).  Every rung is pressure-complete.

At this point the Tower refuses to remain temporal.  Viscosity places \(\Delta V_n\) in every row, transport adds a spatial derivative, and pressure is recovered through spatial singular integrals of velocity products.  Differentiating in time has already opened a spatial direction.  Following that direction to arbitrary depth requires the mixed coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
  \qquad
  \alpha\in\Nzero^3.
\end{equation}
One spatial derivative already shows the same splitting mechanism:
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
For a multi-index \(\alpha\), the spatial derivatives divide between the factors just as the temporal derivatives did.  Applying \(\partial_x^\alpha\) to the temporal recurrence gives
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
Its pressure coordinates satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}

So the object forced by the singularity is larger than the list \(u,u_t,u_{tt},\ldots\).  It is the mixed jet.  Moving down changes the temporal response; moving across sharpens that response in space; and Navier--Stokes couples both movements in every row.  Critical height is where those two directions can finally be read against each other.
