\subsection{Velocity, acceleration, jerk}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

The words acceleration and jerk belong first to a moving particle.
If \(X(a,t)\) is the trajectory of the particle labelled by \(a\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ParticleTrajectory}
  \frac d{dt}X(a,t)=u(X(a,t),t),
  \qquad
  X(a,0)=a.
\end{equation}
The acceleration carried by that particle is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationBody}
  \frac d{dt}u(X(a,t),t)
  =\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u
  =:D_tu.
\end{equation}
\NavierStokes{} gives it directly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
Its jerk is \(D_t^2u=D_t(-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u)\).
The material response already mixes time change, motion through spatial variation, nonlocal pressure, and viscous curvature.

To see which part changes at each fixed position, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
Thus \(V_1\) is not the material acceleration.
It is the fixed-position time change which, together with \((u\cdot\nabla)u\), makes \(D_tu\).
The physical words make the response demand intelligible; the rows \(V_n\) keep temporal order separate enough to calculate.

The first row is already inside the equation.
Solving for it and differentiating successively gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  V_1={}&
  \nu\Delta V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -\nabla Q_0,
  \\
  V_2={}&
  \nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1,
  \\
  V_3={}&
  \nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2,
  \\
  V_4={}&
  \nu\Delta V_3
  -(V_3\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -3(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -3(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_3
  -\nabla Q_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficients \(1,1\), then \(1,2,1\), then \(1,3,3,1\), count the ways the time derivatives split between the transporting and transported velocities.
The first four responses have exposed the binomial pattern.
At arbitrary order it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}

Pressure advances with the same rows.
The decaying whole-space normalization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j).
\end{equation}
After \(n\) time derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
The pressure row is rebuilt from the same velocity rows that created it.
Nothing in the temporal sequence is closed without the spatial derivatives already present in transport, pressure, and \(\Delta V_n\).

Give that second direction its own index.
For \(n\in\Nzero\) and \(\alpha\in\Nzero^3\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p.
\end{equation}
Applying \(\partial_x^\alpha\) to \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} gives
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
The corresponding pressure coordinate satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
Time and space are now independent indices of the pressure-complete mixed jet.
The recurrence tells us how its coordinates are coupled; it does not yet bound them near \(T_*\).

Before using arbitrarily high time rows, their initial values have to exist.
The datum gives \(V_0(0)=u_0\in H_\sigma^m(\R^3)\) for every finite \(m\).
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
Suppose \(V_0(0),\ldots,V_n(0)\) belong to every finite Sobolev space.
The pressure recurrence, finite-order Sobolev multiplication, and boundedness of the Riesz transforms place \(Q_n(0)\) in every \(H^m\).
Solving \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} for \(V_{n+1}(0)\) gives the same conclusion for the next velocity row.
Induction in \(n\), followed by spatial differentiation, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyInitialPressureCompleteMixedJet}
  J_{n,\alpha}(0)\in H_\sigma^m(\R^3),
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}(0)\in H^m(\R^3)
\end{equation}
for every finite \(n\), \(\alpha\), and \(m\).
The datum generates every finite initial coordinate.
This is initialization, not terminal control.

Now return to the finite-time escape.
For any fixed \(n\geq1\), repeated integration gives the Banach-valued Taylor formula
\[
  u(t)
  =
  \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{t^j}{j!}V_j(0)
  +\frac1{(n-1)!}
  \int_0^t(t-s)^{n-1}V_n(s)\,ds.
\]
Every initial term is finite in \(L^3\) by \eqref{eq:BodyInitialPressureCompleteMixedJet}.
If \(V_n\) stayed uniformly bounded in \(L^3\) on the finite interval, the integral would keep \(u\) uniformly bounded there as well.
That contradicts \eqref{eq:FiniteTimeLThreeEscape}.
Consequently, for every fixed \(n\in\Nzero\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerMustEscape}
  T_*<\infty
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}\norm{V_n(t)}_3=\infty.
\end{equation}
Different rows may escape at different times, places, and rates.
The conclusion is separate at each finite order.

The singularity has now pushed downward through
\[
  u,\quad u_t,\quad u_{tt},\quad u_{ttt},\quad\ldots.
\]
But every line of \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} also points sideways through \(\Delta V_n\), the spatial derivatives in transport, and the reconstructed pressure.
What looked like a one-directional Tower is a grid.
