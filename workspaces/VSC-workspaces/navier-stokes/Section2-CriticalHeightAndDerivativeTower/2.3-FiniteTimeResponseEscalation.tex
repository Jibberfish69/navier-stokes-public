\subsection{Why finite-time escape opens the temporal Tower}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

Suppose the velocity really does escape in \(L^3\) before \(T_*\).
Could its first time derivative stay uniformly bounded there?
No, because
\[
  u(t)=u_0+\int_0^t u_t(s)\,ds
\]
would keep \(\norm{u(t)}_3\) finite on the finite interval.
Could the second time derivative stay bounded instead?
Then
\[
  u_t(t)=u_t(0)+\int_0^t u_{tt}(s)\,ds
\]
would bound \(u_t\), and the first integral would bound \(u\).
The same reasoning repeats at every fixed order.

At common spatial coordinates, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
The general integration step is the Banach-valued Taylor formula.

\begin{lemma}[Repeated finite-time integration]\label{lem:RepeatedFiniteTimeIntegration}
Let \(n\geq1\), and let \(f:[0,T_*)\to X\) have the derivatives required by the integral Taylor formula in a Banach space \(X\).
For \(0<t<T_*\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RepeatedFiniteTimeBound}
  \norm{f(t)}_X
  \leq
  \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{t^j}{j!}\norm{\partial_t^jf(0)}_X
  +\frac{t^n}{n!}\norm{\partial_t^nf}_{L^\infty(0,T_*;X)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The integral Taylor formula gives
\[
  f(t)
  =\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{t^j}{j!}\partial_t^jf(0)
  +\frac1{(n-1)!}\int_0^t(t-s)^{n-1}\partial_t^nf(s)\,ds.
\]
Taking the \(X\)-norm and bounding the integral by its essential supremum gives \eqref{eq:RepeatedFiniteTimeBound}.
\end{proof}

Apply the lemma with \(f=u\) and \(X=L^3(\R^3)\).
The smooth datum supplies every finite initial time derivative, and the finite value of \(T_*\) keeps the polynomial factors finite.
The escape in \eqref{eq:FiniteTimeLThreeEscape} forces, separately for every fixed \(n\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerMustEscape}
  \sup_{0<t<T_*}\norm{V_n(t)}_3=\infty.
\end{equation}
No common blow-up point, rate, or sequence is claimed.
The conclusion is rowwise: no selected finite temporal response can remain bounded in the same critical space while the base velocity escapes before a finite time.

This is the intuitive entrance to the temporal derivative Tower,
\[
  u,\quad u_t,\quad u_{tt},\quad u_{ttt},\quad\ldots.
\]
The words acceleration and jerk are useful for feeling the escalation, but the physical derivatives experienced by a moving particle are not \(u_t,u_{tt},\ldots\) alone.
If \(X(a,t)\) is the trajectory beginning at the material label \(a\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ParticleTrajectory}
  \frac d{dt}X(a,t)=u(X(a,t),t),
  \qquad
  X(a,0)=a,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationBody}
  \frac d{dt}u(X(a,t),t)
  =\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u
  =:D_tu.
\end{equation}
The equation makes the particle acceleration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
One more material derivative differentiates pressure and curvature and also differentiates the moving frame through which they are observed.
This is why the physical response already mixes time and space before the formal mixed jet is written down.

The fixed-coordinate rows reveal that structure without changing frames.
At order zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyFirstTemporalVelocityRow}
  V_1+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0+\nabla Q_0
  =\nu\Delta V_0.
\end{equation}
Differentiating once gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySecondTemporalVelocityRow}
  V_2
  +(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  +(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  +\nabla Q_1
  =\nu\Delta V_1.
\end{equation}
The first nonlinear term changes the velocity doing the carrying.
The second changes the response being carried through the existing flow.
Viscosity acts two spatial derivatives above the current row.

At arbitrary order, the temporal recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}
Incompressibility reconstructs pressure from the same velocity history:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j),
\end{equation}
with the decaying whole-space normalization.
Time differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
Every new temporal row retains all differentiated transport splits, the reconstructed pressure response, and the two-spatial-derivative viscous rung.
The next question is what this ascent exposes in space.
