\subsection{The Tower opens toward more spatial regularity}\label{sub:ThePressureCompleteMixedDerivativeTower}

Here is the strange turn.  We followed the vortex toward smaller scales
because that is where spatial control was supposed to fail.  The Newtonian
question produced velocity, acceleration, jerk, and the higher temporal
responses.  Yet the equation generating those responses keeps moving through
the Laplacian, and the Laplacian moves upward in spatial order.

The critical height lets us see the two directions on the same scale.  Under
\eqref{eq:BodyNavierStokesScaling},
\[
  H_\lambda(t)=H(\lambda^2t),
  \qquad
  H_\lambda^{(n)}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}H^{(n)}(\lambda^2t).
\]
The spatial energy at height \(n+\tfrac12\) carries exactly that weight:
\[
  E_{n+1/2,\lambda}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}E_{n+1/2}(\lambda^2t).
\]
Scaling has placed the \(n\)-th temporal response of \(H\) beside the
\((n+\tfrac12)\)-spatial energy.  The equation tells us why they belong
together.

Start from the exact balance already found in
\eqref{eq:BodySpatialEnergyBalanceIntro}:
\[
  E_s'=\mathcal P_s-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\]
At critical height,
\[
  H'=\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}.
\]
Differentiate this identity and use the balance at the next height:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  H''
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}'
  \\
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2E_{5/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
One more derivative gives
\[
  H'''
  =\partial_t^2\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\partial_t\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2\mathcal P_{5/2}
  -(2\nu)^3E_{7/2}.
\]
Nothing below the new top term has disappeared.  Every differentiated
transport--pressure response remains in the row, while viscosity exposes one
higher spatial energy.  For \(n\geq1\), induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightDiscovery}
  H^{(n)}
  =(-2\nu)^nE_{n+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
Since \(\nu>0\), the top spatial energy can be read back from the completed
row:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCompletedCriticalDiscovery}
  E_{n+1/2}
  =\frac{
    H^{(n)}-
    \displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
    (-2\nu)^j\partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}
  }{(-2\nu)^n}.
\end{equation}
This is an identity, not a free estimate.  The transfer terms have not been
discarded, and each term on the right still has to be controlled.  What has
changed is the direction of the problem: higher temporal response does not
lead us toward lower spatial order.  It exposes
\[
  E_{1/2},
  \quad E_{3/2},
  \quad E_{5/2},
  \quad E_{7/2},
  \quad\ldots
\]
inside the completed critical-height Tower.

The scalar height rows remain tied to the velocity responses.  Because
\[
  H(t)=\frac12
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}
       {\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}_{L^2},
\]
Leibniz's rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeightDerivativeTemporalContraction}
  H^{(n)}(t)
  =\frac12\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_a(t)}
       {\Lambda^{1/2}V_{n-a}(t)}_{L^2}.
\end{equation}
The \(n\)-th height derivative is not the velocity response \(V_n\), and it
is not the spatial energy \(E_{n+1/2}\).  It is the quadratic contraction
through which the temporal responses expose that energy once every
transport--pressure term is retained.

Now fix one response \(V_r\).  The same calculation begins again inside that
row.  Set
\[
  E_{r,s}:=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^sV_r}_2^2
\]
and let \(\mathcal P_{r,s}\) be the complete pairing of \(V_r\) with the
transport and pressure terms in its differentiated equation.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryResponseDiscovery}
  E_{r,s}'=\mathcal P_{r,s}-2\nu E_{r,s+1}.
\end{equation}
Repeated differentiation opens the spatial column
\[
  V_r\in H_x^0,
  \quad V_r\in H_x^1,
  \quad V_r\in H_x^2,
  \quad V_r\in H_x^3,
  \quad\ldots
\]
at every fixed temporal order \(r\).  Temporal order chooses the response;
spatial order chooses how finely that response is resolved.  They are two
independent directions inside one mixed derivative structure.

This is where the intersection enters.  If one fixed response belongs to
every finite spatial Sobolev space, then
\[
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}H^m(\R^3)
  =H^\infty(\R^3)
  \hookrightarrow C^\infty(\R^3).
\]
If every finite temporal response carries that spatial column, the resulting
object is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyDesiredMixedIntersection}
  \bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}
  H^r(I;H^m(\R^3)).
\end{equation}
For any prescribed temporal derivative and spatial derivative, one chooses a
finite coordinate high enough to reach it and then applies the corresponding
Sobolev embedding.  Smoothness is not being assigned to a chosen row.  It is
the common content of the complete intersection.

The discovery is now visible.  A singularity asks the fluid to ascend through
faster temporal responses while losing spatial regularity.  The viscous
recurrence opens the opposite grading: every response contains a direction
toward higher spatial regularity, and every spatial column contains the
intersection that yields smoothness.

The identities have shown us what must be assembled; they have not yet paid
the terminal bounds.  We now formalize the two-dimensional grid, cut it into
finite adjacent rectangles, and state the estimate that carries those
rectangles to the endpoint.
