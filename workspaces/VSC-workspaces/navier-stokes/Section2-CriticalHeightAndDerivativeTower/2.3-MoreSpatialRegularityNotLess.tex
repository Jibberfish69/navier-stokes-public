\subsection{More spatial regularity, not less}\label{sub:ThePressureCompleteMixedDerivativeTower}

The two directions first meet in the scaling.
For
\[
  E_s(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su(t)}_2^2,
  \qquad
  H=E_{1/2},
\]
the calculation in the opening gives
\[
  E_s[u_\lambda](t)
  =\lambda^{2s-1}E_s[u](\lambda^2t).
\]
At critical height the spatial factor vanishes, so
\[
  H[u_\lambda](t)=H[u](\lambda^2t).
\]
Differentiate this identity \(n\) times in \(t\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightTemporalScaling}
  \frac{d^n}{dt^n}H[u_\lambda](t)
  =\lambda^{2n}H^{(n)}[u](\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
Now set \(s=n+\tfrac12\) in the spatial scaling law:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySpatialHeightScalingDiagonal}
  E_{n+1/2}[u_\lambda](t)
  =\lambda^{2n}E_{n+1/2}[u](\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
The \(n\)-th temporal derivative of critical height and the spatial energy at height \(n+\tfrac12\) carry exactly the same scaling weight.
The heat clock predicted this diagonal: every time derivative costs a factor \(\lambda^2\), and every additional Sobolev step contributes that same factor to the quadratic energy.

Navier--Stokes turns the scaling match into an exact row of the equation.
Apply \(\Lambda^s\) to the momentum equation and pair with \(\Lambda^su\).
The time derivative gives \(E_s'\), viscosity gives
\[
  \nu\pair{\Lambda^su}{\Lambda^s\Delta u}_{L^2}
  =-\nu\norm{\Lambda^{s+1}u}_2^2
  =-2\nu E_{s+1},
\]
and incompressibility cancels the global pressure pairing after integration by parts.
Keep the complete transport--pressure contribution in
\[
  \mathcal P_s
  :=-
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^su}
  {\Lambda^s\bigl((u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p\bigr)}_{L^2}.
\]
The exact balance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalEnergyBalancePreview}
  E_s'=\mathcal P_s-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\end{equation}
At \(s=\tfrac12\), one temporal derivative of critical height exposes the next spatial height:
\[
  H'=\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}.
\]
Differentiate once more and substitute the same balance at \(s=\tfrac32\):
\begin{align*}
  H''
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}'
  \\
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2E_{5/2}.
\end{align*}
The next derivative uses the row at \(s=\tfrac52\) and gives
\[
  H'''
  =\partial_t^2\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\partial_t\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2\mathcal P_{5/2}
  -(2\nu)^3E_{7/2}.
\]
Nothing from the earlier rows has been discarded.
Each differentiation retains the full transfer already present and adds one higher viscous energy.
Induction gives, for \(n\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightSpatialAscentPreview}
  H^{(n)}
  =(-2\nu)^nE_{n+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient of the top spatial energy is nonzero because \(\nu>0\).
The formula can isolate \(E_{n+1/2}\) once the derivative of \(H\) and the transfer terms are controlled.
Its appearance is not yet that control.

This is the reversal.
The singularity direction requires the temporal response to become more violent as the heat clock contracts.
The viscous side of the same equation reaches farther into spatial Sobolev regularity at every temporal order.
One direction predicts increasing temporal instability; the other exposes more spatial regularity, not less.
They are balanced and opposing rather than complementary and self-feeding.

The distinction between exposure and control now matters.
No single finite row says that the velocity is smooth to every spatial order.
For one fixed response \(V_r\), spatial smoothness means that all of its finite Sobolev memberships hold together:
\[
  V_r(t)\in\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}H^m(\R^3).
\]
The same requirement at every finite temporal order gives the mixed intersection
\[
  \bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}
  H_t^rH_x^m.
\]
This notation does not sum derivative orders and does not ask for one constant uniform in \(r\) and \(m\).
It says that every chosen finite time--space coordinate is controlled on the same solution.

The geometry is easiest to see before the estimates are imposed:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 & H^0_x & H^1_x & H^2_x & H^3_x & \cdots\\
\hline
V_0=u      & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_1=u_t    & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_2=u_{tt} & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_3=u_{ttt}& \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
\vdots     & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \ddots
\end{array}
\]
Moving downward chooses a later temporal response.
Moving across asks for greater spatial regularity inside that response.
The recurrence built the grid; the scaling diagonal revealed why its two directions meet.

An infinite grid is approached through finite questions.
Fix a temporal depth \(N\) and a spatial depth \(M\); the coordinates with indices below those cutoffs form a finite rectangle.
Larger choices retain the smaller coordinates and add new rows or columns.
The next section turns this discovery into an estimate: it identifies the adjacent rows needed for a time trace, controls every chosen finite rectangle, and then assembles the resulting intersection.
