\subsection{The spatial height inside each response}\label{sub:ThePressureCompleteMixedDerivativeTower}

At critical height, a time derivative and one additional Sobolev level carry the
same scaling weight.  The equation decides whether that correspondence is only
dimensional or is an exact relation.  Work first on a strict classical interval;
the calculation below is an identity, not yet a bound.

For
\[
  E_s(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su(t)}_2^2,
  \qquad
  E_{s,\lambda}(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su_\lambda(t)}_2^2,
\]
\Cref{prop:CriticalSobolevScaling} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySpatialEnergyScaling}
  E_{s,\lambda}(t)
  =\lambda^{2s-1}E_s(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
At \(s=1/2\), this is the critical height:
\[
  H_\lambda(t)=H(\lambda^2t).
\]
Differentiating \(n\) times in time adds the factor \(\lambda^{2n}\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightTemporalScaling}
  H_\lambda^{(n)}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}H^{(n)}(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
The spatial energy at height \(n+\tfrac12\) has that same scale weight:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySpatialHeightScalingDiagonal}
  E_{n+1/2,\lambda}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}E_{n+1/2}(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
This agreement does not make the two quantities equal.
It says which spatial height belongs beside the \(n\)-th critical response.

The equation supplies their exact relation.
After applying \(\Lambda^s\) and pairing with \(\Lambda^su\), viscosity contributes
\[
  \nu\pair{\Lambda^su}{\Lambda^s\Delta u}_{L^2}
  =-\nu\norm{\Lambda^{s+1}u}_2^2
  =-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\]
Keep the remaining transport--pressure response together as
\[
  \mathcal P_s
  :=-
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^su}
  {\Lambda^s\bigl((u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p\bigr)}_{L^2}.
\]
The pressure pairing vanishes globally only after incompressibility is used; retaining it in \(\mathcal P_s\) keeps the row attached to the pressure-complete equation.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalEnergyBalance}
  E_s'=\mathcal P_s-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\end{equation}

At the first critical response,
\[
  H'=\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}.
\]
Differentiate and substitute the same balance at the next height,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  H''
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}'
  \\
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2E_{5/2},
\end{aligned}
\]
and one more derivative gives
\[
  H'''
  =\partial_t^2\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\partial_t\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2\mathcal P_{5/2}
  -(2\nu)^3E_{7/2}.
\]
Every previous transfer remains in the row while viscosity exposes one higher energy.
For \(n\geq1\), induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightSpatialAscent}
  H^{(n)}
  =(-2\nu)^nE_{n+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
Because \(\nu>0\), the highest exposed energy can be solved for:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCompletedCriticalRow}
  E_{n+1/2}
  =(-2\nu)^{-n}
  \left(
    H^{(n)}
    -\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
      (-2\nu)^j
      \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}
  \right).
\end{equation}
This is exactly what the ascent gains and exactly where it stops.
\Cref{prop:CriticalHeightSpatialAscent} isolates the higher Sobolev energy, but the entire right-hand side must still be controlled before that energy is bounded.

The critical response is itself a contraction of the temporal rows.
Since
\[
  H(t)
  =\frac12
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}
       {\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}_{L^2},
\]
Leibniz's rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeightDerivativeTemporalContraction}
  H^{(n)}(t)
  =\frac12\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_a(t)}
       {\Lambda^{1/2}V_{n-a}(t)}_{L^2}.
\end{equation}
One rung therefore has three related but distinct readings: the field response \(V_n\), the scalar contraction \(H^{(n)}\), and the spatial energy \(E_{n+1/2}\) exposed by viscosity.

The temporal column follows the successive responses forced by a finite-time escape.
The spatial column records the energies exposed beside them,
\(E_{1/2},E_{3/2},E_{5/2},E_{7/2},\ldots\).

The ladder still is not an estimate: its transfer terms may carry the size of the highest rung, and so far it has been displayed only inside \(V_0=u\).

The same spatial ascent occurs inside every temporal response.
For \(r\in\Nzero\), let \(E_{r,s}:=\tfrac12\norm{\Lambda^sV_r}_2^2\), and define the complete transport--pressure transfer in the \(r\)-th recurrence by
\[
  \mathcal P_{r,s}
  :=-
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^sV_r}
  {\Lambda^s\!\left[
    \sum_{a=0}^{r}\binom{r}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{r-a}
    +\nabla Q_r
  \right]}_{L^2}.
\]
Pairing the \(r\)-th temporal equation with \(\Lambda^{2s}V_r\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowSpatialEnergy}
  E_{r,s}'=\mathcal P_{r,s}-2\nu E_{r,s+1}.
\end{equation}
After any finite ascent depth \(k\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowCompletedHeight}
  \partial_t^kE_{r,1/2}
  =(-2\nu)^kE_{r,k+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,k-1-j}\mathcal P_{r,j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
The index \(r\) chooses a temporal response; \(k\) chooses a spatial height inside it.
Neither choice determines the other.

Applying the ascent in \Cref{prop:CriticalHeightSpatialAscent} inside each
temporal response gives the grid
{\small
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 & H^0_x & H^1_x & H^2_x & H^3_x & \cdots\\
\hline
V_0=u      & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_1=u_t    & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_2=u_{tt} & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_3=u_{ttt}& \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
\vdots     & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \ddots
\end{array}
\]
}
Every bullet is a derivative of the same velocity field, not an estimate already won.
Moving down changes temporal response; moving across changes spatial height.
On a strict interval \(I=(0,T)\) with \(T<T_*\), control of all coordinates would mean
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyDesiredMixedIntersection}
  u\in
  \bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}
  H^r(I;H^m(\R^3)).
\end{equation}

The grid is still only a family of identities on strict intervals.  At a finite
endpoint, a useful coordinate must possess a time trace.  That requirement will
choose the neighbouring rows, and hence the finite block, that must be controlled
together.
