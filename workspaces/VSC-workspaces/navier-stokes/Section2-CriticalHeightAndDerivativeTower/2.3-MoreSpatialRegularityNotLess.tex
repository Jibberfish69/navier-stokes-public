\subsection{More spatial regularity, not less}\label{sub:ThePressureCompleteMixedDerivativeTower}

The two directions make opposite predictions.
Under a finite singular time, every fixed temporal row loses \(L^3\) control by \eqref{eq:TemporalTowerMustEscape}.
Viscosity points across those rows toward higher spatial order.

Critical height records the same opposition.
For
\[
  E_s(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su(t)}_2^2,
  \qquad
  H=E_{1/2},
\]
one differentiation gives
\[
  H'
  =
  \mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2},
\]
where \(\mathcal P_{1/2}\) contains the full nonlinear and pressure transfer.
Differentiating again exposes \(E_{5/2}\) as the new highest viscous energy; the next derivative exposes \(E_{7/2}\).
Each step downward in temporal order opens another step upward in spatial regularity while retaining the earlier transfer terms.

The table matches orders; it does not identify \(V_n\) with the scalar quantity \(H^{(n)}\).
Nor does the appearance of \(E_{n+1/2}\) prove that it is bounded.
It reveals the reversal inside the equation.
The singularity requires increasingly violent temporal response, while viscosity exposes increasingly high spatial regularity.
A singularity would suggest less spatial regularity as the temporal order rises.
The Tower produces more.

For any fixed temporal row \(V_r\), complete finite-order spatial control would place that row in
\[
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}H^m(\R^3)
  =H^\infty(\R^3)
  \subset C^\infty(\R^3).
\]
The spaces intersect; their norms are not summed, and the constants at different orders need not agree.
The same implication is available separately in every temporal row.

Lay the rows beneath one another:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 & H^0_x & H^1_x & H^2_x & H^3_x & \cdots\\
\hline
V_0=u      & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_1=u_t    & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_2=u_{tt} & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_3=u_{ttt}& \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
\vdots     & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \ddots
\end{array}
\]
The downward coordinate is temporal order; the across coordinate is spatial regularity.
Their intersection is already present in each coordinate of the pressure-complete mixed jet.

No estimate controls the infinite grid in one step.
Fixing finite temporal depth \(N\) and finite spatial depth \(M\) cuts out a rectangle.
The exact \NavierStokes{} rectangle includes the adjacent rows and pressure relations required by the recurrence.
Those finite rectangles are the objects the next section controls.
