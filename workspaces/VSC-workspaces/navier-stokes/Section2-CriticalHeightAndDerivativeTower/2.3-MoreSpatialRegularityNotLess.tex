\subsection{More spatial regularity, not less}\label{sub:ThePressureCompleteMixedDerivativeTower}

Critical scaling gives one exact time--space match.  The \(n\)-th time derivative of the scalar height \(H\) and the spatial energy at height \(n+\tfrac12\) both carry the factor \(\lambda^{2n}\).  This does not identify either object with the Eulerian response \(V_n\); it tells us which temporal readout and spatial height occupy the same scale rung.

For
\[
  E_s(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su(t)}_2^2,
  \qquad
  E_{s,\lambda}(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su_\lambda(t)}_2^2,
  \qquad
  H=E_{1/2},
\]
the scaling calculation from the stretching vortex gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySpatialEnergyScaling}
  E_{s,\lambda}(t)
  =\lambda^{2s-1}E_s(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
At critical height the spatial factor disappears, so
\[
  H_\lambda(t)=H(\lambda^2t).
\]
Differentiating \(n\) times in time introduces \(\lambda^{2n}\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightTemporalScaling}
  H_\lambda^{(n)}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}H^{(n)}(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
The spatial energy at height \(n+\tfrac12\) carries exactly the same factor,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySpatialHeightScalingDiagonal}
  E_{n+1/2,\lambda}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}E_{n+1/2}(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
This is a scale match, not an identification of objects.  The temporal response \(V_n\), the scalar derivative \(H^{(n)}\), and the spatial energy \(E_{n+1/2}\) remain distinct.  What scaling has told us is where to look: the \(n\)-th response of critical height and the Sobolev height \(n+\tfrac12\) occupy the same rung.

The differentiated equation explains why that spatial height actually appears.  Because
\[
  H(t)=\frac12\pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}{\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}_{L^2},
\]
Leibniz's rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeightDerivativeTemporalContraction}
  H^{(n)}(t)
  =\frac12\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_a(t)}
       {\Lambda^{1/2}V_{n-a}(t)}_{L^2}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(H^{(n)}\) contracts together all temporal rows from \(V_0\) through \(V_n\).  It is the scalar readout of those rows at critical height.

Applying \(\Lambda^s\) to the momentum equation and pairing with \(\Lambda^su\) removes the global pressure pairing by incompressibility, while viscosity gives
\[
  \nu\pair{\Lambda^su}{\Lambda^s\Delta u}_{L^2}
  =-\nu\norm{\Lambda^{s+1}u}_2^2
  =-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Define the complete transport--pressure transfer by
\[
  \mathcal P_s
  :=-
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^su}
  {\Lambda^s\bigl((u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p\bigr)}_{L^2}.
\]
The exact energy row is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalEnergyBalance}
  E_s'=\mathcal P_s-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\end{equation}
Every time derivative of a spatial energy exposes the next Sobolev height through the viscous term.  Starting at \(H=E_{1/2}\), the first rung is
\[
  H'=\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}.
\]
Differentiating once more and using the same identity at \(s=\tfrac32\) gives
\begin{align*}
  H''
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}'
  \\
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2E_{5/2}.
\end{align*}
The next derivative reaches one height farther:
\[
  H'''
  =\partial_t^2\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\partial_t\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2\mathcal P_{5/2}
  -(2\nu)^3E_{7/2}.
\]
The transfer terms remain, and each new response adds one higher viscous energy.

Along the Zeno route, later temporal responses must grow whenever they continue to carry nontrivial changes across collapsing intervals.  In the differentiated equation, those same temporal orders expose successively higher Sobolev energies.  That reversal is the point: worse temporal response appears beside more spatial regularity, not less.

Exposure is not yet control.  The transfer terms still have to be paid, and the exact dependence is visible in the general row.  For every \(n\geq1\), induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightSpatialAscent}
  H^{(n)}
  =(-2\nu)^nE_{n+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
Because \(\nu>0\), the coefficient of the top spatial energy never vanishes.  Solving for that height gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCompletedCriticalRow}
  E_{n+1/2}
  =(-2\nu)^{-n}
  \left(
    H^{(n)}
    -\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
      (-2\nu)^j
      \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}
  \right).
\end{equation}
The conditional is exact: control \(H^{(n)}\) and every retained transfer term at depth \(n\), and the spatial energy \(E_{n+1/2}\) is controlled.  Scaling chose the diagonal; the differentiated equation supplied the terms that must be estimated on it.

The same ascent repeats inside every temporal row.  For a fixed response \(V_r\), define
\begin{align*}
  E_{r,s}
  &:=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^sV_r}_2^2,
  \\
  \mathcal P_{r,s}
  &:=-\operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^sV_r}
  {\Lambda^s\!\left(
    \sum_{a=0}^{r}\binom{r}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{r-a}
    +\nabla Q_r
  \right)}_{L^2}.
\end{align*}
Pairing the \(r\)-th temporal equation with \(\Lambda^{2s}V_r\) repeats the same spatial movement:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowSpatialEnergy}
  E_{r,s}'=\mathcal P_{r,s}-2\nu E_{r,s+1}.
\end{equation}
After any finite ascent depth \(k\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowCompletedHeight}
  \partial_t^kE_{r,1/2}
  =(-2\nu)^kE_{r,k+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,k-1-j}\mathcal P_{r,j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
The scalar on the left involves \(V_r,\ldots,V_{r+k}\); it is not any one of those responses.  The important point is that the spatial ascent repeats inside every finite \(V_r\).  Temporal depth and spatial height have become independent coordinates.

The one scaling diagonal has opened into the full mixed grid:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 & H^0_x & H^1_x & H^2_x & H^3_x & \cdots\\
\hline
V_0=u      & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_1=u_t    & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_2=u_{tt} & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_3=u_{ttt}& \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
\vdots     & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \ddots
\end{array}
\]
Moving down selects a later temporal response.  Moving across selects a higher Sobolev height inside that response.  One diagonal coordinate is not smoothness, and one row is not the Tower.  Smoothness asks for every finite coordinate to belong to the same mixed object.  On a time interval \(I\), that target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyDesiredMixedIntersection}
  u\in
  \bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}
  H^r(I;H^m(\R^3)).
\end{equation}
This is the intersection inside the Tower.  It is where the escalating temporal responses and the ascending spatial energies have to meet.

No finite estimate can pay the entire intersection in one move.  But every coordinate in it lies inside some finite cut.  A temporal depth \(N\) and a spatial height \(M\) cut out the rows \(0,\ldots,N\) and columns \(0,\ldots,M\); increasing either cutoff preserves every coordinate already paid.  The Tower began by asking what a Zeno singularity would require from responses that keep carrying nontrivial changes.  Differentiating Navier--Stokes turns that conditional temporal question into a two-dimensional demand: every finite temporal depth at every finite spatial height.  The rectangle is that demand in the finite form an estimate can actually control.
