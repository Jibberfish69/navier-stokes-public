\subsection{Velocity, acceleration, and the Tower}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

The Zeno route asks the velocity field to complete nontrivial changes on shorter and shorter clocks.
A fixed change in velocity completed in half the time requires twice the rate of change.
Along a fluid particle, that rate is acceleration.
If the acceleration then makes a fixed change on the next shorter clock, its own rate of change has to grow.
Along the particle this next quantity is material jerk, \(D_t(D_tu)\).
This is the beginning of the response Tower: velocity, acceleration, jerk, and every later response the motion continues to activate.

The word \emph{continues} carries the burden.
A short interval forces no large derivative when the quantity has stopped changing.
The Zeno clocks supply the shrinking denominators, while a derivative cascade also needs a nontrivial increment on every rung.

Along a particle path \(X'(t)=u(X(t),t)\),
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}u(X(t),t)
  =D_tu(X(t),t),
  \qquad
  D_tu:=\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u.
\]
Navier--Stokes already resolves that material acceleration into pressure and viscosity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
This is the acceleration felt by a moving particle.
To differentiate the field equation repeatedly, we also need the Eulerian responses at a fixed spatial coordinate.
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
The two notions first separate at order one:
\[
  D_tu=V_1+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0.
\]
The material derivative follows the particle through the velocity field.
The rows \(V_0,V_1,V_2,\ldots\) record how that field changes at a fixed point and are the rows generated by differentiating Navier--Stokes in time.
In particular, \(V_2\) is not the material jerk; transport terms connect the two descriptions.

The elementary estimate behind the Tower keeps the change and the available time separate.
Fix a Banach space \(B\).
On any interval \([s,t]\) for which \(V_n\in W^{1,1}(s,t;B)\) with distributional derivative \(V_{n+1}\),
\[
  V_n(t)-V_n(s)
  =\int_s^tV_{n+1}(\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyResponseEscalation}
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s<\tau<t}
  \norm{V_{n+1}(\tau)}_B
  \geq
  \frac{\norm{V_n(t)-V_n(s)}_B}{t-s}.
\end{equation}
If \(V_n\) makes a definite change while the interval collapses, \(V_{n+1}\) must grow:
\[
  t_k-s_k\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad
  \norm{V_n(t_k)-V_n(s_k)}_B\geq c_n>0.
\]
Both hypotheses are essential.
The clock supplies the shrinking interval; the dynamics must still supply the increment.
Repeating the estimate at the next order requires a new nontrivial increment for that next row.

The equation tells us what those rows contain.
For \(V_0=u\),
\[
  V_1
  =\nu\Delta V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -\nabla Q_0.
\]
One time derivative can land on either copy of velocity in the transport term:
\[
  V_2
  =\nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1.
\]
One more derivative produces the middle placement twice:
\[
  V_3
  =\nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2.
\]
At order \(n\), the binomial coefficients count every way the time derivatives can divide between the transporting velocity and the velocity being transported:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}
This recurrence is the exact Eulerian temporal Tower generated by Navier--Stokes.

Pressure climbs the same Tower.
Taking the divergence of the original equation and using decay at spatial infinity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j).
\end{equation}
Here \(R_j:=\partial_j(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\), and the decaying normalization removes the arbitrary spatial constant in pressure at each time.
After \(n\) time derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
The velocity rows determine \(Q_n\), and \(\nabla Q_n\) returns in the equation for \(V_{n+1}\).
The Tower cannot stay purely temporal.
Viscosity contributes \(\Delta V_n\), transport contributes a spatial derivative, and pressure is recovered through spatial singular integrals of the same velocity products.
The resulting mixed coordinates are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
  \qquad
  \alpha\in\Nzero^3.
\end{equation}
One spatial derivative splits the transport term again:
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
For a general multi-index \(\alpha\), Leibniz's rule gives
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
The pressure coordinates satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}

We followed the responses that an accelerating concentration would need and found more structure than a list of time derivatives.
Every temporal rung contains coupled spatial derivatives, with pressure tied to the same velocity rows.
The apparent route into faster temporal change has exposed a second direction of higher spatial order.
