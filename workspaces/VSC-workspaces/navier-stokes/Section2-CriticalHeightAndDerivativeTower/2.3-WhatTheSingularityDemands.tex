\subsection{Velocity, acceleration, and the Tower}\label{sub:BodyFiniteTimeResponseEscalation}

A fixed change in velocity completed in half the time requires twice the rate of change.
Along a fluid particle, that rate is acceleration.
If the acceleration itself makes a fixed change in the next shorter interval, jerk has to grow as well.
The same thought can continue through every later response.

But every rung has to earn the next one.
A shorter interval forces nothing when the quantity has stopped changing.
The Zeno cascade supplies the collapsing intervals; a full derivative cascade requires a nontrivial change on each of them.

Along a particle path \(X'(t)=u(X(t),t)\),
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}u(X(t),t)
  =D_tu(X(t),t),
  \qquad
  D_tu:=\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u.
\]
Navier--Stokes is already an equation for that material acceleration:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MaterialAccelerationEquationBody}
  D_tu=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u.
\end{equation}
The acceleration is already inside the equation.
To see the repeated field responses, hold the spatial coordinate fixed and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TemporalTowerDefinitionBody}
  V_n:=\partial_t^nu,
  \qquad
  Q_n:=\partial_t^np,
  \qquad
  n\in\Nzero.
\end{equation}
The distinction from material acceleration is exact:
\[
  D_tu=V_1+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0.
\]
The material derivative follows a particle, while the rows \(V_0,V_1,V_2,\ldots\) are the fixed-position responses generated by differentiating the field equation.

The shrinking-interval argument has a precise form.
On any interval \([s,t]\), absolute continuity in a fixed Banach space \(B\) gives
\[
  V_n(t)-V_n(s)
  =\int_s^tV_{n+1}(\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyResponseEscalation}
  \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s<\tau<t}
  \norm{V_{n+1}(\tau)}_B
  \geq
  \frac{\norm{V_n(t)-V_n(s)}_B}{t-s}.
\end{equation}
If \(V_n\) still makes a definite change while the interval collapses, \(V_{n+1}\) must grow:
\[
  t_k-s_k\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad
  \norm{V_n(t_k)-V_n(s_k)}_B\geq c_n>0.
\]
That is the whole condition.
Time compression forces the next Eulerian response only when the present response still has a nontrivial change to make.
The clock supplies the shrinking interval, not the increment.

The equation supplies the rows themselves.
For \(V_0=u\),
\[
  V_1
  =\nu\Delta V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -\nabla Q_0.
\]
One time derivative lands on both copies of velocity in the transport term:
\[
  V_2
  =\nu\Delta V_1
  -(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -\nabla Q_1.
\]
One more derivative produces the middle placement twice:
\[
  V_3
  =\nu\Delta V_2
  -(V_2\cdot\nabla)V_0
  -2(V_1\cdot\nabla)V_1
  -(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_2
  -\nabla Q_2.
\]
At order \(n\), the binomial coefficients count every way the derivatives can divide between the transporting velocity and the velocity being transported:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}
  V_{n+1}
  +\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +\nabla Q_n
  =\nu\Delta V_n,
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot V_n=0.
\end{equation}
This recurrence is the temporal Tower generated by Navier--Stokes.

Pressure climbs with it.
Taking the divergence of the original equation and using decay at spatial infinity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PressureNormalization}
  -\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j),
  \qquad
  p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j).
\end{equation}
After \(n\) time derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence}
  Q_n
  =R_iR_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}
The velocity rows determine \(Q_n\), and \(\nabla Q_n\) returns in the equation for \(V_{n+1}\).
So the Tower never stays purely temporal.
Viscosity contributes \(\Delta V_n\), transport contributes a spatial derivative, and pressure is recovered by spatial singular integrals of the same velocity products.
The resulting mixed coordinates are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedJetDefinition}
  J_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}:=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
  \qquad
  \alpha\in\Nzero^3.
\end{equation}
One spatial derivative splits the transport term again:
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
For a general multi-index \(\alpha\), Leibniz's rule gives
\begin{align}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  +\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  \notag\\
  &=
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
  \label{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}
\end{align}
The pressure coordinates satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence}
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_x^\alpha\partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a)_i(V_{n-a})_j.
\end{equation}

We went looking for the temporal responses a Zeno singularity would demand.
The equation gave us something larger: every temporal rung already carries a spatial ladder.
That is where the direction of the argument changes.
