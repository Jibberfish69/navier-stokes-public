\subsection{More spatial regularity, not less}\label{sub:ThePressureCompleteMixedDerivativeTower}

Nothing in the temporal argument looked like regularity.
A nontrivial change squeezed into a shorter interval produces a larger response, and repeating that demand looks like a ladder away from smoothness.
Now use the viscous term that has been present in every row.
Each temporal row contains \(\nu\Delta V_n\), so the same climb through time also reaches farther into space.

Critical scaling tells us exactly where the two counts line up.
For
\[
  E_s(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su(t)}_2^2,
  \qquad
  E_{s,\lambda}(t):=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su_\lambda(t)}_2^2,
\]
the calculation from critical height gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySpatialEnergyScaling}
  E_{s,\lambda}(t)
  =\lambda^{2s-1}E_s(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
Since \(H=E_{1/2}\),
\[
  H_\lambda(t)=H(\lambda^2t).
\]
After \(n\) time derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightTemporalScaling}
  H_\lambda^{(n)}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}H^{(n)}(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
The spatial energy at height \(n+\tfrac12\) has the same scale factor:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodySpatialHeightScalingDiagonal}
  E_{n+1/2,\lambda}(t)
  =\lambda^{2n}E_{n+1/2}(\lambda^2t).
\end{equation}
This does not make \(H^{(n)}\) and \(E_{n+1/2}\) the same object.
It identifies the diagonal on which the equation can connect them.

Apply \(\Lambda^s\) to Navier--Stokes and pair the result with \(\Lambda^su\).
The viscous contribution is
\[
  \nu\pair{\Lambda^su}{\Lambda^s\Delta u}_{L^2}
  =-\nu\norm{\Lambda^{s+1}u}_2^2
  =-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\]
Keep the complete transport--pressure transfer in
\[
  \mathcal P_s
  :=-
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^su}
  {\Lambda^s\bigl((u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p\bigr)}_{L^2}.
\]
The exact spatial-energy row is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalEnergyBalance}
  E_s'=\mathcal P_s-2\nu E_{s+1}.
\end{equation}

At critical height, the first derivative already contains the next spatial energy:
\[
  H'=\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}.
\]
Differentiate again and use the same row at \(s=\tfrac32\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
  H''
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}-2\nu E_{3/2}'
  \\
  &=\partial_t\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2E_{5/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The third derivative reaches one height farther:
\[
  H'''
  =\partial_t^2\mathcal P_{1/2}
  -2\nu\partial_t\mathcal P_{3/2}
  +(2\nu)^2\mathcal P_{5/2}
  -(2\nu)^3E_{7/2}.
\]
Every differentiation keeps the transfer terms already produced and adds one higher viscous energy.
For \(n\geq1\), induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightSpatialAscent}
  H^{(n)}
  =(-2\nu)^nE_{n+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
Because \(\nu>0\), the top spatial energy can be isolated:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCompletedCriticalRow}
  E_{n+1/2}
  =(-2\nu)^{-n}
  \left(
    H^{(n)}
    -\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
      (-2\nu)^j
      \partial_t^{\,n-1-j}\mathcal P_{j+1/2}
  \right).
\end{equation}
The identity exposes the higher Sobolev energy.
It does not bound that energy until \(H^{(n)}\) and every transfer term beside it have also been controlled.

The temporal Tower enters through \(H^{(n)}\) itself.
Since
\[
  H(t)
  =\frac12
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}
       {\Lambda^{1/2}V_0(t)}_{L^2},
\]
Leibniz's rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyHeightDerivativeTemporalContraction}
  H^{(n)}(t)
  =\frac12\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  \operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}V_a(t)}
       {\Lambda^{1/2}V_{n-a}(t)}_{L^2}.
\end{equation}
The three entries on one rung are now distinct and connected: \(V_n\) is the Eulerian response, \(H^{(n)}\) contracts the temporal rows at critical height, and \(E_{n+1/2}\) is the spatial energy exposed through viscosity.

Read the rows in the order we found them.
The possible singularity asks for larger temporal responses whenever a nontrivial change is compressed into a shorter interval.
The differentiated equation does not send those orders toward weaker spatial spaces.
It reaches \(E_{3/2},E_{5/2},E_{7/2},\ldots\): more spatial regularity, not less.

One diagonal is not smoothness.
The same spatial ascent has to be available inside every temporal response, not only inside \(V_0=u\).
Fix \(r\in\Nzero\) and set
\begin{align*}
  E_{r,s}
  &:=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^sV_r}_2^2,
  \\
  \mathcal P_{r,s}
  &:=-\operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^sV_r}
  {\Lambda^s\!\left(
    \sum_{a=0}^{r}\binom{r}{a}(V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{r-a}
    +\nabla Q_r
  \right)}_{L^2}.
\end{align*}
Pairing the \(r\)-th temporal equation with \(\Lambda^{2s}V_r\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowSpatialEnergy}
  E_{r,s}'=\mathcal P_{r,s}-2\nu E_{r,s+1}.
\end{equation}
After any finite ascent depth \(k\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyEveryTemporalRowCompletedHeight}
  \partial_t^kE_{r,1/2}
  =(-2\nu)^kE_{r,k+1/2}
  +\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
  (-2\nu)^j
  \partial_t^{\,k-1-j}\mathcal P_{r,j+1/2}.
\end{equation}
The temporal index \(r\) chooses the response, and the spatial index \(k\) chooses how high we climb inside that response.
They are independent coordinates of the mixed jet.

Lay those coordinates out together:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 & H^0_x & H^1_x & H^2_x & H^3_x & \cdots\\
\hline
V_0=u      & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_1=u_t    & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_2=u_{tt} & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
V_3=u_{ttt}& \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \bullet & \cdots\\
\vdots     & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \vdots  & \ddots
\end{array}
\]
Moving down selects a later temporal response.
Moving across selects a higher Sobolev height inside that response.
Smoothness requires every finite coordinate in both directions:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyDesiredMixedIntersection}
  u\in
  \bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}
  H^r(I;H^m(\R^3)).
\end{equation}

No finite estimate can control the entire intersection in one step.
Choose a temporal depth \(N\) and a spatial depth \(M\).
The rows through \(N\) and the columns through \(M\) cut out one finite rectangle, and every coordinate in the intersection belongs to some such rectangle.

The vortex began as the sharpest way concentration could preserve the critical balance.
Following it through the heat clock and the response Tower has turned the possible singularity into a concrete finite target: control every chosen rectangle, for every finite \(N\) and \(M\).
