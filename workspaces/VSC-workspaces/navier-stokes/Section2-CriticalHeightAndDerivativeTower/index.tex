\section{Critical Height and the Derivative Tower}\label{sec:CriticalHeightAndDerivativeTower}

The obstruction is easier to understand first as a motion than as a missing
estimate.  In three dimensions a vortex may lengthen along one direction while
its cross-section contracts, carrying the same rotation into a thinner region
and steepening the velocity across it.  Viscosity is acting throughout, but it
acts on the increasingly fine structure produced by the stretching itself.

We follow the geometry most favourable to that concentration.  It does not
stand in for a singular solution.  Its purpose is to give a possible finite-time
failure a definite physical shape, and then to let the equation tell us which
quantities must be followed when that shape is driven toward its limit.
