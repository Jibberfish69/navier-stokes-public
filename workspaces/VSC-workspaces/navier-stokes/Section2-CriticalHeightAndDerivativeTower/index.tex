\section{Critical Height and the Derivative Tower}\label{sec:CriticalHeightAndDerivativeTower}

The question now is what the same fluid would have to do if \(T_*\) were
finite.  Its kinetic energy can remain bounded, so the singular motion has to
appear in the way the velocity changes across space.

Take the cleanest case: a perfectly stretching vortex, with no wobble and no
loss of alignment to interrupt the amplification.  We keep that one vortex in
view as its cross-section narrows, the viscous clock shortens, and infinitely
many smaller clocks become capable of fitting inside a finite time.  But a new
scale is not reached by naming it.  The velocity has to move through the
change, and the first temporal response measures that movement.  If that
response loses control, the next response asks what carried the loss, and the
question can be repeated.  The derivative Tower comes from asking the proposed
singularity to account for every one of these responses in the same fluid.
