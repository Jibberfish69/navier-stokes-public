\subsection{The Pressure-Complete Mixed Jet}\label{sub:ThePressureCompleteMixedJet}

At temporal order \(n\), the recurrences \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence} and \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence} contain \(V_{n+1}\), \(\nu\Delta V_n\), every binomial split of the transport term, and the normalized pressure row \(Q_n\).
To see the spatial derivatives already present in that equation, begin with one derivative of a transport factor.
Differentiate one transport factor in the coordinate direction \(x_\ell\):
\[
  \partial_\ell\bigl((V_a\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}\bigr)
  =
  ((\partial_\ell V_a)\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}
  +(V_a\cdot\nabla)\partial_\ell V_{n-a}.
\]
At higher spatial order, every derivative divides between the two factors.
For \(\alpha\in\Nzero^3\), write the corresponding velocity and pressure derivatives as
\[
  J_{n,\alpha}
  :=
  \partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u,
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}
  :=
  \partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p,
\]
and for \(\beta\le\alpha\), set
\[
  \binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  :=
  \prod_{k=1}^{3}\binom{\alpha_k}{\beta_k}.
\]
The simultaneous temporal and spatial product rules give
\[
  \partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha\bigl((u\cdot\nabla)u\bigr)
  =
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\le\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}
  \binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}.
\]
Substitution into the equation exposes the full mixed recurrence:
\begin{align*}
  J_{n+1,\alpha}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\le\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}
  \binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta}\cdot\nabla)
  J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta}
  \\
  &+\nabla\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \nu\Delta J_{n,\alpha},
  \qquad
  \nabla\cdot J_{n,\alpha}=0.
\end{align*}
Its pressure coordinates satisfy
\[
  -\Delta\Pi_{n,\alpha}
  =
  \partial_i\partial_j
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}
  \sum_{\beta\le\alpha}
  \binom{n}{a}
  \binom{\alpha}{\beta}
  (J_{a,\beta})_i
  (J_{n-a,\alpha-\beta})_j.
\]

Every \(J_{n,\alpha}\) and \(\Pi_{n,\alpha}\) above is a derivative of the original pressure-complete solution.
A finite rectangle will later choose a finite range of \(n\) and \(\alpha\); it will not introduce a second solution or a second derivative family.

The viscous term explains why moving upward in \(n\) also reaches farther into the spatial derivatives: \(J_{n+1,\alpha}\) is coupled to \(\Delta J_{n,\alpha}\).
The critical-height calculation now isolates that spatial gain without discarding the nonlinear and pressure terms beside it.
