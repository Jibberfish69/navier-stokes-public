\subsection{Restarting the Same History}\label{sub:RestartingTheSameHistory}

At \(T_*\), the left history supplies the smooth datum \(u_*\) and its pressure-complete jet.
Local well-posedness gives a right-hand solution from that same datum.
Higher regularity persists on one common restart interval, so the right-hand jet is generated by the same normalized recurrences.
Uniqueness makes the two pieces one history.

\begin{lemma}[Smooth local restart and uniqueness]\label{lem:SmoothLocalRestart}
Let \(t_0\in\R\) and let \(a\in\Hinfty_\sigma(\R^3)\), with the class defined in \eqref{eq:SectionThreeSmoothSolenoidalClass}.
There is a \(\delta>0\) and a unique smooth pressure-normalized solution \((w,q)\) of the same \NavierStokes{} system on \([t_0,t_0+\delta]\times\R^3\) with \(w(t_0)=a\) and
\[
  q=R_iR_j(w_iw_j).
\]
Every finite spatial Sobolev order persists on the same interval, and the temporal jet at \(t_0\) is generated by the pressure-normalized recurrences \eqref{eq:SectionThreeEndpointPressureJet} and \eqref{eq:SectionThreeEndpointVelocityJet}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(a\in H^3(\R^3)\hookrightarrow L^3(\R^3)\), apply Kato's local strong \(L^3\) existence and uniqueness theorem \parencite{Kato1984}.
The standard Sobolev persistence estimates place the solution in \(H^3\) and retain every additional finite Sobolev order of \(a\) on the lifespan of that one strong solution.
Differentiating the smooth solution at its initial face and using \eqref{eq:SectionThreePressureNormalization} gives the stated pressure and velocity recurrences.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global continuation from whole-terminal rectangles]\label{thm:CompatibleIntersectionClosure}
Let \(u_0\) be smooth and divergence-free on \(\R^3\), with \(u_0\) and all of its spatial derivatives decaying rapidly at infinity, let \(\nu>0\), and let \((u,p)\) be the corresponding maximal pressure-normalized classical history.
Assume that this history satisfies the whole-terminal compatible-rectangle conclusion of \Cref{thm:WholeTerminalCompatibleRectangleEstimate}.
Then \(T_*=\infty\), and its normalized velocity and pressure are smooth on every finite time interval.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume for contradiction that the maximal time \(T_*\) is finite.
By the theorem's hypothesis, every finite record \(\cR_{N,M}[u_0]\) exists on \(I_*\), with compatible restrictions.
Their compatible intersection gives the common endpoint \(u_*\in\Hinfty_\sigma(\R^3)\) in \eqref{eq:SectionThreeCommonEndpoint} and the actual pressure-complete endpoint jet in \eqref{eq:SectionThreeEndpointPressureJet}--\eqref{eq:SectionThreeEndpointVelocityJet}.
Apply the local restart lemma at \(t_0=T_*\) with datum \(a=u_*\).
It produces a smooth solution \((w,q)\) on \([T_*,T_*+\delta]\).
Define
\[
  (\widetilde u,\widetilde p)(t)
  :=
  \begin{cases}
    (u,p)(t),&0\leq t<T_*,\\
    (w,q)(t),&T_*\leq t\leq T_*+\delta.
  \end{cases}
\]
The velocity values agree at \(T_*\) by construction.
The left and right pressure and velocity derivatives agree at every finite order because both jets obey the same normalized recurrences from the same endpoint datum.
The piecewise pair is consequently smooth across \(T_*\) and solves the same fixed-viscosity system there.
Its lifespan is longer than the alleged maximal lifespan.
Strong-solution uniqueness identifies this extension as the continuation of the original datum-generated history.
This contradicts the definition of \(T_*\).
Hence
\[
  T_*=\infty.
\]
Every finite temporal and spatial derivative remains in the compatible Sobolev intersection on each finite interval, and Sobolev embedding gives
\[
  u,p\in C^\infty([0,\infty)\times\R^3).
\]
\end{proof}
