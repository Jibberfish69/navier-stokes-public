\subsection{Finite adjacent rectangles}\label{sub:BodyFiniteAdjacentRectangles}

To give one temporal row \(V_n\) a terminal value in \(H^M\), control that row one spatial step above \(M\) and its time derivative one step below.
For a smooth Hilbert-valued function \(v\), let \(A=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}\).
Then, for \(0\leq a<b\leq T\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyAdjacentTraceIdentity}
  \norm{v(b)}_{H^M}^2-
  \norm{v(a)}_{H^M}^2
  =2\operatorname{Re}
  \int_a^b
  \pair{A^{M-1}v_t(t)}{A^{M+1}v(t)}_{L^2}\,dt.
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyAdjacentTracePair}
  v\in L^2(0,T;H^{M+1}),
  \qquad
  v_t\in L^2(0,T;H^{M-1})
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyAdjacentTraceConclusion}
  v\in C([0,T];H^M).
\end{equation}
Indeed, Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the change of the squared \(H^M\) norm by
\[
  2\norm{v_t}_{L^2(a,b;H^{M-1})}
   \norm{v}_{L^2(a,b;H^{M+1})},
\]
and the standard Hilbert-triple argument supplies the strong continuous representative.
Time mollification and Fourier truncation justify the identity for the pair in \eqref{eq:BodyAdjacentTracePair}; weak \(H^M\) continuity and continuity of the norm then give the strong representative in \eqref{eq:BodyAdjacentTraceConclusion}.

The first continuation-grade pair is already concrete:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FirstConcreteAdjacentPair}
  u\in L^2(0,T;H^3),
  \qquad
  u_t\in L^2(0,T;H^1)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  u\in C([0,T];H^2).
\end{equation}
The upper space controls the field one derivative above the desired trace height.
The lower space controls its time derivative one derivative below.
They meet at \(H^2\).

Apply the same pair to \(v=V_n\), so that \(v_t=V_{n+1}\).
For finite temporal depth \(N\) and middle Sobolev height \(M\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyRectangleNorm}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathfrak R_{N,M}(u;T)
  :={}&
  \sum_{n=0}^{N}
  \norm{V_n}_{L^2(0,T;H^{M+1})}^2
  \\
  &+
  \sum_{n=0}^{N}
  \norm{V_{n+1}}_{L^2(0,T;H^{M-1})}^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
When \(M=0\), the lower edge is \(H^{-1}\).
The temporal and spatial cutoffs are independent, and the constant attached to one rectangle is allowed to depend on both.

For example, \((N,M)=(1,2)\) contains two overlapping adjacent pairs:

The repeated \(V_1\) is one actual derivative of the same solution, not a second copy created by the table.

The pressure-complete rectangle \(\cR_{N,M}[u_0;T]\) is a finite view of the mixed jet itself.
Its upper and lower velocity edges retain the coordinates \(J_{n,\alpha}=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha u\) represented by the two adjacent Sobolev levels, and its pressure edge retains the corresponding \(\Pi_{n,\alpha}=\partial_t^n\partial_x^\alpha p\).
The recurrences \eqref{eq:BodyMixedJetRecurrence}--\eqref{eq:BodyMixedPressureRecurrence} identify every retained entry with the jet generated from \(u_0\).
For each strict cutoff \(T<T_*\), classicality on \([0,T]\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:StrictSubintervalRectangleBound}
  \mathfrak R_{N,M}(u;T)<\infty
  \qquad\text{for every finite }N,M.
\end{equation}
This does not yet control the terminal approach.
A norm may be finite on every strict subinterval while its values diverge as the cutoff approaches \(T_*\).

The missing quantifier is now exact.
After \(N\) and \(M\) are fixed, one bound has to survive every \(T<T_*\).
That is the whole-terminal theorem.
