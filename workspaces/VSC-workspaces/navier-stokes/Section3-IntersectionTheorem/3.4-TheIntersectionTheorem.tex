\subsection{The estimate that fills the rectangles}\label{sub:BodyCompatibleIntersection}

\begin{theorem}[Datum-generated whole-terminal rectangles]\label{thm:BodyWholeTerminalRectangleTheorem}
Let \(u_0\in\Ssigma\), let \(\nu>0\), and let \((u,p)\) be the maximal classical
solution generated by \((u_0,\nu)\) on \([0,T_*)\).  If \(T_*<\infty\), then for every
finite \(N,M\in\Nzero\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyWholeTerminalRectangleEstimate}
  \sup_{0<T<T_*}\mathfrak R_{N,M}(u;T)<\infty.
\end{equation}
The associated pressure rows are generated by
\eqref{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence} and use the fixed decaying normalization.
\end{theorem}

The datum supplies the initial face of every finite block.
Because \(u_0\in\Ssigma\), \(V_0(0)\) belongs to every finite Sobolev space.
Assume the rows \(V_0(0),\ldots,V_n(0)\) do as well, and fix a target order \(m\).
At a sufficiently high finite Sobolev order, multiplication and the Riesz-transform formula \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence} give \(Q_n(0)\in H^{m+1}\).
Then \(V_n(0)\in H^{m+2}\), the finite transport products, and \(\nabla Q_n(0)\in H^m\) place \(V_{n+1}(0)\) in \(H^m\) through \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalVelocityRecurrence}.
Induction yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyInitialPressureCompleteMixedJet}
  J_{n,\alpha}(0)\in H_\sigma^m(\R^3),
  \qquad
  \Pi_{n,\alpha}(0)\in H^m(\R^3)
\end{equation}
for every finite \(n\), \(\alpha\), and \(m\).

Read \eqref{eq:BodyWholeTerminalRectangleEstimate} back to the datum.
The constants \(C_{\alpha,K}\) in \eqref{eq:FeffermanDatumClass} put \(u_0\)
in every finite spatial Sobolev class.  The recurrences then generate the
complete initial pressure--velocity jet
\eqref{eq:BodyInitialPressureCompleteMixedJet}.  The theorem carries any
chosen finite block of those coordinates from \(t=0\) across every cutoff
\(0<T<T_*\) and says directly that its supremum remains finite as
\(T\uparrow T_*\).

Fix \(N\) and \(M\).  The integrands in \eqref{eq:BodyRectangleNorm} are
nonnegative, so monotone convergence as \(T\uparrow T_*\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyWholeTerminalAdjacentRows}
  V_n\in L^2(I_*;H^{M+1}),
  \qquad
  V_{n+1}=\partial_tV_n\in L^2(I_*;H^{M-1}),
  \qquad 0\leq n\leq N.
\end{equation}
The pressure recurrence, Riesz-transform bounds, and finite-order Sobolev products give
the corresponding finite pressure coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyWholeTerminalPressureRows}
  Q_n\in L^1(I_*;H^M),
  \qquad 0\leq n\leq N.
\end{equation}

Choose a larger pair \((N,M)\) and then forget its higher rows or lower its Sobolev
height.  The derivatives that remain are literally the derivatives already present in
the smaller block.  If \(N'\leq N\) and \(M'\leq M\), denote this restriction by
\(\rho_{N',M'}^{N,M}\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyRectangleRestrictionCompatibility}
  \rho_{N',M'}^{N,M}\cR_{N,M}[u_0]
  =\cR_{N',M'}[u_0].
\end{equation}
This ordinary overlap is what lets all finite blocks be read at once:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DatumSpecificCompatibleIntersection}
  \boxed{
  \cI[u_0]
  :=\bigl(\cR_{N,M}[u_0]\bigr)_{N,M\in\Nzero},
  \qquad
  \rho_{N',M'}^{N,M}\cR_{N,M}[u_0]
  =\cR_{N',M'}[u_0].}
\end{equation}
There is no new field at the intersection.  Given any finite temporal order and any
finite spatial height, choose one rectangle large enough to contain them.  A still
larger rectangle returns the same coordinate of the same \(u\).

\begin{proposition}[The mixed intersection]\label{prop:BodyCompatibleProjectiveAssembly}
The velocity component of \(\cI[u_0]\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyProducedMixedIntersection}
  u\in
  \bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}
  \bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}
  H^r(I_*;H^m(\R^3)).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CompleteSpatialRow}
  u\in\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}L^2(I_*;H^m(\R^3)).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix finite \(r,m\), and choose \(N\geq r\) and \(M\geq m\).  The upper edges of
that rectangle give
\[
  \partial_t^nu\in L^2(I_*;H^{M+1})
  \hookrightarrow L^2(I_*;H^m)
  \qquad(0\leq n\leq r).
\]
These are the distributional time derivatives of the same velocity field, so
\(u\in H^r(I_*;H^m)\).  Since \(r\) and \(m\) were arbitrary, all finite coordinates
belong to the intersection.  Taking \(r=0\) gives \eqref{eq:CompleteSpatialRow}.
\end{proof}
