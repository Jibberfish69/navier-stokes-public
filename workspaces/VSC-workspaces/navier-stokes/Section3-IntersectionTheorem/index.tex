\section{The Intersection Inside the Tower}\label{sec:TheIntersectionInsideTheTower}

The Tower was built because a finite-time singularity would have to force the
same velocity through faster and faster responses.  But the equation that
generates the next response also contains \(\nu\Delta V_n\).  Before the Tower
can be read as a march toward roughness, we have to ask what that Laplacian has
been doing to its spatial side.

Put the two directions beside each other.  Time runs down through
\(V_0,V_1,V_2,\ldots\); space runs across the Sobolev heights carried by each
response.  If the two directions were cooperating in a singularity, climbing
in time should cost spatial regularity.  The viscous row exposes the opposite
relation.  Each new temporal response opens a higher spatial energy.  We make
that reversal exact, lay the resulting coordinates into one table, and then
ask which finite pieces can reach a common time slice.  Those pieces are the
rectangles.  The next section asks whether their bounds survive the alleged
last time; only then can their compatible intersection become smoothness.
