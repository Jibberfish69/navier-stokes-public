\section{The Missing Estimates and Global Smoothness}\label{sec:TheMissingEstimateAndGlobalSmoothness}

\subsection{The first coordinate already closes the endpoint}\label{sub:FromFiniteRectanglesToClassicalEstimates}

Start with the velocity row that opened the singularity argument.  Choose
\((N,M)=(0,1)\) in \eqref{eq:BodyWholeTerminalAdjacentRows}.  Then
\[
  u\in L^2(I_*;H^2),
  \qquad
  u_t\in L^2(I_*;L^2).
\]
The adjacent trace identity carries these two rows into the missing middle space:
\[
  u\in C([0,T_*];H^1).
\]
Since \(H^1(\R^3)\hookrightarrow L^3(\R^3)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:EndpointCriterionFromLowRectangle}
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}\norm{u(t)}_3<\infty.
\end{equation}
But \eqref{eq:FiniteTimeLThreeEscape} said that a finite maximal time forces this very
norm to escape.  The lowest critical rectangle already puts the singularity and the
estimate on the same velocity, in the same norm, over the same terminal interval.

Now read an arbitrary coordinate.  Fix \(r,m\in\Nzero\), choose \(N\geq r\), and
take the rectangle of middle height \(M=m\).  Its adjacent rows give
\[
  V_r\in L^2(I_*;H^{m+1}),
  \qquad
  V_{r+1}=\partial_tV_r\in L^2(I_*;H^{m-1}).
\]
The same trace calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyUniformMixedSobolevCoordinate}
  \boxed{
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}
  \norm{\partial_t^ru(t)}_{H^m}
  <\infty.}
\end{equation}
Nothing in this step couples \(r\) to \(m\).  We may choose any finite temporal
response and then ask for any finite spatial height inside that same row.

Given \(k\in\Nzero\) and \(2\leq p\leq\infty\), choose an integer \(m\) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SobolevProjectionThreshold}
  m>k+\frac32-\frac3p.
\end{equation}
Spatial Sobolev embedding gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasterSpatialProjection}
  \norm{\nabla^k\partial_t^ru(t)}_{L^p}
  \leq C_{k,m,p}\norm{\partial_t^ru(t)}_{H^m}.
\end{equation}
Combining this with \eqref{eq:BodyUniformMixedSobolevCoordinate},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasterUniformMixedEstimate}
  \nabla^k\partial_t^ru
  \in L^\infty(I_*;L^p(\R^3)).
\end{equation}
Since \(T_*\) is finite under the standing contradiction hypothesis,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasterFiniteTimeMixedEstimate}
  \nabla^k\partial_t^ru
  \in L^q(I_*;L^p(\R^3))
  \qquad(1\leq q\leq\infty).
\end{equation}
The pressure is carried by these same finite coordinates.
For fixed \(r,m\in\Nzero\), the normalized pressure formula \eqref{eq:BodyTemporalPressureRecurrence} contains a finite sum of products of the rows \(V_0,\ldots,V_r\).
The adjacent traces place those rows continuously in every finite Sobolev space, so Sobolev multiplication and the Riesz-transform bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasterPressureSobolevCoordinate}
  \partial_t^rp=Q_r
  \in C([0,T_*];H^m(\R^3)).
\end{equation}
For finite \(k\), \(2\leq p\leq\infty\), and \(1\leq q\leq\infty\), choose \(m>k+\tfrac32-\tfrac3p\).
The same spatial embedding and the finiteness of \(T_*\) then give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasterFiniteTimePressureEstimate}
  \nabla^k\partial_t^rp
  \in L^q(I_*;L^p(\R^3)).
\end{equation}
Each requested norm uses one finite rectangle, and the compatible intersection keeps
these finite coordinates together without replacing them by an all-orders sum.
Fix \(r\).  The singularity asks \(V_r\) to
escape in \(L^3\), while the coordinate \((r,1)\) places that same \(V_r\) continuously
in \(H^1\hookrightarrow L^3\).  The two independent directions of the grid have met
inside one response row, and they give opposite conclusions.

\subsection{The critical-height roof}\label{sub:ClassicalContinuationCriteriaInsideFiniteRectangles}

The same \(H^1\) trace does more than block the \(L^3\) escape.  Since
\(H=\tfrac12\norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u}_2^2\), it puts a finite roof over the entire
first-passage construction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightRoof}
  H_{\max}
  :=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}H(t)
  \leq\frac12\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}\norm{u(t)}_{H^1}^2
  <\infty.
\end{equation}
Only finitely many of the first-passage levels \(L_k=2^kH_0\) fit below this roof.
If \(N_{\mathrm{levels}}\) denotes the number reached, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyFiniteZenoOctaveCount}
  N_{\mathrm{levels}}
  \leq
  \begin{cases}
    0,&H_{\max}<H_0,\\[0.25em]
    1+\left\lfloor\log_2(H_{\max}/H_0)\right\rfloor,
      &H_{\max}\geq H_0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
No estimate on the duration of an individual crossing is needed.
The infinite Zeno ladder fails because the height cannot cross beyond a finite roof.

The next low rectangle, \(\cR_{0,2}\), controls how the height moves beneath that
roof:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LowRectangleZeroTwo}
  u\in L^2(I_*;H^3),
  \qquad
  u_t\in L^2(I_*;H^1),
  \qquad
  u\in C([0,T_*];H^2).
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
  H'(t)
  =\operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)}{\Lambda^{1/2}u_t(t)}_{L^2},
\]
and Cauchy--Schwarz in time gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CriticalHeightTotalVariationBound}
  \int_0^{T_*}|H'(t)|\,dt
  \leq
  \norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u}_{L^2_tL^2_x}
  \norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u_t}_{L^2_tL^2_x}
  <\infty.
\end{equation}
Also \(E_{3/2}\in L^1(I_*)\), so the first completed row
\[
  \mathcal P_{1/2}=H'+2\nu E_{3/2}
\]
shows that the signed critical production is integrable on \(I_*\).
The height can rise and fall sharply, but it cannot accumulate an infinite net rise before \(T_*\).

The same rectangle also gives the physical control needed for the vortex.  In three dimensions,
\(H^3\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}\), and hence
\[
  \int_0^{T_*}\norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty\,dt
  \leq
  T_*^{1/2}\norm{u}_{L^2(I_*;H^3)}<\infty.
\]
Since \(\omega=\nabla\times u\) and \(S=\tfrac12(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T})\),
we obtain the full finite stretching action
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FiniteLipschitzVorticityStrainAction}
  \int_0^{T_*}
  \left(
    \norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty
    +\norm{\omega(t)}_\infty
    +\norm{S(t)}_\infty
  \right)dt
  <\infty.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The two vortex readings}\label{sub:ClosingCriticalHeightZenoAndTheVortex}

There were two possible meanings of the narrowing core in Section~2.  Take them one at
a time.  First suppose the core continues to contain the same material labels.
If \(X(a,t)\) and \(X(b,t)\) are two trajectories in that core, then, for \(t<T_*\),
\[
  \frac d{dt}|X(a,t)-X(b,t)|
  \geq
  -\norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty|X(a,t)-X(b,t)|.
\]
The finite action in \eqref{eq:FiniteLipschitzVorticityStrainAction} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMaterialCoreLowerBound}
  |X(a,t)-X(b,t)|
  \geq
  |a-b|
  \exp\!\left(
    -\int_0^t\norm{\nabla u(s)}_\infty\,ds
  \right)
  >0
\end{equation}
The same \(L^2(I_*;H^3)\) row gives \(u\in L^1(I_*;L^\infty)\), so each trajectory
extends continuously to \(T_*\).  Passing to the limit above gives
\[
  |X(a,T_*)-X(b,T_*)|
  \geq |a-b|\exp\!\left(-\int_0^{T_*}\norm{\nabla u(s)}_\infty\,ds\right)>0.
\]
A material core begins with distinct particles, and those particles remain distinct at
the terminal time.  The drawn radius may shrink, but it cannot represent the collapse of
that same labelled core to one point.

Keep this first reading and now put the vortex exactly on its centreline.
The finite coordinate \((r,m)=(0,3)\) in \eqref{eq:BodyUniformMixedSobolevCoordinate} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyUniformLipschitzBound}
  L_*:=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}\norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty<\infty.
\end{equation}
At a chosen centre \(x_c(t)\),
\[
  |u(t,x)-u(t,x_c)|\leq L_*|x-x_c|.
\]
Let \(C_r(t)\) be a circle of radius \(r\) in a cross-section normal to the vortex axis.
The constant vector \(u(t,x_c)\) integrates to zero around the closed curve, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCenteredCirculationBound}
  \left|
    \oint_{C_r(t)}u(t,x)\cdot d\ell
  \right|
  \leq2\pi L_*r^2
  \longrightarrow0
  \qquad(r\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
Thus perfect centring changes neither conclusion.  If the radius tends to zero while
the velocity remains Lipschitz, the relative tangential speed is \(O(r)\) and the
circulation is \(O(r^2)\); no nonzero circulation survives at the limiting point.

The second reading lets the visible core recruit new material as earlier labels leave
it.
Particle separation no longer follows the changing membership of that core, so the test moves to the whole-field high-frequency tail.
For \(s>1/2\) and \(K\geq1\), define
\[
  H_{>K}(t)
  :=\frac12\int_{|\xi|>K}
  |\xi|\,|\widehat u(t,\xi)|^2\,d\xi.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyUltravioletCriticalTail}
  H_{>K}(t)
  \leq
  \frac12K^{1-2s}\norm{u(t)}_{\dot H^s}^2.
\end{equation}
Choose an integer \(m\geq s\).
Equation \eqref{eq:BodyUniformMixedSobolevCoordinate} with \(r=0\), together with
\(H^m\hookrightarrow\dot H^s\), gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyUniformUltravioletVanishing}
  \lim_{K\to\infty}
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}H_{>K}(t)=0.
\end{equation}
Even when material is continually recruited, the complete velocity field cannot retain a fixed amount of critical content beyond every frequency.

The two meanings of the core now have separate conclusions.  Fixed material labels
retain positive separation at \(T_*\).  Changing membership cannot preserve a nonzero
whole-field critical tail beyond every frequency.  The perfectly stretching vortex
reaches zero scale in neither of the two singular senses posed in Section~2.

\subsection{No finite singular time}\label{sub:NoFiniteSingularTime}

\begin{theorem}[Whole-space existence and smoothness]\label{thm:MainGlobalRegularity}
For every \(\nu>0\) and every \(u_0\in\Ssigma\), the maximal classical solution of \eqref{eq:NavierStokesSystem} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyGlobalSmoothnessConclusion}
  T_*=\infty,
  \qquad
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For every \(t\geq0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GlobalEnergyConclusion}
  \frac12\norm{u(t)}_2^2
  +\nu\int_0^t\norm{\nabla u(s)}_2^2\,ds
  =\frac12\norm{u_0}_2^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume that \(T_*<\infty\).
\Cref{thm:BodyWholeTerminalRectangleTheorem} supplies every finite pressure-complete rectangle on \(I_*\).
Equation \eqref{eq:DatumSpecificCompatibleIntersection} assembles their shared coordinates into \(\cI[u_0]\), and \Cref{prop:BodyCompatibleProjectiveAssembly} gives its complete spatial row \eqref{eq:CompleteSpatialRow}.
\Cref{lem:CompleteSpatialRowEndpoint} carries the same velocity to a common endpoint \(u_*\in\Hinfty\), while \Cref{prop:PressureCompleteEndpointJet} carries every temporal row and its normalized pressure response to that terminal face.
Starting from \(u_*\), \Cref{lem:SmoothLocalRestart} produces a smooth solution on the right of \(T_*\).
Both one-sided jets are generated from \(u_*\) by the same pressure-normalized recurrences, and strong uniqueness joins the two pieces into one classical history beyond \(T_*\).
This contradicts maximality.

Hence \(T_*=\infty\).  Every finite interval now lies inside the maximal
classical history, so \(u\) is smooth there; the normalized pressure formula
gives the same conclusion for \(p\).  Thus
\[
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:BodyGlobalSmoothnessConclusion}.

The energy identity \eqref{eq:BodyEnergyIdentity} holds on every finite
interval of this global solution and gives \eqref{eq:GlobalEnergyConclusion}.
In particular,
\[
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx
  \leq
  \int_{\R^3}|u_0(x)|^2\,dx
  <\infty,
\]
where the last finiteness follows from the rapid decay in
\eqref{eq:FeffermanDatumClass}.  Global smoothness of \(u,p\) and the
uniform kinetic-energy bound are exactly the two conclusions stated in
\eqref{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}.
\end{proof}

\section{Further Consequences and the Euler Boundary}\label{sec:FurtherConsequencesAndEulerBoundary}

\subsection{A finite sum across infinitely many scales}\label{sub:AFiniteSumAcrossScales}

Fix any finite \(T>0\).  The global solution is classical on \([0,T]\), so its finite
block \(\cR_{0,2}[u_0;T]\) controls one Sobolev row strongly enough to sum the
vorticity carried by every dyadic scale.
Let \((\Delta_j)_{j\geq-1}\) be an inhomogeneous Littlewood--Paley decomposition \parencite[Chapter~2]{BahouriCheminDanchin2011}.
For \(j\geq0\), the block \(\Delta_j\omega\) is concentrated near wavelength \(2^{-j}\), and \(\norm{\Delta_j\omega}_\infty\) measures the strongest rotation present in that band.
Define
\[
  \norm{\omega}_{B^0_{\infty,1}}
  :=\sum_{j\geq-1}\norm{\Delta_j\omega}_\infty.
\]
Bernstein's inequality gives, for \(j\geq0\),
\[
  \norm{\Delta_j\omega}_\infty
  \leq C2^{3j/2}\norm{\Delta_j\omega}_2.
\]
Insert \(2^{3j/2}=2^{-j/2}2^{2j}\), sum, and use Cauchy--Schwarz in the shell index:
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{j\geq0}\norm{\Delta_j\omega}_\infty
  &\leq
  C\left(\sum_{j\geq0}2^{-j}\right)^{1/2}
  \left(\sum_{j\geq0}2^{4j}\norm{\Delta_j\omega}_2^2\right)^{1/2}
  \\
  &\leq C\norm{\omega}_{H^2}
  \leq C\norm{u}_{H^3}.
\end{align*}
Bernstein costs \(3/2\) derivatives in each high-frequency shell.  The \(H^2\)
vorticity weight supplies two derivatives, leaving the factor \(2^{-j/2}\); its square
is summable.  That spare half derivative is what turns one finite Sobolev row into an
\(\ell^1\) sum across all dyadic shells.
The low block \(\Delta_{-1}\omega\) is controlled by \(\norm{\omega}_2\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VorticityBesovFromHThree}
  \norm{\omega}_{B^0_{\infty,1}}
  \leq C\norm{u}_{H^3}.
\end{equation}
Since \(\cR_{0,2}[u_0;T]\) gives \(u\in L^2(0,T;H^3)\), Cauchy--Schwarz in time yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FiniteDyadicVorticityAction}
  \sum_{j\geq-1}
  \int_0^{T}\norm{\Delta_j\omega(t)}_\infty\,dt
  =\int_0^{T}\norm{\omega(t)}_{B^0_{\infty,1}}\,dt
  <\infty.
\end{equation}
What has been made finite is the sum across all shells, not the number of shells that
may become active.  A recruited core can hand activity from one scale to the next, but
the time-integrated shell suprema cannot build a nonsummable cascade before a finite
time.  On every chosen \([0,T]\), the same low rectangle supplies \(L^2_tH^3_x\) for
this shell action and \(C_tH^2_x\) for uniform decay of the critical Fourier tail.
This is still a finite-order consequence:
it does not assert an analytic radius or a weighted sum over every derivative order.

\subsection{Three classical criteria inside one low rectangle}\label{sub:ClassicalCriteriaInsideFiniteRectangles}

The \(L^3\) endpoint first entered as the escape forced by a finite last time.  Now fix
any finite \(T>0\).  The block \(\cR_{0,1}[u_0;T]\) returns to that same norm from the
other direction.  Its adjacent pair gives \(u\in C([0,T];H^1)\), and hence
\[
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\norm{u(t)}_3<\infty.
\]
Thus \(\cR_{0,1}\) supplies the hypothesis of the Escauriaza--Seregin--\v{S}ver\'ak endpoint continuation theorem \parencite{EscauriazaSereginSverak2003}.
The norm that defined the singular threat is already bounded by the first critical rectangle.

The upper edge of the same rectangle gives a different historical reading:
\[
  u\in L^2(0,T;H^2)
  \hookrightarrow L^2(0,T;L^\infty).
\]
This is the endpoint pair \((q,p)=(2,\infty)\) on the Ladyzhenskaya--Prodi--Serrin line \(2/q+3/p=1\) \parencite{Prodi1959,Serrin1962,Serrin1963,Ladyzhenskaya1967}.
The first meets the exact \(L^3\) norm that defined the terminal threat.  The second
places the same velocity on the classical scale-balanced integrability line.

There is a third reading closer to the vortex itself.  From
\(u\in L^2(0,T;H^2)\),
\[
  \nabla u\in L^2(0,T;H^1)
  \hookrightarrow L^2(0,T;L^3),
\]
which supplies the gradient criterion of Beir\~ao da Veiga
\parencite{BeiraoDaVeiga1995}.  The rectangle that blocks velocity escape also
controls the shear responsible for vortex stretching.  Together, the three criteria
read velocity escape, scale-balanced integrability, and vortex-producing shear from the
same low finite block.  Repeating the finite-coordinate calculation on the chosen
\([0,T]\) gives, for finite \(r,k\in\Nzero\), \(2\leq p\leq\infty\), and
\(1\leq q\leq\infty\),
\[
  \nabla^k\partial_t^ru\in L^q(0,T;L^p(\R^3)),
\]
after choosing a finite Sobolev height
\(m>k+\tfrac32-\tfrac3p\).

\subsection{Why the Euler Tower is different}\label{sub:WhyTheEulerTowerIsDifferent}

Now remove viscosity and watch exactly which part of the argument disappears.  The
mixed derivative jet can still be written for Euler; the spatial ascent that drives
this paper cannot.
To keep the two histories distinct, write
\[
  V_n^{\mathrm{NS}}:=\partial_t^nu^{\mathrm{NS}},
  \qquad
  Q_n^{\mathrm{NS}}:=\partial_t^np^{\mathrm{NS}},
\]
and define \(V_n^{\mathrm E},Q_n^{\mathrm E}\) from a separate Euler solution.
Their temporal recurrences are
\begin{align}
  V_{n+1}^{\mathrm{NS}}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a^{\mathrm{NS}}\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}^{\mathrm{NS}}
  +\nabla Q_n^{\mathrm{NS}}
  =\nu\Delta V_n^{\mathrm{NS}},
  \label{eq:NSTemporalRecurrenceComparison}
  \\
  V_{n+1}^{\mathrm E}
  &+
  \sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{n}{a}
  (V_a^{\mathrm E}\cdot\nabla)V_{n-a}^{\mathrm E}
  +\nabla Q_n^{\mathrm E}
  =0.
  \label{eq:EulerTemporalRecurrenceComparison}
\end{align}
Both equations retain the differentiated transport and the nonlocal pressure response.
Only Navier--Stokes keeps the term \(\nu\Delta V_n^{\mathrm{NS}}\), the linear path
from temporal row \(n\) to two additional spatial derivatives of that same response.

For the two separate histories, set
\[
  E_s^{\mathrm{NS}}:=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su^{\mathrm{NS}}}_2^2,
  \qquad
  E_s^{\mathrm E}:=\frac12\norm{\Lambda^su^{\mathrm E}}_2^2,
\]
with corresponding transfer terms \(\mathcal P_s^{\mathrm{NS}}\) and \(\mathcal P_s^{\mathrm E}\).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NSEulerEnergyComparison}
  (E_s^{\mathrm{NS}})'
  =\mathcal P_s^{\mathrm{NS}}-2\nu E_{s+1}^{\mathrm{NS}},
  \qquad
  (E_s^{\mathrm E})'
  =\mathcal P_s^{\mathrm E}.
\end{equation}
For Navier--Stokes, repeated differentiation and division by \((-2\nu)^n\) isolate \(E_{n+1/2}^{\mathrm{NS}}\) in \eqref{eq:BodyCompletedCriticalRow}.
Euler retains the nonlinear mixing of time and space, but it has no signed coercive rung that isolates the next Sobolev height in this way.

An Euler history lying in an all-orders compatible intersection would satisfy every
fixed Sobolev bound and, in particular, the Beale--Kato--Majda condition
\(\int_0^T\|\omega^{\mathrm E}(t)\|_\infty\,dt<\infty\)
\parencite{BealeKatoMajda1984}.
The fixed-viscosity theorem used here does not supply that Euler intersection, and its constants are not claimed to remain bounded as \(\nu\downarrow0\).
This is the exact boundary of the method.  Euler keeps the mixed jet and all of its
nonlinear coupling.  What it loses is the signed viscous rung that lets the completed
row isolate the next Sobolev height.
