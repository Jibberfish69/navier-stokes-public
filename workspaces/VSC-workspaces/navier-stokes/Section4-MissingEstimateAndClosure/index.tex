\section{The Missing Estimate and Global Smoothness}\label{sec:TheMissingEstimateAndGlobalSmoothness}

Now let the cutoff move.  Keep \((N,M)\) fixed while \(T\uparrow T_*\).
Classical smoothness answers every fixed \(T<T_*\) separately; the argument
needs one bound that survives them all.  The order of those two quantifiers is
the entire question.

\subsection{The intersection reaches one terminal datum}\label{sub:NoFiniteSingularTime}

Keep the alleged finite branch in view.  Under
\Cref{ass:BodyWholeTerminalRectangleCondition}, the mixed intersection does not
end as a list of norms.  Its adjacent rows force every spatial reading of the
same velocity to arrive at the same time slice.

\begin{proposition}[One terminal datum from the mixed intersection]
\label[proposition]{prop:BodyTerminalDatumFromIntersection}
Suppose \(T_*<\infty\) and the maximal history obeys
\Cref{ass:BodyWholeTerminalRectangleCondition}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCommonSmoothEndpoint}
  u_*:=\lim_{t\uparrow T_*}u(t)
  \in\Hinfty_\sigma(\R^3)
  =\bigcap_{m<\infty}H^m_\sigma(\R^3).
\end{equation}
Every temporal response has one trace
\[
  V_N^*:=\lim_{t\uparrow T_*}\partial_t^Nu(t)\in\Hinfty(\R^3),
  \qquad N\in\Nzero,
\]
and those traces retain the normalized pressure and velocity recurrences
\begin{align*}
  Q_N^*
  &=R_iR_j\sum_{a=0}^{N}\binom{N}{a}V_{a,i}^*V_{N-a,j}^*,
  \\
  V_{N+1}^*
  &=\nu\Delta V_N^*
  -\sum_{a=0}^{N}\binom{N}{a}(V_a^*\cdot\nabla)V_{N-a}^*
  -\nabla Q_N^*.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{prop:BodyCompatibleProjectiveAssembly} gives
\[
  u\in\bigcap_{r,m<\infty}H^r(I_*;H^m(\R^3)).
\]
Hold one spatial height \(m\) fixed.  The temporal row \(r=1\) puts \(u\) in
\(H^1(I_*;H^m)\), so the actual history has an \(H^m\) limit as
\(t\uparrow T_*\).  A higher spatial row cannot produce a different limit:
after embedding it into \(H^m\), both limits come from the same \(u(t)\).
The complete-row argument in \Cref{lem:CompleteSpatialRowEndpoint} makes these
limits the one datum in \eqref{eq:BodyCommonSmoothEndpoint}.

Now hold \(N\) fixed.  The row \(r=N+1\) places
\(V_N=\partial_t^Nu\) in \(H^1(I_*;H^m)\) for every finite \(m\).  Its trace
is \(V_N^*\in\Hinfty\).  Passing to the limit in the two recurrences identifies
the pressure and the next velocity response exactly as written above; this is
the pressure-complete endpoint jet proved in
\Cref{prop:PressureCompleteEndpointJet}.
\end{proof}

The alleged last time has now become a genuine smooth time slice.  Do not
restart it yet.  First return, on this same finite branch, to the critical
height and to the gradient that stretches the vortex.  Every conclusion through
\Cref{sub:TheLocalSingularityPictureCloses} still depends on the whole-terminal
rectangle condition; none of them derives that condition from \((u_0,\nu)\).

\subsection{The critical-height roof}\label{sub:ClassicalContinuationCriteriaInsideFiniteRectangles}

On this alleged finite branch, once the \(H^1\) trace reaches the terminal face,
the critical height cannot keep climbing underneath it.  Indeed,
\(H=\tfrac12\norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u}_2^2\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCriticalHeightRoof}
  H_{\max}
  :=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}H(t)
  \leq\frac12\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}\norm{u(t)}_{H^1}^2
  <\infty.
\end{equation}
Start at any positive height \(H_0\), double it successively by
\(L_k:=2^kH_0\), and count the levels the same history actually reaches:
\[
  N_{\mathrm{levels}}
  :=
  \#\bigl\{k\in\Nzero:\text{there is a }t\in[0,T_*]\text{ with }H(t)=L_k\bigr\}.
\]
The roof leaves room for only finitely many such doublings:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyFiniteZenoOctaveCount}
  N_{\mathrm{levels}}
  \leq
  \begin{cases}
    0,&H_{\max}<H_0,\\
    1+\left\lfloor\log_2(H_{\max}/H_0)\right\rfloor,
      &H_{\max}\geq H_0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This conclusion never asks how quickly one level is crossed.  It rules out an
infinite succession of critical-height doublings.  It does not rule out an
infinite succession of smaller widths at the same height: the rescaled profile
in Section~\ref{sec:CriticalHeightAndDerivativeTower} was built to show exactly
that possibility.  The roof closes escalation in \(H\); the geometric picture
still has to close concentration at fixed \(H\).

The next rectangle, \(\cR_{0,2}\), follows the motion below the roof rather than
only its maximum:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LowRectangleZeroTwo}
  u\in L^2(I_*;H^3),
  \qquad
  u_t\in L^2(I_*;H^1),
  \qquad
  u\in C([0,T_*];H^2).
\end{equation}
The two adjacent rows now contain both factors in the derivative
\[
  H'(t)
  =\operatorname{Re}
  \pair{\Lambda^{1/2}u(t)}{\Lambda^{1/2}u_t(t)}_{L^2},
\]
and Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its total variation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CriticalHeightTotalVariationBound}
  \int_0^{T_*}|H'(t)|\,dt
  \leq
  \norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u}_{L^2_tL^2_x}
  \norm{\Lambda^{1/2}u_t}_{L^2_tL^2_x}
  <\infty.
\end{equation}
For a vortex, the decisive entry in \(\cR_{0,2}\) is
\(u\in L^2(I_*;H^3)\).  In three dimensions
\(H^3\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}\), whence
\[
  \int_0^{T_*}\norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty\,dt
  \leq
  T_*^{1/2}\norm{u}_{L^2(I_*;H^3)}<\infty.
\]
Both vorticity and strain are parts of this gradient.  Their accumulated action
before \(T_*\) is finite in the precise sense
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FiniteLipschitzVorticityStrainAction}
  \int_0^{T_*}
  \left(
    \norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty
    +\norm{\omega(t)}_\infty
    +\norm{S(t)}_\infty
  \right)dt
  <\infty.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The two vortex readings}\label{sub:ClosingCriticalHeightZenoAndTheVortex}

What does a narrowing core mean for the fluid inside it?
There are two answers to separate.  The first follows the same material
particles.  The second allows the visible concentration to replace its
particles as it moves.  Neither exhausts singular geometry, but each makes a
different claim that the rectangle bounds can test.

Follow the fixed labels first.
Let \(X(a,t)\) solve
\[
  \partial_tX(a,t)=u(X(a,t),t),
  \qquad
  X(a,0)=a.
\]
Two such trajectories satisfy
\[
  \frac d{dt}|X(a,t)-X(b,t)|
  \geq
  -\norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty|X(a,t)-X(b,t)|,
\]
and the finite Lipschitz action integrates this differential inequality to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyMaterialCoreLowerBound}
  |X(a,t)-X(b,t)|
  \geq
  |a-b|
  \exp\!\left(
    -\int_0^t\norm{\nabla u(s)}_\infty\,ds
  \right)
  >0
\end{equation}
Because the same row also puts \(u\) in \(L^1(I_*;L^\infty)\), every trajectory
has a limit at \(T_*\).  Letting \(t\uparrow T_*\) preserves the lower bound:
\[
  |X(a,T_*)-X(b,T_*)|
  \geq |a-b|\exp\!\left(-\int_0^{T_*}\norm{\nabla u(s)}_\infty\,ds\right)>0.
\]
Distinct labels remain spatially distinct at the terminal time.
If a drawn core collapses to one point, it cannot still contain exactly the
material core with which it began.

Even perfect centring cannot rescue a nonzero circulation from this fixed-label
picture.  The adjacent trace of \(\cR_{0,3}\) gives
\(u\in C([0,T_*];H^3)\); since \(H^3(\R^3)\hookrightarrow
W^{1,\infty}(\R^3)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyUniformLipschitzBound}
  L_*:=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}\norm{\nabla u(t)}_\infty<\infty.
\end{equation}
Relative velocity at any chosen centre \(x_c(t)\) obeys
\[
  |u(t,x)-u(t,x_c)|\leq L_*|x-x_c|.
\]
Around a circular cross-section \(C_r(t)\), the constant centre velocity
integrates to zero.  What remains is bounded by its Lipschitz variation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyCenteredCirculationBound}
  \left|
    \oint_{C_r(t)}u(t,x)\cdot d\ell
  \right|
  \leq2\pi L_*r^2
  \longrightarrow0
  \qquad(r\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
As \(r\) shrinks, the tangential velocity relative to the centre is \(O(r)\)
and the circuit has length \(O(r)\).  Their product is \(O(r^2)\), so this
centred material core cannot carry a nonzero limiting circulation.

Now let membership change.  Particle separation no longer tracks the visible
core.  One precise spectral version of repeated recruitment says that a fixed
amount of critical content survives at smaller and smaller wavelengths.  That
version can be tested in the high-frequency tail of the whole velocity field.
For \(s>1/2\) and \(K\geq1\), define
\[
  H_{>K}(t)
  :=\frac12\int_{|\xi|>K}
  |\xi|\,|\widehat u(t,\xi)|^2\,d\xi.
\]
For every \(s>1/2\), the tail satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyUltravioletCriticalTail}
  H_{>K}(t)
  \leq
  \frac12K^{1-2s}\norm{u(t)}_{\dot H^s}^2.
\end{equation}
Choose one integer \(m\geq s\).  The compatible assembly and adjacent trace
proved in \Cref{prop:BodyCompatibleProjectiveAssembly,lem:AdjacentHilbertScaleTrace},
followed by \(H^m\hookrightarrow\dot H^s\), make the estimate uniform in time
and force
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyUniformUltravioletVanishing}
  \lim_{K\to\infty}
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}H_{>K}(t)=0.
\end{equation}
Activity may move between scales, but it cannot leave a fixed amount of critical
height beyond every cutoff.  This rules out the spectral version just stated.
It does not prove that every geometrically narrowing core with changing
membership must create such a tail; that geometric-to-Fourier implication would
require a separate localization argument.  What the two calculations establish
is narrower: fixed labels cannot collapse and their centred circulation
vanishes, while a changing-membership model cannot preserve a fixed critical
tail arbitrarily far into the ultraviolet.

\subsection{The singularity conditions fail at both scales}\label{sub:TheLocalSingularityPictureCloses}

The local signature disappears at the same alleged endpoint.  The common
terminal trace in \Cref{lem:CompleteSpatialRowEndpoint} puts \(u\) in
\(C([0,T_*];H^m)\) at every finite height.  Choose
\(m>3/2\).  Since \(H^m(\R^3)\) is an algebra, the normalized pressure formula
\(p=R_iR_j(u_i u_j)\) gives the common ceiling
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FiniteIntervalVelocityPressureSupremum}
  K_*
  :=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_*}
  \left(
    \norm{u(t)}_{L^\infty}
    +\norm{p(t)}_{L^\infty}
  \right)
  <\infty.
\end{equation}
Now choose (z_*=(x_*,T_*)) on the terminal face and place a backward
parabolic cylinder there:
\[
  Q_\rho(z_*)
  =B_\rho(x_*)\times(T_*-\rho^2,T_*)
  \subset\R^3\times(0,T_*].
\]
Its spatial volume and time length contribute \(\rho^5\).  Dividing by the
critical factor \(\rho^2\) still leaves three powers of the radius:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VanishingLocalScaleInvariantContent}
  \rho^{-2}
  \iint_{Q_\rho(z_*)}
  \left(|u|^3+|p|^{3/2}\right)\,dx\,dt
  \leq
  C\rho^3\left(K_*^3+K_*^{3/2}\right)
  \longrightarrow0
  \qquad(\rho\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
A Caffarelli--Kohn--Nirenberg singular point would require a positive
scale-invariant amount of velocity-pressure content to survive along shrinking
cylinders \parencite{CaffarelliKohnNirenberg1982}.  Here that content vanishes.

This is the return promised by the vortex picture.  The alleged endpoint has
become a smooth time slice.  The critical height cannot climb through
infinitely many doublings, but that roof is not being used as a bound on the
number of spatial scales.  The remaining calculations answer the precise
fixed-height scenarios posed above: the stretching action is integrable, a
fixed material core cannot collapse its labels, a fixed critical tail cannot
survive arbitrarily far into high frequency, and the local velocity-pressure
content loses the mass a singular point would need at every smaller scale.

\subsection{Restarting the same history}\label{sub:ConditionalGlobalRestart}

The terminal slice still belongs to the history that began at \(u_0\).  It can
now be used as initial data without changing the velocity, the pressure
normalization, or any response by which the history arrived there.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional global closure from whole-terminal rectangles]
\label{thm:MainGlobalRegularity}
Let \(\nu>0\) and \(u_0\in\Ssigma\), and assume that the maximal classical
history generated by \((u_0,\nu)\) obeys
\Cref{ass:BodyWholeTerminalRectangleCondition}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BodyGlobalSmoothnessConclusion}
  T_*=\infty,
  \qquad
  u,p\in C^\infty\!\bigl(\R^3\times[0,\infty)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For every \(t\geq0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GlobalEnergyConclusion}
  \frac12\norm{u(t)}_2^2
  +\nu\int_0^t\norm{\nabla u(s)}_2^2\,ds
  =\frac12\norm{u_0}_2^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose \(T_*<\infty\).  The rectangle condition activates on this branch, and
\Cref{prop:BodyTerminalDatumFromIntersection} gives the common smooth datum
\(u_*\) and its pressure-complete left jet.  Use \(u_*\) as initial data at time
\(T_*\).  The smooth local construction in \Cref{lem:SmoothLocalRestart} gives a
right-hand history on \([T_*,T_*+\delta]\).  Its recurrence begins from the same
datum and the same pressure normalization, so its jet agrees with the left-hand
traces.  The join is smooth.  Strong uniqueness makes it the continuation of
the history generated by \(u_0\), contradicting the definition of \(T_*\).

Hence \(T_*=\infty\), and the normalized pair is smooth on every finite time
interval.  The kinetic-energy identity in
\Cref{prop:ClassicalKineticEnergyIdentity} holds on every initial segment and
gives \eqref{eq:GlobalEnergyConclusion}.  In particular,
\[
  \sup_{t\geq0}\int_{\R^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx
  \leq
  \int_{\R^3}|u_0(x)|^2\,dx
  <\infty.
\]
This is the global history required in
\eqref{eq:WholeSpaceRegularityTarget}.
\end{proof}

The restart closes the implication from whole-terminal rectangles to global
smoothness.  The unpaid direction is still the one isolated in
\eqref{eq:BodyFirstRectangleSignedPrefixEquivalence}: this manuscript has not
derived even the lowest rectangle, or equivalently \(D_*<\infty\), from an
arbitrary pair \((u_0,\nu)\).
